## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 1860 by James Paterson (ex-Pickfords) with Walter Carter backing; started with six horse vans at George Yard, Aldermanbury
- **Formalized:** Carter, Paterson & Co., Ltd., 1887 — aligning with Charles Norman's employment from mid-1880s
- **Head office:** 128 Goswell Road, EC1 (Clerkenwell); adjacent operations on Central Street
- **Chiswell Street presence by 1883** — coincides with the start of parcel post and with Charles Norman starting work there
- **Scale by 1890:** 700 horses, some stabled on upper floors; three daily collections/deliveries in London
- **First motor van:** 1897 Daimler 10-cwt; by 1944 over 1,000 motor vans
- **1931:** Handled 21 million parcels
- **1933:** Bought by Big Four railways under Hays Wharf Cartage; went public 1934
- **1948:** Nationalized into British Transport Commission / British Road Services; green livery retained
- **Legacy:** BRS parcels became Royal Mail Parcelforce Worldwide in 1980s–1990s

## Notes

No direct records tie Carter Paterson to a Smithfield depot, but the Goswell Road / Chiswell Street cluster is immediately north of Smithfield. Charles Norman's role as foreman horse keeper at the "Smithfield Carter Paterson stables" is consistent with a depot in that corridor. The company's massive horse operation (700 horses by 1890) would have required multiple stabling points across central London.

Research leads for Charles Norman specifically: Kelly's Post Office London Directory (1880s, via Ancestry/TheGenealogist), National Archives Rail 1130 series. Published history: *Transport Saga, 1646–1947* by Hays Wharf Cartage (1947).

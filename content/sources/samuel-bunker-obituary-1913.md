Funeral notice for Samuel Bunker, published in the *Bedfordshire Times and Independent*, 20 June 1913. Found via FindMyPast search: "bunker" + "millbrook" + "sexton", 1900–1930. Raw file: `Search Newspapers 3.md`.

## Text (as OCR'd)

> MILLBROOK. The funeral took place on Monday of the late Mr. Samuel, [in] the churchyard. The deceased had been for about 20 years clerk and [sexton] at the parish church, until the present Rector came to [Millbrook]. His father also [served in the same capacity].

The word "sexton" is confirmed by the search having matched on that term. The snippet OCR drops some words; bracketed insertions reflect the evident meaning.

## Key facts established

- **Samuel Bunker (1842–1913) was the Millbrook church clerk and sexton** for approximately 20 years.
- His role ended "until the present Rector came" — suggesting a new incumbent discontinued the appointment or Samuel retired around that time.
- His **father John Bunker (1803–1889) had also served** as clerk and/or sexton at Millbrook — making this a two-generation parish role in the same family.
- Samuel died in 1913; his funeral was held on a Monday (probably 16 June 1913) and he was buried in the Millbrook churchyard — the very churchyard where he had served.

## Significance for the Huett effigy story

This obituary resolves the sexton question entirely. [[samuel-bunker-1842|Samuel Bunker]] was the Millbrook church sexton. His widow [[harriet-dean-1844|Harriet Dean (1844–1931)]] is therefore the **Mrs Bunker** of the 1919 Huett effigy story — the aged widow who led the excavation party to the buried effigies and produced the stone head her late husband had kept. Harriet was approximately 75 in 1919 and is confirmed alive until 1931.

[[mary-ann-wilcox-1842|Mary Ann Wilcox]], wife of Joseph (1840–1914), was a good candidate before this obituary was found, but is not Mrs Bunker.

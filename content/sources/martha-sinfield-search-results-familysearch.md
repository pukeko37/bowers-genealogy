## Key finding: John Sinfield in Millbrook, 1757

The third result — **Martha Sinfeild, christened 18 May 1757, Millbrook, daughter of John Sinfeild and Martha** — places a Sinfield family in the very village where the 1761 marriage took place. This baby Martha, born 1757, is too young to be the bride herself (who must have been born roughly 1736–1741 to be of marriageable age in 1761). She was almost certainly a younger sister — or a child named after a deceased older sibling of the same name, a common pattern.

**Most probable interpretation:** John Sinfield and his wife Martha were living in Millbrook in 1757 and had at least two daughters named Martha — one born ~1736–1741 who married Thomas Bunker in 1761, and another born May 1757. The earlier daughter's birth record has not been found. This may reflect a Nonconformist family whose births were not registered in the Church of England, or records simply not yet digitised.

## Marmaduke Sinfield (Bedford)

The first two results (same person, two records) show a **Marmaduke Sinfield** and Eliza with a daughter Martha christened 17 March 1757 at Bedford — both Church of England and Nonconformist records. This is a distinct Bedford family, less directly connected to the Millbrook marriage. Cannot be ruled out entirely, but the John Sinfield Millbrook family is the stronger candidate.

## What remains unknown

No birth record for Martha (the 1761 bride) has been identified. Her parentage is probable (John Sinfield and Martha of Millbrook) but unconfirmed. No death record has been found for her either.

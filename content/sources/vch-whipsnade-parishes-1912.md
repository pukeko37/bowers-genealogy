## Key Facts

- **Source:** *A History of the County of Bedford: Volume 3*, ed. William Page (London, 1912). Digitised by British History Online.
- **Coverage:** Parish history, manors, church, geography — as of 1912 (before the zoo).

## Geography and Village Layout

Whipsnade is a small parish of 928 acres on the Hertfordshire border. The land reaches 800ft at the north (Dunstable Downs) and 700ft at the village; from the village nine counties are visible on a clear day. Soil is clay over chalk — the chalk has been quarried in the west of the parish. The village is grouped around a large green through which the road runs east–west. **Two roads cross the green leading to Dell Farm and Hall Farm respectively.** The rectory faces the church (St Mary Magdalene) across the green; opposite the rectory is Hill Farm, with the Methodist chapel to its west.

The old names for the settlement: *Wylibesende* (13th century), *Wehsnade* (14th century).

## Enclosure

The parish was enclosed by Act of Parliament in **1798**. William Beckford acquired the right to enclose common lands in return for a £30 yearly rentcharge applied to the poor rates. This is the framework within which the Bachelor, Batchelar, Matthews, and Cook families farmed in the 19th century.

## Manor History (summary)

Two manors: the principal one followed the descent of Eaton Bray; a second manor (also called Whipsnade Manor) was held from the early 13th century by a family who took Whipsnade as a surname (de Whipsnade). After passing through multiple hands it was sold by Thomas Vaux to Leonard Hampson in 1800. By 1912 it was the property of Earl Brownlow.

Notable 1606 extent: the manor house had 16 rooms, 21 bays of barns and stables, a brick dovehouse, orchards, and 268 acres of demesne land. Total annual value £100, timber valued at £300.

William de Whipsnade received a pardon in 1321 for giving land in mortmain to **St. Bartholomew's, Smithfield** — an early connection between Whipsnade and Smithfield, a link echoed six centuries later when [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] from Whipsnade married Charles Norman whose family was centred on the Smithfield/Hosier Lane area of London.

## Church: St Mary Magdalene

A small brick building: chancel, nave, west tower (16th century). The 17th-century pulpit and altar rails are the only old fittings. Three bells: treble and second of 1740, tenor of 1630 ("God Save the King"). Parish registers survive from 1712.

## Notes

This article was written in 1912, predating ZSL's purchase of Hall Farm (1927). It confirms Hall Farm as a working farm on the green — the farm where [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] worked as a cook before her 1883 departure for London.

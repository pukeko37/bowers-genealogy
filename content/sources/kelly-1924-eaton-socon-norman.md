## Source

Kelly's Directory of Bedfordshire, 1924. Full parish entry for Eaton Socon and surrounding hamlets (Begwary, Bushmead, Crosshall, Duloe, Eaton Ford, Honeydon, Staploe, Upper Staploe, Wyboston), transcribed from bernardoconnor.org.uk. Includes a parish description, list of private residents, and commercial section for each hamlet.

Entry relevant to this research: **"Norman Albert Newman, Crown P.H."** — in the Eaton Socon commercial section.

## Key Facts

- **Albert Newman Norman** is listed in the commercial section of Kelly's 1924 Directory for Eaton Socon
- His address/business is the **Crown P.H.** (Public House)
- This is the commercial directory entry for his occupation as licensed premises keeper
- The full directory lists five other licensed premises in Eaton Socon and Eaton Ford: Old Sun P.H. (Ernest George Barnes), The Bell P.H. (Mrs Jane Boddington), George & Dragon P.H. (Thomas Huckle), Waggon & Horses P.H. (William Henry Frank Simons), and in Eaton Ford: Barley Mow P.H. (Joseph William Altrip), Rose & Crown P.H. (Reuben Hedge) — placing the Crown in its local context
- Population of Eaton Socon parish in 1921: 2,231; parish on the Great North Road, 2.5 miles west of St. Neots station

## Interpretation

This is independent corroboration — from a trade directory rather than census or keeper records — that Albert Newman Norman was the Crown's licensee in 1924. It aligns precisely with the CCAN source giving his tenure as 1922–1931 (see [[crown-beerhouse-eaton-socon-stneots-ccan]]).

The designation **P.H.** (Public House) is notable. Tom Norman's 1911 census occupation described the premises as a "beerhouse" (the cheaper tier of licence under the 1830 Act). By 1924 Kelly's lists it as a full public house. Whether this reflects a genuine licence upgrade between 1911 and 1924, or simply Kelly's using P.H. generically for all licensed premises, is unclear. The other licensed premises in the same directory entry are also designated P.H., suggesting Kelly's may have used the term generically.

Albert Newman Norman (1888–1970) was the sixth child of [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] and [[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]]. Tom was still alive in 1924 (he died 1925), but Albert had held the licence for at least two years by this point, suggesting a handover while Tom was still living.

## Significance

- Confirms Albert Newman Norman in Eaton Socon in 1924 from an independent source type
- Strengthens the identification of the Crown as the Norman family's licensed premises
- Notes possible beerhouse → public house upgrade between 1911 and 1924; Kelly's generic use of P.H. for all licensed premises weakens this as evidence of an upgrade

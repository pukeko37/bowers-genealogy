## Summary

Probate index entry for Ruth Birchmore (known in the wiki as Ruth Cooke), from the England and Wales National Index of Wills and Administrations, 1858–1957. Screenshot of the printed index page.

## Transcription

> BIRCHMORE Ruth of the "Chequer's" inn Whipsnade Bedfordshire widow died 22 May 1904 Administration London 7 October to Susan Sear widow Effects £45.

## Key Facts

- **Registered name:** Ruth Birchmore — she died under George Birchmore Plummer's surname, having taken it on their 1867 marriage
- **Address:** The Chequers Inn, Whipsnade — resident at the pub when she died, as with Charles Cook four years later
- **Death date:** 22 May 1904 — confirms what was already known
- **Record type:** Administration (not Probate) — Ruth died intestate, without a will
- **Administration granted:** 7 October 1904 — four and a half months after her death
- **Administrator:** Susan Sear, widow — not a family member previously known to the wiki
- **Estate value:** £45

## Significance

**Intestate.** Ruth left no will, which explains the longer delay (4.5 months versus Charles Cook's 8 days four years later). Her known living children — [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] (by then Mary Bransom) and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (by then Lucy Thody) — did not take administration. Whether they declined, were not contactable, or deferred to Susan Sear is unknown.

**Susan Sear, widow — open question.** The administrator is an unknown figure. Possible scenarios:
- A Whipsnade neighbour who stepped in when the family did not
- A creditor of the pub granted administration in lieu of family
- A family connection not yet identified — a daughter, granddaughter, or relative by a name not yet encountered in the research
- Ruth's son [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook (1847)]] disappeared from records after 1861; if he predeceased Ruth, Susan Sear's role becomes more explicable as there would have been one fewer potential administrator

**Estate £45.** After twenty years running The Chequers alone (1884–1904), Ruth's personal estate was very small. This likely reflects modest accumulated savings rather than any claim on the pub building (owned by the brewery). For comparison, her nephew Charles Cook (1843), who held the licence for only four years, left £134 5s. 5d. in 1908.

**Legal name Birchmore.** The wiki uses "Ruth Cooke" throughout for navigability, but she was legally Ruth Birchmore from her 1867 marriage and was indexed under that name in the probate records.

Article by Dr Joan Navarre, published by the Museum of Soho (mosoho.org.uk). Concerns the Oxford Express Dairies at 26 Frith Street, Soho — a Welsh-owned dairy operated by the Pugh family for 51 years.

**Key content:**

- Oxford Express Dairies at 26 Frith Street, Soho, closed 27 July 1985 after 51 years (opened ~1934).
- Owned and operated by the **Pugh Family** from the county of **Ceredigion** (Cardiganshire).
- "For over a century, the Welsh were the cow keepers and dairy suppliers of London."
- The **early morning milk train from the West Coast of Wales** ensured daily delivery of fresh dairy products to London.
- Butter delivered in 40 lb blocks, separated with wooden butter pats.
- Mr R J Pugh served as ARP Warden during the Blitz. Daughter Jean Pugh recalls measuring milk from the large milk crock.
- Museum of Soho holds: milk stool, milk bottles, bottle caps, metal cream pitchers, greaseproof butter wrappers, blue-and-white glazed milk crock marked "PURE MILK."

**Relevance to Mason family:**
The Pugh family came from Ceredigion — the same county as Richard Mason (Llanbadarn Fawr, Ceredigion). The article establishes that Welsh dairy operation in London was a recognised family trade passed between generations, rooted in specific Welsh counties, and supported by the milk train from West Wales. The Mason milk shop at Hosier Lane (1874–1900) is an earlier example of the same phenomenon — a Ceredigion family in London's milk trade — predating the Pughs' Frith Street operation by sixty years.

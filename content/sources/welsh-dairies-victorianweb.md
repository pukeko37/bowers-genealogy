Victorian Web article on Welsh-owned dairies in London. URL: victorianweb.org/victorian/history/work/welshdairies.html

**Key content:**

Welsh-owned dairies were concentrated along the Marylebone and Euston Roads — the route directly from Paddington Station, the Great Western Railway terminus serving South Wales. A surviving example: "J Evans Dairy Farmer" tile shopfront near Warren Street, London — dark blue and cream tiles, gold lettering on blue background.

Welsh dairy owners advertised as "Dairy Farmers," suggesting milk was sent up by rail from Welsh farms. The model persisted into the 1960s: two Welsh-speaking ladies kept a dairy in North Gower Street well into that decade.

Internal layout: tiled walls, counter, pictorial tiles of milkmaids and cows. Milk was ladled from churns into customers' own jugs. Butter, eggs, and cheese also sold.

**Relevance to Mason family:**
The Mason family (Richard Mason, 1850–1890, and Catherine James, 1852–1900) came from the Llanbadarn Fawr/Cynnull-mawr area of Cardiganshire (Ceredigion) and ran a milk shop at 27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield. Hosier Lane is roughly 1 mile southeast of the Paddington/Marylebone Welsh dairy cluster described here — in the Smithfield Market corridor. The Masons were participating in the same Welsh dairy migration pattern as the Euston/Marylebone dairies, though at a different node of the London milk trade.

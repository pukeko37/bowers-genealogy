FindMyPast newspaper search: "bunker" + "stevington," Bedfordshire, 1800–1899. Returns 38 results; this clipping shows the first 12. British Newspaper Archive, primarily the *Bedfordshire Mercury* and *Bedfordshire Times and Independent*.

## Item-by-item assessment

**12 October 1889 — Miss Bunker at Stevington gathering**
*Bedfordshire Times and Independent.*
"Mary Hart, Miss Hardwick, Mrs. Lacy, Mrs. H. Field, Mrs. W. Hulett, Miss Bunker, the Misses Gell, Miss Foxwell..."
A list of women at a Stevington function. An unmarried Bunker woman from the Stevington branch — almost certainly from the [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph (1792)]] line's third generation (children of John 1817 or James 1824), but unidentifiable without family pages for those men.

**2 October 1880 — Marriages at Stevington**
*Bedfordshire Times and Independent.*
Lists several marriages at Stevington ("Janes Inskir to Exon Towy both of Stevington" etc.). No Bunker name appears. **Not relevant.**

**26 September 1896 — Stevington Flower Show (damsons)**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Damsons, 1 L. Prentices, 2 J. Bunker, 3 A. Hulatt."
"J. Bunker" wins second prize for damsons at the Stevington Flower Show. The most plausible match is **James Bunker (1824–1904)**, son of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph (1792)]], who married at Stevington in 1850 and was apparently still resident there — he'd be 72 in 1896 and tending a kitchen garden is entirely consistent.

**11 June 1892 — Miss Bunker at Stevington event**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Miss Bunker managed bran-pie; Miss Gillingham had a stand for the sale of numerous household requisites..."
The same Miss Bunker as the 1889 appearance (or a woman of the same generation), at a church fete or charity stall in Stevington. Unidentified — see 1889 entry above.

**13 January 1877 — William Bunker, gamekeeper, Wilstead**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"William Bunker, the gamekeeper, said he saw the defendant and three others on Mr Peacock's land [at Wilstead]."
A gamekeeper at Wilstead (adjacent to Stevington). No William Bunker of appropriate birth year (~1830–1845) appears in the current wiki. Wilstead is a different village from Stevington. No plausible match; **not ingested.**

**11 June 1880 — Ruth Bunker, affiliation case, Stevington**
*Luton Times and Advertiser.*
"George Draper Whiting, St. Paul's, Bedford, was summoned by Ruth Bunker, of Stevington, to show cause [as to paternity]. Mr. Clare for complainant, and Mr. Mitchell for defendant. Order 1s. 6d. per week and costs."

**12 June 1880 — Same case**
*Bedfordshire Times and Independent.*
"At one time the parties were in the service of Mr. Smith, farmer, and lived in the same house, being domestic servant and [farm servant?]."
**Now identified: [[ruth-bunker-1855|Ruth Bunker (1855)]]**, daughter of [[james-bunker-1824|James Bunker (1824)]] and Sophia Panter, granddaughter of Joseph (1792). She was 24, a domestic servant at Mr Smith's farm in Stevington; Whiting (19, from Bolnhurst) was a fellow-servant at the same farm. She brought the bastardy order successfully — Whiting ordered to pay 1s. 6d. per week. Just 3½ months later they married at Stevington (27 September 1880). They had 11 children and emigrated to Sherbrooke, Quebec in 1885. Ruth died there in 1910. See [[ruth-bunker-1855]] and [[ruth-bunker-1855-familysearch]].

**28 September 1872 — Jane Bunker (20), Stevington, theft**
*Bedfordshire Times and Independent.*
"Jane Bunker (20), a single woman, of Stevington, was brought up by Police-constable George Mardlin and charged with stealing, on the 23rd, from the person [of someone]."
Born approximately 1852. Possibly **Jane Bunker (1852–1938)**, the eldest daughter of [[james-bunker-1824|James Bunker (1824)]] and Sophia Panter — she would be exactly 20 in September 1872. Unconfirmed; she could also be a daughter of John Bunker (1817–1887), whose children are untraced.

**21 June 1884 — John Bunker, Pilcroft-street, Bedford, assault**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Samuel Sergeant was charged with assaulting John Bunker, of Pilcroft-street, on the 5th inst. — Defendant did not appear. — The complainant deposed that defendant went into his shop [...]"
John Bunker had a shop on Pilcroft-street, Bedford. The likeliest match is **[[john-watts-bunker-1831|John Watts Bunker (1831–1899)]]**, who was definitively in Bedford from the 1850s onwards (metalworker, Bedford St Mary); in 1884 he'd be 53 and active. The word "shop" could mean a workshop (consistent with his trade as shoesmith/hammer man). The alternative is **John Bunker (1817–1887)** from the Stevington line — he'd be 67, and if he'd relocated to Bedford by then. Both are plausible; the Bedford residency of John Watts Bunker makes him the stronger candidate.

**12 January 1884 — Deaths, Stevington**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Jan. 6, at Stevington, Mrs Alice Scrivener, aged 53 [...]"
No Bunker. **Not relevant.**

**9 October 1886 — Miss Bunker at Stagsden event**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Mary and Miss Bunker did a good trade at the bran tub [at a Stagsden function]."
Stagsden is adjacent to Stevington. The same Miss Bunker (or a sister) from the 1889/1892 Stevington appearances. Unidentified.

**12 October 1867 — William Bunker, Westoning, labourers' prize**
*Bedfordshire Mercury.*
"Third prize, £1 10s., William Bunker, Westoning."
A William Bunker of Westoning (Bedfordshire) wins third prize in a labourers' husbandry class. Possibly the same person as the 1877 Wilstead gamekeeper (the two villages are close). No match in the current wiki. Westoning is where Thomas Bunker (1704) was born, but no working connection to this man is established. **Not ingested.**

## Summary

Of the 12 items, two are clearly irrelevant (marriages list, deaths notice), two are unconnected William Bunker entries from adjacent parishes, and one (J. Bunker 1896) is a probable match to James Bunker (1824–1904) of the Stevington Joseph line. The remaining items — Miss Bunker (1886/1889/1892), Ruth Bunker (1880), Jane Bunker (1872), John Bunker Pilcroft-st (1884) — point to a third generation of the Stevington Bunker branch that is not yet documented in the wiki. The Joseph (1792) line continued through John (1817–1887) and James (1824–1904), whose children are unresearched.

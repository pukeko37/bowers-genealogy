FamilySearch sources page for Richard Norman (FamilySearch ID 94N7-QWG). 11 sources attached, covering christening 1853, all census records 1861–1911, and marriage.

## Key data extracted

**Christening:** 3 July 1853, Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire. Two sources confirm: Huntingdonshire Parish Registers and England, Births and Christenings 1538–1975. Father Thomas Norman, mother Anne/Ann.

**1861 census:** Richard Norman, age 8, at High Street, Swavesey, Huntingdonshire. Occupation listed as "Agricultural Labourer" — almost certainly the enumerator recording the household head's occupation, not the 8-year-old's. Household: Thomas Norman (head, 34, Ag Lab), Ann Norman (32), George (10), Richard (8), Tom (4), Charles (1).

**1871 census:** Richard Norman, age 18, Agricultural Labourer, Swavesey. Still in the family home. Siblings listed: George (20), Tom (14), Charles (12), Richard (18), Mary A (9), Alfred E (5), Robert H (2). "James Norman" (M, 20, Swavesey) appears separately as "Other People on This Record" — not listed as a sibling. May be a neighbour, cousin, or enumerator adjacent entry; not yet explained.

**Marriage (October 1875):** FreeBMD record — Richard Norman married **Annie Elizabeth Randall** at GRO Hartlepool, October 1875. Volume not specified in clipping.

**Note on erroneous source:** The FamilySearch profile also has attached a Marriage Registration Index record (England and Wales, Marriage Registration Index 1837–2005) showing "Richard Norman married Margaret Garry, 1875, Hartlepool, Durham, Vol 10A page 190." This is almost certainly George Norman's marriage record (George married Margaret J Garry at Christ Church, West Hartlepool, December 1875) wrongly attached to Richard's profile. All census records confirm Richard's wife was Annie/Ann from Huntingdonshire, not Margaret Garry.

**1881 census:** Richard Norman (28, Labourer), at **13 Baily Street, Stranton, Durham**. Wife: Annie E Norman (27, Huntingdon, Huntingdonshire, England). Children: Charles (0, West H Pool) and Mary E (3, West H Pool). A "William Mole" (29, Swavesey, Cambridgeshire) is also on the record — a Swavesey man in the same Stranton street.

**1891 census:** Richard Norman (38, General Labourer), Stranton, Durham. Wife Annie E (37, Huntingdonshire). Children at home: Willie H (0), Richard R (4), Charles (10), Frances A (9), John T (6), Mary E (2 — likely a different child from the 1881 Mary E who was born 1878). "William Moule" (39, Cambridgeshire) again on the same record — the Swavesey neighbour persists.

**1901 census:** Richard Norman (48, **Labourer Cement Works**), West Hartlepool, Durham. Wife: **Annie Eliz Norman (47, Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire)** — confirms her birthplace as Fenstanton. Children: Annie Eliz (3), William Hy (10), Albert (8), George (6), Frances Ann (18), Charles (1), John T (16), Richard (14), Mary E (12 — born ~1889, a third Mary or the 1891 one).

**1911 census:** Richard Norman (58, **General Labourer, Corporation**), West Hartlepool. Wife Annie (57, Fenstanton Huntingdon, Huntingdonshire). Seven in household. Children at home: Frances Ann (28), Richard Randall (24), William H (20), Albert (18), George (16), Annie Louisa (13), Charles (11).

Richard never entered skilled trade. His entire working life in Hartlepool was unskilled labouring — in 1901 specifically at cement works (West Hartlepool had chemical and cement industries); in 1911 as a Corporation labourer (municipal work — roads, sanitation, infrastructure). This is the same trajectory as his father Thomas Norman (lifelong agricultural labourer) but translated from rural Cambridgeshire to urban County Durham.

## Key finding — Ann Elizabeth Randall from Fenstanton

Ann Elizabeth Randall, Richard's wife, was born in **Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire** — confirmed by the 1901 and 1911 census records. Fenstanton is the same village where:
- Richard's grandmother **Ann Tabram** was christened in 1829
- Richard's great-grandmother **Mary Eusden** died and was buried
- Richard's great-great-grandfather **Thomas Euseden** lived at Bell Lane and was buried in 1842
- Richard and George Norman themselves were christened in 1853

The Norman family had deep roots in Fenstanton through the Tabraham-Eusden line. That Richard married a woman from this specific village — rather than from Swavesey where he grew up, or from Hartlepool where he settled — is a remarkable fact. Whether he met her through maintained family connections to Fenstanton, or through coincidence, cannot be determined from available records.

## Swavesey neighbour in Stranton

William Mole/Moule (29, Swavesey, 1881) and William Moule (39, Cambridgeshire, 1891) appear on the same census records as Richard in Stranton. This is almost certainly the same man — a Swavesey contemporary who came to Hartlepool at around the same time as Richard and George Norman, and lived in the same street. The Hartlepool migration from Swavesey may have been broader than just the two Norman brothers.

## Source Note

This is a compiled AI research response (Perplexity AI, 2026-05-02), synthesising from ZSL archive materials, Bedfordshire Archives community histories, OS and tithe map references, and Wikipedia. It is not a primary document. Claims should be treated as secondary-level — useful for orientation, to be verified against primary sources when precision matters.

## Key Facts

- **Hall Farm** was a working farm on Dunstable Downs that had fallen into **derelict condition by the 1920s** before ZSL acquisition.
- **ZSL purchased the Hall Farm estate in late 1926** (transaction recorded 1926/1927), for approximately **£13,480**. Described as roughly **500–600 acres**.
- Prior to this, its detailed agrarian history is thinly documented precisely because it was not an active, thriving farm in its final decades.

## Geography: Hall Farm and Houchen End

The clipping confirms that **Houchen End is at the western edge of Whipsnade village**, and that **Hall Farm lay at the western end of the parish**, adjacent to fields and commons at the village's western edge. The two locations are therefore in close proximity — a fact consistent with (but not explicitly spelled out in) the 1912 VCH description of the village layout.

**Genealogical implication:** GBP Sr's address at Houchen End placed him immediately adjacent to Hall Farm. His daughter [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] later worked there as a cook before her 1883 departure for London. The farm was not a distant employer — it was the working farm next to the family's hamlet.

## Maps Reference

The Bedfordshire Archives hold the **1842 tithe map** and **1880–1924 OS 25-inch maps** for Whipsnade, which would show Hall Farm as a named holding with field boundaries, and establish precise distances to Houchen End and other hamlet clusters. These have not been consulted for this wiki; they are the natural next step for anyone wanting to reconstruct the physical geography of the family's world.

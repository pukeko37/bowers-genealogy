Historical account of Kinsbourne Green compiled from the late Eric Brandreth's articles, written 1991 with updates by Geoff Woodward to 2018. Published by the Harpenden History Society.

**Key facts extracted:**

- Kinsbourne Green is a hamlet on the northern edge of Harpenden, along the A1081 Luton Road, historically associated with the Luton Hoo Estate
- Four pubs historically: The Fox (originally the Smyth Arms, 1710), The Whip (closed 1935), The Harrow, and The First and Last (demolished 1974)
- **Wesleyan Methodist chapel:** In 1856 three Luton Wesleyans paid £5 for a plot of land and built a small chapel. It burned down and was rebuilt on the same foundations. A new hall was added in 1931; the old building demolished 1949; new chancel completed 1951. The last service was held 28 April 2002; the building was sold and demolished 2006, houses built on the site.
- **Green Lawns Garage:** Built c.1930. Behind the garage was a tea room and tennis courts (opened June 1937, closed early 1950s) and an 18-hole golf course (added April 1938, short-lived). The garage became a meeting place for touring car owners and cycling clubs. Frank Pratley owned it 1960–1977. Bowers Motors took over in August 1981.
- **Kinsbourne Green School** (St Mary's combined church and school, built 1869): closed July 1955, pupils transferred to Roundwood Park.
- **Market garden** (Kinsbourne Nursery, later Perry's): sold to builders Pearce and Barker in 1968 for redevelopment as Kinsbourne Close. Major housing development therefore post-dates Ron and Ivy's arrival c.1962.
- New house building visible from the 1960s: the church expanded its nave in June 1968 specifically because of rising population from new housing.

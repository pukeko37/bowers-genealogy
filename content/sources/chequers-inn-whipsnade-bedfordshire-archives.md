## Summary

A community history entry from the Bedfordshire Archives website describing the history of the Chequers public house in Whipsnade — the pub that George Birchmore Plummer and Ruth Cooke held the license for from 1867 until Ruth's death in 1904.

Source: https://bedsarchives.bedford.gov.uk/CommunityHistories/Whipsnade/TheChequersPublicHouseWhipsnade.aspx  
Archive references: CLP13 (countywide licensing register, 1822), BH409 (1843 sale particulars)

**Note on raw file:** The clipping was saved under a GBP Sr FamilySearch filename in error; the content is from the Bedfordshire Archives community history page.

## Key Facts

- **First licensing record:** 1822, countywide licensing register [CLP13]
- **Early 19th century owner:** Edward Burr
- **1843:** Edward Burr's Dunstable Brewery (including the Chequers) put up for sale by auction
- **1843 tenant:** Samuel Heeles, rent £16 per annum

## 1843 Property Description (from sale particulars BH409)

Bar, tap room, parlour, washhouse, pantry, liquor closet, underground cellar, three bedrooms. Outbuildings: cow house, two wood barns. Grounds: small garden, pond, and paddock of two acres.

## Likely Licensee Sequence

1. Edward Burr (Dunstable Brewery) — owner from at least early 19th century
2. Samuel Heeles — tenant 1843 (from BH409 sale particulars); related to Hannah Maria Heels
3. [[abraham-batchelor-1825|Abraham Batchelor]] and [[hannah-maria-heels-1814|Hannah Maria Heels]] — probable licensees ~1843–1867; census data places them in Whipsnade throughout this period; Hannah's surname links to Samuel Heeles
4. [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|GBP Sr]] and [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] — from December 1867 marriage until GBP Sr's death in 1884
5. [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] — alone, 1884–1904; died at the inn
6. [[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook]] — 1904–1908; Ruth's nephew (son of her brother [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]]); he died in 1908 at Luton
7. [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Cook (née Brandsom)]] — 1908; Charles's wife; took over on his death

**Note:** The FamilySearch licensee list gives "Louis Cook" for the 1908 entry, but the accompanying prose explicitly identifies this as Lois Cook (née Brandsom), Charles's wife — "Louis" is a transcription error or variant spelling of "Lois".

## Notes

The 1843 description gives a clear picture of the physical property Ruth would have managed after GBP Sr's death in 1884 — a working rural pub with agricultural outbuildings and livestock capacity. The pub had been operating for at least 45 years by the time GBP Sr took the licence.

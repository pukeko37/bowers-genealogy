## Joseph Bunker (1840–1914) — confirmed appearances

**October 1872 — Travelling without a ticket**
*Bedfordshire Mercury and Bedfordshire Times, 12 October 1872.*
"Joseph Bunker, aged 32, of [Millbrook], labourer, was charged with travelling third class on the Midland Railway from Wellingborough to Bedford." Age 32 in October 1872 = born 1839–40; perfectly consistent with Joseph born May 1840. He had been in Wellingborough (Northamptonshire) and was returning to Bedford — an unusual journey for a Millbrook farm labourer, suggesting work or family business further afield.

**November 1883 — Maliciously Wounding case**
*Bedfordshire Mercury and Bedfordshire Times, 24 November 1883.*
"Joseph Bunker said: I live at [Millbrook] and am a labourer. On Monday, the 12th, I was at work in the field with the prisoner and the complainant." Witness in a knife attack case in Millbrook. Confirms his occupation and residence in his own words.

**January 1884 — Serious Affray at Marston**
*Bedfordshire Times, 19 January 1884.*
"Joseph Bunker, labourer, [Millbrook], remembered the eventful day, and said he saw the parties in question at the Marston Arms." Witness at Marston (the adjacent parish). He is drinking or present at the Marston Arms public house.

**September 1873 & October 1878 — Labourers' Friend Society prizes**
*Bedfordshire Times, 20 and 23 September 1873; Bedfordshire Mercury, 5 October 1878.*
Joseph Bunker of Silsoe appears in prize lists at the Silsoe & Ampthill Labourers' Friend Society annual show, winning prizes for:
- 1873: Best-kept cottage — "the Society's Testimonial and 15s"
- 1878: Vegetables (apples and garden produce), placed alongside Thomas Lavender, Betsy Chapman, and others

Silsoe borders Millbrook to the north; his allotment may have been within Silsoe parish even while he lived on Millbrook Road. These prizes suggest a man who took care of his home and garden.

**October 1881 — Millbrook court/inquest article**
*Bedfordshire Mercury, 22 October 1881.*
A "MILLBROOK" article includes both Samuel Bunker and a Joseph (the OCR reads "Joseph Sharp" but "Samuel Bunker" is clearly named). Both brothers appear to be named in the same Millbrook proceedings, with Annie Peters as a witness. The article is too fragmentary from the snippet to characterise further.

**Uncertain/non-matches:**
- Aug 1877 (Luton Times): The "Joseph" named is "Joseph Harris," not Bunker.
- Sep 1877 (Bedford Record): Birth notice for wife of "Joseph Bunker, Chesham" — different family, Chesham is Buckinghamshire.
- Jun 1884 (Bedfordshire Mercury): Joseph Bunker (14), Marston — age 14 in 1884 means born ~1870; not our Joseph (who was 44). Could be a nephew or unrelated.
- 1873–1878 prize listings: The "Silsoe" address on some entries may reflect the show venue or allotment location, not residence.

## Samuel Bunker (1842–1913) — confirmed appearances

**December 1877 — School attendance summons**
*Bedfordshire Mercury, 8 December 1877.*
Samuel Bunker charged with neglecting to send his children to school. Co-defendants: Joel Thompson, George Cook. Location: Millbrook. In December 1877, Samuel's daughter Minnie Maria Dean Bunker was approximately 7 — school age. His infant son Herbert (b.1875) had just died (d.1877). The family had school-aged children, consistent with the charge.

**October 1881 — Millbrook article (joint with Joseph)**
*Bedfordshire Mercury, 22 October 1881.*
Samuel Bunker named alongside other Millbrook men in proceedings where Annie Peters gave evidence. See Joseph's entry above.

**October 1882 — Benefit Society membership prize (1st)**
*Bedfordshire Times, 7 October 1882.*
"1, Samuel Bunker, Ampthill" — first prize for longest continuous membership of a certified Benefit Society. Silsoe & Ampthill Labourers' Friend Society. Our Samuel aged 40 in 1882.

**October 1892 — Longest service with same employer (1st) — attribution uncertain**
*Bedfordshire Mercury, 1 October 1892.*
"Agricultural labourer over 40, longest in same place, upon same farm, or with same employer, 1 Samuel Bunker, 73, years with Mr G Allen, Ampthill; Joseph Sinfield, 57, 44 years with Mr J Dowdeswell, Maulden."

If 73 is his **age**, this Samuel was born ~1819 — which matches the **lost Samuel (~1820, son of William Bunker 1767 and Sarah Beal)**, who vanished from records after the 1841 census. That Samuel would be ~72–73 in 1892, working for Mr G Allen of Ampthill. This would be a significant find — the lost Samuel in his old age, still working and winning prizes.

If 73 is his **years of service**, neither Samuel fits (our 1842 Samuel was 50 in 1892 and couldn't have 73 years service; even the older Samuel was only ~72 years old in 1892 and couldn't have 73 years service).

**Most likely reading:** the older, lost Samuel (~1820), aged ~73, won first prize for longest service. Our 1842 Samuel won the Benefit Society prize in 1882 (above) but probably not this one. The two Samuels may both have been active in Ampthill area prize competitions in the same decades.

**Uncertain/non-matches:**
- Apr 1875 (Bedfordshire Mercury): "Samuel Bunker, Old Warden, keeper to Mr Whitbread" — a gamekeeper at Old Warden; different man entirely.
- Sep 1869 (Hertfordshire Express): "Samuel Bunker (Ampthill)" — a flower/livestock show entry at a young age. Our Samuel was 27 in 1869; possible but uncertain.
- Sep 1874 (Bedfordshire Mercury): "3rd, Samuel Bunker, Ampthill, 10s" — Labourers' Friend Society. Our Samuel aged 32 in 1874; possible.
- Mar 1876 (Bedfordshire Mercury): False match — the "Samuel" in the article is "Samuel Wheeler."

## Source

*Derby Telegraph*, Thursday 25 January 1894. Derby Borough Court proceedings. Bench: John Bailey (chair), Ald. Newbold, Mr. W. Turner, Mr. S. Johnson.

## Transcription (OCR)

> **IMPUDENT THEFT FROM A TILL.** — Walter Jennings and Ernest Beard, two little boys, were charged with stealing from a till 2s. 11½d., in money, three balls, and a top, valued at 1s. 1d., the property of Caleb Bunker, of Traffic-street. — From the evidence of the prosecutor's wife it appeared that she had left another [person] in the shop the previous afternoon, and having missed money, she then left the shop and went across to Beard, who was waiting on the other side of the road. They both hurried off down the street. Beard said when later accused that some money had been taken from the till and he had given some back to another youth, but he denied that Jennings had given him any of the money he had taken, and they went to the detective and Jennings was taken to the station. [The detective described] going up Friar-gate with Beard, and arresting Jennings. Beard would not admit anything about the case, and Jennings confessed that he took the money. Beard then explained where he had spent the money, and added that it appeared he went to the shop twice. The second time I went in for some silver, but only got 3d., which made 10d. The detective said these were well-known bad boys, but he was told that Jennings had been sent to the Boys' Home at Birmingham. His mother stated this was not the case. She was living in Birmingham at the time but was at the Home on a visit. He had been in Derby five weeks. The Bench ordered the boys to receive six strokes of the birch rod each.

## Extracted Facts

- **Date**: Thursday, 25 January 1894
- **Caleb Bunker** is identified as the property owner; his shop is at **Traffic Street, Derby**
- **Margaret Bunker** (referred to as "prosecutor's wife") was in charge of the shop at the time of the theft, noticed the missing money, and gave evidence at court
- The shop's stock included **toys** (balls, a top) alongside cash — consistent with Caleb's 1881 census occupation "Toys Small Dealer Boot & Shoe Maker"
- Items stolen: 2s. 11½d. in cash, three balls, one top (total value of goods ~1s. 1d.)
- Two boys, Walter Jennings (age 12) and Ernest Beard (age 12), were found guilty; sentenced to six strokes of the birch rod each

## Significance

This is the only known record placing Caleb and Margaret at **Traffic Street, Derby** — filling the gap between the 1891 census (Derby, St Peter's parish) and the 1901/1911 address (60 High Street, Derby). The address had changed to High Street by 1901, so the family lived and traded at Traffic Street for some period in the early-to-mid 1890s.

Margaret's role is noteworthy: she was running the shop alone when the theft occurred and was active enough to track the boys and give evidence. The toy stock confirms Caleb was still operating as a "Toys Small Dealer" over a decade after that occupation first appeared in the 1881 census.

Source: mywesleyanmethodists.org.uk, compiled from Bedfordshire Historical Record Society Vol. 75, the 1873 Returns of Wesleyan Methodist Chapels, and the Bedfordshire Times.

## Key facts

- Millbrook Wesleyan Methodist Chapel was **built in 1858**
- Registration for worship: **5 February 1861**, made by **Charles Carter**, minister, Harpur Street, Bedford
- The chapel belonged to the **Bedford Circuit** of the Wesleyan Methodist connexion
- Capacity: **106 sittings** (1873 Returns)
- Sometimes called **"Solomon's Temple"** after Solomon Beale, a local preacher
- Closed 1973; ruined shell still standing in 2015

## Relevance to the Bunker family

The Bunkers left Millbrook in 1852, six years before the chapel was built. A 2013 account of the chapel's history states explicitly: "There had been a group of Methodists meeting in Millbrook since at least the mid 18th century." This pushes the informal Methodist community back to well before 1800 — predating the Bunkers' departure by generations. [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] (born 1764) grew up in a village where Methodism was already active. The 1858 chapel formalised a community that had been meeting in homes and other spaces for over a century.

More significantly, the chapel was registered by **Charles Carter of Harpur Street, Bedford**, as minister — placing it within the **Bedford Circuit**. This is the same circuit named in the christening record of **Sarah Ann Bunker** (daughter of George Bunker and Ann Pedder), who was christened at a Wesleyan Methodist chapel in the Bedford Circuit on 9 December 1860 — just ten months before the Millbrook chapel was formally registered under the same circuit.

The George Bunker family were in Bedford in 1860, and chose a Wesleyan Methodist chapel for their daughter's christening. The Millbrook chapel they may have known in childhood was part of the same circuit. This is consistent with a family whose Nonconformist practice was specifically Wesleyan Methodist, maintained across the move from Millbrook to Bedford.

## The broader Bunker Nonconformist pattern

Multiple data points across the Bunker family align with Wesleyan Methodism:

- **1806:** James Thomas Bunker mass-christened seven children at Millbrook parish church on a single day — the standard practice of Nonconformists registering belatedly with the Church of England
- **1860:** Sarah Ann Bunker christened Wesleyan Methodist, Bedford Circuit
- **1868:** Caleb Bunker married Rebecca Tall at **Howard Chapel**, Mill Street, Bedford — a Nonconformist chapel
- **Naming patterns:** Caleb, Ebenezer, Ruth, Phoebe, Comfort — heavily Old Testament names, consistent with Wesleyan Methodist and Dissenting naming conventions

None of this is individually conclusive, but together it suggests the Bunker family's religious affiliation across several generations was Wesleyan Methodist — worshipping in Dissenting congregations but registering with the Church of England for civil record purposes.

## Research lead

Wesleyan Methodist circuit records for the Bedford Circuit may include class books, membership lists, and baptism registers that could name Bunker family members directly. The Bedford Circuit covered both Millbrook and Bedford. If such records survive at the Bedfordshire Archive or the Methodist Archive (Manchester), they could confirm the Bunker affiliation and possibly reveal family members not captured in Church of England records.

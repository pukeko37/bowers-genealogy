Find a Grave memorial for Sarah Ann Matthews (1844–1853), Pioneer Children's Memorial, Salt Lake City. Memorial ID 232650456.

## Vital Data

- **Born:** 11 May 1844, Bedfordshire, England
- **Died:** 1853, aged 8–9, Nebraska, USA
- **Monument:** Pioneer Children's Memorial, Salt Lake City, Salt Lake County, Utah

## Key Finding — Family Composition During 1853 Crossing

The memorial notes contain company records placing the **entire Matthews family** in the Cyrus H. Wheelock Company, in the division under Captain Kendall. This is the most important fact in this source:

> "Nine-year-old Sarah Ann Matthews traveled with her family in the division under Captain Kendall;  
> Daniel Willis Matthews (Age 32),  
> **Sarah Matthews (Age 31)**,  
> Eliza Matthews (Age 9),  
> Mary Matthews (Age 5),  
> Emma Matthews (Age 2)."

**[[sarah-garner-1822|Sarah Garner Matthews]] emigrated with the family in 1853.** She was alive and present in the company at the time of departure. Her age of 31 is consistent with her confirmed birth date of 13 February 1822 (she would have turned 31 in February 1853, before the June 1853 departure). This resolves a previously open question — she did not die in England; she made the crossing.

Sarah Ann (9) and Emma (2) died in Nebraska during the crossing. Sarah Garner (31) survived the Nebraska leg at minimum; her subsequent fate in Utah is still unrecorded.

Mary Matthews (listed as aged 5 in 1853 = born ~1848) is also confirmed alive at departure, though younger than the 1851 census age of 2 suggests (1851 census gives birth year ~1849; the company record gives birth year ~1848). The discrepancy is minor.

## Company Notes

- **Company:** Cyrus H. Wheelock Company
- **Division:** under Captain Kendall
- **Departure:** 1–3 June 1853 from Keokuk, Iowa (outfitting post)
- **Missouri River crossing:** 11 July 1853
- **Arrival Salt Lake Valley:** 6–16 October 1853
- **Size:** approximately 400 individuals, 52 wagons (company report lists 427 + 7 from a California company travelling together)

The company report sent to Brigham Young on 19 September 1853 does not include a record of those who died en route — hence Sarah Ann and Emma's deaths were not captured in the company paperwork.

## Summary

Probate index entry for Charles Cook, from the England and Wales National Index of Wills and Administrations, 1858–1957. Screenshot of the printed index page.

## Transcription

> COOK Charles of The Chequers Inn Whipsnade Bedfordshire died 5 January 1908 Probate 13 January 1908 London 13 January 1908 to Lois Cook widow. Effects £134 5s. 5d.

## Key Facts

- **Full name and address:** Charles Cook, of The Chequers Inn, Whipsnade, Bedfordshire
- **Death date:** 5 January 1908
- **Probate granted:** 13 January 1908 — eight days after death
- **Probate place:** London (Principal Probate Registry)
- **Grantee:** Lois Cook, widow
- **Estate value:** £134 5s. 5d.

## Significance

**Resident at the Chequers Inn.** The record gives his address as "The Chequers Inn Whipsnade" — confirming that Charles was living at the pub when he died, not merely holding the licence from elsewhere. The Cooks were resident innkeepers.

**A will, not intestacy.** The entry reads "Probate" rather than "Letters of Administration," which means Charles left a will. Lois was his named executor and sole beneficiary. The original will would be held at the Principal Probate Registry.

**Estate of £134 5s. 5d.** This is a modest but real estate — personal effects, trading stock, livestock and any savings, but almost certainly not the freehold of the pub (which was owned by the brewery). It places the family in comfortable but not wealthy circumstances.

**The eight-day turnaround** is almost certainly driven by the Chequers Inn licence. Charles held the licence from 1904; on his death Lois needed legal authority over the estate to continue operating without interruption. The Bedfordshire licensing records confirm she held the licence for the remainder of 1908.

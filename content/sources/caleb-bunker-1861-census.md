**Elizabeth's occupation:** Lace maker — Bedford was a major centre of the pillow lace industry. This is the first documented occupation for Elizabeth Watts.

**Caleb's occupation:** Shoe maker, aged 15. This is almost certainly the start of an apprenticeship. He is in **St Paul's parish**, Bedford. The shoe and boot trade was a major Bedfordshire and Northamptonshire industry; Caleb's early training in it provides context for why Frederick Bunker (his son) was living in **Kettering** in the 1890s — one of England's premier boot and shoe manufacturing centres.

**"Ann Bunker":** The household's youngest child is listed as Ann (born ~1849). This corresponds to [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann Bunker]] (abt 1848) — known by her middle name Ann in this record. The birth year (1849 estimated from age 12) is close to the existing "abt 1848."

**Absent children:** John Watts Bunker (30), George Bunker (25), and David Bunker (24) are not in this household. They had already established their own residences.

**Note on Samuel Plackett Lane:** Confirmed. Samuel Plackett Lane was a boot maker apprentice at **Hassett Street, St Paul's parish** in 1861 — the same census piece (RG09, piece 991) as this household at Dane Street. Both streets are in the same enumeration district. He married Caleb's sister [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]] in 1863, two years after both appear in the same trade and parish.

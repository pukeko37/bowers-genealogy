A compiled research spreadsheet containing data assembled by a local collaborator (likely a Millbrook local historian who corresponds with Andrew). The file `bunkermillbrookcensus.xls` contains multiple sheets:

1. **Census table** — all Bunker entries in Millbrook and surrounding parishes from the 1841, 1851, 1861, 1871, 1881, 1891, and 1901 censuses, with addresses, occupations, birth years, and presence indicators per census year. Largely confirms what is already in the wiki; the key confirmations are that James (1807) and Elizabeth Watts appear precisely as expected in 1841 (Dwelling 8, Village, 7 children) and 1851 (Dwelling 64, Village, 8 children).
2. **Parish register — baptisms** — all Bunker baptisms from the Millbrook register, pre-1841. Includes the 1806 mass christening in detail.
3. **Parish register — burials** — all Bunker burials from the Millbrook register.
4. **Parish register — marriages** — all Bunker marriages from the Millbrook register.
5. **Miscellaneous records** — including the 1815 fence maintenance list, the 1840/41 Holland estate sale, a school punishment book entry, a 1919 newspaper story, and trade directory entries.
6. **Compiler's research notes** — numbered observations from the collaborator, addressing Andrew directly.

## Key findings

### Martha Bunker (1801) confirmed in parish register

The 1806 mass christening entry includes **Martha Bunker (b. 21 Dec 1801)**, daughter of James [Thomas] and Lydia. This definitively confirms she existed and was alive on 7 April 1806. She remains untraced in subsequent records — no marriage, burial, or census appearance has been found.

### James (1807) not in the Millbrook baptism register

The compiler notes: *"the registers do not show his baptism in Millbrook, at least up to 1811."* The mass christening of April 1806 covered seven children born between 1790 and 1804 but James — born 1807 — was apparently never presented at the church for baptism. This is consistent with the family's Nonconformist practice: the 1806 ceremony appears to have been a one-time clearing of civil requirements rather than a regular pattern.

### James Thomas Bunker (1764) — 1815 fence maintenance

A list of properties adjoining the Millbrook church in 1815 with responsibility to maintain the church fences names **James BUNKER** as occupier of the 13th pole, a former farmhouse (landlord Lord Ossory, formerly occupied in 1795 by Wm Mallison). This confirms James Thomas's presence in the village nine years before his death in 1824.

Also listed: **William BUNKER** at the 4th pole (cottage near the parsonage, former farmhouse, landlord Lord Ossory) and **Widow BUNKER** at the 8th pole (Kits Lane cottage). These are collateral Bunkers.

### James Bunker (1807) — Holland estate tenant, 1840/41

The inventory of Lady Holland's Millbrook estate sold to the Duke of Bedford in 1840 (the transaction that made the Duke the Bunkers' immediate landlord for their final twelve years in the village — see [[millbrook]]) lists **James BUNKER** as tenant of:
- Inventory 423: Cottage and Garden (0 acres, 0 rods, 39 poles), co-tenant T. Cartwright
- Inventory 431: Garden (0 acres, 0 rods, 36 poles), co-tenant T. Cartwright
- Inventory 432: (additional, co-tenant T. Cartwright)

The compiler holds a copy of the estate map and confirms this can be pinpointed in Millbrook today.

### Albert Edward Bunker — school punishment book, September 1902

The Millbrook Village School Punishment Book contains the following entry dated 23 September 1902:

> **Albert Bunker, Age 8** — Offence: fighting. Punishment: Kept in School by Mistress (Polly) Ashton.

This is **[[millbrook|Albert Edward Bunker]]** (b. September 1894, son of John Bunker 1870 and Ellen Mary Roffe), who would be killed at the Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele) on 31 July 1917, aged 22, and is commemorated on the Menin Gate. The eight-year-old fighting in the schoolyard is the same person on the memorial panels. The compiler holds a photocopy of the original punishment book.

### Mrs Bunker the sexton's widow — 1919 newspaper story

A 1919 newspaper cutting describes the rediscovery of two stone effigies of **William and Mary Huett** (died 1621 and 1602 respectively), lost since church renovations in the 1840s. **Mrs Bunker**, described as the aged widow of **Mr Bunker the sexton**, led a party of Bedford schoolboys to the spot in the churchyard where her late husband had struck stone while digging graves. The effigies were found — except for Mary Huett's head, which Mrs Bunker had been keeping in her shed. The compiler holds copies of three newspaper articles about this.

Mr Bunker the sexton is unidentified in these sources. He was presumably a late-Victorian or Edwardian Millbrook Bunker from the collateral (Edward 1742) line, possibly from the Samuel (1842) or Joseph (1837) households. He died before 1919, leaving an aged widow.

### Trade directory entries, 1864–1903

From the trade directories consulted by the compiler:
- **John BUNKER**: Kelly's 1864, Kelly's 1877
- **Joseph BUNKER**: 1885
- **Samuel BUNKER**: 1894, 1903

These correspond to John (1803)'s son John or Joseph in the 1860s–70s; Joseph (1837) from 1881; Samuel (1842) from 1881 to 1911. Samuel's directory appearances through 1903 are consistent with his census trail.

A Perplexity AI summary synthesising the 1919 Millbrook churchyard story from [[britishfolklore.com/millbrook/]] and related local history sources. The raw clipping is a Perplexity session export; it does not constitute a primary source but provides a coherent narrative with pointers to primary materials.

## Key facts extracted

- In 1919, Rector **H.P. Pollard** initiated an excavation in Millbrook churchyard to recover the effigies of **William Huett** (died c.1621–22) and **Mary Huett** (died 1602), which had been lost since the dismantling of their Tudor altar-tomb. The Perplexity clipping says the tomb was lost in "the 1840s"; the britishfolklore page gives the year as **1857** — the later date is more specific and probably more reliable.
- **Mrs Bunker**, widow of the Millbrook sexton, held a stone head that her late husband had dug up in the churchyard while digging on an earlier occasion. This led excavators to the shallow grave where the two effigies lay side by side.
- The effigies were found largely intact. Both had heads apparently "chopped off at the neck, possibly by Parliamentary Soldiers" during the Civil War. The Perplexity text claims the head in Mrs Bunker's possession was Mary's; the britishfolklore page does not specify whose head it was, and the Perplexity text contradicts itself (calling it Mary's head in one paragraph and William's in another). Attribution of the head is uncertain.
- After recovery, the effigies were moved inside the church, where they now rest near the altar. William is shown in armour and a ruff; Mary wears a gown once carved with flowers.
- The effigies acquired folk nicknames — **"The Warriors"** or **"Worriers"** — as stories about their removal and burial circulated.
- The excavation involved the **Bedfordshire Archaeological Society** and **Bedford Modern School Archaeology Society**, along with Bedford schoolboys.
- Three contemporary newspaper articles about the event are held by a local researcher.
- Mr Bunker the sexton was from the **Edward (1742) line**, probably from the **Samuel (1842)** or **Joseph (1837)** households. He is not yet identified in the records. He had died before 1919.

## On Mrs Bunker's stone head

The Perplexity clipping says Mrs Bunker kept "Mary Huett's head" in her shed. The britishfolklore page says Mr Bunker "dug up a stone head in the churchyard" and it came into Mrs Bunker's possession after his death — but does not say whose head it was. Given that both effigies had their heads damaged (apparently chopped), either head is possible. The Perplexity attribution to Mary should not be taken as established.

## James Bunker (1824–1904)

Son of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph Bunker (1792)]] and Mary Markham. Born 1824 at Stevington; christened 26 September 1824 — the same mass ceremony as his uncles [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer]] and [[ephraim-bunker-1821|Ephraim]], both three years his senior. He is the youngest of Joseph's surviving children to appear in records.

Married **Sophia Panter** (1829–1903) on **25 December 1850** at Stevington. The marriage was registered in Bedford district. (The user's notes give the surname as "Painter"; FamilySearch records it as "Panter"; the distinction is unresolved.)

Occupation: agricultural labourer throughout his life. Residence: Silver Street, Stevington, recorded in every census from 1841 to 1901. He died on 26 November 1904 at Stevington — the death registered in Bedford/Turvey district — having lived his entire life in the village. FamilySearch ID: L227-TV6.

## Ruth Bunker (1855–1910) — the bastardy case and its sequel

**Birth:** 3 June 1855 at **Lavendon, Buckinghamshire** (christened there 25 October 1857). The delayed christening echoes the Dissenter practice seen across this family. Lavendon is ~8 miles from Stevington, just over the county border into Buckinghamshire — the family was living there temporarily, returning to Stevington (Silver Street) by the 1861 census.

**1861 census:** Shown as "Bash Banker" (OCR corruption of "Ruth Bunker"), age 5, at Silver Street, Stevington, in James and Sophia's household alongside sister Jane (8).

**1880 — affiliation case:** Ruth Bunker summoned George Draper Whiting before the Bedford magistrates on 11–12 June 1880. The *Luton Times* and *Bedfordshire Times* both reported it: the parties had been in domestic service together at Mr Smith's farm, Stevington. Ruth brought the affiliation (bastardy) order successfully; Whiting was ordered to pay 1s. 6d. per week. See [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-findmypast]].

**1880 — marriage:** On **27 September 1880** — just 3½ months after the affiliation order — Ruth Bunker married George Draper Whiting at Stevington. He was 19; she was 25. Witness: John Sanders. George was born 13 December 1860 at Bolnhurst, Bedfordshire.

**The first child, George John Whiting (1880)**, appears in the 1881 census aged 1. Born in 1880 before the September marriage, he was almost certainly the child of the affiliation case. The marriage followed the order rapidly; George Draper's compliance — first with the maintenance order, then with the marriage — suggests a degree of willingness rather than pure compulsion.

**1881 census:** Ruth Whiting (née Bunker), age 25, occupation lacemaker, wife of George Whiting (20, general labourer), with infant son George John (1), at Stevington.

**Emigration to Canada:** The 1901 Canadian census records arrival year **1885** for both George and Ruth. The first three children (George John, Sarah, William, James) were born in England; John (1887) onwards were born in Quebec. They settled at **Sherbrooke, Quebec**, in the Eastern Townships.

**1901 Canada census:** Ruth ("Pule Whiting" — OCR corruption of "Ruth") age 45, born 3 June 1855, birthplace England, arrived 1885, Church of England, at Sherbrooke with George (40) and eight children still in the household.

**Death:** Ruth died on **17 February 1910** in Sherbrooke, Quebec, aged 54. George survived her, dying in 1939. Several children dispersed to the northeastern United States (New Hampshire, Vermont, Ontario).

## Resolving the 1880 newspaper open question

The "Ruth Bunker of Stevington" in the June 1880 affiliation case — previously unidentified in the wiki — is now confirmed as **Ruth Bunker (1855)**, daughter of James (1824) and Sophia Panter, granddaughter of Joseph (1792) and great-granddaughter of James Thomas Bunker (1764). The open question in [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-findmypast]] and [[bunker-brown-1788]] is resolved.

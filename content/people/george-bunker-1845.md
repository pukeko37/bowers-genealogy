George Bunker was born in 1845 in Millbrook, the youngest child of [[john-bunker-1803|John Bunker (1803–1889)]] and Maria West (1806). He was christened on 5 November 1848, three years after birth — a gap that marks out either delayed registration or Nonconformist practice. He appears in the Millbrook censuses of 1851 (aged 6) and 1861.

**Move to London.** By 1871 George was in Lambeth, Surrey, where he had married **[[harriet-brandon-1846|Harriet Brandon]]** in December 1865 — his first documented act outside Millbrook. The marriage in Lambeth suggests he went to London in the mid-1860s, leaving behind the village where his father and two brothers would spend their entire lives.

**Tottenham.** From 1881 through at least 1901, George and Harriet are at Tottenham, Middlesex. Their trajectory — Lambeth then Tottenham — follows a common path of working-class Londoners moving north and east as the city expanded. His occupation in London is not noted in these sources.

**Children.** Eight children with Harriet: George (1864, born before marriage — either pre-marital or a date approximation), John (1867), Sarah (1869), Alfred (1870), James Samuel (1873), Alleas (1876), Albert (1877), Minnie Louisa (1878–1914). None of the children are further researched.

**In context.** George is the last of John (1803)'s children to leave Millbrook, and the one who went furthest. His brother [[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph (1840)]] never left; his sister [[elizabeth-west-bunker-1824|Elizabeth West]] settled at Maulden three miles away. George went to the capital and stayed there. His death date and place are not yet recorded in FamilySearch; the 1901 Tottenham census is the last confirmed sighting.

Not to be confused with [[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker (1836)]], the son of James (1807), who followed a parallel path from Millbrook to Derbyshire and Derby.

John Tabram was born on 13 November 1836 in Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire, the third child of [[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]] and [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary Eusden]] in the [[tabraham-eusden-1828|Tabraham-Eusden family]]. His mother died around 1840–1841, when John was four or five. His eldest sister [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann]] married [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] in 1850 and moved to Swavesey, forming the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]].

John remained in Fenstanton for his entire life. He married **Elizabeth Lewis** on 8 November 1857 at Fenstanton — Elizabeth was born in 1837 at St Ives, Huntingdonshire, the daughter of James Lewis. She died in 1903. They had seven children, three of whom died young:

- Fanny Tabram (1858–1929)
- George Richard Tabram (1860 — death year uncertain; listed as 1871 in FamilySearch but appears in the 1881 census aged 21)
- [[john-tabram-1861|John Tabram]] (1861–1939) — died Buckden, Huntingdonshire; probate to Ethel Fuller Lomas (likely a daughter); effects £506 12s.
- Ellen Tabram (1863–1871)
- Rebecca Tabram (1866–1868)
- James Lewis Tabram (1868–1941)
- Sarah Lewis Tabram (1874–Deceased)

Three of the first four children — Fanny, John, and George Richard — were baptised together on 7 April 1861, a bulk baptism typical of the period. Two daughters, Ellen (1863–1871) and Rebecca (1866–1868), died in childhood.

In every census from 1871 to 1901 John is recorded in Fenstanton as an agricultural labourer. The household shrank over time: in 1871 there were five children at home; by 1891 and 1901 only John and Elizabeth remained. Elizabeth died in 1903. John is **absent from the 1911 census** — he would have been about 74 — and no confirmed GRO death entry has been found in the Huntingdonshire registers. The closest candidate is a death registration at Kettering, Northamptonshire, in 1912, but the connection is uncertain. FamilySearch records him as "1836–1901," which reflects his last census appearance rather than a confirmed death record. His actual death date and place remain unresolved.

### Note on spelling

Like his father and sisters, John appears variously as Tabram, Tabraham, Tabrain, and Tabrane across different records — phonetic variants that were transcribed differently by different clerks and indexers.

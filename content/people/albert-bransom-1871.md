Albert Bransom (1871 – 1 April 1940), eldest child of [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] and [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]]. Birth registered GRO Luton, September quarter 1871. Christened 8 October 1871. He married on 26 September 1893 — his wife was named Mary (born 1872, died 30 October 1950). The family lived at Yew Tree Cottages, Whipsnade. Both are buried at St Mary Magdalene.

Their son [[frederick-bransom-1894|Frederick Bransom]] served with the 2nd Battalion, Bedfordshire Regiment, and died of wounds on 1 August 1916 during the Battle of the Somme, aged 22 — the first man from Whipsnade parish to fall in the Great War.

From 1930 Albert was Edmund Blyth's sole helper in planting the Whipsnade Tree Cathedral — a living First World War memorial laid out as a medieval cathedral ground plan across twenty acres at the north-western edge of the parish. He and Blyth worked together for the full nine years of planting, from 1930 to 1939, and are described in one source as friends. Blyth had lived at Rosemary Cottage in Whipsnade before purchasing Chapel Farm in 1927; Albert at Yew Tree Cottages nearby — they were neighbours before they became collaborators. Blyth's commemorated comrades were Arthur Bailey and John Bennett (killed in the Spring Offensive of March 1918) and Francis Holland (died 1928 in a car crash); Albert was not the subject of the memorial but its maker, nine years of shared labour in a village that had already buried its own dead.

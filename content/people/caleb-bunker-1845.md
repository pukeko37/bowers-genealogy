Caleb Bunker was born in 1845 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire, christened 19 October 1845. He was the eighth of nine children of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and Elizabeth Watts. His father died in 1852 when Caleb was seven, and the family moved from Millbrook to Bedford. Caleb grew up with four brothers and four sisters who would later scatter across Bedfordshire, Derbyshire, Luton, London, and Peterborough. See [[bunker-watts-1830]].

By the 1861 census, the family was living at **Dane Street, St Paul's parish, Bedford**. Caleb was 15 and recorded as a **shoe maker** — almost certainly the start of an apprenticeship. His mother Elizabeth was head of household, working as a lace maker; his siblings Lydia, Charles, Martha, Phoebe, and Ann (Comfort Ann) were all present. The shoe and boot trade was a major Bedfordshire and Northamptonshire industry, and Caleb's early training in Bedford provides context for why his son [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick]] later settled in **Kettering** during the 1890s — one of England's premier boot and shoe manufacturing centres. The trade was in the family from Caleb's apprenticeship onwards. See [[caleb-bunker-1861-census]].

He married [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] on Christmas Day 1868 at Howard Chapel, Mill Street, Bedford. Their only child, [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick William Tall Bunker]], was born on 15 November 1869 in the Ampthill registration district — most likely Maulden, where Rebecca's family lived.

Rebecca died in July 1870, leaving Caleb a widower with a one-year-old son. Frederick was left with his maternal grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] in Maulden while Caleb moved to Matlock, Derbyshire — not, as it turns out, without connections there: his siblings [[george-bunker-1836|George]] and [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] also migrated to the Derbyshire area around the same period.

The **1871 census** shows Caleb as a lodger in the Joseph Fletcher household in Matlock, occupation **Porter** — a labourer's role. He is indexed as "Caleb Bunken" (transcription error). His birthplace is given as Ampthill, Bedfordshire; his marital status as widowed. The shoe-making craft he had trained in at Bedford had not yet been re-established; he arrived in Matlock as a recently bereaved labourer, not a skilled craftsman. Also in the same enumeration district that year: the Barnsley family, with Margaret (then 20) working as a shop assistant with her parents.

**The Derbyshire sibling cluster.** Caleb was not the only Bunker in Derbyshire. His sister [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] settled at Pilsley, and his brother [[george-bunker-1836|George]] — who had married [[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]] in Bedford in 1858 and was a Storekeeper there in 1871 — moved with his family to New Tupton between 1872 and 1875. The 1881 census reveals a striking convergence: George's son **Albert Bunker** was born in **New Tupton ~1875**, and Caleb's son **[[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker]]** was also born in **New Tupton ~1875**. Two brothers, each with a newborn child in the same small north Derbyshire village at the same time. Caleb was based in Matlock but clearly spent time in the Tupton area around 1875. This was the brief moment when three Bunker siblings were closest together in Derbyshire — before George drifted into Derby, Phoebe stayed at Pilsley, and Caleb consolidated his trade in Matlock. See [[new-tupton-derbyshire]].

He married [[margaret-barnsley-1850|Margaret Barnsley]] in 1871 at the **Wesleyan Chapel, Matlock Bath**, Derbyshire — just over a year after Rebecca's death, and in the same census year he arrived in Matlock. The Derbyshire Registrars Marriage Index (ref. RO/07/020) records the venue as "Wesleyan Chapel, Matlock Bath" — confirming that Caleb maintained his Nonconformist practice for both marriages. See [[caleb-bunker-marriage-1871-derbyshire-index]]. By the **1881 census** Caleb had established himself as a **Toys Small Dealer Boot & Shoe Maker** — a combined occupation suggesting a small shop selling toys alongside his craft trade. The business had already survived a financial crisis: in October 1880, Caleb instituted proceedings for **Liquidation by Arrangement** (a composition with creditors, not full personal bankruptcy) in the County Court of Derbyshire. Two creditors' meetings were held in Derby in November 1880. The notices in the *London Gazette* record him as of **Matlock Bath** — a distinct spa village in the Derwent valley, more specific than the "Matlock" of census records — and describe him as a **Boot and Shoe Maker, and Toy and Smallware Dealer**, trading as **Thomas Barnsley**. That last detail is striking: Caleb was conducting his business under his father-in-law's name. Whether Thomas Barnsley had originally established or lent capital to the shop, or whether Caleb had simply registered the business in Thomas's name, the record makes clear the enterprise was publicly identified with the Barnsley family rather than Caleb's own name. See [[london-gazette-1880-10-19-caleb-bunker-bankruptcy]] and [[london-gazette-1880-11-05-caleb-bunker-bankruptcy]]. The arrangement with creditors apparently succeeded: the 1881 census found Caleb still at his trade, and he continued in it for decades. Together Caleb and Margaret had six children: [[annie-elizabeth-bunker-1872|Annie]] (1872), [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert]] (1875), [[maggie-eliza-bunker-1876|Maggie]] (1876), [[agnes-maud-bunker-1878|Agnes]] (1878), [[may-gertrude-bunker-1880|May]] (1880), and [[ernest-caleb-bunker-1882|Ernest]] (1882). Frederick's 1881 census entry also places him in Matlock — father and son had contact alongside this growing second family. See [[bunker-barnsley-1871]].

By 1891 Caleb was in Derby (St Peter's), working as a Boot & Shoe Maker. A January 1894 court report in the *Derby Telegraph* places him and Margaret at **Traffic Street, Derby**, where he ran a shop selling toys alongside boot and shoe goods — consistent with his 1881 "Toys Small Dealer" description. Margaret was running the shop the day two boys stole from the till; she gave evidence at Derby Borough Court and the boys received six strokes of the birch. By 1901 and through 1911 he was at **60 High Street, Derby**, recorded in 1911 as "Boot Maker and Small Shopkeeper" with several lodgers in the household — the address was evidently a combined shop and boarding house. His daughter **[[annie-elizabeth-bunker-1872|Annie Elizabeth]]** had married by 1911 and appears in the census under her married name **Borrington**. Between 1911 and his death he moved to **Osmaston Road, Derby** — his address in all 1918 obituary notices.

He died suddenly on **22 May 1918** at **Lullington, Derbyshire**, while away from home on business — Lullington is a small village near Swadlincote in South Derbyshire, registered in the Burton upon Trent GRO district (hence the Staffordshire registration). The death was sudden, with no forewarning evident in the sources. Obituary notices appeared in Derby papers on **23 May** (*Derby Daily Telegraph*) and **24 May** 1918 (*Derby and Chesterfield Reporter*, *Derby Daily Telegraph*); further notices followed in the *Derbyshire Advertiser and Journal* on 31 May and 1 June. He was buried at **Nottingham Road Cemetery, Chaddesden, Derby, on 25 May 1918** — a Saturday. Margaret (who died in April 1922) was buried alongside. Caleb was 73. See [[caleb-bunker-death-notices-1918]].

Caleb was survived by his son Frederick by 14 years but seems to have had little contact with Frederick's family in Luton. His estrangement parallels his son Frederick's later estrangement from his own wife Eliza — a pattern of distance across the Bunker men of this generation.

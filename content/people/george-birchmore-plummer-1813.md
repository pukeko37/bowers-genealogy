George Birchmore Plummer was born in 1813 in Bedfordshire. The 1861 census records his birthplace as **Studham, Bedfordshire** — a village immediately east of Whipsnade — while the family narrative says he was christened at Harpenden, Hertfordshire (about 5 miles away). The discrepancy likely reflects a birth in Studham with a christening at a different parish. He was the illegitimate son of George Birchmore (1786–1838), a prosperous farmer at Roe End Farm, Flamstead, Hertfordshire, and Elizabeth Plummer (1790–1866). He was given his mother's surname. Elizabeth raised him alone; she never married.

His father George Birchmore married Ann Norton in 1815 — two years after George BP's birth — and had four legitimate children. The spelling Birchmore/Burchmore varies across records.

### Name History

George's name shifted throughout his life as he navigated the stigma of illegitimacy:
- Registered as **George Birchmore** in the 1841 census
- In his 1859 marriage record: **George Plummer Birchmore**
- From about 1860–61, after marrying Eliza, the birth of Ellen, and his father's death: **George Birchmore Plummer**
- The two surnames were used interchangeably for a period

### Three Marriages

1. **[[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]]** (1815–1858) — married 1 November 1835, [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]]. One son: [[george-birchmore-plummer-jr-1850|George Birchmore Plummer Jr]] (b. 1850). Anne's family converted to Mormonism and emigrated to Utah in 1853; Anne chose to stay with George. See [[birchmore-plummer-matthews-1835]].

2. **[[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]]** (1829–1867) — married 18 June 1859, Old Church, Saint Pancras. Three children: [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen]] (1860), [[elizabeth-birchmore-1862|Elizabeth]] (1862), Thomas (1865–1865). See [[birchmore-plummer-batchelor-1859]].

3. **[[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]]** (1820–1904) — married 26 December 1867, Whipsnade (nine months after Eliza's death). No children from this marriage, but Ruth brought three illegitimate children including [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]]. See [[birchmore-plummer-cooke-1867]].

### Residential History

The census records confirm George was firmly anchored in Whipsnade throughout his life:

- **1841**: Whipsnade (as George Birchmore)
- **1851**: Whipsnade
- **1861**: Whipsnade — **By Whipsnade Green**, in the household of Thomas Batchelar (his father-in-law). Occupation: **Agricultural Labourer**. Listed as "Son" (son-in-law). This entry was unlinked in FamilySearch because he is recorded as "George Birchmore" rather than "George Birchmore Plummer."
- **1871**: Whipsnade
- **1881**: Whipsnade
- **Houchen End** — a hamlet within Whipsnade parish; likely his specific address during one of these periods

George therefore spent his entire adult life as an agricultural labourer and later pub licensee within the Whipsnade area.

George died in 1884 — he had been at the Chequers Inn for seventeen years by then, so death at Whipsnade is almost certain, though not yet confirmed by a document.

### The Two London Visits

Despite a life rooted in Whipsnade, two events placed George at St Pancras, London:

1. **1850**: His son [[george-birchmore-plummer-jr-1850|GBP Jr]] was born at St Pancras Old Church — meaning George and [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne]] were in London in 1849–50.
2. **1859**: He married [[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]] at Old Church, Saint Pancras — and the print view now confirms Eliza was also a Whipsnade woman her whole life (born, lived, and died there).

Two Whipsnade residents choosing St Pancras for their marriage is striking. They did not go to London because either of them lived there; both were lifelong Whipsnade residents. Possible reasons:
- **A prior London connection**: George had been at St Pancras in 1849–50 when GBP Jr was born there; he may have had contacts or familiarity with the church.
- **Avoiding local notice**: Marrying away from the home parish would have bypassed village scrutiny for whatever personal reason.
- **St Pancras Old Church** had a long history as a marriage venue for those wishing to marry outside their home parish.

Whatever the reason, George returned to Whipsnade after the 1859 marriage — Eliza was still in Whipsnade in the 1861 census, and the children were born in Whipsnade.

### The Chequers Inn

George held the license for The Chequers pub in Whipsnade. The license had connections to Eliza's family and was passed to Ruth. Ruth died at the Chequers Inn in 1904.

George died 13 March 1884, aged about 71.

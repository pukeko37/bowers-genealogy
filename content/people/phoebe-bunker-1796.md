Phoebe Bunker (10 October 1796 – September 1849) was the fourth child of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. She never married and appears to have remained in Millbrook longer than any of her siblings: the 1841 census records her at the Village, Millbrook, at age 44, after her brothers Caleb and Joseph had already settled in Stevington and her brother James (1807) had his young family at Millbrook. She was buried at Bedford on 19 September 1849 — probably in a Nonconformist burial ground there, a move that came sometime after 1841 and before her death.

She had no children. The name Phoebe also appears in her father's generation: her great-aunt Phoebe Bunker (b. 1796, daughter of Edward 1742) is a collateral namesake.

The name Phoebe recurs in the next generation: [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe Alice Bunker]] (1843–1923), daughter of James (1807) and Elizabeth Watts, may have been named in her memory, as Phoebe (1796) died only four years before Phoebe Alice was born.

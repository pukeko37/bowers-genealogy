Alan Geoffrey Bowers was born 25 February 1928 in Portsmouth, the younger son of [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]] and [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Norman]]. He was born Alan Geoffrey Bunker; in 1936, when Alan was eight, his father changed the family name to Bowers — a decision never explained.

Alan and his brother Norman attended St Albans Cathedral School as day pupils, cycling the five miles each way between Harpenden and St Albans. Alan had stories of grabbing onto the backs of lorries on the long hill up into St Albans to ease the climb. *Source: family memory.* After finishing school in 1945, he served his National Service in the Army (1945–1947), posted to an army hospital in the desert in Egypt. He then studied medicine at Liverpool University Medical School (graduating 1954), where he met [[jacqueline-reynolds-1930|Jacqueline Lucy Reynolds]].

Alan and Jacqueline married in 1954 at Newcastle-under-Lyme, forming the [[bowers-reynolds-1954|Bowers-Reynolds family]]. Alan trained at Worcester Royal Infirmary (1954–1956).

In 1957, Alan and Jacqueline emigrated to Auckland, New Zealand. There were no jobs for newly qualified doctors in post-war Britain — Alan's entire Liverpool class had to emigrate. The passage was arranged through Jacqueline's relative Laurie Copeland, a captain with the Port Line; Alan worked as ship's doctor in exchange for one fare.

Their children: Andrew Nicholas Bowers (b. 1959), Richard Ian Bowers (b. 1962), and Philippa Jane Bowers (b. 1964) — all born in New Zealand.

In 1968, Alan's parents Ivy and Roland visited Auckland. The visit prompted them to retire to New Zealand: they returned to England, wound down the furniture business with Harry Thody, sold their Kinsborne Green house in Harpenden, and emigrated to Auckland in 1970. Alan died in 2012, aged 84.

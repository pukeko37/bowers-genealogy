Newman Thulborn (also spelled Thurlborn, Thurlbone, Thurlbourn, Thurlbon in various records) was born in Oakington, Cambridgeshire — most likely in 1824 according to a bishop's transcript, though the death registration in 1888 gives his age as 67, implying 1821. FamilySearch uses "1821"; the baptism record says 25 July 1824. The census ages from 1841 through 1881 consistently suggest ~1824. He spent his entire life in Oakington, an occupation of agricultural labourer from at least 1851 to 1881. He died in 1888 in Chesterton, the registration district for Oakington.

He married **[[hannah-how-1824|Hannah How]]** (born ~1824, Kingston, Cambridgeshire) probably around 1849–1850. Their eight children were born at Oakington between 1851 and 1872:

1. **Lydia Thurlborn** (1851–1883) — married Samuel Trundley at Swavesey, 15 July 1873; died 1883 aged ~32
2. **[[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]]** (21 Aug 1856–1922) — married [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] 30 October 1877, Swavesey; 9 children; died Huntingdonshire
3. **Joseph Thurlborn** (1859–1947) — long-lived; died aged ~88
4. **Ezra Thurlbon** (1861–Deceased) — present in 1871 (age 10) and 1881 (age 20) censuses
5. **Lemuel / Samuel Thurlbourn** (c.1864–1959) — called "Lemuel" in 1871 census (age 7) and "Samuel" in 1881 census (age 17); likely the same person; died aged ~95
6. **Albert Thurlbon** (1866–Deceased) — present in 1871 (age 5) and 1881 (age 14) censuses
7. **Clara Thurlbon** (1870–Deceased) — present in 1871 (age 1) and 1881 (age 11) censuses
8. **Walter Thurlbourn** (1872–Deceased) — present in 1881 (age 9)

By 1871, the elder daughters Lydia and Emily were no longer at home. Emily's 1871 residence is recorded as Willingham, Cambridgeshire — about 6 miles from Oakington — suggesting she was in domestic service at age 15. By 1873 Lydia was marrying in Swavesey. Newman's household in 1871 and 1881 consisted entirely of younger children still at home.

Newman's parents were **James Thulbourn** (1781–1851) and **Sarah Curtis** (1779–1867), who married at Oakington on 12 October 1800. Newman was the youngest or near-youngest of a large sibling group including Dickerson, William (multiple), James, Sarah, John, William, Jane, Thomas, Joseph, and Francis Thurlbourn. His parents' family is not yet researched beyond identifying the generation.

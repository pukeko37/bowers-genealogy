Ruth Bunker (3 June 1855 – 17 February 1910) was the second daughter of [[james-bunker-1824|James Bunker (1824–1904)]] and Sophia Panter, a granddaughter of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph Bunker (1792)]] and Mary Markham, and a great-granddaughter of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. She was born in Lavendon, Buckinghamshire, spent her working life in Stevington, married under a bastardy order, and died in Sherbrooke, Quebec, aged 54. FamilySearch ID: LVSD-5QH.

## Birth and childhood

Ruth was born at **Lavendon, Buckinghamshire** — across the county border from Stevington, about 8 miles away — where her parents were living temporarily. She was not christened until **25 October 1857**, more than two years after her birth, at Lavendon parish church. The delayed christening follows the Dissenting practice seen throughout this family (the 26 September 1824 mass christening of her father and his brothers is the clearest earlier instance). By 1861 the family had returned to Silver Street, Stevington, where the census records her age 5 as "Bash Banker" — a double OCR corruption of "Ruth Bunker."

## The affiliation case, 1880

In 1880 Ruth was a domestic servant at the farm of **Mr Smith** in Stevington. A fellow-servant there was **George Draper Whiting** (born 13 December 1860, Bolnhurst, Bedfordshire) — six years her junior. Ruth became pregnant by George, and in June 1880 she took him before the Bedford magistrates:

> "George Draper Whiting, St Paul's, Bedford, was summoned by Ruth Bunker, of Stevington, to show cause [as to paternity]. At one time the parties were in the service of Mr Smith, farmer, and lived in the same house, being domestic servant and [farm servant]. Mr Clare for complainant, and Mr Mitchell for defendant. Order 1s. 6d. per week and costs."
> — *Luton Times*, 11 June 1880; *Bedfordshire Times*, 12 June 1880

The order was granted. Ruth was 24; George was 19. She had secured legal recognition of his paternity and a maintenance payment.

## Marriage and its sequel

Three and a half months later, on **27 September 1880**, Ruth Bunker and George Draper Whiting married at Stevington. The witness was John Sanders. George was still only 19; Ruth was 25 — a 6-year age gap with the woman older. Their first child, **George John Whiting** (1880), appears in the 1881 census as 1 year old — born before the marriage, the child of the affiliation case. The 1881 census shows the family at Stevington: Ruth Whiting (25, lacemaker), George Whiting (20, general labourer), and infant George John.

The marriage that followed a bastardy order proved lasting. George and Ruth went on to have **eleven children** across fifteen years:

## Emigration to Canada

The family emigrated to Canada in **1885**, settling at **Sherbrooke, Québec** in the Eastern Townships. The four oldest children were born in England; all subsequent children were born in Quebec. The 1901 Canadian census records Ruth as "Pule Whiting" (OCR corruption of Ruth Whiting), age 45, born 3 June 1855, birthplace England, religion Church of England, with George (40) and eight children in the Sherbrooke household.

## Death

Ruth died on **17 February 1910** in Sherbrooke, Québec, aged 54. George survived her by 29 years, dying in 1939. Several of their children dispersed across the northeastern United States — George John and John moved to Ontario and New Hampshire respectively; Frederick settled in Vermont; Sarah married a French-Canadian (Edmond Martel) in New Hampshire.

## Note: the name Ruth in the Bunker family

Ruth is a recurring name in this family. Earlier Ruths: [[ruth-bunker-1794|Ruth Bunker (1794)]], daughter of James Thomas and Lydia Brown, died on her christening day aged 11; Ruth Bunker (1822), daughter of Caleb (1790), listed as deceased without date; Ruth Martha Bunker (1790), daughter of Joseph (1754). This Ruth (1855) is the first of the name to leave a substantial record. See [[bunker-brown-1788]] for the pattern.

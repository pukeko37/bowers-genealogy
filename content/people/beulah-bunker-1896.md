Beulah Rebecca Lucy Bunker was born on 2 August 1896 in Kettering, Northamptonshire, the elder child of [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] and [[eliza-geary-1859|Eliza Geary]]. Her brother was [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland]] (Ron), born two years later. The family moved from Kettering to Ilford, Essex by 1901, and then to Luton, Bedfordshire, where Beulah grew up.

Her full name carries two layers of family memory: **Rebecca** honours her paternal grandmother [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] (1844–1870), who died when Frederick was a year old; **Lucy** honours her maternal grandmother [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]] (1826–1895), who died the year before Beulah was born. Both grandmothers were gone before Beulah arrived.

By 1911, aged 14, Beulah was working as an **apprentice dressmaker** — an early step into Luton's textile trades. In 1921, she had progressed to working as a **milliner** at **J C Kershaw Hat Manufacturers, George Street, Luton**. The Luton hat trade was a major local industry; millinery was one of its skilled branches.

Beulah married [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]] on 2 August 1920 — her 24th birthday — at Little Gaddesden, Hertfordshire. She was living at 50 Hampton Road, Luton at the time; Harry was in Little Gaddesden. Her brother [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland]] witnessed the wedding alongside [[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah Winifred Norman]] — Ivy's youngest sister, then 18. The two witnesses stand for the two families about to be joined: Roland for the Bunkers, Norah for the Normans. That Ivy's sister was already present at this wedding, nearly a year before Roland and Ivy married, shows how close Harry and the Normans already were. Less than a year later, Roland married [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Norman]]. The two couples were the bridge between the Thody/Cook world of Whipsnade and the Norman/Birchmore world of London.

Harry and Beulah lived in Luton, raising three children. Harry and Roland ran **Thody Bros** — Harry doing furniture removals and upholstery, Roland the shop. By 1939, Beulah and Harry were living at **"Carnoy," 9 Oakley Road, Luton**, their mother Eliza (then 80) in the same household. The house was named after Carnoy on the Somme — Harry had served there in the Great War. Beulah's occupation in 1939 was recorded as "Unpaid Domestic Duties."

Beulah died suddenly on **29 June 1948**, aged 51, from an **asthma attack**. Roland and Ivy were on holiday in Killarney, Ireland at the time; they had to cut their trip short and return to Luton on receiving the news. She was buried at **Luton General Cemetery on 3 July 1948**. The memorial card read: *"In Loving Memory of Beulah Thody whose spirit departed most peacefully on 29th June, 1948 Aged 51 Years Laid to rest in Luton General Cemetery on Saturday, 3rd July, 1948. 'Carnoy,' 9 Oakley Road, Luton."*

Probate was granted in London on 26 August 1948. Beulah had left a will; her estate was valued at **£1,920 0s. 4d.** — a respectable sum in 1948. Notably, probate was handled by two professionals — **John Quint Clayton** (solicitor) and **Joseph Taylor** (chartered accountant) — rather than by Harry directly. This may reflect that her estate included assets tied to the Thody Bros business, requiring professional administration.

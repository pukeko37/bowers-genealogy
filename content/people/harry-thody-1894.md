Harry Thody was born 26 August 1894, the only child of [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] (1850–1911) and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856–1930). William had been previously married to Mathilde Elizabeth Parent, and Harry grew up with four step-siblings from that first marriage; of these, Clarence Archibald Thody (born 1884, Houghton Regis) and Florence Lena Thody (born ~1890, Totternhoe) appear in the 1901 and 1911 censuses. Harry grew up at Studham and then Flamstead, on the Bedfordshire/Hertfordshire border.

William was a Police Constable in 1901. He died before 1911, leaving Lucy widowed with Harry (then 16) and step-daughter Florence still at home. Harry was apprenticed as an upholsterer.

Harry volunteered for the Army in September 1914, aged 20, joining the Bedfordshire Regiment (No. 19831) and later the Royal Army Service Corps. He was deployed to France in 1915, driving supply wagons to the front lines. He fought through the Somme, Arras, Passchendaele, Lens, and Cambrai, was wounded, and participated in the Advance into Germany. He was demobilised in February 1919, earning the 1914–15 Star, General Service Medal, and Victory Medal. The family home was thereafter known from Studham near Dunstable.

Harry married [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Rebecca Lucy Bunker]] on 2 August 1920 — Beulah's 24th birthday — at Little Gaddesden, Hertfordshire. He was residing in Little Gaddesden at the time; Beulah at 50 Hampton Road, Luton. The witnesses were Beulah's brother [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]] and [[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah Winifred Norman]] — Ivy's youngest sister, then 18. That Ivy's sister stood witness at this wedding, nearly a year before Roland and Ivy married, shows that Harry and the Norman family were already closely connected before the Roland-Ivy match was made. The couple formed the [[thody-bunker-1920|Thody-Bunker family]].

Roland married [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Norman]] less than a year later.

Before the partnership, Harry was trading independently from **35 Dunstable Road, Luton**. A photograph of the van — dated approximately 1928–29 by the appearance of Harry's children Robert (b. 1924) and Marguerite (b. 1926), who are present — confirms this: the livery reads *H. THODY, 35 Dunstable Rd, Luton* with services listed as *Upholstery, Bedding, Curtains, Loose Covers* and, in large letters, **REMOVALS** (phone 1342). Removals was the primary business; the soft furnishings work was secondary. The photograph predates the partnership with Ron by at least a year.

After Roland left the Navy in 1930, Harry and Roland started **Thody Bros. Ltd** in Luton — incorporated as a limited company. Harry ran the removals and upholstery side; Roland ran the shop at the corner of **Dunstable Road and Oak Road, Luton**. The name "Bros" was a stretch; they were brothers-in-law, not brothers. A photograph of one of the new pantechnicon vans — bodied by **H G Creasey & Sons** of Knebworth, Hertfordshire — the photograph was published by Creasey as an example of their coachbuilding work, and the Kensworth contact number visible in it is Creasey's own — shows the full livery: *THODY BROS. Ltd REMOVALS* on the front, *REMOVALS & STORAGE* and *THODY BROS. Ltd. HOUSE FURNISHERS, Dunstable Road Luton* on the side. The *Removals & Storage* wording reflects a real asset: Thody Bros operated a lockup yard and storage building at the far end of Oak Road, near Luton Town FC's Kenilworth Road ground. When the business wound up in 1970, the yard was sold to Luton Town FC, which converted it into a gymnasium. It has since been demolished as the stadium has expanded. Harry was still running the company as director in 1968, when he gave his occupation on his marriage certificate as "Company Director." Thody Bros was wound up in 1970.

In 1939, Harry and Beulah were living at **"Carnoy," 9 Oakley Road, Luton** — the house named, almost certainly, after Carnoy on the Somme, where Harry had served in the Great War. Beulah's mother Eliza Geary (then 80) also lived with them. Three children were in the household: Robert Eric, Marguerite Dorreen, and John Brian.

Beulah died suddenly on 29 June 1948, aged 51, from an asthma attack. Harry lived to 92, dying on 29 September 1986 at Silsoe, Bedfordshire. He married Nellie Walker (née Haze, born ~1906, died 1989) on 6 July 1968 at St James' Church, Silsoe. There were no children of this second marriage. The probate calendar records his name as "Harry Thoday" — a spelling variant. At his death he was living at **15 Bedford Avenue, Silsoe**. Probate was granted in London on 25 February 1987; his estate was £449.

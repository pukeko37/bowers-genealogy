David Bunker was born in 1837 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire (birth registration Ampthill), the fourth child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]]. He died in 1920 in Peterborough, aged ~83 — the longest-lived of James and Elizabeth's nine children. He was Caleb's elder brother.

He married **Marian Bentley** on **11 October 1859** at St Paul's Church, Bedford. Marian was born about 1831 in Biggleswade, Bedfordshire — some six years older than David. They had **four children**, all born in the Bedford area before the family's move northeast:

1. Elizabeth Jane Bunker (b. ~1860, born Bedford)
2. Marian Bunker (b. ~1862, born Bedford)
3. Albert Bunker (b. ~1863, born Bedford)
4. Charles Bunker (b. ~1866, born Bedford)

**The northeast migration.** Unlike his siblings, who moved either to Derbyshire (Caleb, George, Phoebe) or London (Charles) or stayed in Bedfordshire (Lydia, Martha, Comfort Ann), David moved northeast. By the **1871 census** he was in **St Ive, Huntingdonshire**, working as a **Groom** — a step down in skilled terms from his father's agricultural labourer background, but placing him in service rather than field labour. He was already 15 miles outside Bedford at this stage.

By **1881** he had settled at **Peterborough St John the Baptist** with a decisive occupational step up: **Wagon Builder Storekeeper (Rail)** — a combined role in the railway works, likely GNR or Midland Railway which both operated through Peterborough. This is a salaried, literate, indoor position; a man trusted with inventory and parts management in a major Victorian industry. He remained in this orbit through 1891 (**Engineers Store Keeper**) — a white-collar railways role.

The **1891 census** records him as "Patient" rather than "Head" in the relationship field — he appears to have been in some kind of institution (hospital or infirmary) at census time, though he recovered and continued in Peterborough through 1911.

By **1901** only his wife Marian (now 68) and daughter Elizabeth (40) remained in the household, suggesting the other children had left. In **1911**, David (73) and Marian (79) are still together in Peterborough — a marriage of over 50 years, both born in Bedfordshire, finishing their lives 80 miles northeast in a cathedral city built on railways.

David died in 1920 in Peterborough. His wife Marian died in 1914.

**Residence history:** 1837–1852 Millbrook · 1852–~1870 Bedford · 1871 St Ive, Huntingdonshire · 1881–1920 Peterborough

James Hain (1877–1932) was the farmer at Dell Farm, Whipsnade, son of [[james-edward-hain-1832|James Edward Hain]] (1832–1911). He married [[margaret-anne-bransom-1880|Margaret Anne Bransom]] in December 1905, bringing the Bransom family — who had been rooted in Whipsnade for generations — into the Dell Farm household.

The 1911 census records James as head of household at Dell Farm, aged 33, occupation Farmer. Also in the household were his widowed father [[james-edward-hain-1832|James Edward Hain]] (private means), his sister Margaret D Hain, and his sister-in-law [[minnie-rose-bransom-1884|Minnie Rose Bransom]] (27, Housekeeper) — [[margaret-anne-bransom-1880|Margaret Anne]]'s younger sister, who had come to help with the household. On the same census day, the Hains' younger daughter Dora (born 1910, age 0) appears in the household of Margaret Anne's parents [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] and [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] — the baby was visiting her maternal grandparents.

James and Margaret Anne had two daughters:
- [[margaret-anne-hain-1907|Margaret Anne Hain]] (1907–1994)
- [[dora-minnie-hain-1910|Dora Minnie Hain]] (1910–1996)

Dell Farm was part of the Pedley Settled Estates, auctioned in November 1915. James purchased the farm at that auction, becoming owner rather than tenant. A 1926 valuation assessment records him as a "Good Farmer." James died in London in the June 1932 quarter, aged 54 (GRO: London C, vol. 1c, p. 2). He is not buried at St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade. The Hain family had roots in the Pimlico/Grosvenor area of London — London C (Westminster) would encompass that district — so his death there almost certainly reflects family connections rather than a move away from farming.

Charlotte Matthews was born 16 June 1823 in Whipsnade, Bedfordshire, christened 29 June 1823. She was the daughter of [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews]] and [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]], and the younger sister of [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]].

Charlotte married [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]] (1816–1900), who was born in Kensworth — the same village as [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]], his sister. This connection meant Charlotte was the sister-in-law of Ruth Cooke, who later became the third wife of [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] (Anne's widower). See [[cook-matthews-whipsnade]].

Unlike her brothers [[able-matthews-1813|Able]] and [[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis]], Charlotte did not emigrate. She appears in every census from 1851 to 1901 in Whipsnade, and her specific address is given as By Whipsnade Green — the same hamlet as her parents and the Cook family. Her occupation in 1851 was Straw Plaiter, a common occupation for women in the Bedfordshire straw hat trade.

Charlotte outlived nearly all of her generation. She died in 1910 aged 86 — 52 years after her sister Anne, and after a lifetime spent in the same small village where she was born. Her death was registered in the Luton district.

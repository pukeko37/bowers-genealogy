John James Mason (known as Jack) was born 21 November 1878 in Clerkenwell, London. He was one of three sons of [[mason-james-1874|Richard Mason and Catherine James]], Welsh immigrants who ran a milk shop at 27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield. Both parents died young — Richard in 1890, Catherine in 1900 — leaving John and his brothers orphaned.

In 1891, aged 12, John was living at St Sepulchre, Newgate — immediately adjacent to Smithfield and Hosier Lane, where the Norman family had moved. This proximity almost certainly explains how the Mason and Norman families became acquainted. By 1901, John was working as a dairy shop keeper at St Sepulchre with his brothers; by 1911 in Lambeth, assisting in the business.

John married [[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel Annie Norman]] in 1912 in Hackney, forming the [[mason-norman-1912|Mason-Norman family]]. Six years later, his brother Isaac married Mabel's sister Alice — two brothers, two sisters. They ran a newsagent and stationer's shop at 150 Acre Lane, Clapham.

Their children were John Isaac Mason (1913–1984) and Catherine Ellen Mason (1916–1970). John Isaac emigrated to Brazil in 1961, a year before his father's death.

By 1939 the household at 150 Acre Lane included not just John and Mabel but also John's older brother [[richard-william-mason-1875|Richard William Mason]] (then 64) and Mabel's unmarried sister Nellie Norman (then 54). When the shop was damaged by V-weapons in 1944, John, Mabel, and Nellie took refuge at [[harpenden|Park Hill, Harpenden]] with Ivy and Roland. After the war, they rebuilt and continued running the shop.

John died on **8 February 1962** at **16 Daysbrook Road, London SW2**, aged 83. He died intestate (no will); administration was granted to his widow **Mabel Annie Mason**. His estate was valued at £344 6s. — modest, suggesting the newsagent business was not counted in the estate or had already passed on. His brother [[isaac-mason-1882|Isaac]] had died seven years earlier at **17 Daysbrook Road** — literally next door.

### Note

The birthplace is Clerkenwell, not Hackney — all census records confirm Clerkenwell (1881) or London broadly. Previous records showing "Hackney" reflect census indexer estimates, not stated birthplace.

The Smithfield connection between the Masons (27 Hosier Lane) and the Normans (Hosier Lane area) is the most likely explanation for how these families came together. The geography made it almost inevitable.

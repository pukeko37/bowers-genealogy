Thomas Norman was born in 1827 in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire, and died in 1905 in Huntingdonshire. He was an agricultural labourer throughout his working life — typical for rural Cambridgeshire in the mid-Victorian era.

He was the third surviving child of [[thomas-norman-1793|Thomas Norman (1793–1855)]] and [[elizabeth-fear-1794|Elizabeth Fear (1794–1832)]], born in Swavesey. His father was himself from Over, Cambridgeshire — just a few miles away — and was the son of [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman (1769–1842)]] and [[frances-green-1769|Frances Green (1769–1810)]]. The Norman family had been in the Over/Swavesey area for at least three generations before Thomas (1827).

He married [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] on 3 February 1850, forming the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]]. Together they had seven children: George (1851), Richard (1853), Tom (1856), Charles (1859), Mary Ann (1861), Alfred Eusden (1865), and Robert Henry (1868).

His son [[charles-norman-1859|Charles]] was the only child to leave Swavesey for London; the rest remained in Cambridgeshire or the neighbouring Huntingdonshire parishes throughout their lives. The FamilySearch print view records Thomas as having moved to Alconbury Weston by 1901 — his son [[tom-norman-1856|Tom]] was also at Alconbury Weston — but his death in 1905 was registered in the **St Ives registration district**, not the Huntingdon district that covers Alconbury Weston. This suggests he either did not move to Alconbury Weston or had returned to the Swavesey area (which falls within St Ives) before his death. The specific parish of death is not yet confirmed. Thomas died in 1905, aged about 78.

Thomas's mother Elizabeth Fear died when he was about five (1832), giving him an early experience of maternal loss that would echo in his own family: his wife Ann's mother died when Ann was twelve.

Thomas Barnsley was born in 1817 in Bradwell, Derbyshire — a village in the Hope Valley of the Peak District. His father was **Luke Barnsley**. He died at Church-street, Bradwell on 11 August 1890, having returned to his birthplace after decades away.

He married **Eliza Bradwall** on 28 May 1838 at Hope, Derbyshire — the parish church for the Hope Valley. Eliza was also from Bradwell (born there c. 1819); her father was John Bradwall. The match was a local one, made before the family moved to Lancashire.

## Children

All four known children were christened at St Michael and All Angels, Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire:

1. **Elizabeth Barnsley** — christened 11 October 1840
2. **Martha Barnsley** — christened 2 June 1844
3. **Joseph Barnsley** — born 23 March 1847, christened 9 May 1847; married Maria Ellen Boon in Lancashire on 17 May 1869; later an oil and tallow manufacturer at Pontefract-place, Leeds. It was Joseph who received administration of Thomas's estate in 1890.
4. **[[margaret-barnsley-1850|Margaret Barnsley]]** — born 14 April 1850, christened 12 May 1850; later married [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] in 1871.

## Occupation history

Thomas was not a specialist tradesman but a versatile small retailer who sold whatever the local market supported.

**1841** — Ashton-under-Lyne (Cricketty district). Household: Thomas (age 20), Eliza (20), daughter Elizabeth (age 1). No occupation recorded in 1841 census format.

**1851** — Ashton-under-Lyne. Occupation: **Dealer in Old and New Clothes** — a secondhand and new clothing trader, a common trade in the industrial cotton towns of Lancashire. Household: Thomas (34), Eliza wife (30, born Bradwell), Elizabeth daughter (10), Joseph son (4), Margaret daughter (0), plus Caroline Bradurle (28, born Bradwell) — probably Eliza's sister, a lodger or visitor.

At some point between 1851 and 1871, Thomas left Ashton-under-Lyne and moved to Matlock, Derbyshire. The 1861 census record has not been located. The move took him from an industrial cotton town to a Derbyshire spa and tourist destination — a shift in market requiring a different kind of retail.

**1871** — Matlock, Derbyshire. Occupation: **Stationer** — appropriate for a spa and tourist trade. Household: Thomas (54), Eliza wife (52, born Bradwell), Margaret daughter (20, born Ashton-under-Lyne). This is the census year in which [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] also arrived in Matlock, met Margaret, and married her at the Wesleyan Chapel, Matlock Bath. Thomas was 54 and still active in his shop.

**1880** — The Caleb Bunker bankruptcy notices in the *London Gazette* (October–November 1880) describe Caleb as of **Matlock Bath** and trading as **Thomas Barnsley** — boot and shoe maker and toy and smallware dealer. The business carried Thomas's name but Caleb's trades. Thomas was 63 at the time of the bankruptcy.

**1881** — Darley aka North Darley (Darley Dale), Derbyshire. Occupation: **Formerly Shop Keeper** — past tense, retired. Household: Thomas (64), Eliza wife (62, born Bradwell). The bankruptcy had occurred six months earlier; by April 1881 the shop was gone and Thomas had stepped back to Darley Dale.

**1890** — Died at Church-street, Bradwell — his birthplace — on 11 August 1890. The probate entry describes him as *"formerly of Matlock Bath in the Parish of Matlock but late of Church-street Bradwell"*, confirming the direct connection between Thomas and the Matlock Bath address of Caleb's business.

## The Barnsley name on Caleb's business

The most likely reading of the evidence is that Thomas held the shop premises and trading name at Matlock Bath while he was still active, and that Caleb — who married into the family in 1871 — took over the day-to-day running and pivoted the trade from stationery to boots, shoes, and toys. Thomas's name remained on the door as the registered trader, perhaps for credit or licensing reasons, while Caleb ran the business entirely. When the crisis came in 1880, it was Caleb who instituted the bankruptcy proceedings and bore the consequences. Thomas retired to Darley Dale shortly after, describing himself simply as a former shopkeeper. He took no further visible part in commerce.

## Probate

Administration (with the Will) granted at Derby on 3 October 1890 to son **Joseph Barnsley** of Pontefract-place, Leeds, oil and tallow manufacturer — one of the next of kin. Estate: **£50 1s. 7d.** — a very modest sum for a man of seventy-three, consistent with having wound up his business interests a decade earlier and lived quietly in retirement. See [[thomas-barnsley-probate-1890]] and [[thomas-barnsley-probate-1890-screenshot]].

The Matlock period is significant: this is when Caleb Bunker also arrived in Matlock after Rebecca Tall's death in 1870. Thomas Barnsley, his daughter Margaret, and Caleb Bunker were all in the same Matlock area at the same time — the likely point of connection for Caleb and Margaret.

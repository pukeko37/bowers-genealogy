George Stewart Main — known in the family as **George Main** — was born in 1919 in Dalston, North London, the only child of Charles Stewart Main (1884–1963) and [[elizabeth-ann-norman-1888|Elizabeth Ann Norman]]. See [[main-norman-1916]].

Dalston placed him in the same Kingsland/Hackney area where most of the Norman family lived. His mother Elizabeth was at 143 De Beauvoir Road; his aunt [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Norman]] at 121; other aunts and uncles nearby. He grew up embedded in the Norman family network.

## Childhood and War

He had, by his own account, an uneventful childhood, though he remembered seaside holidays in Kent — Margate, Broadstairs, and Ramsgate — and visits to Dreamland Amusement Park. When war broke out he served as Rifleman 689895 through six years of overseas Army postings. He was demobilised in 1946.

## New Zealand High Commission

After demob, George resumed his pre-war employment at the **New Zealand High Commission** in London, where he remained until retirement. This career is almost certainly the key to understanding how his aunt [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Bunker]] and uncle [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]] were able to emigrate to Auckland in 1968 — George had NZ government contacts and understood the emigration process from the inside.

He settled in **Birchington-on-Sea, Kent** — a small seaside town on the Isle of Thanet, not far from Broadstairs and Margate, the childhood holiday coast he had loved. He was living there from at least the 1960s, possibly earlier, while still working at the New Zealand High Commission in the Haymarket, London — a demanding daily commute, likely around 1.5 hours each way. *The precise commute time in the 1960s is not yet verified.*

## The Kent Household

George married **Dorothy**, who was a substantial partner in the care the household provided. Together they supported three relatives through their final decades:

- Aunt [[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]] (widowed 1955, died 1980)
- Aunt [[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah Norman]] (widowed 1978, died 1996)
- Cousin [[gwendoline-may-norman-1915|Gwendoline May Norman]] (Thomas George's daughter, died 2000)

George regarded this as his duty, but Dorothy's contribution was essential to sustaining it. While George was commuting to central London, the day-to-day care of the household fell substantially to her. The commitment ran from at least the 1960s through to 2000 — the better part of four decades. George retired from the New Zealand High Commission around 1984.

## Later Life

In retirement George entered what his FamilySearch record calls his "songwriting phase." He wrote songs including "Birchington-on-Sea" and "Thanet Forever" — titles that reflect his deep attachment to the Kent coast he had known since childhood. He made several television appearances performing his songs.

His death date is not recorded in available sources.

Richard Norman was born around 1723 — christened 26 November 1723 at Cottenham, Cambridgeshire — and died in 1783 at Over, Cambridgeshire. He is the father of [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman]] (1769–1842), and through him the great-great-grandfather of [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Helen Norman]] (1899–1994).

His parents were **William Norman** (1687–1747) and **Alice Briggs** (1685–1766), who married at Cottenham on 3 May 1710. Richard was the youngest of their surviving children; his siblings included Susan (1710, died same year), Alice (1711), Thomas (1712, 1713, 1714, 1715 — several short-lived Thomas entries suggesting high infant mortality), William (1717), Lydia (1718), and Susanna (1722). See [[norman-briggs-1710]].

**Occupation.** Richard is recorded as a **Weaver** in 1753 at Over — a skilled trade. The Cambridgeshire fens had a tradition of domestic weaving alongside agriculture. His move from Cottenham (birthplace) to Over (where he settled and died) was probably connected to trade or work opportunity.

**First marriage — Mary Essex.** Richard married [[mary-essex-1722|Mary Essex]] on 27 April 1740 at Cottenham — his birth parish, and hers. Mary (1722–1765) was the daughter of John Essex (1703–1762) and Alice. They had two known children: William Norman (1739–1822) and Richard Norman (1745). Mary died in 1765 at Cottenham. See [[norman-essex-1740]].

**Second marriage — Mary Hall.** On 1 October 1749 at Over, Richard married **Mary Hall** (1730–1782). Their son [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman]] was christened at Over in February 1769 — born when Richard was around 46. Mary Hall died in 1782, a year before Richard. See [[norman-hall-1749]].

Richard died in 1783 at Over, having outlived both wives. His son Robert (by Mary Hall) was about fourteen at his father's death — old enough to have known him, but young to lose him.

The Norman family's deep roots in the Over/Swavesey/Cottenham cluster are now visible across at least four generations: William Norman (Cottenham) → Richard Norman (Cottenham, then Over) → Robert Norman (Over) → Thomas Norman (Over/Swavesey). The Cambridgeshire fens were their world for well over a century.

Richard Norman was born around 1723 — christened 26 November 1723 at Cottenham, Cambridgeshire — and died in 1783 at Over, Cambridgeshire. He is the father of [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman]] (1769–1842), and through him the great-great-grandfather of [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Helen Norman]] (1899–1994).

His parents were **William Norman** (1687–1747) and **Alice Briggs** (1685–1766), who married at Cottenham on 3 May 1710. Richard was the youngest of their surviving children; his siblings included Susan (1710, died same year), Alice (1711), Thomas (1712, 1713, 1714, 1715 — several short-lived Thomas entries suggesting high infant mortality), William (1717), Lydia (1718), and Susanna (1722). See [[norman-briggs-1710]].

**Occupation.** Richard is recorded as a **Weaver** in 1753 at Over — a skilled trade. The Cambridgeshire fens had a tradition of domestic weaving alongside agriculture. His move from Cottenham (birthplace) to Over (where he settled and died) was probably connected to trade or work opportunity.

**Marriage — Mary Hall.** The one marriage that can be asserted with reasonable confidence is to **Mary Hall** (1730–1782), recorded on 1 October 1749 at Over, Cambridgeshire. Their son [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman]] was christened at Over in February 1769. Mary Hall died in 1782, a year before Richard. See [[norman-hall-1749]].

**The Mary Essex attribution — disputed.** FamilySearch profile P68D-T5X (our Richard) also attributes a first marriage to [[mary-essex-1722|Mary Essex]] (1722–1765), married 27 April 1740 at Cottenham. This attribution is likely a FamilySearch data error and should not be accepted without further evidence. The reasons are:

- **Bigamy.** Mary Essex died in 1765, yet the marriage to Mary Hall is dated 1749 — sixteen years earlier. If both marriages belong to the same man, he was simultaneously married to two women from 1749 to 1765. This is implausible in Church of England records.
- **Duplicate profiles.** FamilySearch has a second Richard Norman profile — **L565-ZVH**, dated 1718–1753 — which is *also* attached to Mary Essex's same 1740 Cottenham marriage. The children listed under L565-ZVH are entirely different from those listed under P68D-T5X. Two Richard Norman profiles cannot both have legitimately married the same woman in the same place on the same date. One attribution is wrong.
- **L565-ZVH resolves the problem.** If the Essex marriage belongs to L565-ZVH (who died in 1753), Mary Essex would have been widowed by 1753 and dead by 1765 — entirely consistent. Our Richard (P68D-T5X) would then have married only Mary Hall in 1749, with no prior marriage, and no bigamy.
- **Contaminated profile.** P68D-T5X has accumulated obviously erroneous records — including a daughter Mary Norman supposedly born in 1834, fifty-one years after Richard's death. A profile this contaminated cannot be trusted for every attachment.
- **William Norman (1739) pre-dates the 1740 marriage.** One of the Essex children listed under P68D-T5X was born before the marriage — a further inconsistency.

The most likely reconstruction is that our Richard Norman (P68D-T5X) married only Mary Hall, and the Mary Essex connection has been incorrectly duplicated onto this profile from L565-ZVH. The family page [[norman-essex-1740]] is retained as a holding record but should be treated as unconfirmed.

**What is more secure.** The link between Richard Norman (P68D-T5X) and Robert Norman (1769) is a direct FamilySearch relationship reflected in Robert Norman's own print view, which names P68D-T5X as his father alongside Mary Hall as his mother. This connection, while it cannot be verified independently from the sources currently held, rests on different evidence from the Essex attribution and is not subject to the same logical impossibility.

Richard died in 1783 at Over. His son Robert was about fourteen at his father's death — old enough to have known him, but young to lose him.

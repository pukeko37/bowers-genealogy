James Bunker was born in 1807 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire, the youngest son of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. He lived most of his life in Millbrook, and his death in 1852 was registered in the Ampthill district (volume 3B, page 223), aged about 45. He is Roland Bunker's great-grandfather.

He married **Elizabeth Watts** (1810–1868) on 22 November 1830 in Millbrook. Together they had nine children:

1. [[john-watts-bunker-1831|John Watts Bunker]] (1831–1899)
2. [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]] (1833–1922)
3. [[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]] (1836–1917)
4. [[david-bunker-1837|David Bunker]] (1837–1920)
5. [[charles-bunker-1839|Charles Bunker]] (1839–?)
6. [[martha-sarah-bunker-1841|Martha Sarah Bunker]] (1841–1901)
7. [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe Alice Bunker]] (1843–1923)
8. [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] (1845–1918) — Roland's grandfather
9. [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann Bunker]] (1848–?)

The 1841 census captures the household at Millbrook with seven children under the same roof, and James's mother [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]] (70) living with them — she died the following September 1842. By 1851 the three eldest boys had left: only George (15), David (13), Charles (11), Martha (10), Phoebe (8), Caleb (5), and Comfort (2) remained. In 1851 James is recorded as an **Agricultural Labourer** — the only census record to document his occupation. He farmed in Millbrook his whole life, and Elizabeth supplemented the household income as a lace maker.

James Bunker died in 1852 when his youngest child Comfort was only four and Caleb was seven. His wife Elizabeth Watts survived until 1868, raising the younger children. A note in George Bunker's record states that the family moved from Millbrook to Bedford in 1852 on the death of James — suggesting Elizabeth relocated the household after his death.

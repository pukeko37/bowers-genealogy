George Bunker was born in 1836 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire, the third child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]]. He died on 27 January 1917 in Derby, Derbyshire, aged 81, and an obituary appeared in the Derby press the same day. He is a paternal great-uncle of [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]].

**Early life.** George grew up in Millbrook. The 1841 census records him aged 6 in the family household; by 1851 he was 15 and working as an agricultural labourer alongside his father. In 1852, on his father's death, the family relocated from Millbrook to Bedford.

**Marriage and Bedford years.** George married **[[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]]** on 8 April 1858 at St Paul's Church, Bedford. Ann was born ~1838 in Westoning, Bedfordshire — a Bedfordshire woman, not a Derbyshire one. Together they had five children, all born in Bedford until the family moved to Derbyshire. The christening of their eldest daughter Sarah Ann in December 1860 was conducted at a **Wesleyan Methodist** chapel in the Bedford Circuit — consistent with the Nonconformist practice seen elsewhere in the Bunker family. See [[bunker-pedder-1858]].

By 1871 George was working as a **Storekeeper** in Bedford, St Mary's parish — a supervisory or clerical role, above common labouring. He and Ann had two daughters at home: Sarah Ann (10) and Harriett (6).

**The Derbyshire migration.** Sometime between 1872 and 1875 — bracketed by Alice's birth in Bedford (~1872) and Albert's birth in New Tupton, Derbyshire (~1875) — George moved the family from Bedford to Derbyshire. The FamilySearch life history notes he moved "with family from Bedford to Tupton." His brother [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb]] had already been in Matlock since 1871; his sister [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] and her husband [[samuel-fensome-1842|Samuel Fensome]] settled at nearby Pilsley in the same period. Albert's New Tupton birthplace places George in the same village where Caleb's son Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker was also born (~1875) — strong evidence the two brothers were in close proximity at that moment.

The stay in New Tupton was brief. George's next child, Amy, was born in **Derby** (~1878) — meaning the family had already moved to Derby within two to three years of Albert's birth. The 1881 census confirms the family in **St Peter's parish, Derby**. George's time in New Tupton was a way-point, not a settled residence.

**Occupation change on arrival.** The move involved a significant occupational step down. George had been a Storekeeper in Bedford; in Derbyshire he is recorded as a **General Labourer** from 1881 through 1901. Whatever supervisory role he had in Bedford did not transfer.

**Derby years.** By 1881 the family was in St Peter's parish, Derby, and remained in Derby for the rest of George's life. By 1911 George had "Finished Work" — the census return's way of recording retirement. He and Ann had been married 53 years. Only their youngest daughter Amy remained in the household. Amy married **Lewis Allsop** in Derby on 1 July 1911, the same year as the census. His son **Albert Bunker** — born in New Tupton ~1875 — spent the rest of his life in Derby and died there on **11 October 1924**.

George died in Derby in 1917, aged 81, the year before his brother Caleb died in Burton upon Trent.

**Residence history:** 1841–1852 Millbrook · 1852–~1875 Bedford · ~1875 New Tupton, Derbyshire · ~1877–1917 Derby

Norah Winifred Norman was born in 1902, the youngest of nine children of [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] and [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]]. She was only five when her father died in 1907.

On **2 August 1920**, aged 18, Norah was a witness at the wedding of [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]] and [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Bunker]] at Little Gaddesden, Hertfordshire. The other witness was Beulah's brother [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]]. Norah stood for the Norman family — her sister [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy]] and Harry were already close, and Roland and Ivy would marry less than a year later in June 1921. The wedding certificate is the earliest documentary record of the Norman-Thody-Bunker social circle that would define the next chapter of both families' lives.

At 18 Norah was working as an Invoice Clerk at Provision Merchants J Beer & Sons Ltd (1921 census). She married Henry Alfred Jeans in 1930. In 1939, Norah was living at 121 De Beauvoir Road with her mother Ellen (then aged 79 and incapacitated). Norah cared for Ellen until Ellen's death on 28 February 1940, as the Blitz was beginning.

Norah was widowed in 1978 and moved to Kent, where she was cared for by her nephew George Stewart Main. She lived to 94, dying in 1996 — the same remarkable longevity as her sister [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy]].

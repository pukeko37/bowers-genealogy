Ebenezer Bunker (1822 – 21 June 1902) was the fourth child of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph Bunker (1792–1853)]] and Mary Markham, and a grandson of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. He was born in Stevington, Bedfordshire, christened on 26 September 1824 at Stevington parish church — the same mass ceremony as his first cousin **Ephraim** (son of uncle [[caleb-bunker-1790|Caleb]]) — and died at Campbelltown, New South Wales, aged approximately 80. FamilySearch ID: L227-TG7.

## Early life in Stevington

The 1841 census places Ebenezer, age given as 15 (the standard rounding of pre-1837 births in the 1841 census), in his father Joseph's household at Silver Street, Stevington, alongside parents Joseph and Mary and siblings Ann (20), John (20), James, and Samuel. The family were Baptist in affiliation — Joseph was later buried at Stevington Baptist Chapel Chapelyard.

## Marriage and emigration

Ebenezer married **Sarah Cocking** on **28 November 1844** at Stevington, Bedfordshire (GRO vol. 6, p. 89, Bedford district; England Marriages 1538–1973, FamilySearch). A Samuel Cocking witnessed the marriage, likely her brother or father. Ebenezer and Sarah emigrated on the same vessel as cousin [[ephraim-bunker-1821|Ephraim]]: the ***Elizabeth***, arriving **31 March 1845**. The NSW Assisted Immigrants Inwards register records Ebenezer (age 23) and Sarah (age 20) as assisted immigrants, **engaged by Robert Coopershaw of "Rocky Point," Cooks River** — an area south of Sydney — on arrival. Ephraim appears separately in the same scheme, listed alone. The cousins had departed England within weeks of the November 1844 wedding.

## Later life and death

On arrival in 1845, Ebenezer and Sarah were engaged to work for **Robert Coopershaw at Rocky Point, Cooks River** (southern Sydney). At some point they moved to the **Campbelltown / Camden** district, where their first child was born by 1849 and christened in September 1850. Ebenezer remained in that area throughout his Australian life. The 1891 NSW census places him at Camden. He died on 21 June 1902 at Campbelltown, aged 80, and is buried at **St Peters Anglican Church Cemetery, Campbelltown**. The cemetery inscription record includes "Sarah Bunker" (his wife), who predeceased him, dying 1899.

## Misattributed record

A 1861 England and Wales census record attached to Ebenezer in FamilySearch — male, Jersey, occupation "Pensioner Chelsea", age 44 — belongs to his first cousin **Ebenezer Bunker (1820)**, son of [[caleb-bunker-1790|Caleb Bunker (1790)]] and Catherine Bull. That Ebenezer was absent from Caleb's 1841 household (suggesting early departure, possibly to the army), and "Pensioner Chelsea" is consistent with a man who served in the armed forces in the 1840s–50s. Ebenezer (1822) was definitively in Australia from March 1845 and cannot be the person in Jersey in 1861.

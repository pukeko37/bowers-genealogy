Tom Norman (baptised Thomas) was born 11 October 1856 in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire, the third child of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] in the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]]. His birth was registered at St Ives.

He married [[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]] (born Oakington, Cambridgeshire 1856) on 30 October 1877 in Swavesey. They had nine children between 1878 and 1893. See [[norman-thulborn-1877]].

By 1891 the family had moved to **Alconbury Weston**, Huntingdonshire, where Tom was working as **Farm Bailiff** — a supervisory role managing a farm on behalf of a landowner, a significant step up from ordinary labourer. By 1901 he had reverted to "Ordinary Agr Labourer" at the same address. His father [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] (age 74) was living in Tom's household in 1901, having followed his son from Swavesey after Ann's death in 1897.

By 1911 the family had moved to **Eaton Socon** (Huntingdonshire/Bedfordshire border), where Tom's occupation was **"Farm Labourer and Beerhouse Keeper"**. A beerhouse (Beerhouse Act 1830) was a lower-tier licensed premises — easier and cheaper to run than a full pub, often operated from home. Running one alongside farm labour was a common way to supplement wages. Only daughter Hannah (17) remained at home.

The beerhouse was almost certainly the **Crown Beerhouse, Great North Road, Eaton Socon** — now the Crown Inn at 1 Great North Road, still operating. The evidence: Tom's son **Albert Newman Norman (1888–1970)** is recorded as keeper of the Crown Beerhouse from 1922–1931 (owner Mrs Archbald), strongly suggesting Tom held it before him. The 1921 census places Tom, Emily, and son Thomas in the locality of the Crown Inn, further corroborating this. See [[crown-beerhouse-eaton-socon-stneots-ccan]] and [[kelly-1924-eaton-socon-norman]].

In 1921 Tom was still at Eaton Socon with Emily and their son Thomas (b. 1891). Emily died in 1922 — still at Eaton Socon, one year after this census. Tom died in 1925 in Huntingdonshire, aged 68. He was the most mobile of the Swavesey Norman siblings who remained in the region.

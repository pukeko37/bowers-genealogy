James Thomas Bunker was born on 17 April 1764 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire, christened on 7 April 1765, and died there on 10 May 1824, aged 60. His entire life was spent in the village where the Bunker family had been rooted for three generations. He is Roland Bunker's great-great-grandfather.

His parents were **[[thomas-bunker-1738|Thomas Bunker]]** (1738–1825) and **[[martha-sinfield|Martha Sinfield]]** (dates unknown), who married at Millbrook in 1761. He had one known sibling, Edward Bunker (christened 24 January 1762). James Thomas was the second child; his father outlived him by a year.

He married **[[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]** on 31 December 1788 at St Paul's Walden, Hertfordshire — a village roughly 12 miles north of Millbrook. Together they had eight children, all born in Millbrook:

1. Caleb Bunker (28 May 1790–1848)
2. Joseph Bunker (3 Dec 1792–1853)
3. Ruth Bunker (17 Aug 1794–1806) — died young
4. Phoebe Bunker (10 Oct 1796–1849)
5. Ebenezer Bunker (6 May 1798–1819) — died young
6. Martha Bunker (21 Dec 1801–?) — previously undocumented; confirmed in 1806 christening record
7. Lydia Bunker (4 Feb 1804–1883)
8. James Bunker (1807–1852) — Roland's great-grandfather

A striking feature of this family is that seven of the children (all but James, born 1807) were christened together at Millbrook parish church on a single day — **7 April 1806** — despite birth years spanning 1790 to 1804. This mass christening strongly suggests the family were Nonconformists (likely Baptist or another dissenting congregation) who registered their children in the Church of England parish for civil record purposes, either at a particular life event or when it became administratively necessary. By 1806, the youngest child being registered (Lydia, born 1804) was already two years old.

James Thomas Bunker died in 1824 leaving behind a wife and several children. His youngest son James (1807) was only seventeen at the time. Lydia survived her husband by 18 years, remaining in Millbrook until her death in 1842.

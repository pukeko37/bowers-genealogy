Hannah Maria Heels was born in 1814 in Lockinge, Berkshire — a village some 30 miles from Whipsnade. She died in 1886 (death registered in the Luton district). She was married twice: first to [[samuel-heels-1797|Samuel Heels]], the Chequers Inn tenant; then, after Samuel's death, to [[abraham-batchelor-1825|Abraham Batchelor]]. Her alternate name in FamilySearch is recorded as "Batchelor," confirming the second marriage.

## First Marriage: Samuel Heels

Hannah married Samuel Heels around 1842. Samuel and Hannah shared the Heels surname — she was almost certainly born Heels, making them distant relatives who married. Her Lockinge, Berkshire origins and his Bedfordshire/Whipsnade base suggest separate branches of the same family. Their one child was Thomas Heels (1843–?).

Samuel died in 1845 (death registered in the Luton district), leaving Hannah a widow at 31 with a two-year-old son. At the time of Samuel's death he was the Chequers Inn tenant on Whipsnade Green (documented in the 1843 pub auction record). Hannah was left with the family's connection to the pub.

## Second Marriage: Abraham Batchelor

Hannah subsequently married [[abraham-batchelor-1825|Abraham Batchelor]] (1825–1892), son of [[thomas-bachelor-1781|Thomas Bachelor]] of Frog Corner, Whipsnade. The marriage date is not yet confirmed by clippings, but both were in Whipsnade from 1841 onward. Through this marriage, the Chequers Inn licence most plausibly passed from the Heels family into Abraham's hands — a family transition in tenure rather than an open-market change of tenant.

Hannah appears in the census alongside Abraham: Whipsnade 1841–1861, Studham 1871–1881. She died in 1886, six years before Abraham. She does not appear to have had further children with Abraham.

## The Chequers Licence Transition

Hannah is the connecting figure in the Chequers licensee sequence:

**Samuel Heeles (tenant 1843)** → *Samuel dies 1845; Hannah his widow* → **Hannah + Abraham Batchelor (tenure ~1845–1867)** → **GBP Sr + Ruth Cooke (from December 1867)**

Her presence on Whipsnade Green before 1843 (she is in the 1841 census), her connection to Samuel Heeles through marriage, and her subsequent marriage to Abraham Batchelor are the cleanest explanation of the Heeles → Batchelor pub transition.

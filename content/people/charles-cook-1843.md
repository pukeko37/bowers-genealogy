Charles Cook (born 1843 – died 5 January 1908) was the son of [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]] (1816–1900) and his first wife Elizabeth Frost (1818–1843). He is entirely distinct from [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook (1847)]], the son of [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]], who shared the same name and village.

He married [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] on 21 March 1868 at Whipsnade. Together they had eight children, all raised in Whipsnade. He worked variously as an Agricultural Labourer, General Labourer, and Wood Man.

## Origins

The David Cook FamilySearch Family Tree 1 print view confirms Charles as a child of David Cook and Elizabeth Frost — see [[cook-frost-1842]]. Elizabeth died in 1843, the same year Charles was born; his father David remarried Charlotte Matthews in 1856. An 1851 census record placed an eight-year-old Charles in a household attributed to George Cook and Susan Cook (George being David's brother). Whether Charles was temporarily in his uncle George's care after his mother's death, or whether this is a separate Charles Cook, is not confirmed; the Family Tree print view is treated as authoritative on parentage. A second 1851 record shows him in the household of Daniel Cook alongside Ruth Cook (age 31) and Mary Sanders Cook (age 2) — Ruth Cooke, a different household, visiting or lodging nearby. This proximity contributed to earlier confusion between the two Charles Cooks.

## Later Life

By 1901 only the youngest son Albert (15) remained at home with Charles and Lois. Charles's occupation in the 1901 census is recorded as "Wood Man." He died on 5 January 1908 aged 64, his death registered in the Luton district. Probate was granted just eight days later, on 13 January 1908, at the Principal Probate Registry in London — [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois]] named as executor and sole beneficiary, his estate valued at £134 5s. 5d. The speed almost certainly reflects the urgency of the Chequers Inn licence: she needed legal authority over the estate to continue operating the pub without interruption. His son [[walter-cook-1869|Walter]] was a witness at the 1894 Whipsnade wedding of [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] and [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] — connected to that family through his mother Lois's brother [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]], who had married [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] (Lucy's sister).

## The Chequers Inn

Charles was the licensee of the Chequers Inn, Whipsnade from 1904 until his death on 5 January 1908 — taking over when his aunt [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] died at the inn on 22 May 1904. His father [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]] (1816–1900) was Ruth's brother; both were children of [[daniel-cook-1790|Daniel Cook]] and [[mary-birchmore-1785|Mary Birchmore]] (see [[cook-birchmore-1810]]).

The probate index entry records his address as "The Chequers Inn Whipsnade" — confirming he was resident at the pub when he died, not merely a remote licensee. He left a will (Probate, not Administration), naming [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois]] as executor and sole beneficiary. Probate was granted just eight days after his death, on 13 January 1908, with his estate valued at £134 5s. 5d. — personal effects, trading stock and livestock, but not the freehold of the building. Lois held the licence for the remainder of 1908. See [[charles-cook-1843-probate-1908]] and [[chequers-inn-whipsnade-bedfordshire-archives]].

Caleb Bunker (28 May 1790 – 31 May 1848) was the eldest child of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]], and the eldest surviving sibling of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] (1807), Roland Bunker's great-grandfather. He was born in Millbrook, christened there in the 1806 mass ceremony, and — despite spending most of his adult life in Stevington — died at Millbrook in 1848.

He married **Catherine Bull** (1786–1839) on 19 May 1815 at Stevington, Bedfordshire, about a year after his brother Joseph had also married at Stevington. The two eldest brothers appear to have moved to Stevington together around 1814. The 1841 census shows Caleb at "West End" in Stevington, by which point Catherine had already died (1839). He had four children, all christened together at Stevington in a mass ceremony on 26 September 1824 — repeating the Dissenter practice of his own childhood:

**The soldier.** Ebenezer Bunker (1820), Caleb's eldest child, is absent from Caleb's 1841 household at West End, Stevington — unlike siblings Ephraim (20), Ruth (15), and Lydia (15), who are all present. The 1861 England and Wales census records an Ebenezer Bunker in **Jersey**, occupation "**Pensioner Chelsea**", age 44 (implying birth ~1817, within census rounding range of 1820), birthplace Stevington, Bedfordshire, marital status married. The Chelsea Pension was an army pension administered by the Royal Chelsea Hospital; out-pensioners received it while living in the community. This record has been misattributed in FamilySearch to his first cousin Ebenezer (1822, son of Joseph), who was definitively in Australia from 1845. Caleb's Ebenezer most likely enlisted in the army around 1840, served through the 1840s–50s, and was drawing a Chelsea out-pension by 1861 while living in Jersey. His wife is not yet identified; no further record has been found.

**The Australian emigrant.** Ephraim Bunker (1821–1887), Caleb's son, emigrated to Australia alongside his first cousin **Ebenezer Bunker** (1822–1902), son of uncle [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph]]. Before leaving Bedfordshire, Ephraim had at least one brush with the law: in August 1837, aged 16, he was charged at Bedford with two other boys (Charles and Robert Simpson) with stealing a quantity of peas from a farm near Stevington (*Bedfordshire Mercury*, 5 August 1837). The outcome of the case is not recorded in the snippet. See [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-1837]]. The two cousins settled in the **Camden / Campbelltown** district of New South Wales. Ephraim arrived on 31 March 1845 on the vessel ***Elizabeth*** as an assisted immigrant from Stevington — he was single on arrival and did not marry until 1852, when he wed **Mary Ann Betts** at Camden, NSW (St John's, Camden). He later moved to **Crookwell** in the Southern Tablelands, where he died on 5 July 1887, aged 67, and is buried at Crookwell General Cemetery. Known children include David George Bunker (b.~1870, married at Crookwell 1896), Catherine Bunker (married Martin Moore at Crookwell 1895), and a son Ephraim who died in infancy in 1877. Ebenezer, by contrast, married **Sarah Cocking** at Stevington on 28 November 1844 before departing, and settled at Campbelltown where his first child was christened in September 1850. See [[ephraim-bunker-1821-familysearch]] and [[ebenezer-bunker-1822-familysearch]].

Caleb died on 31 May 1848 in Millbrook — his birthplace — three days after his 58th birthday. The death was registered in the Bedford district (GRO vol 6, page 45). Whether he had returned to Millbrook in old age or simply died while visiting is unknown; his census record four years earlier placed him at Stevington.

The name Caleb recurs in this family: Caleb Bunker (1845), Roland Bunker's grandfather, was named for this uncle.

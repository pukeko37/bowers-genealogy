Captain Clarence James Thody (16 October 1884 – 30 August 1918) was the third child of [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] and [[mathilde-parent-1853|Mathilde Parent]]. He was born at Houghton Regis, Bedfordshire, after his mother's return from Canada.

In the 1891 census he appears at Totternhoe, Bedfordshire, age 6, Scholar. The 1901 census shows him at Studham, age 16, born Dunstable, in the household of his father and step-mother [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]]. He is listed in the FamilySearch tree as "Clarence Archibald Thody" in the 1901 census.

Before the war he worked in Manchester as **Master of Ardwick Green Industrial School** — an institution for destitute or delinquent boys requiring considerable administrative and disciplinary authority. The role likely contributed to his commissioning as an officer at the outbreak of war.

He served as a **Captain in the 8th Battalion, Manchester Regiment**, a New Army Service Battalion formed in 1914. He was killed in action on **30 August 1918**, near Riencourt-lès-Bapaume in the Pas-de-Calais, during the final Allied offensive of the war. The Manchester Cemetery at that village was named for the regiment that took it — Clarence fell in the operations immediately preceding the Second Battle of Bapaume (31 Aug – 3 Sep 1918). He was buried at **Manchester Cemetery, Riencourt-lès-Bapaume**, Plot A. 11. He was 33 years old.

He was a half-brother of [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]], who was then 24 and also on active service. Clarence did not survive to see the Armistice, which came ten weeks after his death.

The CWGC entry records his father as "W. A. Thody, of Dunstable, Beds." — but William Allen Thody had died in 1911. The address likely reflects the family's known Dunstable-area residence, or may have been supplied by another family member.

Clarence also appears in the Find a Grave records for **St Mary the Virgin Churchyard, Studham** — the home parish where his father and step-mother Lucy are buried. As he was interred in France, this is a **memorial inscription** rather than a burial: a family commemoration at the church where he had grown up.

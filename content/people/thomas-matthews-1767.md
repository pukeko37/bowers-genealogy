Thomas Matthews was born in 1767 in Kensworth, Hertfordshire, and died on 4 January 1862 in Whipsnade, Bedfordshire, aged approximately 94 or 95 — one of the longest-lived people in this family network. An obituary was published in the Luton newspaper on 11 January 1862. His occupation was recorded as agricultural labourer (listed as "pauper ag lab" in later records — a common designation for elderly farm workers by mid-century).

He married [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]] (b. 1779, Stanbridge, Bedfordshire) on **3 June 1801 at Kensworth**. The 1851 census records Thomas's birthplace as Kensworth and Mary's as Stanbridge — direct corroboration of this identification. They lived first in Kensworth, where all their children through 1808 were christened, before moving to Whipsnade. Their Whipsnade address was Houchen End / By Whipsnade Green, where they remained for the rest of their lives. They had ten children, among them:

- [[able-matthews-1813|Able Matthews]] (1813–1891) — emigrated to Utah 1861 (via St. Louis); settled Lehi, Utah
- [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] (1815–1858) — married George Birchmore Plummer, stayed in England
- [[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]] (1820–1903) — emigrated to Utah 1853; settled Virgin, Washington County
- [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte Matthews]] (1823–1910) — married David Cook, stayed in Whipsnade

In the 1840s the family converted to Mormonism (LDS). Several children emigrated to Utah; Thomas and Mary remained in Whipsnade. Thomas outlived his daughter Anne (died 1858) by four years, dying in the same village where he had spent his entire life.

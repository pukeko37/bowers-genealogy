Joseph Bunker (1754–1808) was the youngest of twelve children born to [[thomas-bunker-1704|Thomas Bunker (1704–1784)]] and [[mary-reade-1702|Mary Reade (1702–1775)]] at Millbrook. He was christened on 13 October 1754 and spent his entire life in Millbrook, dying there on 27 November 1808 aged 54.

## Occupation

His only documented occupation comes from a parish apprenticeship indenture: on 8 January 1784, Jonathan (or John) Bunker of Maulden was apprenticed to **Joseph Bunker of Millbrook, cordwainer** (a maker of new shoes). At thirty years old, Joseph was established enough in his trade to take a parish apprentice — suggesting a working craftsman with a household and a recognised position in the village. See [[beds-archives-p31-14-64a-bunker-apprenticeship]].

The shoemaking/cordwaining trade appears throughout the Millbrook Bunker lines in the following generations.

## Marriage and family

Joseph married **[[martha-garratt-1756|Martha Garratt]]** on 1 October 1777 at Millbrook, when he was twenty-three. Together they had twelve children, all christened at Millbrook between 1778 and 1803. Three died in childhood: Jesse (1785) and Jonas (1803) as infants, Mark buried 22 Feb 1789, and Avis who reached her mid-teens before dying in 1800. Nine children survived to adulthood.

The name of the youngest surviving son — **Joseph Garratt Bunker** (1800) — preserves Martha's maiden name as a middle name. The Old Testament names Jesse, Joel, and **Thomas Jehu** suggest nonconformist influence, though no chapel affiliation is yet confirmed.

See [[bunker-garratt-1777]] for the full family narrative.

## Position in the family

Joseph was the last of his generation in Millbrook. His older brothers Thomas (1738) and Edward (1742) had established their own lines by the time Joseph married; Thomas's line and Edward's line are the two branches that carry the Millbrook Bunker community into the nineteenth century. Joseph's line is a third, collateral branch — still rooted in Millbrook through 1808 but less well documented in the census era.

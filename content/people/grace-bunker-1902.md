Grace Bunker was born in Luton, Bedfordshire, in the September quarter of 1902 (GRO birth index). She appears in the **1911 census** at Millbrook, aged 8, listed as granddaughter in the household of [[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph Bunker (1840–1914)]] and [[mary-ann-wilcox-1842|Mary Ann Wilcox]].

## Probable parentage

Grace is **probably the illegitimate daughter of [[mabel-bunker-1883|Mabel Bunker (1883)]]**, Joseph and Mary Ann's youngest daughter. The case rests on convergent circumstantial evidence:

- **Luton birth**: The only Grace Bunker in the GRO birth index of the right age was born in Luton, Q3 1902. Mabel is the only member of Joseph's family with a documented Luton connection at that time — she was working as a domestic servant there in the 1901 census, aged 18.
- **Surname**: Grace carries the surname **Bunker**, not Cook (Mabel's married name from 1904). Children born to unmarried mothers were registered under the mother's maiden name. No other unmarried Bunker woman has been placed in Luton around 1902.
- **Age and situation**: Mabel was 18–19 at the time of conception and birth — a domestic servant, a setting in which unwanted pregnancies were common and fathers were rarely identified.
- **Timeline**: Grace is born Q3 1902; Mabel marries **Mark Edward Cook** at Millbrook in May 1904 — roughly eighteen months later. The interval is consistent with Mabel returning to Millbrook after the birth, then meeting and marrying Cook.
- **1911 placement**: Grace is living with her grandparents in Millbrook, not with Mabel and Mark Cook in Luton. If Cook had accepted Grace as part of the new household, she would most likely have been with them. Her absence suggests she was left with Joseph and Mary Ann when Mabel married — a common resolution in this period.

The birth certificate for Grace Bunker, Luton, Q3 1902 would confirm or refute this. If it names Mabel Bunker as mother, the identification is established. The father is almost certainly unnamed on the certificate, as was standard for illegitimate births at this time.

## After 1911

Grace's later life is untraced. By 1911 both her grandparents were elderly (Joseph 72, Mary Ann 69); Joseph died in 1914. Whether Grace remained at Millbrook, moved to Luton, or went elsewhere after her grandparents' deaths is unknown.

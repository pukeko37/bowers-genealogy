Joseph Bunker was christened on 10 May 1840 at Millbrook, Bedfordshire, the seventh child of [[john-bunker-1803|John Bunker (1803–1889)]] and Maria West (1806). He died in 1914 at Ampthill, Bedfordshire, aged approximately 74 (GRO vol 3B, page 356). FamilySearch ID: MM18-ML4.

He was the second Joseph in the family — the first, born 1837, died in infancy. He spent his entire life in Millbrook, appearing in the village at every census from 1841 to 1911. In this he was exceptional: his cousins from the James (1807) line had dispersed to Bedford, Derbyshire, Peterborough, and London by the 1850s–70s, and his own siblings are largely untraced. Joseph remained.

## Marriage and family

He married **Mary Ann Wilcox** (born 1842, Millbrook) on 2 October 1859 at Millbrook. She was seventeen; he was nineteen. Together they had eleven known children, all christened at Millbrook, across a span of nearly thirty years (1859–1888). See [[bunker-wilcox-1859]] for the full family.

By 1911 Joseph had retired. He died in 1914 in Ampthill — possibly in a workhouse infirmary or hospital. The Ampthill registration district covered Millbrook.

## In the newspapers

The Bedfordshire Mercury and Times preserve several glimpses of Joseph's life in the field and beyond:

- **October 1872:** Summonsed for travelling on the Midland Railway from Wellingborough to Bedford without a ticket. He was 32, and clearly ventured further afield than Millbrook on occasion.
- **November 1883:** Witness in a knife attack at Millbrook: "I live at Millbrook and am a labourer. On Monday the 12th, I was at work in the field with the prisoner and the complainant." His own words, confirming his occupation and village.
- **January 1884:** Witness at an affray at the Marston Arms public house in the adjacent parish of Marston.
- **September 1873 & October 1878:** Won prizes at the Silsoe & Ampthill Labourers' Friend Society annual show — best-kept cottage in 1873 (Society's Testimonial and 15s.), and vegetable produce in 1878. Silsoe borders Millbrook to the north.

These appearances paint Joseph as a man grounded in the Millbrook area, working the fields, maintaining his cottage and garden, and present at local events across the neighbouring parishes. No entry mentions him as sexton.

## Note: not the Millbrook sexton

Joseph was initially a strong candidate for the Millbrook sexton of the 1919 Huett effigy story. His brother [[samuel-bunker-1842|Samuel (1842–1913)]] is confirmed in that role by Samuel's obituary (*Bedfordshire Times*, 20 June 1913): Samuel served as clerk and sexton for approximately 20 years; their father [[john-bunker-1803|John (1803)]] held the same roles before him. The Mrs Bunker of 1919 is Samuel's widow [[harriet-dean-1844|Harriet Dean]], not Joseph's wife Mary Ann.

## Notes on other attributed children

FamilySearch records two children attributed to Joseph by an "Unknown Name" mother — Alfred Edwin Bunker (1866–1946) and Frederick Bunker (1869). Both fall within the Mary Ann marriage years and their separation in the tree may be a data error. Alfred's marriage record (1886) gives his age as 23, suggesting birth ~1863 rather than the tree's 1866. This discrepancy is unresolved.

A Bertha Florence Bunker (christened 25 September 1881, Millbrook) appears in a FamilySearch source as Joseph and Mary Ann's daughter but is absent from the print view, suggesting early death.

George Norman was born in 1851 in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire — the eldest child of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] (1827–1905) and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] (1829–1897). He and his younger brother [[richard-norman-1853|Richard]] were christened together on 3 July 1853 at **Fen Stanton**, Huntingdonshire — Ann Tabram's home parish — George being about two years old and Richard a newborn.

### Swavesey years, 1851–1871

George appears in the Swavesey census returns of 1851, 1861, and 1871. At twenty years old in 1871 he was still at home with his parents and younger siblings, working as an agricultural labourer in the same trade as his father. At some point between 1871 and 1875 he left Swavesey for good — and went north, not south.

### West Hartlepool, 1875–1903

George did not go to London. He followed or accompanied his brother [[richard-norman-1853|Richard]] to **West Hartlepool, County Durham** — 240 miles north of Swavesey, on the industrial Durham coast. The timing is striking: Richard married [[ann-elizabeth-randall-1854|Ann Elizabeth Randall]] in Hartlepool in **October 1875**, and George married [[margaret-j-garry-1854|Margaret J Garry]] at **Christ Church, West Hartlepool**, on **7 December 1875** — barely two months later. The two eldest Norman brothers both arrived in Hartlepool and both married there in the same autumn.

George and Margaret settled in **Stranton**, the township that made up much of West Hartlepool, and remained in the Hartlepool area for the rest of their lives. George's working life in Hartlepool followed the port and industrial economy of the town:

- **1881:** Grocers Porter, Stranton — the family's first census in Durham, with son Thomas (4) and son William (1)
- **1891:** Grain Porter NE Railway, Stranton — a step up into the railway freight economy; four children at home
- **1901:** Malster at a brewery, West Hartlepool — a skilled manual trade in the brewery industry; most of the children still at home

Their son William was christened on 18 June 1879 at **Lynne Street United Free Methodist Chapel**, West Hartlepool — placing the family in the Nonconformist tradition common among working families in this period.

George and Margaret had **ten children**, all born in the West Hartlepool area:

1. Thomas Norman (1877)
2. William Norman (1879)
3. Ethel J Norman (1882)
4. Alfred Norman (1884)
5. George H Norman (1888) — probably died in childhood; absent from 1901 census
6. George Norman (1889)
7. Jack Norman (1891)
8. Francis Norman (1892)
9. Kate Alberta Norman (1895–1979)
10. Susan Norman (1897)

George died in **September 1903** in Hartlepool, Durham, aged about 51. Margaret survived him by thirty years, dying in 1933.

### A note on FamilySearch confusion

An earlier FamilySearch record for George Norman (labelled "1851–Deceased") conflated him with a different person of the same name, adding London residences (Wandsworth 1881, Croydon 1891) and a Stilton, Huntingdonshire 1901 residence that do not belong to him. The correct record (LKVF-D8B, "1851–1903") confirms George was in West Hartlepool from 1875 onwards without interruption. ### The brothers in Hartlepool

George and Richard Norman were the two eldest sons of Thomas and Ann — christened together, and the first to leave Swavesey. They left together and settled in the same town. Richard married in October 1875, George in December 1875. Through the 1880s and 1890s both families lived in Stranton, a walk apart. George's death in September 1903 came two years after the last census that records both men in the same town.

Richard and Ann named their ninth child **George Norman**, born in 1895 — while his uncle George was still alive. The name carried forward; it was not a posthumous memorial but an honour given in the moment. George himself named one of his own sons George (born 1889). See [[norman-garry-1875]] and [[norman-randall-1875]].

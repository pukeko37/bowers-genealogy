Thomas George Norman was born 17 October 1886, the third child and eldest son of [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] and [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]]. He was 20 when his father died in 1907.

Thomas married Mary Elizabeth Angus (1885–1969) on 19 August 1911 at DeBeauvoir Town, Middlesex, forming the [[norman-angus-1911|Norman-Angus family]]. They had one daughter, Gwendoline May Norman (1915–2000).

Thomas had varied occupations: drape and fancy lace goods manufacturer (1921), cushion case manufacturer (1939), and during the war he ran a baker's shop in Dalston/Kingsland. The family lived at 106 Hertford Road, Hackney (1921) and then 6 Englefield Road, Kingsland (1939). Their house at Englefield Road was bombed during WW2, and the family moved into the baker's shop to live.

106 Hertford Road and 6 Englefield Road are on the same corner block — the two roads intersect there, and the properties are adjacent. Thomas almost certainly never moved in any meaningful sense. The apparent shift between the two addresses reflects occupation of neighbouring properties on the same corner, not a change of neighbourhood. When the Englefield Road side was bombed, the return to 106 Hertford Road was a move of a few steps.

Thomas died on **5 March 1949** at **The Hospital, Hackney**, aged 63 — he died in hospital, not at home. The administration record gives his address as 106 Hertford Road, Hackney, consistent with the above: he had been at that corner for the better part of thirty years. He died intestate (no will); letters of administration were granted on 10 June 1949 in London to his wife **Mary Elizabeth Norman** and their daughter **[[gwendoline-may-norman-1915|Gwendoline May Norman]]** (described as spinster, confirming she was unmarried at 33). His estate was valued at **£8,889 11s. 9d.** — a substantial sum, consistent with his manufacturing and bakery businesses.

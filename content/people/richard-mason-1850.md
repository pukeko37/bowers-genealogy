Richard Mason was born in 1850 in Aberystwyth, Cardiganshire, Wales. His father was **Morgan Mason** (b. 1822, Llanbadarn Fawr, Cardiganshire), an ostler in 1851 and postillion in 1861 — horse-related occupations typical of the area. His mother was **Catherine Mason** (b. 1826, Trerddol, Cardiganshire). They were a married couple, consistently recorded as such in both the 1851 and 1861 censuses. Richard had at least two siblings: **Lewis Mason** (b. ~1849, Aberystwyth) and **Anne Mason** (b. ~1854, Aberystwyth).

By 1861 the family lived on **Thespian Street, Aberystwyth**, where Richard (13) is recorded as a Scholar. He would have been in Aberystwyth through his teens — the same town where he later met and married **[[catherine-james-1852|Catherine James]]**.

Richard married Catherine (known as Kate) James on **5 May 1874** in Aberystwyth — confirmed by two Wales Marriage Bonds records and a civil registration entry. The marriage in Aberystwyth, before both appear in London records, suggests they came to London as a couple shortly after the wedding rather than migrating separately. By 1881 they were established in **Bethnal Green**, where Richard is recorded as a **Cowkeeper (Milk Seller)** — a specific trade, not simply a labourer looking for work. Two sons, Richard William (b. ~1875) and John James (b. ~1878), were both born in Clerkenwell — meaning the family had arrived in the City fringe by the mid-1870s, and Richard was probably working in the dairy trade from soon after their arrival.

The lodger in the 1881 household was **Rachel Evans** (18, Cardiganshire) — a fellow Welsh migrant, the kind of arrangement common in the Cardiganshire community where families helped bring younger relatives or neighbours to London.

At some point between 1881 and 1890 the family moved from Bethnal Green to **27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield** — the address recorded on both Richard's and Catherine's death records. Hosier Lane is steps from Smithfield Market; a milk shop there served the market workers and surrounding streets. Richard died at that address on **6 January 1890**, aged 40. The cause is not documented. He was buried at **Abney Park Cemetery, Stoke Newington** — the principal Nonconformist cemetery in London, widely used by Welsh immigrants.

Richard died leaving Catherine with three sons aged roughly 15, 11, and 8. Catherine continued running the dairy at Hosier Lane for a further ten years.

Ephraim Bunker (16 August 1821 – 5 July 1887) was the second child of [[caleb-bunker-1790|Caleb Bunker (1790–1848)]] and Catherine Bull, and a grandson of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. He was born in Stevington, Bedfordshire, and christened on 26 September 1824 at Stevington parish church — a mass ceremony that also christened his first cousin **Ebenezer** (son of uncle [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph]]), repeating the Dissenting practice of their grandparents' generation. He died at Crookwell, New South Wales, aged 67, and is buried at Crookwell General Cemetery. FamilySearch ID: M9S7-BX7.

## Early life in Stevington

The 1841 census places Ephraim, aged 20, in his father Caleb's household at West End, Stevington, along with sisters Ruth (15) and Lydia (15). His mother Catherine Bull had died in 1839. Before leaving England he had at least one brush with the law: in August 1837, aged 16, he was charged at Bedford with two other boys (Charles and Robert Simpson) with stealing peas from a farm near Stevington (*Bedfordshire Mercury*, 5 August 1837). The outcome is unrecorded. See [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-1837]].

## Emigration to Australia

Ephraim emigrated to New South Wales as an **assisted immigrant**, arriving on **31 March 1845** on the vessel ***Elizabeth***. He was 24, and listed alone — unmarried at departure. His origin is recorded as "Steavington, Bedfordshe." His cousin [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer]] was on the same ship with his new wife Sarah Cocking; both cousins were **engaged by Robert Coopershaw of "Rocky Point," Cooks River** (southern Sydney) on arrival. They had sailed together from England shortly after Ebenezer's marriage in November 1844. Both cousins subsequently moved to the Camden / Campbelltown district of NSW.

## Marriage and family

Ephraim married **Mary Ann Betts** in **1852** at **Camden, New South Wales** (St John's, Camden) — seven years after arriving. Known children:

## Later life and death

By the time of his children's marriages (1895–1896), Ephraim had long settled at **Crookwell** in the Southern Tablelands, roughly 190 km south-west of Sydney — inland from the Camden area where he first arrived. He died on 5 July 1887, aged 67, and is buried at Crookwell General Cemetery. The death index (registration no. 6018, Crookwell) names his father as Caleb Bunker and his mother as Catherine; the birth year is given as ~1820 (one year early).

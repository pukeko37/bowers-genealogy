Anne Matthews was born 7 May 1815 in [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], Bedfordshire, the daughter of [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews]] (1767–1862) and [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]] (b. 1779). The family had at least nine children; her known siblings were [[able-matthews-1813|Able Matthews]] (c.1813–c.1891), [[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]] (1820–1903), and [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte Matthews]] (1823–1910). The family lived at Houchen End / By Whipsnade Green — the same hamlet later associated with GBP Sr himself.

She married [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] on 1 November 1835 at Whipsnade. They had one child: George Birchmore Plummer Jr (b. 1850). See [[birchmore-plummer-matthews-1835]] for the full family story.

### The Mormon Emigration

In the 1840s, Anne's family converted to Mormonism (LDS). Her brother Daniel Willis was baptised LDS in 1846; Able followed. By 1853, Daniel Willis had emigrated to Utah on the ship "Ellen Marie" (sailing from Liverpool with 332 converts); Able followed in 1855. **Anne did not emigrate** — she was already married to GBP Sr and chose to stay in England. She never saw her Utah siblings again. Her father Thomas remained in Whipsnade until his death in 1862; her sister [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte]] also stayed, never emigrating.

The family story in later generations described a "Quaker background" — possibly a sanitising of the more controversial Mormon connection over time.

Anne died in 1858 aged 43, her death registered in the Luton district. George married Eliza Batchelor the following year.

### The Cook Connection

Anne's sister [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte]] married [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]], who was the brother of [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]]. Ruth later became GBP Sr's third wife in 1867 — meaning he married into both the Matthews family (through Anne) and the Cook family (through Ruth), two families that were themselves connected by Charlotte and David's marriage.

### Notes

- The Mormon connection was later reframed as Quaker — a notable example of family narrative editing across generations.
- Anne's father Thomas lived to approximately 94–95, dying in Whipsnade in 1862 — four years after Anne.

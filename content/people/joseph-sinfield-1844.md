Joseph Sinfield was christened on 28 January 1844 at All Saints' Church, Kempston, Bedfordshire — a parish immediately adjoining Bedford, which explains why census records give his birthplace as "Bedford." He died on 12 April 1914 in Luton, aged 70 (GRO vol 3B, page 429). An obituary was published in the *Luton Bedfordshire Advertiser and Luton Times* on 17 April 1914 — the same newspaper that had published his wife Martha's obituary thirteen years earlier.

**Parents:** His father **William Sinfield** (born ~1811, Kempston) was a labourer throughout his recorded life. His mother **Sarah** (born ~1812, Eynesbury/Eaton area, Huntingdonshire) came from across the county border near St Neots. The Sinfield family was rooted in Kempston, Bedfordshire.

He married [[martha-sarah-bunker-1841|Martha Sarah Bunker]] on 25 May 1863 at Saint Paul's Church, Bedford. She was 21 and he was 19. See [[bunker-sinfield-1863]].

**Career.** Joseph came of age as a labourer (1861 census, Thurlow Street Bedford, in his parents' household), then trained as a **Hammerman** — a metal-forging trade, working with heavy striking tools. By 1881 he had switched careers entirely, joining the Luton Borough Police as a **Police Constable**. He held that position through at least 1891. By 1901 he had left the force and was working as a **General Labourer** — the 1911 census describes him simply as "Ex Police Constable," his police identity outlasting his active service.

**Later life.** After Martha died in July 1901, Joseph continued in Luton. By 1911 he was living as a widower in the household of his daughter **Annie Hyde** (née Sinfield, born ~1864, Bedford) and her husband **Alfred John Hyde** — a Blocker in the Straw Hats industry. The straw hat trade had defined his wife and sister-in-law's working lives; now his son-in-law worked in the same industry.

**The Sinfield–Bunker echo.** Joseph's family was from Kempston, about 8-10 miles from Millbrook. Thomas Bunker (1738) married a **Martha Sinfield** at Millbrook in 1761. Whether the Kempston Sinfields of the 1800s connect to those Millbrook Sinfields of 1761 is unresearched.

- 1871: **Hammerman**, St Paul Bedford
- 1881–1891: **Police Constable (Borough)**, Luton
- 1901: General Labourer, Luton

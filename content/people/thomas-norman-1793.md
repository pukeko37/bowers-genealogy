Thomas Norman was born on 27 May 1793 in Over, Cambridgeshire — the third child of [[robert-norman-1769|Robert Norman]] and [[frances-green-1769|Frances Green]]. He died on 19 December 1855 in Swavesey, buried there on 23 December. His whole adult life was spent in the Swavesey area, working as an agricultural labourer — a pattern that his son [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas (1827)]] and grandson [[charles-norman-1859|Charles (1859)]] would partially perpetuate and partially break.

He married [[elizabeth-fear-1794|Elizabeth Fear]] in Swavesey in August 1815 (sources give both 13 and 20 August; the two-date discrepancy likely represents banns reading and ceremony). Their first child, a son also named Thomas, was christened just two months later at Willingham — presumably where the couple had settled initially. This baby Thomas died in childhood and is absent from later records.

The family had at least five children in total: **Thomas** (1815, died young, christened at Willingham), **Robert** (~1821), **Sarah** (~1822), **[[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas]]** (1827–1905), and **Ann** (1832–1843). Elizabeth died on 29 July 1832 — probably in or from the birth of Ann — leaving Thomas a widower with four surviving children. He did not remarry.

By 1841 Thomas was at Boxworth End, Swavesey, with Robert (20), Sarah (15), the younger Thomas (13), and Ann (9) — a household of working-age and nearly working-age children. By 1851 he was a lodger in his son Robert's household at Swavesey, now 57, a widower still working as an agricultural labourer, born Over. Robert had a young wife and three small children; Thomas was fitting himself into the next generation's household. He died four years later.

Thomas's son [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman (1827)]] continued the agricultural labourer's life in Swavesey before his own children scattered — to Hartlepool, London, and the Huntingdonshire farms. The continuity from Thomas (1793) to Thomas (1827) to [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman (1859)]] marks the transition from settled Cambridgeshire fenland family to London urban wage-earner across three generations.

## Siblings

Thomas had six siblings: Alice (~1787), Mary (1790), **Thomas (1793)**, Elizabeth (1795–1877), James (1801–1872), Luke (1803–1878), Richard (1805–1890). Their subsequent lives are not yet researched; they may represent further Norman branches in the Over/Swavesey area.

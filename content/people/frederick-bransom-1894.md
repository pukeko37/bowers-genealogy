Private Frederick Bransom (abt 1894 – 1 August 1916), son of [[albert-bransom-1871|Albert Bransom]] and his wife Mary, of Yew Tree Cottages, Whipsnade. He served with the **2nd Battalion, Bedfordshire Regiment** and died on 1 August 1916 from wounds received during the Battle of the Somme, aged 22. He is buried at **La Neuville British Cemetery**, France. His name is inscribed on the St Mary Magdalene church war memorial, Whipsnade.

Local accounts describe him as "the first from the parish to pay the deadly price of war" and "a youth greatly loved and respected by all." Before enlisting he had worked on the land at Hall Farm for over nine years — placing his start there around 1907, aged about thirteen, when the farm was still an active agricultural concern more than a decade before the ZSL purchased it in 1926.

His death, and his father Albert's subsequent role in helping Edmund Blyth plant the Whipsnade Tree Cathedral from 1930, places the Bransom family at the intersection of both threads of Whipsnade's First World War remembrance. Frederick is the parish's first fallen son; Albert is one of the builders of the living memorial planted for others who fell in the same war.

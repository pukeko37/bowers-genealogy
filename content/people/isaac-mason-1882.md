Isaac Mason (known as "Ike") was born on 13 March 1882 in Clerkenwell, London (birth registered in Bethnal Green). He was the youngest of three sons of [[mason-james-1874|Richard Mason and Catherine James]], Welsh immigrants who ran a milk shop at 27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield. His father died in 1890 and his mother in 1900, leaving the three brothers orphaned while still young.

In 1891, aged eight or nine, Isaac was living at St Sepulchre Newgate — the street directly bordering Smithfield, adjacent to Hosier Lane where the Norman-Birchmore family had recently settled. This geographic proximity almost certainly explains how the Mason and Norman families came to know each other well enough to produce two marriages.

### Residences

- 1891: St Sepulchre Newgate, London
- 1901: City of London
- 1911: Lambeth, London
- 1921 onward: 4 Yukon Road, Balham, SW12 — newsagent

### Marriage and family

Isaac married [[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]] in 1918, the sixth daughter of [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] and [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]]. This was the second of the double Mason-Norman marriages — his brother [[john-james-mason-1878|John]] had married Alice's sister [[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel]] in 1912. They formed the [[mason-norman-1918|Mason-Norman (1918) family]] and had one son, George R. Mason (b. 1919).

Isaac ran a newsagent's shop at 4 Yukon Road, Balham — parallel to his brother John's shop at 150 Acre Lane, Clapham. His wife's aunt [[elizabeth-birchmore-1862|Elizabeth Birchmore]] lived with them from around 1921 until her death in 1954. Ivy Norman's memoir recalls Nellie Norman working alongside Ike in the shop.

Isaac died on **14 May 1955** at **17 Daysbrook Road, Streatham, London SW16** — not at the 4 Yukon Road, Balham address associated with his newsagent years; he had moved to Streatham by the time of his death. His estate of **£7102** was substantial, suggesting the business had done well. Probate was granted on 2 August 1955 to **Barclays Bank Limited** as executor — a corporate executor rather than a family member, which may indicate his wife Alice was not in a position to act (or that he specifically arranged it this way).

Notably, his brother [[john-james-mason-1878|John]] died seven years later at **16 Daysbrook Road** — they were next-door neighbours on the same street at death.

His wife Alice, widowed at 65, eventually moved to Kent where she was cared for by her nephew [[george-stewart-main-1919|George Stewart Main]]. Alice died in 1980.

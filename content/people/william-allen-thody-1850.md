William Allen Thody (11 November 1850 – 1 February 1911) led a life that crossed the Atlantic and back, encompassed an army enlistment and desertion, two marriages, and an illegitimate child — before settling into a Bedfordshire police career and dying quietly at Studham at 60.

## Origins

He was born at Cotton End, a hamlet in the Cardington registration district west of Bedford, the fifth and youngest child of [[allen-thody-1813|Allen Thody]] and [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]]. His father Allen was a farm labourer born in Spain of British parentage — an unusual origin that may reflect a soldier father in the Peninsular War. Allen could not write; he made his mark on William's birth certificate.

The 1861 census finds William (age 10) at Eastcotts with his sister [[elizabeth-thody-1847|Elizabeth]], suggesting some dispersal of the family in his early childhood. His parents and surviving siblings are recorded as living into old age; the Thody family had a notable strain of longevity — his brother [[george-thody-1844|George]] died aged ~91, sister [[elizabeth-thody-1847|Elizabeth]] aged ~93, and William's own son [[louis-albert-thody-1876|Louis Albert]] reached nearly 99.

## America (1871–c. 1876)

Sometime around 1870 William emigrated to America. On 11 May 1871, aged 21½, he enlisted in the United States Army at Detroit, Michigan — occupation Teamster, 5'8", brown eyes and hair, dark complexion. He was posted to Newport Barracks, Kentucky. On 14 May 1873 he married [[mathilde-parent-1853|Mathilde "Tillie" Elizabeth Parent]] in Chicago at the First Congregational Church, officiated by Pastor Edward P. Godwin. He deserted the Army on 3 July 1873 — seven weeks after the wedding — and appears never to have returned.

The couple initially lived in or near Canada. Their eldest son [[george-allen-thody-1874|George Allen]] was born c. 1874 in Sandwich, Ontario. By the end of 1876 they had returned to Bedfordshire: [[louis-albert-thody-1876|Louis Albert]] was born on 8 December 1876 at Cotton End and christened on 13 February 1877 at the Wesleyan Methodist chapel, Toddington.

## Bedfordshire Police Constable — First Marriage

By 1881 William was a Police Constable lodging at 155 Up Street, Sharnbrook, Bedfordshire. He is listed in the census as "Widower" — a misleading description, as Tillie was still alive. She had returned to Canada to care for her father, taking Louis Albert with her; six-year-old George Allen stayed behind with William.

During this period of Tillie's absence, William fathered an illegitimate child: [[mary-ann-nash-1880|Mary Ann Nash]] (c. 1880, Kentish Town, London) with [[matilda-nash-1858|Matilda Nash]]. How a Bedfordshire police constable came to have a child with a London woman is unexplained.

Tillie returned from Canada — probably around 1882–1883 — and the family was reunited. Two further children were born: [[clarence-james-thody-1884|Clarence James]] (1884, Houghton Regis) and [[lena-florence-thody-1889|Lena Florence]] (1889, Totternhoe). The 1891 census shows the family at Totternhoe: William (40, Police Constable), Tillie (38, born Sandwich, Canada), Allen/George (16, Printers Apprentice), Albert/Louis (14, Printers Apprentice), Clarence (6), Florence (1), and "Minnie Thody" (10, born Kentish Town — Mary Ann Nash, listed as "niece [illegitimate daughter]").

Tillie died in 1892, aged approximately 39. She had given William four children across nearly twenty years, in a marriage that spanned two continents.

## Second Marriage and Studham

On 6 February 1894 William married [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] at [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]] Parish Church. He was 43, a widowed Police Officer residing at Markyate; Lucy was 37, a spinster of Whipsnade with no named father. The witnesses were [[florence-bransom-1873|Florence Bransom]] (Lucy's niece, daughter of [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] and [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]]) and [[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cook]] (son of [[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook (1843–1908)]] and [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] — Lois being the sister of Edwin Bransom, and thus Walter and Florence Bransom first cousins). William named his father Allen Thody, Labourer, on the certificate. Their son [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]] was born the same year.

The 1901 census at Studham shows a blended household: William and Lucy, step-children Clarence Archibald (16) and Florence Lena (11) from his first marriage, and young Harry (6). The older step-sons — George Allen and Louis Albert — had by then left home.

## Death

William died on 1 February 1911 at Studham, aged 60. His funeral card reads: *"Ex-P.C. Beds. Constabulary, Born at Cotton End, Bedford, Nov. 11th, 1850, Died at Studham, February 1st, 1911, Aged 60 Years. Interred in Studham Churchyard."* He was buried on 4 February 1911 (Studham Parish Burial Register, entry #52).

He had been a Police Constable throughout his time in Bedfordshire — Sharnbrook (1881), Totternhoe (1891), Studham (1901) — and died as a retired officer. Harry was 16 when his father died. [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy]] remained at Rose Cottage, Studham, until her own death in 1930.

## Notes

- Also recorded as William Alan Thody, William A Thody, William Thody.
- His name is alternately spelled "Thodey" in some 1851–1873 records.
- Cotton End (his birthplace) is a hamlet within the Cardington registration district, approximately 10 miles from Studham — all William's known Bedfordshire addresses are within the same tight corner of the county.

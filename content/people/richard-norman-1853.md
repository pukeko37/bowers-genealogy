Richard Norman was born in 1853 in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire — the second child of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] (1827–1905) and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] (1829–1897). He and his elder brother George were christened together on 3 July 1853 at Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire — Ann's home parish.

## Swavesey years (1853–c.1873)

Richard grew up in the Norman household at High Street, Swavesey, alongside Thomas, George, Charles, Tom, Mary Ann, Alfred Eusden, and Robert Henry. The 1871 census shows him still in Swavesey at age 18, working as an **Agricultural Labourer** — following his father's trade. By this point his elder brother George (20) was also still at home. Both would leave Swavesey within the next four years.

The 1871 census record also shows a "James Norman" (M, 20, Swavesey, Cambridgeshire) appearing among "other people" on the same census piece — neither a listed sibling nor clearly a household member. He may be a neighbour, cousin, or the enumerator's adjacent entry. The Norman family tree does not include a "James" in any other source; the anomaly is unresolved.

## Hartlepool settlement (1875 onward)

Richard left Swavesey between 1871 and 1875 and moved to **West Hartlepool, County Durham**. He was not alone: his elder brother [[george-norman-1851|George]] made the same journey at the same time. Richard married **[[ann-elizabeth-randall-1854|Ann Elizabeth Randall]]** in October 1875 at Hartlepool; George married **Margaret J Garry** at Christ Church, West Hartlepool, in December 1875 — barely two months later. The two eldest Norman brothers arrived in the same industrial town and both married there in the same autumn.

Richard settled in **Stranton** (a township of West Hartlepool) at **13 Baily Street**, and remained in the Hartlepool area for the rest of his documented life. George lived in Stranton too. For nearly thirty years the brothers were near-neighbours.

A Swavesey contemporary, **William Mole/Moule** (born Swavesey ~1852), appears on both the 1881 and 1891 census records living near Richard in Stranton — evidence that the Hartlepool migration from Swavesey may have involved more than just the two Norman brothers.

## Occupation

Richard's working life in Hartlepool was entirely unskilled manual labour:

| Year | Occupation | Location |
|------|-----------|----------|
| 1871 | Agricultural Labourer | Swavesey |
| 1881 | Labourer | Stranton, Durham |
| 1891 | General Labourer | Stranton, Durham |
| 1901 | Labourer (Cement Works) | West Hartlepool |
| 1911 | General Labourer, Corporation | West Hartlepool |

The 1901 occupation — cement works labourer — places him in West Hartlepool's chemical and heavy-industry sector. By 1911 he was a Corporation labourer, working for West Hartlepool Borough Council on municipal infrastructure. He followed the same trajectory as his father Thomas (lifelong agricultural labourer), but the work was industrial rather than rural.

## The Fenstanton connection

Ann Elizabeth Randall, Richard's wife, was born in **Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire** — confirmed by the 1901 and 1911 census records. This is a remarkable fact: Fenstanton was the village where Richard's grandmother Ann Tabram was christened, where his great-grandmother Mary Eusden was buried, and where Richard and George themselves were christened in 1853. The Norman family's Fenstanton roots ran four generations deep through the Tabraham-Eusden line. Whether Richard met Ann through maintained family connections to Fenstanton, or through coincidence in Hartlepool, is unknown. See [[ann-elizabeth-randall-1854]].

## Children

Richard and Ann had **eleven children**, of whom several died young. Three sons were successively named "Charles" after Richard's brother [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]]:

1. Charles Norman (1878–1878) — died in infancy
2. Mary Elizabeth Norman (1878–1908) — died aged 30
3. Charles Norman (~1880–1898) — died aged 18
4. Frances Ann Norman (1882–Deceased)
5. John Thomas Norman (1885–Deceased)
6. Richard Randall Norman (1886–1969) — carries father's first name and mother's surname; lived to 83
7. Willie H Norman (1891–Deceased)
8. Albert Norman (1893–Deceased)
9. George Norman (1895–Deceased) — named after father's brother George
10. Annie Louisa Norman (1898–1945)
11. Charles Norman (1900–1987) — the third son named Charles; survived to 87

The 1901 census also records a daughter "Mary E" aged 12 (born ~1889) who appears distinct from Mary Elizabeth (born 1878) — suggesting there may have been a second Mary among the children, possibly unnamed in the FamilySearch print view. The "Annie Eliz Norman, daughter, 3" in 1901 is almost certainly Annie Louisa (1898).

The naming pattern — "Charles" attempted three times, "George" once — shows that Richard maintained the bonds of Swavesey brotherhood throughout his life in Durham. Richard Randall Norman (1886–1969) carries his mother's maiden name as a middle name — a practice echoing Ann Tabram's family's own habit of memorial naming.

## Note on FamilySearch data error

The FamilySearch sources page for Richard Norman (94N7-QWG) includes a Marriage Registration Index entry for "Richard Norman married Margaret Garry, 1875, Hartlepool, Vol 10A page 190." This is almost certainly George Norman's marriage record — George married **Margaret J Garry** at Christ Church, West Hartlepool, December 1875. All census records for Richard show his wife was Annie/Ann from Huntingdonshire; no record supports Margaret Garry as Richard's wife. The source appears to have been wrongly attached to Richard's profile in FamilySearch.

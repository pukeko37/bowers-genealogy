Allen Thody (5 October 1813 – 22 March 1898) was a Bedfordshire farm labourer and the father of [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]]. He married [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]] on 19 October 1834 at St. Mary Cardington, Bedfordshire, and together they had five children.

## Origins

Allen was born on 5 October 1813 in Spain — confirmed by Louis Albert Thody's family history notebook, compiled by Allen's grandson. His father was **[[william-thody-1774|William Thody]]** (abt 1774–1858), a soldier of the 40th Regiment of Foot who fought in the Peninsular War and received the Waterloo Medal in 1815. His mother was Elizabeth (née unknown, formerly Meyers), whom William married at Badajoz, Spain, around 1809.

Allen's birth in Spain was an accident of wartime: his father was on campaign with the British Army across the Iberian Peninsula. The family's roots were in Cardington, Bedfordshire, where the Thody name is documented from at least the 1740s. Allen grew up in Cardington and never left it.

The 1851 census records his birthplace as "Spain British Subject," confirming that he was a British national born abroad. The 1881 census anomalously records "St Madeira, Portugal" — almost certainly an enumerator's mishearing — contradicted by all other sources.

## Life

Allen is described as a Farm Labourer in every census from 1841 to 1891. He could not write — he signed his mark on his son William's birth certificate in 1850, and appears on the 1894 Whipsnade marriage certificate still described as a Labourer. He and Elizabeth settled at Cotton End, the hamlet in Eastcotts parish west of Bedford, and remained there for the rest of their lives.

By the final decades, Allen's children had dispersed: William had emigrated and returned from America; Ann (56) and Elizabeth (43, now Rogers) were living with their parents when the 1891 census was taken, along with granddaughter May Rogers (3). Ann was present at Allen's death.

He died on 22 March 1898 at Cotton End, Eastcotts; cause of death: "Decay." He was 84. His wife Elizabeth died six months later, on 11 October 1898. They had been married for 64 years.

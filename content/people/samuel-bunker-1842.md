Samuel Bunker was christened on 19 June 1842 at Millbrook, the eighth child of [[john-bunker-1803|John Bunker (1803–1889)]] and Maria West (1806) — two years younger than his brother [[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph (1840–1914)]]. He died in 1913 at Ampthill, Bedfordshire. FamilySearch ID: MM18-7H2.

Like Joseph, Samuel spent his entire life in Millbrook. He appears at every census from 1851 to 1911, working as an agricultural labourer throughout. The two brothers are almost mirror images in the census record: same village, same occupation, same pattern of gradual retirement. Both died in Ampthill — the registration district town — within a year of each other: Samuel in 1913, Joseph in 1914.

## Marriage and family

Samuel married **Harriet Dean** (born 1844, died 1931) on **25 December 1865** at Millbrook — Christmas Day, when he was 23 and she was 21. Their daughter Minnie Maria Dean Bunker was born in 1870. They had two children; only one survived infancy. See [[bunker-dean]].

## In the newspapers

- **December 1877:** Summonsed for failing to send his children to school (Millbrook). His daughter Minnie was 7 at the time — school age.
- **October 1881:** Named in a Millbrook court article alongside his brother Joseph and several other local men; Annie Peters was a witness. Circumstances unclear from the newspaper snippet.
- **October 1882:** First prize at the Silsoe & Ampthill Labourers' Friend Society for longest continuous Benefit Society membership. Samuel aged 40.
- **October 1892:** A "Samuel Bunker, 73" won first prize for longest service with the same employer (Mr G Allen, Ampthill). If 73 is his age, this is the **older Samuel (~1820, son of William 1767)**, not our 1842 Samuel who was only 50 in 1892. If it is our Samuel, the age is wrong. This entry may trace the previously-lost elder Samuel into his seventies.

## Clerk and sexton of Millbrook parish church

Samuel was the **clerk and sexton of St Michael and All Angels, Millbrook** for approximately 20 years — confirmed by his funeral notice in the *Bedfordshire Times*, 20 June 1913:

> "The deceased had been for about 20 years clerk and sexton at the parish church, until the present Rector came to Millbrook. His father also [served in the same capacity]."

His father [[john-bunker-1803|John Bunker (1803–1889)]] had held the same roles before him, making this a two-generation parish appointment. Samuel was buried in the Millbrook churchyard where he had served.

## The Huett effigy story

Samuel is **confirmed as Mr Bunker the sexton** of the 1919 Huett effigy story. He died in 1913, six years before the excavation. His widow [[harriet-dean-1844|Harriet Dean (born 1844)]] was approximately 75 in 1919 — the "aged widow" who led excavators to the spot and produced the stone head. Harriet lived until 1931.

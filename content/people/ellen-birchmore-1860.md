Ellen Birchmore was born 21 March 1860 in [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], Bedfordshire — christened **Ellen Plumer** on 6 May 1860 at Whipsnade, reflecting the unsettled state of her father's surname. She was the eldest of three children of [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] and Eliza Batchelor (1829–1867) in the [[birchmore-plummer-batchelor-1859|Birchmore-Plummer-Batchelor family]]. Her siblings were [[elizabeth-birchmore-1862|Elizabeth Birchmore]] (1862–1954) and Thomas Birchmore (1865–1865, died in infancy).

Her mother Eliza died in 1867 when Ellen was seven. Her father married Ruth Cooke nine months later. Ruth brought three illegitimate children, including [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (b. 1856) — only four years older than Ellen. Lucy and Ellen were raised together and became like sisters. This step-sisterhood ultimately connected the Norman and Bunker families two generations later.

Ellen grew up in Whipsnade. She trained as a cook — at The Chequers Inn (her father and stepmother Ruth held the license) and at Hall Farm, Whipsnade (possibly Whipsnade House). In the 1881 census she was not with her family — likely enumerated with an employer as a live-in cook.

Ellen met [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] when he was hauling hay from Hall Farm to London for Carter Paterson & Co. They married in 1883 in Kensington, and she moved to London. See [[norman-birchmore-1883|Norman-Birchmore family]] for the full family story.

### Residences
- 1860–1881: Whipsnade, then live-in cook
- 1883–c.1889: Islington, London — Mabel (1883), Thomas George (1886), and Elizabeth Ann (1888) born here
- c.1889–1891: [[hackney|Hackney]], London — Alice (1890) born Hackney; 1891 census confirms
- 1891–1907: [[hosier-lane-city-of-london|Hosier Lane]], Smithfield, London
- 1907–1940: [[dalston|Dalston]]/Kingsland, Hackney — final address 121 De Beauvoir Road

### Widowhood

Charles died on 1 September 1907. Ellen was 47, with nine children aged 5 to 24. She survived for 33 years as a widow — supported by her daughters' wages, her cooking skills, and the family network including the Mason brothers who married into the family.

By 1939 Ellen was listed as "incapacitated," living at 121 De Beauvoir Road with her youngest daughter [[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah]]. Her daughter [[elizabeth-ann-norman-1888|Elizabeth Ann]] lived just 22 houses away at number 143.

Ellen died on 28 February 1940 at 121 De Beauvoir Road, aged 79 — as the Blitz was beginning. An excellent cook who raised nine children and endured 33 years of widowhood.

Mary Eusden was born around 1807 and christened on 16 January 1808 at Trinity Church, Ely, Cambridgeshire. Her father was [[thomas-euseden-1764|Thomas Euseden]] (1764–1842); her mother's name is unknown. She was also known as Mary Ewsden, a phonetic variant.

The christening at Ely is somewhat puzzling, because the Eusden family was firmly rooted in Huntingdonshire — not Cambridgeshire. The Huntingdonshire marriages index (1754–1837) shows the family concentrated first around Ellington in the 1760s–1770s, then around Fenstanton from the 1820s onwards, with no Cambridgeshire presence. Thomas may have been temporarily resident in Ely around 1807–1808 (perhaps for work or family reasons on his wife's side), then returned to the Fenstanton area where Mary would spend her adult life.

**Siblings.** The FamilySearch record for Thomas Euseden lists four children, all now with person pages:
- [[william-eusden|William Eusden]] — married Sarah Cox, Fenstanton 1825
- Mary (this page)
- [[sarah-eusden-1804|Sarah Eusden]] (1804–1833) — married John Asplan, Fenstanton 1829
- [[henry-eusden|Henry Eusden]] — married Mary Papworth, Fenstanton 1833

A fifth sibling: [[thomas-eusden-1799|Thomas Eusden]] (1799–1835) — married Mary Ann James (widow) at Fenstanton, 21 February 1833; died two years later aged 36, leaving a son [[alfred-eusden-1834|Alfred Eusden]] (b. Christmas Day 1834). Alfred was raised by his stepfather Skinner but kept the Eusden name, marrying in Shoreditch in 1859.

**Family of origin.** Thomas Euseden (1764–1842) was himself probably a son of the Ellington Eusdens. The 1762 Huntingdonshire marriage of "Rt Eusden × Eliz Bellamy" at Ellington is the most likely record of Thomas's parents — "Rt" (Robert?) marrying in 1762 and Thomas born two years later in 1764 fits precisely. Three other Eusdens married at Ellington in 1762–1772, probably siblings of Rt.

She married [[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]] on 3 August 1828 in Fen Stanton, forming the [[tabraham-eusden-1828|Tabraham-Eusden family]]. Their four children were:
1. [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] (1829–1897) — later married Thomas Norman; grandmother of Charles Norman's London family
2. Susan Tabram (1831–1911)
3. John Tabram (1836–1901)
4. George Richard Tabram (1840–1857)

Mary died around 1841 in Fen Stanton, when her eldest daughter Ann was twelve and her youngest George Richard was less than a year old. At the time of her death, her elderly father Thomas (then ~77) was living in the same household at Bell Lane, confirmed by the 1841 census. Thomas outlived her by only seventeen months, dying in November 1842.

Her maiden name "Eusden" was preserved in the family by her daughter Ann, who named her sixth child Alfred **Eusden** Norman in 1865 — 24 years after Mary's death. This act of naming is the only reason Mary Eusden's memory persists visibly in the wider family record.

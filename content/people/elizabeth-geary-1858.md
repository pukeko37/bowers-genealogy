Elizabeth Geary was born in 1858 in Haynes, Bedfordshire (christened 21 February 1858), the fifth surviving child of [[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]]. She died in 1936 in Bedfordshire, aged 78 — the longest-lived of all John and Lucy's children.

Elizabeth never married. She spent her adult life building an independent existence in **Luton**, working in the straw trade.

In 1871 she is listed as a **straw platter** at the parental home, aged 13. By 1881 she had moved to Luton as a **housemaid domestic servant** in the household of Richard L Cooke, a solicitor and Registrar of the County Court — a step up from agricultural labouring into respectable domestic service. By 1901 she had left domestic service entirely, living as head of her own household in Luton and working as a **straw bonnet sewer** — a skilled trade in the town that was the centre of England's straw hat industry. The 1901 census records her as single, aged 41, and financially independent.

She is the only one of John and Lucy's adult children confirmed to have remained unmarried and without children. Her trajectory — from Haynes platter to Luton bonnet sewer, independent householder — represents a modest but significant form of economic self-determination.

She and her brother [[albert-geary-1866|Albert Geary]] both settled in Luton; both connections to that city reflect the gravity of the straw hat trade on Bedfordshire's working families.

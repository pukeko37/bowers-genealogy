Elizabeth Birchmore was born on 13 October 1862 in Whipsnade, Bedfordshire, and christened on 7 December 1862 in Whipsnade. She was the second child of [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer Sr]] and [[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]], and thus the younger sister of [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore Norman]].

Eliza Batchelor died in 1867, when Elizabeth was four years old. Elizabeth was raised in Whipsnade; she is recorded there in the 1871 census.

She then followed the path of many rural girls of her generation into London domestic service:
- **1881:** Islington, London — General Servant, aged 18
- **1891:** Clerkenwell, London — Domestic Servant General, aged 28

In Clerkenwell, she was in the same neighbourhood as the Norman-Birchmore family who lived in Hosier Lane, Smithfield. Whether she had contact with her half-sister Ellen's family during these years is not documented.

By 1921, Elizabeth had moved to **4 Yukon Road, Balham** — the household of her niece [[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]] and Alice's husband [[isaac-mason-1882|Isaac Mason]]. She remained there until her death in 1954, aged 92. In the 1939 Register, the Yukon Road household was Alice, Isaac, and Elizabeth — three people, two of whom were the last remaining connections between the Whipsnade Birchmores and the London Normans.

Elizabeth never married. She outlived her half-sister Ellen (died 1940) and Isaac Mason (died 1955), though by a matter of months — she died in 1954. She was the last surviving child of GBP Sr by any of his three marriages.

### Context

Elizabeth's presence in the Mason household closes a generational loop: her father GBP Sr was the Whipsnade farmer/publican; her half-sister Ellen was the cook who went to London; and Elizabeth's own domestic service career in the same London parishes eventually led her to her niece's home in Balham, where she spent the last three decades of her life.

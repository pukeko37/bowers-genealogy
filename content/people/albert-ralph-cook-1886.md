Albert Ralph Cook (1886 – 13 January 1932) was the youngest of the eight children of [[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook]] (1843–1908) and [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] (1843–1924), all born in Whipsnade. His eldest brother was [[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cook]] (1869), who had witnessed the 1894 Thody-Cook wedding at Whipsnade.

Albert was 5 in the 1891 census and 15 in 1901, when he was the only child still at home with his parents. His father Charles died on 5 January 1908, having held the Chequers Inn licence from 1904. His mother Lois outlived both her husband and Albert, dying in 1924.

In 1915 Albert married **Hilda Kate Turvey** (26 February 1894 – 1987), the daughter of [[james-purton-turvey|James Purton Turvey]] (1867–1910) — the man who had been co-tenant of [[dell-farm|Dell Farm]] alongside Albert's near-neighbour [[james-hain-1877|James Hain]] until his death in 1910. The marriage joined the Cook and Turvey families, two households rooted in the same small village. They had one known daughter: **Hilda Jessie Cook** (1915–2001), who carried her father's surname as a middle name and later married Wallsam. Albert died on 13 January 1932 and is buried at St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade. After his death Hilda Kate remarried — she appears in the churchyard list under the name Taylor.

**Birth year note:** The gravestone inscription reads "aged 47 years," which would place his birth around 1884. However, census ages (5 in 1891, 15 in 1901) are consistent with 1886, and the GRO index for the Luton district shows no Albert Cook registered in 1883 or 1884. The March 1886 quarter has two Albert Cook registrations in Luton (vol. 3b, refs 430 and 480); one is almost certainly him, confirmable by the mother's maiden name (Brandsom) on the scan. The stone age appears to be wrong — not uncommon on gravestones — and 1886 is the most reliable date.

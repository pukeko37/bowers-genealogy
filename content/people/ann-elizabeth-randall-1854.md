Ann Elizabeth Randall was born around 1854 in **Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire** — a village in the low-lying fenland country between Cambridge and Huntingdon. She married [[richard-norman-1853|Richard Norman]] in October 1875 at Hartlepool, Durham, and died in 1933, some years after Richard (whose death date is unknown).

Her Fenstanton birthplace is confirmed by the 1901 and 1911 census records, both of which give her birthplace as "Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire." The 1881 and 1891 censuses give "Huntingdon, Huntingdonshire" and "Huntingdonshire" respectively — vaguer, but consistent.

## The Fenstanton connection

Fenstanton was no casual address for the Norman family. It is the village where:
- Richard Norman's **grandmother [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]]** was christened in 1829 and grew up before moving to Swavesey
- Richard and his brother George were christened together on 3 July 1853 at Fenstanton church (their mother Ann's home parish)
- Richard's **great-grandmother [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary Eusden]]** died and was buried, around 1841
- Richard's **great-great-grandfather [[thomas-euseden-1764|Thomas Euseden]]** lived at Bell Lane and was buried on 14 November 1842

The Norman family's connection to Fenstanton ran four generations deep — through the Tabraham and Eusden lines. That Richard married a woman from Fenstanton rather than from Swavesey (where he grew up) or from Hartlepool (where he settled) is striking. Whether he met Ann through maintained family connections in Fenstanton, or through a Swavesey community link, or by coincidence in Hartlepool, is not documented.

Her family background — who the Randalls of Fenstanton were, and whether she had any prior connection to the Tabram or Norman families — is unresearched.

## Life in West Hartlepool

Ann lived with Richard at 13 Baily Street, Stranton (1881), and then in West Hartlepool through the 1891, 1901, and 1911 censuses. She and Richard had eleven children over the period 1878–1900. She appears consistently in the census records as "Annie E" or "Annie Eliz" — the name was formally Ann Elizabeth but she used Annie.

By 1901 she was 47 with nine children still at home. Richard worked as a Labourer (Cement Works) that year; Ann managed the household. By 1911 seven were still at home, the eldest Frances Ann (28) unmarried and remaining with her parents.

Ann outlived Richard — his death date is unknown, but Ann died in 1933, and the FamilySearch record still shows Richard as "Deceased" with no date. She would have been about 79 at death.

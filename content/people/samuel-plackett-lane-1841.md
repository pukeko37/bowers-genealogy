Samuel Plackett Lane was born about 1841 in **Bedford, Bedfordshire** (St Paul's parish). Also recorded as "Samuel Stockett Lane" in some documents — both spellings appear in FamilySearch. His father was George Lane.

He is first documented in the **1861 census** on **Hassett Street, St Paul's parish, Bedford**, age 18, as a **Boot Maker Apprentice** in the household of John Howard — a boot maker who employed four men. The user previously noted Samuel as a probable apprentice shoe maker, a hypothesis this record confirms. Hassett Street and Dane Street are both in St Paul's parish; Caleb Bunker (age 15, shoe maker) was in the same parish at the same date, and both were in the boot and shoe trade.

On **21 September 1863**, Samuel married **[[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]]** at **St Peter's Martin, Bedford**. Lydia was the second child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and Elizabeth Watts — making Samuel Caleb Bunker's brother-in-law. The marriage connected the Lane and Bunker families directly. At marriage Samuel was 22 and Lydia was 30.

By the **1871 census** Samuel had his own household at St Paul's Bedford, working as a **Boot & Shoemaker**. His wife Lydia (37, Millbrook) and daughters Edith Mercy (6) and Elizabeth Maryanne (2) were with him. Albert John Lane (14, Maulden) also appears in the household.

Samuel died in **1872 in Bedford**, aged 29, leaving Lydia a widow with three young children. He had trained and worked in Bedford's boot and shoe trade for his entire short working life — the same trade Caleb Bunker had apprenticed to in the same parish a decade earlier. See [[bunker-lane-1863]].

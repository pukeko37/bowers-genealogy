James Bunker (1824 – 26 November 1904) was the youngest surviving child of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph Bunker (1792–1853)]] and Mary Markham, and a grandson of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. He was born in Stevington, Bedfordshire, christened on **26 September 1824** at Stevington — the same mass ceremony that christened his older brothers [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer]] (1822) and the infant William, and which also christened his cousins [[ephraim-bunker-1821|Ephraim]] (1821) and Ebenezer from the Caleb branch. He spent his entire life in Stevington and died there 80 years later. FamilySearch ID: L227-TV6.

## Marriage and family

James married **Sophia Panter** (1829–1903) on **25 December 1850** at Stevington. (The surname is recorded as "Panter" in FamilySearch; family notes give "Painter" — the distinction is unresolved from available sources.) Sophia was born in 1829; her birthplace is given in the 1861 census as "Warrington, Bedfordshire", which is likely a transcription error for an unidentified Bedfordshire village.

## Life in Stevington

James appears in every census from 1841 to 1901 at Silver Street, Stevington — the same address as his father Joseph's household in 1841 and 1851. His occupation throughout was agricultural labourer. The 1861 census shows him as "James Banker" (OCR corruption), age 36, head of household at Silver Street, with Sophia (32) and daughters Jane (8) and Ruth (5, recorded as "Bash Banker").

His brothers had scattered by the 1850s: [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer]] emigrated to Australia in 1845; John (1817) was in Stevington until his death in 1887. James outlived both, dying on 26 November 1904 and being buried in the Stevington area (death registered Bedford/Turvey district). He was 80 years old — echoing the longevity of his grandfather James Thomas (died 1824) and his own father Joseph (died 1853, aged 60, the youngest of the main line).

## Connection to the Stevington newspaper record

The **"J. Bunker"** who won second prize for damsons at the **Stevington Flower Show in September 1896** (*Bedfordshire Mercury*) is James — aged 72, a lifelong Stevington villager and presumably a keen kitchen gardener. See [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-findmypast]].

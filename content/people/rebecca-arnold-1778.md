Rebecca Arnold (born about 1778 – buried August 1812, Cardington) was the daughter of a Cotton End farmer, wife of a Cardington shoemaker, and the mother of [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]].

## Origins

Her father was **Samuel Arnold**, a farmer at Cotton End in the Parish of Cardington. His will, signed in May 1790 and proved in November of that year, names his children as Rebecca, Samuel, Mary, and an unborn child. He left Rebecca the specific sum of £150 — a significant bequest, marking her out among the children — with the remainder of the estate to be managed for the family's maintenance and education. His brothers **Robert Arnold** and **John Arnold** were appointed co-executors and guardians. Samuel's wife was Mary Arnold.

Rebecca was about twelve years old when her father died.

## Marriage and family

On 14 May 1799, Rebecca Arnold married **[[joseph-sanders-1774|Joseph Sanders]]** at St Mary's Cardington — both of the same parish. She signed with a mark; he signed his name. They were a modest Cardington household: Joseph a shoemaker, Rebecca illiterate. They had three children together, all christened at Cardington:

1. **Mary Saunders** (born 28 December 1800, christened 26 May 1801)
2. **Anne Saunders** (christened 29 July 1804)
3. **Sarah Saunders** (born c. 1808, christened 25 August 1811 — a delayed baptism of over three years)

Joseph Sanders died on 22 June 1810, leaving Rebecca a widow with three children. She was about 32.

## Widowhood and death

In the thirteen months after Joseph's death, Rebecca had an illegitimate child — **[[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth]]**, born approximately 24 June 1811, baptised 25 August 1811 as "Elizabeth, illegitimate Child of Rebecca Saunders, Widow, aged nine weeks." Elizabeth's father is unknown. On the same day, Rebecca also had her long-delayed daughter Sarah baptised — the two children received the sacrament together.

Rebecca died in August 1812, approximately 14 months after Elizabeth's birth. She was buried at Cardington as "Rebecca Sanders, Widow." Elizabeth was just over a year old; her half-siblings Mary, Anne, and Sarah were 11, 8, and c. 4 years old. Rebecca was approximately 34 years old.

## Legacy

Elizabeth Saunders grew up knowing her grandfather's family — the Cotton End Arnolds. The witnesses at Elizabeth's 1834 marriage to Allen Thody were **John Arnold** and **Elizabeth Arnold**, who signed with marks. John Arnold was Samuel Arnold's brother, named in the 1790 will as co-executor. Whether this is the same man (who would have been in his late sixties or seventies by 1834) or a member of the next generation of Arnolds is uncertain, but the family connection is clear.

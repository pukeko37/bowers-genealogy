Joel Bunker (c.1782–1861) was the sixth child of [[joseph-bunker-1754|Joseph Bunker (1754)]] and [[martha-garratt-1756|Martha Garratt]]. He is the first of his parents' nine surviving children to be traced into the next generation, and the only one for whom a detailed life record exists.

## Birth and christening

FamilySearch estimates his birth as "from 1782 to 1786, Bedfordshire," derived from census data. A christening record at Millbrook on 26 April 1789 has also been attached to his profile, but this date conflicts with the census estimate and is almost certainly a **delayed registration** — the family registering a child with the Church of England years after birth, consistent with nonconformist practice. The Millbrook Wesleyan community is documented registering children belatedly; the same pattern appears elsewhere in the Bunker lines. Joel was probably born c.1782–1786 and christened in 1789 when he would have been aged 3–7.

## First marriage and family

Joel married **[[martha-taylor-1786|Martha Taylor]]** (c.1786–1836) on 30 January 1809 at Millbrook — two months after his father's death. Their children were Job (1804), Joseph (1810), Eliza (1815–1885), and Patty (1817). Job, born five years before the marriage, was likely a pre-marital child if Joel was born c.1782.

Martha died on **11 October 1836, aged 49**, at the Green Man Inn, Eversholt — her death was reported in the Bucks Gazette. See [[bunker-taylor-1809]].

## The Green Man Inn, Eversholt

Joel was living at **Eversholt, Bedfordshire** from at least early 1815 — his daughter Eliza was born and christened there in June 1815. He was established as publican of the **Green Man Inn at Eversholt** — a village near Woburn, about eight miles from Millbrook. He ran the pub for over a decade, appearing in newspaper records through the 1830s and 1840s:

- **1833:** Named as tenant at the Green Man in an auction notice for neighbouring property
- **1838:** His daughter Eliza married Stephen Danby, an excise officer, at Eversholt
- **1844:** Victim of an assault by three customers (John Smith, John Brazier, William Brazier), all fined; separately fined £1 himself for short measures
- **1846:** Auctioned the contents of the Green Man and left Eversholt

The 1841 census confirms him at Eversholt.

## Second marriage

Three years after Martha's death, Joel married **[[elizabeth-inskipp-1792|Elizabeth Inskipp]]** (1792–Deceased) on **16 April 1839 at Flitton with Silsoe, Bedfordshire**. No children from this marriage are recorded. See [[bunker-inskipp-1839]].

## Later life and death

Joel left Eversholt in September 1846. By the 1861 census he was at **Fairlight, Sussex**, his occupation recorded as "Formerly Publician And Serv" — retired from the pub trade. He died later in 1861 at **Ampthill, Bedfordshire**, aged approximately 79. The move back toward Bedfordshire at the end of his life, dying in Ampthill just a few miles from his Millbrook birthplace, closes the circle.

Catherine James (known as Kate) was born in 1852 in **Llanfihangel Geneur Glynn**, a parish in north Cardiganshire, Wales. In the 1861 census she is found age 9 in **Rhydypenne, Cynnull-mawr parish**, in the household headed by **Thomas James** (26, groom, single) — her brother — alongside **Mary James** (28, same parish). Catherine was a scholar. Her parents are not identified in the surviving sources, though she and Thomas were from the same Llanfihangel Geneur Glynn community.

She married **[[richard-mason-1850|Richard Mason]]** on 5 May 1874 in Aberystwyth — a civil and church event confirmed by two Marriage Bond records and a registration index entry. The couple were both Cardiganshire-born and had the same regional background. After the marriage they moved to London, part of the well-documented **Welsh Cardiganshire dairy migration** into the city.

By 1881 Catherine (recorded as Kate Mason) was living in **Bethnal Green** with Richard and their sons. She appears without an occupation in the 1881 census — Richard was the recorded head and Cowkeeper. After Richard's death in January 1890, Catherine is head of household in the **1891 census** at St Sepulchre Newgate (the Holborn/Smithfield parish that includes Hosier Lane), now recorded as **Widowed** with occupation **Dairy**. She continued operating the dairy at **27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield** for a full decade after her husband's death, raising three sons while running the business alone.

Catherine died on **29 October 1900** at 27 Hosier Lane, aged 50. She was buried on 2 November 1900 at **Abney Park Cemetery, Stoke Newington** — the same cemetery as Richard, ten years earlier. At Catherine's death her sons were 25, 22, and 18 — the two younger were still minors when the household was broken up. The three brothers subsequently made their lives as tradesmen in London.

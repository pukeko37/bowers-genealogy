Martha Sarah Bunker was born in 1841 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire, the sixth child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]]. She died on 7 July 1901 at **21 Cumberland Street, Luton**, aged 60, buried 10 July. A death notice appeared in the *Luton Times and Advertiser* on 12 July 1901. Caleb's elder sister.

She married **Joseph Sinfield** on **25 May 1863** at Saint Paul, Bedford — confirmed by both the church marriage register and the GRO index (vol 3B, page 488). Joseph was born ~1844 in Bedford. See [[bunker-sinfield-1863]].

They had two daughters:
1. Annie Maria Sinfield (1864)
2. Elizabeth Julia Sinfield (1867)

**Lifelong straw industry worker.** The 1851 census records Martha aged 10 as a **Plaiter** — straw plaiting in Millbrook, the cottage industry that fed the hat trade. By 1861 she was a **Bonnet Sewer** in Bedford. From 1871 she appears in census records as a **Straw Hat Sewer** in the Sinfield household, and she was still at it in the 1901 census, aged 59, three months before her death. The same trade connects her to her sister [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann]], who worked alongside her in Luton as a straw hat sewer for decades.

**The Sinfield echo.** Thomas Bunker (1738) — Martha Sarah's great-great-grandfather — married a **Martha Sinfield** at Millbrook in 1761. Now a Bunker woman marries a Sinfield, reversing the direction across four generations. Whether Joseph Sinfield descended from the Millbrook Sinfields of 1761 is unconfirmed.

The family moved from Bedford to Luton by 1881, following Joseph's transfer as a Police Constable. By 1901 he had left the police and was working as a General Labourer. Martha died in Luton on 7 July 1901, aged 60, registered as "Martha Sarah Sinfield." Joseph outlived her by 13 years, dying in 1914.

**Residence history:** 1841–1852 Millbrook · 1852–1863 Bedford · 1863–~1880 Bedford (married) · 1881–1901 Luton

**Open question:** The 12 July 1901 death notice is a brief column entry, not a biographical obituary. A longer memorial piece may exist elsewhere in the same or adjacent editions of the *Luton Times and Advertiser*. The BNA full page image is available.

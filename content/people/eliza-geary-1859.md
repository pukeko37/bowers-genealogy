Eliza Geary was born on 25 July 1859 in Haynes (also written Hawnes), Bedfordshire — the sixth child of [[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] (1825–1895) and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]] (1826–1895). Her birth was registered at Ampthill. She was christened 27 November 1859 in Haynes.

She was named after her elder sister **Eliza** (1849–1856), who had died aged seven. The re-use of the name was common practice when a child died young.

**Childhood in Haynes.** Haynes (Hawnes) is a scattered rural parish south of Bedford, near the Ampthill cluster of villages. Eliza grew up in a large family — eleven children in total — though four did not survive to adulthood: Eliza (1849), Emma (1856–1874), Ellen (1864–1865), and Frederick Louis (1871–1872). Her parents are recorded in Haynes in every census from 1841 through 1881. See [[geary-morris-1848]].

**Luton 1881.** By the 1881 census, Eliza was in Luton, Bedfordshire — aged 22, working as a **cook and domestic servant** in the Brown household (RG11, piece 1649, folio 15, page 24). She had left Haynes and was in service, eleven years before her marriage. This places her in Luton a full decade before she appears in Maulden — the domestic service route out of rural Bedfordshire was a common one for young women of her background.

**Maulden 1891.** The most significant entry in her record: by 1891 Eliza was living in Maulden, Bedfordshire — the same village where her future husband [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] had been raised by his grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] after 1870. Her parents John and Lucy Geary were also in Maulden that year; her father died there and is buried at St Mary the Virgin, Maulden, in February 1895. This is almost certainly where Eliza and Frederick met. They married in Ampthill in 1892 — the nearest town.

**Marriage and family.** She married [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick William Tall Bunker]] in 1892 in Ampthill. She was 33; he was 23. They had two children: [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Rebecca Lucy Bunker]] (1896) and [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Herbert Bunker]] (1898), both born in Kettering, Northamptonshire. The children's names carry family memory: "Rebecca" for Frederick's mother [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]], "Lucy" for Eliza's own mother Lucy Morris.

**Residence history:** 1861 Haynes · 1871 Haynes · 1881 Luton · 1891 Maulden · 1901 Ilford, Essex · 1911 105 Hartley Road, Luton · **1921 Southend-on-Sea** · 1939 9 Oakley Road, Luton.

Both her parents died in 1895 — the year before Beulah was born (1896). The family moved to Kettering around this time and later to Ilford (1901), before settling in Luton (by 1911).

**1921 — Southend-on-Sea.** The 1921 census places Eliza at **12 Gordon Road, Southend-on-Sea, Essex** — on a seaside holiday with her sister [[lydia-rose-geary-1868|Lydia Rose Geary]] and Lydia's son **Jasper Fred Geary** (born Luton 1902, railway fireman). Lydia describes herself as "widowed" on the 1921 census, but this was a social fiction: she was never married, and Jasper was illegitimate. Eliza is recorded as "Married." Frederick meanwhile is head of household at 50 Hampton Road, Luton, with Beulah and Harry Thody. The census is a snapshot of a single night in June 1921 — Eliza's presence in Southend need not represent a permanent separation, though it is clear she was not with Frederick on that date. She was back in Luton by 1939 living at 9 Oakley Road with Beulah and Harry. Whether the marriage had effectively broken down by 1921 or simply drifted apart over subsequent years is not established from the available sources.

Eliza returned to Luton at some point after 1921. By 1939 she was living at 9 Oakley Road with Beulah and Harry Thody, and she died there on 19 January 1942, aged 82.

**Why Luton?** The move to Luton before 1911 almost certainly reflects Eliza's gravitational pull toward her own family rather than Frederick's work. Two of her siblings were already established in the city: [[elizabeth-geary-1858|Elizabeth Geary]] had been there since at least 1881, working first in domestic service then independently as a straw bonnet sewer; [[albert-geary-1866|Albert Geary]] had moved to Luton by 1901. When Frederick and Eliza settled at 105 Hartley Road before the 1911 census, she was moving toward people she knew and trusted — into sibling networks, not away from them. Given her reported "difficult" personality and the ten-year age gap with Frederick, this has the texture of a woman who got what she wanted: a return to her own ground.

**105 Hartley Road.** The 1911 household was at 105 Hartley Road, Luton — a large, double-fronted house, materially above the typical working-class Luton terrace. This requires explanation. The most parsimonious one is Frederick's trade: the Edwardian building boom was at its peak in 1904–1911, and a skilled bricklayer and builder would have had both the income and the trade connections to secure better housing. Their household also included a paying boarder (Dora Emily Bunker), which would have helped offset a higher rent. Inheritance from Eliza's parents is possible but weak: John Geary was a woodman, almost certainly a tenant rather than a property owner, and whatever modest estate he left in 1895 would have been split among six surviving adult children. There is no documented inheritance event. What can be said is that 105 Hartley Road represents the high-water mark of their household's prosperity.

**The downward shift.** By August 1920 — Beulah's marriage certificate gives 50 Hampton Road, Luton as her address — the family had moved to a smaller, simpler terrace. By 1921 Frederick was there alone (with Beulah and Harry on census night), working for the Kentish Town Building Scheme in London. Building work had been devastated during the war years and did not recover quickly. The children had left. The large house at 105 Hartley Road was no longer needed or affordable. The contrast between the two addresses is striking and consistent with a family whose financial position had contracted significantly since 1911.

**Note on the age gap.** Eliza was ten years older than Frederick. She was also from a settled Bedfordshire woodman's family with generations of roots in the Haynes–Maulden–Ampthill cluster — the same cluster where Frederick's own maternal line (the Talls) was rooted. Their worlds were geographically very close; the Maulden connection in 1891 appears to have been the moment they intersected.

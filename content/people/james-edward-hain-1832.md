James Edward Hain (8 July 1832 – 12 September 1911) was the farmer at Dell Farm, Whipsnade. He is buried at St Mary Magdalene church, Whipsnade — the same church that served as the parish register for the Cook, Birchmore, Matthews, and Bransom families.

His wife's name is not yet established; she had predeceased him by 1911, when the census records him as "widowed, private means" in his son's household. He had at least two children: his son [[james-hain-1877|James Hain]] (1877–1932), who succeeded him as farmer at Dell Farm, and a daughter Margaret D Hain (dates unknown).

The 1911 census records James Edward in the Dell Farm household alongside his son James (33, Farmer), his daughter Margaret D Hain, and his daughter-in-law's sister [[minnie-rose-bransom-1884|Minnie Rose Bransom]] (27, Housekeeper). His presence on the census was brief — he died later that same year.

Dell Farm was one of two farms reached by roads across the Whipsnade village green, as described in the 1912 Victoria County History. It was part of the Pedley Settled Estates, auctioned in November 1915 after his death. The Hain family had connections to the Pimlico/Grosvenor area of London — James Edward's origins and how the family came to be at Whipsnade have not yet been established.

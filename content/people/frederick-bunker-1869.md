Frederick William Tall Bunker was born on **15 November 1869** in the Ampthill registration district, Bedfordshire — the birth was announced in the *Bedfordshire Mercury* on 29 November 1869, fourteen days after the event. The GRO district is Ampthill; the actual village is unconfirmed. Maulden, where his maternal grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] lived and where his mother [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] was raised, lies within the Ampthill district and is the most plausible birth location, the only child of [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] (1845–1918) and [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] (1844–1870). His mother died in July 1870 when he was less than a year old; his maternal grandfather [[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]] died nine days after Rebecca. Frederick was left in the care of his maternal grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] in Maulden, Bedfordshire. His middle name "Tall" honoured his mother's family.

**Upbringing in Maulden.** Frederick grew up in Maulden with Sarah Ward and the wider Tall family. It was through this family connection that he learned his trade: the Tall family were in the building trade, and Frederick became a bricklayer and builder. By 1881 he was with his father Caleb in Matlock, Derbyshire, and by 1891 he had moved to Kettering, Northamptonshire.

**Residence history:** 1871 Maulden (with grandmother Sarah Ward) · 1881 Matlock, Derbyshire (with father Caleb) · 1891 Kettering, Northamptonshire · 1901 Ilford, Essex · 1911 105 Hartley Road, [[luton|Luton]] · 1921 50 Hampton Road, Luton.

The 1921 census shows Frederick working for the **Kentish Town Building Scheme**, with his place of work at **Prince of Wales Road, Kentish Town**, north London. He was living at 50 Hampton Road, Luton but working in London — likely commuting or working away during the week. By this date Eliza was no longer living with him (she was in Southend-on-Sea; see [[eliza-geary-1859]]).

The Kettering years (1891 through at least 1898) explain why both children were born there: [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah]] in 1896 and [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland]] in 1898. The family had not yet settled in Luton. Notably, his first cousin **[[edward-ernest-bass-1864|Edward Ernest Bass]]** was also in Kettering during this period — present in the 1901 census after Frederick had moved on to Ilford. Edward's mother [[mary-ward-tall-1827|Mary Ward Tall]] and Frederick's mother [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] were sisters, making the two men first cousins. The Kettering connection between them was almost certainly deliberate.

Frederick married Eliza Geary (1859–1942) in 1892, forming the [[bunker-geary-1892|Bunker-Geary family]]. Eliza was ten years his senior. They had two children: [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah]] (1896) and [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland]] (1898).

Frederick worked as a bricklayer and builder in [[luton|Luton]]. Later in life he and Eliza separated, living at different addresses in Luton. Frederick died on 23 December 1932, aged 63, from a **tetanus infection** — an occupational death consistent with building-site injury in an era before civilian tetanus vaccination. Four years before Roland changed the family name from Bunker to Bowers.

**Character.** A single anecdote survives that illuminates Frederick directly. While living in Luton — probably in the early 1930s — he encountered a homeless man who had no shoes. Frederick gave him his own work boots. As a bricklayer, boots were not optional equipment; they were tools of the trade. Eliza was furious. The act is consistent with Wesleyan nonconformist conviction, in which giving to the destitute was a moral obligation rather than a matter of surplus.

*Source: family oral history, from Hazel Newham (granddaughter of Harry Thody and Beulah Bunker), transmitted via her mother Marguerite, who was living with her family in Luton at the time.*

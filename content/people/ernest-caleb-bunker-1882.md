Ernest Caleb Bunker was born in 1882 in Derbyshire (registered Derby), the sixth and youngest child of [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] and [[margaret-barnsley-1850|Margaret Barnsley]]. He died in April 1967 in Watford, Hertfordshire, aged ~85 — the longest-lived of all Caleb's children across both marriages.

His given name Caleb — his father's name — marks him as the youngest son named for the patriarch, a practice consistent with the family's naming traditions.

**Bristol years.** By 1913 Ernest was living in Bristol, where he married **[[helena-annie-melrose-1883|Helena Annie Melrose]]** (1883–1960) on 1 September 1913 at the Wesleyan Chapel, Gloucester Road, Horfield, Bristol. Their two sons — [[kenneth-ernest-bunker-1917|Kenneth Ernest]] (1917–1985) and [[herbert-arthur-bunker-1922|Herbert Arthur]] (1922–2007) — were born there. Herbert Arthur's given name honours his uncle [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker]], who had died in 1917, the same year Kenneth was born. See [[bunker-melrose-1913]].

**WWI service.** Ernest served 1914–1920 in France, Belgium, and Germany, earning the British War Medal and Victory Medal. His son Kenneth was born in 1917 during his father's wartime absence. Ernest's service extended two years beyond the Armistice into the occupation period.

**Contact with Roland's family.** [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Bunker]] maintained contact with Ernest. Roland's son [[alan-geoffrey-bowers-1928|Alan Bowers]] (born 1928) remembers visiting uncle Ernest and his two sons Kenneth and Herbert Arthur in Bristol in the 1930s, when the boys would have been teenagers and primary-school age respectively.

**Later life.** Ernest moved from Bristol to Watford, Hertfordshire, at some point after the 1930s. His wife Helena died in 1960; Ernest survived her by seven years, dying in Watford in April 1967.

Ann Tabram (also spelled Tabraham) was born on 3 January 1829 in Fen Stanton, Huntingdonshire, the eldest child of [[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]] and [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary Eusden]] in the [[tabraham-eusden-1828|Tabraham-Eusden family]]. Her mother died around 1841 when Ann was twelve, leaving her the eldest of four children in a widower's household.

By 1841 she was living in Elsworth, Cambridgeshire; by 1851 she had moved to Swavesey. She married [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] on 3 February 1850 in Cambridgeshire, forming the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]]. Together they had seven children. Her sixth child, born 1865, she named Alfred **Eusden** Norman — a deliberate memorial to her mother's maiden name, preserved 24 years after Mary Eusden's death.

Ann died on 11 July 1897 in Swavesey, aged 68. She predeceased her husband Thomas by eight years — he lived on until 1905, moving to Alconbury Weston after her death. Her son [[charles-norman-1859|Charles]], who had moved to London, died ten years after her in 1907; [[robert-henry-norman-1868|Robert Henry]] lived until 1954 and [[mary-ann-norman-1861|Mary Ann]] until 1951.

### Note on the surname

The family name appears in records as Tabraham, Tabram, and Tabrahem — all phonetic variants of the same name. FamilySearch consistently uses "Tabram" for Ann; her father Richard is "Tabraham" in the christening record but "Tabram" as an alternate name.

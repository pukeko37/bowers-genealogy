Elizabeth Watts was born in 1810 in Cranfield, Bedfordshire — the second child of [[john-watts-1790|John Watts]] (1790–1814) and [[sarah-capel-1791|Sarah Capel]] (1791–1851+). Her father died in 1814 when she was only four years old. Her mother Sarah subsequently remarried twice and raised the Watts children in the Millbrook area.

Elizabeth married [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] on 22 November 1830 at Millbrook, Bedfordshire, forming the [[bunker-watts-1830|Bunker-Watts family]]. They had nine children, all born in Millbrook. James died in 1852 leaving Elizabeth a widow with children ranging from infant Comfort (b. 1848) to adult John Watts Bunker (b. 1831). A note in George Bunker's record states the family moved from Millbrook to Bedford after James's death. Elizabeth survived as a widow until 1868, dying in Bedford.

She appears in census records at Millbrook in 1841 and 1851. The 1861 census places her at **Dane Street, St Paul's parish, Bedford** — confirmed as the household head, widowed, aged 51, with six of her nine children still living under her roof. Her recorded occupation was **lace maker**. Bedford was a major centre of the pillow lace industry, and this is the first documentary record of Elizabeth's occupation. She was raising Comfort Ann (12, a scholar) and Caleb (15, a shoe maker) alongside older children who were already working. See [[caleb-bunker-1861-census]].

Through Elizabeth, [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] descends from the Watts and Capel families of Cranfield, and beyond them the Knights and Gurneys of Cranfield — all deeply rooted in the same central Bedfordshire cluster.

Ruth Cooke was born in 1820 in Kensworth, Hertfordshire — though her christening date of 10 August 1819 suggests she may have been born in late 1819, with 1820 as an approximation. She was the fifth of seven children of [[daniel-cook-1790|Daniel Cook]] (abt 1790–1857) and [[mary-birchmore-1785|Mary Birchmore]] (1785–?), married 12 October 1810. Her siblings were Sarah, James, George, [[david-cook-1816|David]], Jesse, and Lois. See [[cook-birchmore-1810]]. Her mother's maiden name was **Birchmore** — Ruth's Birchmore ancestry may connect to the same Whipsnade Birchmore family from which GBP Sr derived his name, potentially making them second cousins before their marriage.

Before marriage, Ruth had three illegitimate children: [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook]] (1847, Whipsnade), [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] (1848, born Market Street, Hertfordshire — Ruth was briefly living there — christened Whipsnade 1850), and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856, Whipsnade). Their fathers are unknown. All three lived at By Whipsnade Green. Charles drops out of records after 1861; Mary remained in Whipsnade until after 1911 and died in 1937 (death registered in the Luton district); Lucy's story is the most significant for the later family narrative.

Ruth married [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] on 26 December 1867 at [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]] — nine months after the death of his second wife Eliza Batchelor. No children were born from this marriage, but Lucy (then 11), Charles, and Mary came with Ruth into the household. Lucy grew up alongside George's daughter [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] — only four years her junior — as if they were sisters.

Ruth and George held the license for The Chequers pub, Whipsnade. George died in 1884; Ruth continued holding the license for another twenty years. She died on 22 May 1904 at the Chequers Inn itself, aged 84 — resident there to the end, as the probate record confirms with her address given as "The Chequers Inn Whipsnade."

She died **intestate**, leaving no will. Letters of Administration were granted on 7 October 1904 — four and a half months after her death — not to her daughters [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Bransom]] or [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Thody]], but to a **Susan Sear, widow**, whose connection to Ruth is unknown. Her estate was valued at £45. She was indexed in the probate records under the name **Ruth Birchmore**, having taken George's surname on their marriage in 1867.

See [[birchmore-plummer-cooke-1867]] for the full family story. See [[ruth-cooke-probate-1904]] for the probate record.

### Significance

Ruth's step-daughter [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] became the crucial link connecting the Norman and Bunker families two generations later: Lucy's son Harry Thody was friends with Roland Bunker, and introduced Roland to Ivy Norman.

### Open Questions

- **Who is Susan Sear?** The administrator of Ruth's estate was Susan Sear, widow — not a name previously encountered in the research. She may be a Whipsnade neighbour, a pub creditor, or an unidentified family connection. If Ruth's son [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook (1847)]] had predeceased her (he disappears from records after 1861), this would have left only Mary and Lucy as obvious administrators — and their non-appearance makes Susan Sear's role more puzzling, not less.
- The Birchmore/Cook connection through Ruth's mother Mary Birchmore is intriguing — it may be coincidence, or may indicate a pre-existing family network in the Hertfordshire/Bedfordshire area.

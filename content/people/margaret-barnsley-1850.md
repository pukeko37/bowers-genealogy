Margaret Barnsley was born **14 April 1850** in Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire, and christened 12 May 1850 at the Church of St Michael and All Angels, Ashton-under-Lyne. She was the daughter of [[thomas-barnsley-1817|Thomas Barnsley]] and **Eliza** (born Bradwell, Derbyshire — the same birthplace as Thomas, suggesting they were from the same Derbyshire village before moving to Lancashire). Her siblings were Joseph Barnsley (born 1847, Ashton) and Elizabeth Barnsley (born ~1841, Ashton).

The Barnsley family moved from Lancashire back toward Derbyshire over the following decades. By 1871 Thomas (now a stationer) and Eliza had settled at **Matlock, Derbyshire**, with Margaret (20) living with them and working as a **shop assistant**. Caleb Bunker arrived in Matlock that same year as a newly widowed lodger in the same enumeration district. They were almost certainly in the same small community before marrying in October 1871.

She married [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] in October 1871 at Matlock, Derbyshire. They had six children: [[annie-elizabeth-bunker-1872|Annie]] (1872–1951), [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert]] (1875–1917), [[maggie-eliza-bunker-1876|Maggie]] (1876–1915), [[agnes-maud-bunker-1878|Agnes]] (1878–1919), [[may-gertrude-bunker-1880|May]] (1880–1960), and [[ernest-caleb-bunker-1882|Ernest]] (1882–1967). They lived together in the Matlock/Derbyshire area through 1891, then settled in Derby. By 1901 and through 1911 they were at **60 High Street, Derby** — a combined shop and lodging house, with several boarders in residence. Her daughter **Annie Elizabeth** had married by 1911 (surname Borrington) and was still living with them. See [[bunker-barnsley-1871]].

After Caleb died in May 1918, Margaret returned to her home county: the 1921 records place her at **Newton, Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire**, with William and Emma (likely relations). She died on **10 June 1922 in Staffordshire** (registered volume 6B, page 376) — in the Burton upon Trent area, the same county where Caleb had died. She was buried at **Nottingham Road Cemetery, Chaddesden, Derby** — the same cemetery as Caleb — despite dying four years after him and apparently having relocated to Lancashire.

In January 1894 Margaret gave evidence at Derby Borough Court: she had been running the family shop at **Traffic Street, Derby** when two boys stole 2s. 11½d. from the till along with some toys. She spotted the missing money, tracked the boys, and went to the detective. The case was reported in the *Derby Telegraph* on 25 January 1894; the boys received six strokes of the birch rod. This confirms she was an active presence in Caleb's retail business, not merely a householder. See [[derby-telegraph-1894-01-25-bunker-theft]].

**Residence history:**
- 1850–1861: Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire
- 1871–1881: Matlock, Derbyshire
- 1891: Derbyshire (St Peter's Derby area)
- c.1894: Traffic Street, Derby
- 1901–1911: 60 High Street, Derby
- 1921: Newton, Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire
- Died 1922, Staffordshire; buried Chaddesden, Derby

Thomas Euseden was born in 1764 in Huntingdonshire. His wife was [[mary-eusden-1771|Mary]] (1771–1825), identified from the Fenstanton burial register as the only unaccounted-for Eusden death in the parish across the whole period 1797–1850; her maiden name is unknown. They married no later than ~1796. He outlived her by seventeen years, dying in November 1842. Their five children, in birth order: [[william-eusden|William]] (1797–1843), [[thomas-eusden-1799|Thomas]] (1799–1835), [[sarah-eusden-1804|Sarah]] (1804–1833), [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary]] (1807–1841), and [[henry-eusden|Henry]] (1809–1839).

His exact birth place is uncertain. FamilySearch records it as Fenstanton, but the Huntingdonshire marriages index places the Eusden family at **Ellington** in the 1760s–1770s, one generation earlier. The best candidate for Thomas's parents is "Rt [Robert?] Eusden" who married Eliz Bellamy at Ellington in 1762, two years before Thomas's birth. Three other Eusdens married at Ellington between 1764 and 1772 — likely Thomas's aunts and uncles. Thomas may have been born at Ellington and moved to the Fenstanton area as an adult.

By the 1820s, all four of his known children were marrying at Fenstanton, establishing the family there. His daughter Mary married Richard Tabraham at Fenstanton on 3 August 1828, forming the [[tabraham-eusden-1828|Tabraham-Eusden family]].

The christening of his daughter Mary at Ely in January 1808 (rather than in Huntingdonshire) suggests the family may have been temporarily resident in Ely around 1807–1808, or that Thomas's wife had Cambridgeshire roots.

In the **1841 census**, Thomas was recorded at Bell Lane, Fenstanton (age given as 70, with rounding typical of the period) — in the household of his son-in-law [[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]]. His daughter Mary had died around this same time, and Thomas appears to have been living with the family. He was buried at Fenstanton on **14 November 1842**, aged 78, approximately seventeen months after the census.

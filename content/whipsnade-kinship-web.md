The families who converged at and around Whipsnade — the Birchmores, Cooks, Matthewses, Bramsons, Normans, Thodys, and Bunkers — are not merely connected by geography. Through three generations, a web of marriages, step-siblinghoods, and sibling pairs tying themselves to each other bound these families into something much tighter than a neighbourhood. This page maps that web, naming each relationship precisely.

---

## The Core Node: George Birchmore Plummer

[[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] (1813–1884), known as GBP Sr, is the person through whom most of the threads pass. He was an agricultural labourer and later publican at the Chequers Inn, Whipsnade. His three marriages — each to a woman connected to the others — created the initial knot.

- **Marriage 1 (1835):** [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] (1815–1858), daughter of Thomas Matthews and Mary Wilson of Whipsnade. One son: [[george-birchmore-plummer-jr-1850|GBP Jr]].
- **Marriage 2 (1859):** [[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]] (1829–1867), also of Whipsnade. Three children including [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] (1860–1940) and [[elizabeth-birchmore-1862|Elizabeth Birchmore]] (1862).
- **Marriage 3 (1867):** [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] (1820–1904), also of Whipsnade. No children from this marriage, but Ruth brought three illegitimate children: [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook]] (1847), [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] (1848), and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856).

When GBP Sr died in 1884, Ruth continued running the Chequers until her own death in 1904.

---

## Layer 1: The Matthews–Cook Cross

The reason GBP Sr's first and third marriages connect to the same family network is that the Matthews and Cook families were already joined before he came along — through a marriage that ran perpendicular to both of his.

- [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] had a younger sister: **[[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte Matthews]]** (1823–1910).
- [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] had an older brother: **[[david-cook-1816|David Cook]]** (1816–1900).
- **Charlotte Matthews married David Cook in 1856 at Whipsnade.**

This single marriage is the keystone of the whole structure. Once it happened:

| Person | Relationship to Anne Matthews | Relationship to Ruth Cooke |
|--------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Charlotte Matthews | Sister | Sister-in-law (wife of Ruth's brother David) |
| David Cook | Brother-in-law (husband of Charlotte) | Brother |

GBP Sr therefore married his first wife's sister's husband's sister — or equivalently, his third wife's brother's wife's sister. Charlotte Matthews is the hinge: she is simultaneously Anne's sister and David's wife.

Both families lived **By Whipsnade Green**, the same hamlet. They were neighbours as well as in-laws.

---

## Layer 2: Ruth's Pre-Marriage Children Enter the Web

Ruth Cooke had three children before marrying GBP Sr in 1867. No father is named on any of their records. These were:

1. **[[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook]]** (1847–?) — lost to record after 1861
2. **[[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]]** (1848–1937) — lifelong Whipsnade; married Edwin Bransom 1871
3. **[[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]]** (1856–1930) — born Whipsnade; married William Thody 1894; mother of Harry Thody

When GBP Sr married Ruth in 1867, these three became step-children of GBP Sr — step-siblings of GBP Sr's children by Eliza Batchelor, chiefly [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] (born 1860). **Lucy and Ellen were step-sisters**, only four years apart. They were raised together as sisters, not step-sisters. This bond is the crucial link that ultimately joins the Norman and Bunker families two generations later.

---

## Layer 3: The Bransom Siblings Both Marry into the Cook Family

The Bransom family of Whipsnade produced two siblings who each independently married a Cook — but into different branches of that family:

**[[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]]** (1843–1924) married **[[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook]]** (1843–1908) in 1868. Charles Cook (1843) was the son of David Cook from his first marriage to Elizabeth Frost — making him David's son, Ruth's nephew, and Charlotte Matthews's stepson. Charles held the Chequers Inn licence from 1904 until his death in 1908.

**[[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]]** (1850–1928) — Lois's younger brother — married **[[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]]** (1848–1937) in 1871. Mary was Ruth Cooke's middle illegitimate daughter.

So:
- **Lois Brandsom** became Lois Cook by marriage; her husband was Ruth's nephew.
- **Edwin Bransom** married Ruth's daughter.
- The two Bransom siblings tied themselves to the Cook family in two different directions simultaneously.

Their daughter [[florence-bransom-1873|Florence Bransom]] (1873–1963) — Edwin and Mary's child — was a witness at Lucy Cook's wedding to William Thody in 1894. The other witness was [[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cook]] (1869), son of Lois Brandsom and Charles Cook (1843). Two cousins — one from each Bransom–Cook marriage — stood together as witnesses.

---

## Layer 4: The Chequers Inn Succession

The Chequers Inn, Whipsnade's only public house, passed through a tight chain of related hands:

1. **Ruth Cooke and GBP Sr** held the licence from their 1867 marriage.
2. GBP Sr died 1884; Ruth continued alone until her death in 1904.
3. **Charles Cook (1843–1908)** — Ruth's nephew (David's son), Lois Brandsom's husband — took the licence from 1904 until his death in January 1908.
4. **Lois Cook (née Brandsom)** — Charles's widow — held the licence briefly before it passed out of the family network.

The Chequers was in the hands of connected kin for over forty years.

## Layer 5: Ellen Birchmore Marries Charles Norman

[[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] (1860–1940) was GBP Sr's daughter by his second wife Eliza Batchelor. She grew up in Whipsnade, where she worked as a cook — at Hall Farm and later at the Chequers. [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] (1859–1907) was a carter with the London haulage firm Carter Paterson & Co., who came to Whipsnade to collect hay from Hall Farm. They met there and married in Kensington in 1883.

They had nine children, born over nearly twenty years, and the family moved through north London — Islington, Hackney, then the City of London near Smithfield, where Charles was foreman at the Carter Paterson stables at Hosier Lane. Charles died in 1907 aged 48.

The nine Norman children are the next generation of the web:

1. **[[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel Annie Norman]]** (1883–1969)
2. **[[nellie-norman-1885|Nellie Norman]]** (1885–1960)
3. **[[thomas-george-norman-1886|Thomas George Norman]]** (1886–1949)
4. **[[elizabeth-ann-norman-1888|Elizabeth Ann Norman]]** (1888–1953)
5. **[[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]]** (1890–1980)
6. **[[lillian-frances-norman-1893|Lillian Frances Norman]]** (1893–1968)
7. **[[charles-norman-jr-1895|Charles Norman Jr]]** (1895–1955)
8. **[[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Helen Norman]]** (1899–1994)
9. **[[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah Winifred Norman]]** (1902–1996)

## Layer 6: The Mason Brothers and the Norman Sisters

At Hosier Lane, Smithfield, the Mason family lived at number 27, running a milk shop. The Normans lived nearby at the stables. [[john-james-mason-1878|John James Mason]] (Jack, 1878–1962) was a son of Welsh dairy immigrants Richard and Catherine Mason. His brother [[isaac-mason-1882|Isaac Mason]] (Ike, 1882–1955) was likewise in the milk and later newsagent trades.

Two brothers married two sisters:

- **John James Mason married [[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel Annie Norman]] in 1912.** They ran a newsagent and stationer's at 150 Acre Lane, Clapham. [[nellie-norman-1885|Nellie Norman]] lived with them for much of her life.
- **Isaac Mason married [[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]] in 1918.** They settled in Balham. [[elizabeth-birchmore-1862|Elizabeth Birchmore]] — Ellen's other daughter, GBP Sr's granddaughter — lived with them.

The Mason brothers were not from the Dunstable/Whipsnade area; they were London Welsh. But by both marrying Norman sisters, they became part of the wider family circle. Isaac died in 1955 leaving a substantial estate; John died in 1962. Both brothers lived into their eighties, almost next-door to each other at Daysbrook Road, Streatham.

## Layer 7: The Thody–Bunker–Norman Triangle

This is where the Whipsnade threads rejoin across two generations.

[[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856–1930) — Ruth's youngest daughter, Ellen Birchmore's step-sister — married **[[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]]** (1850–1911) in 1894 at Whipsnade. William was a police constable and widower with four children from his first marriage. Their only child together was **[[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]]** (1894–1986), born Markyate, Hertfordshire.

William died in 1911. Lucy moved to Rose Cottage, Studham, where she became a beloved figure to the Norman family. Ivy and Norah Norman visited often. Lucy was like a second mother to Ivy — a bond that traced directly back to Lucy's mother Ruth having married Ellen Birchmore's father GBP Sr in 1867.

Harry Thody returned from WWI and forged friendships with both the Norman family (through Lucy's connection to Ellen) and with [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Herbert Bunker]] (1898–1978). Roland's sister was **[[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Rebecca Lucy Bunker]]** (1896–1948).

- **Harry Thody married Beulah Bunker on 2 August 1920** at Little Gaddesden, Hertfordshire. The witnesses were Roland Bunker (Beulah's brother) and Norah Winifred Norman (Ivy's youngest sister).
- **Roland Bunker married Ivy Norman in June 1921** at DeBeauvoir Town. They had honeymooned at Rose Cottage, Studham — Lucy Cook's home — after the wedding.

After Roland left the Navy in 1930, Harry and Roland went into business together as **Thody Bros**, a furniture business in Luton — named "Bros" though they were brothers-in-law, not brothers.

## The Complete Circuit

The full loop of the web closes like this:

1. **GBP Sr** married **Anne Matthews** (1835)
2. Anne's sister **Charlotte Matthews** married **David Cook** — Ruth Cooke's brother
3. GBP Sr married **Ruth Cooke** (1867) — David Cook's sister, Charlotte's sister-in-law
4. Ruth's daughter **Lucy Cook** was raised as step-sister to GBP Sr's daughter **Ellen Birchmore**
5. **Ellen Birchmore** married **Charles Norman** (1883) → nine children including Ivy Norman
6. Lucy Cook married **William Thody** (1894) → son **Harry Thody**
7. **Harry Thody** married **Beulah Bunker** (1920) — sister of Roland Bunker
8. **Roland Bunker** married **Ivy Norman** (1921) — daughter of Ellen Birchmore

Harry Thody's mother (Lucy) was the step-sister of his wife's mother-in-law (Ellen). The step-sisterhood created by GBP Sr's 1867 marriage to Ruth is the hinge on which the whole circuit turns.

## The Whipsnade Birchmore Background

One further thread runs beneath all the others, unconfirmed but plausible. **[[mary-birchmore-1785|Mary Birchmore]]** (1785–?), Ruth Cooke's mother, came from the Whipsnade Birchmore family — daughter of William Birchmore (1751) and Mary Simonds. GBP Sr's father was **George Birchmore (1786)**, himself the illegitimate son of **George Birchmore (1762)** of Flamstead (Row End Farm). If the Flamstead Birchmores and the Whipsnade Birchmores share common ancestry — which is possible given the shared surname in a small geographic area — then GBP Sr and Ruth Cooke were second cousins through the Birchmore line before they married. This has not been confirmed by records, but the pattern of tight community marriage makes it unsurprising. See [[cook-birchmore-1810]] and [[birchmore-plummer-flamstead]] for the relevant family backgrounds.

## Parents

[[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]] (1836–1917) and [[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]] (abt 1838–unknown) married on **8 April 1858** at St Paul's Church, Bedford, Bedfordshire. George was 22; Ann was approximately 20. They were married for at least 53 years, as confirmed by the 1911 census.

Ann was from Westoning, Bedfordshire — a Bedfordshire woman, not a Derbyshire one. The move to Derbyshire in 1872–1875 was therefore a migration of a married couple with existing children, not a single man following his siblings.

## Children

Five children confirmed from census records:

1. **Sarah Ann Bunker** (19 Oct 1860–?) — born Bedford; christened 9 December 1860 at a **Wesleyan Methodist** chapel, Bedford Circuit. Present in 1871 (age 10) and 1881 (age 20, Bedford-born). Not in 1891 household — had left home by then.
2. **Harriett E Bunker** (abt 1865–?) — born Bedford. Age 6 in 1871, 16 in 1881. Not in later censuses.
3. **Alice Bunker** (abt 1872–?) — born Bedford. Age 9 in 1881, 19 in 1891. Last recorded 1891.
4. **Albert Bunker** (abt 1875–1924) — born **New Tupton, Derbyshire**. Age 6 in 1881, 16 in 1891, 26 in 1901 (still in household). His birthplace in New Tupton pinpoints the family's arrival in Derbyshire to ~1875. Spent the rest of his life in Derby; died there **11 October 1924**.
5. **Amy Bunker** (abt 1878–?) — born **Derby, Derbyshire**. Age 3 in 1881, 33 in 1911. Unmarried through 1911 census; married **Lewis Allsop** on **1 July 1911** in Derby.

## Family Life

The family's Bedford years (1858–1875) saw George working up from labourer to **Storekeeper** — a clerical or supervisory role recorded in the 1871 census. The four older children were born in Bedford; the family appears settled and stable. Sarah Ann's Wesleyan Methodist christening confirms Nonconformist religious practice.

The move to Derbyshire, sometime between ~1872 and ~1875, reversed George's occupational trajectory. In Derbyshire he was a **General Labourer** from 1881 through to his retirement. Whatever skills or relationships gave him the Storekeeper role in Bedford did not carry across.

The family's Derbyshire route was: New Tupton first (~1875, Albert born there), then rapidly to Derby — Amy was born in Derby ~1878, and the 1881 census places the whole family in **St Peter's parish, Derby**. George's New Tupton stay lasted roughly two to three years at most. Derby was the family's settled home from ~1877 onwards.

**The New Tupton connection.** Albert Bunker was born in New Tupton ~1875 — the same village where Caleb Bunker's son [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker]] was also born (~1875). Both George and Caleb had children born in New Tupton in the same year. This is strong evidence that the Bunker siblings were living in close proximity during this period, and that the Derbyshire cluster had a shared nucleus at New Tupton before the families dispersed to different parts of the region.

By 1901 the older children had left the household. By 1911 only Amy remained; she married shortly after the census. George retired ("Finished Work"). He and Ann spent their last years in Derby, having been married over half a century.

George died in Derby in January 1917, with an obituary in the Derby press. Ann's fate after 1911 is unknown.

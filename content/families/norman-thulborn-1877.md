## Parents

[[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] (1856–1925), third child of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] in the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]], married [[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]] (1856–1922) on **30 October 1877** in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire. Both were 21 years old. Emily came from Oakington, Cambridgeshire — about 10 miles from Swavesey.

## Children

Nine children born 1878–1893:

1. **Fred Norman** (1878–1955) — born Cambridgeshire (Swavesey)
2. **Charles Norman** (1879–Deceased) — born Cambridgeshire (Swavesey)
3. **Kate Norman** (1881–1956) — born Huntingdonshire; family had already moved
4. **Alfred Norman** (1884–1949) — born Alconbury Weston, Huntingdonshire
5. **Florence Norman** (1886–Deceased) — born Huntingdonshire
6. **Albert Newman Norman** (1888–1970) — born Alconbury Weston; known as "Bertie" in 1901; later kept the Crown Beerhouse 1922–1931
7. **Emily Norman** (1889–Deceased) — born Huntingdonshire
8. **Thomas Norman** (1891–1981) — born Alconbury, Huntingdonshire; longest-lived documented; died aged ~90
9. **Hannah Norman** (1893–Deceased) — born Alconbury Hill, Huntingdonshire; last child at home in 1911

## Family Life

Emily came from a family of agricultural labourers at Oakington, Cambridgeshire — the second child of [[newman-thulborn-1821|Newman Thulborn]] and [[hannah-how-1824|Hannah How]]. By 1871 she was in Willingham (6 miles from Oakington), probably in domestic service. She married Tom six years later. See [[thulborn-how-oakington]] for Emily's family background.

Tom and Emily began married life in Swavesey with the rest of the Norman family. Their first two children (Fred, 1878; Charles, 1879) were born in Cambridgeshire. By the time Kate was born in 1881 the family was already in Huntingdonshire — they moved to **Alconbury Weston** around 1880. By 1891 Tom was working there as **Farm Bailiff** — a supervisory position managing a farm on behalf of a landowner, a significant step above ordinary labourer. The 1891 census shows the whole family: seven children at home, from Fred (12) down to Emily (1), with Kate, Alfred, Florence, Albert, and Emily all born in Huntingdonshire.

By 1901 Tom is recorded back as "Ordinary Agr Labourer" at Alconbury Weston — perhaps the farm changed hands or he left the bailiff role. The household was smaller: Bertie (Albert, 12), Emily (11), Thomas (9), and Hannah (7) remained at home; the five older children had dispersed. Notably, Tom's elderly father **Thomas Norman (age 74)** is living in the same household in 1901, having followed his son from Swavesey after Ann's death in 1897. Emily's recorded occupation that year is "HOUSEKEEPER" — she was running the household for both the remaining children and the elderly grandfather.

By 1911 the family had moved again, to **Eaton Socon** (on the Huntingdonshire/Bedfordshire border). Tom's occupation in 1911 is remarkable: **"Farm Labourer and Beerhouse Keeper"** — he was running a licensed premises alongside farm work. A beerhouse (created by the Beerhouse Act 1830) was the lower tier of licensed premises: cheaper to licence than a full public house, subject to annual magistrate renewal but not requiring the full public house licence. Running a beerhouse from home alongside another occupation was a common strategy for agricultural workers looking to supplement wages. Only daughter Hannah (17) remained at home; the older children had presumably dispersed.

By 1921 Tom, Emily, and their son **Thomas (b. 1891)** are recorded together at Eaton Socon in the 1921 census — in the locality of the Crown Inn — confirming the family's continued presence there. Hannah had left; Thomas had returned or remained. Emily died the following year, in 1922, still at Eaton Socon. (1921 census index entry; record image not viewed.)

The beerhouse was almost certainly the **Crown Beerhouse, Great North Road, Eaton Socon** — now the Crown Inn at 1 Great North Road, still operating today. The evidence: Tom's son **Albert Newman Norman (1888–1970)** is recorded as keeper of the Crown Beerhouse from **1922–1931** (owner Mrs Archbald); and independently, **Kelly's 1924 Bedfordshire Directory** lists him in the commercial section as "Norman Albert Newman, Crown P.H." — corroboration from a trade directory. With Tom as the 1911 keeper and Albert holding the licence by 1922, this is a father-to-son succession at the same premises. Tom died in 1925 with Albert already running it for at least three years, suggesting a handover in Tom's final years. Kelly's designates it "P.H." (Public House) in 1924 rather than beerhouse — either the licence was upgraded between 1911 and 1924, or Kelly's used P.H. generically. See [[crown-beerhouse-eaton-socon-stneots-ccan]] and [[kelly-1924-eaton-socon-norman]].

Emily died in 1922, Tom in 1925.

## Wider Family

Tom was the most mobile of the Swavesey Norman siblings. His brother [[charles-norman-1859|Charles]] went to London; his brothers George and Richard went to Hartlepool. Tom stayed within Huntingdonshire/Cambridgeshire but moved more than any of the others who remained in the region. His nine children represent the largest documented surviving line of the Norman-Tabram family.

## Parents

**[[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]]** (born 24 June 1820, christened 17 December 1820, Whipsnade, Bedfordshire) was the eighth child of [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews]] and [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]]. He worked as an agricultural labourer in Whipsnade like his father.

**[[sarah-garner-1822|Sarah Garner]]** (christened 10 March 1822, Caddington, Hertfordshire) came from the neighbouring parish — Caddington lies at the foot of the same chalk ridge as Whipsnade, separated by only a few miles. The two families would have known each other well. They married on **9 October 1842** at **Kensworth, Hertfordshire** — Thomas Matthews's birthplace, suggesting that Daniel may have kept connections to the Kensworth community into adulthood.

## Children — English Years

All known children were born in Whipsnade before the 1853 emigration:

| Child | Born | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Eliza Matthews | Christened 9 Jul 1843, Whipsnade (LDS gives b. 9 Feb 1844) | Survived; married Taylor; died Tooele, Utah 1930 |
| Sarah Ann Matthews | b. 11 May 1844, Bedfordshire | Died 1853, Nebraska — trail death; buried Pioneer Children's Memorial, Salt Lake City |
| Ann Matthews | b. ~1845, Whipsnade | Present in 1851 census aged 6; fate untraced |
| Mary Matthews | b. ~1849, Whipsnade | Present in 1851 census aged 2; fate untraced |
| Emma Matthews | b. 29 Apr 1851, Bedfordshire | Died 1853 on trail; buried Pioneer Children's Memorial, Salt Lake City |

The 1851 census confirms three daughters in the Whipsnade household: Eliza (8), Ann (6), Mary (2). Emma was born April 1851 and may postdate the census date (30 March 1851) or was simply missed.

## LDS Conversion — 1846

Both Daniel and Sarah were baptised into the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints on **12 September 1846**, at the Bedfordshire branch of the British Mission. This was a branch-wide conversion day: Daniel's mother [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]] was baptised on 4 January 1851 at the Hensworth Branch, and his brother [[able-matthews-1813|Able]] was also in the fold by the time of emigration.

The LDS membership records for Sarah give a "marriage date" of 10 June 1847 — likely the date of their LDS ecclesiastical sealing, distinct from the civil marriage of 9 October 1842.

## Emigration — January 1853

The emigration took place in two legs. The **sea crossing** was Liverpool to New Orleans, departing **17 January 1853** and arriving **6 March 1853**, confirmed by Saints by Sea (passenger records linked to Daniel's FamilySearch ID LTWY-3J3). Daniel's brother [[william-matthews-1806|William Matthews]] sailed on the same ship. After landing at New Orleans the group travelled up the Mississippi and Missouri rivers to Keokuk, Iowa.

The **Cyrus H. Wheelock Company** — approximately 400 people in 52 wagons — departed Keokuk overland on **13 June 1853**, crossed the Missouri River on 11 July 1853, and arrived in the Salt Lake Valley **6–16 October 1853**. The family travelled in the division under Captain Kendall.

The Saints by Sea passenger record lists **six** family members on the ship — one more than the overland company report:

| Passenger | Age on ship | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Daniel Matthews | 31 | Head |
| Sarah Matthews | 30 | Wife — [[sarah-garner-1822]] |
| Eliza Matthews | 9 | Survived; married Taylor; died Tooele, Utah 1930 |
| **Ann Matthews** | **8** | Confirmed emigrant; fate after crossing unknown |
| Mary Matthews | 4 | Fate after crossing unknown |
| Emma Matthews | 1 | Died on trail, Nebraska |

The Wheelock Company overland report and the Find a Grave memorial do not list Ann — the Saints by Sea sea manifest is the fuller record.

The crossing was catastrophic. **Sarah Ann** (aged 9) and **Emma** (aged 2) died on the trail. **[[sarah-garner-1822|Sarah Garner]] herself also died circa October 1853, never reaching Salt Lake City** — confirmed by her FamilySearch record and specifically noted in the obituary of her surviving daughter Eliza Matthews Taylor (died Tooele, Utah 1930). Of the five people in the family's trail division, only **Eliza** (10) and **Mary** (4) survived the crossing. Daniel arrived in Utah a widower with two daughters, having buried his wife and two children on the plains.

## Daniel Willis Matthews and Mary Lea Groves — Second Family

After Sarah's death, Daniel married **[[mary-lea-groves-1836|Mary Lea Groves]]** (born ~1836, Missouri) around 1872. Mary Lea had previously been married to a man named Lee, and brought sons Jacob Lee and John H. Lee to the new marriage. She and Daniel had at least two children together:

- **Daniel Willis Matthews Jr.** (born 24 November 1872, Virgin, Washington County, Utah; died 21 May 1947, Cedar City, Iron County, Utah)
- **Charlotte Matthews** (born ~1877, Utah)

The extended household in the 1880 census at Virgin City, Kane [Washington], Utah also included Maryam (24) and Olive Lucy (22) — Utah-born daughters whose birth years (~1856–1858) predate the marriage to Mary Lea. These may be Daniel's daughters by Sarah Garner who survived the trail and settled in Virgin before the father's remarriage.

## Life in Virgin City, Utah

Daniel Willis Matthews settled in the **Virgin City / Grafton area of Washington County**, in the extreme south of Utah Territory. This was LDS frontier country — remote, desert, irrigated valleys carved out of canyon country. He farmed there for fifty years.

The 1900 census found him at Grafton, Washington, Utah, aged 80, still head of household. He died **15 April 1903** in Virgin, Washington County, and was buried in the **Virgin Cemetery**.

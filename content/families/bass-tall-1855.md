## Parents

[[daniel-bass-1829|Daniel Bass]] (1829–1868) and [[mary-ward-tall-1827|Mary Ward Tall]] (1827–1907) married in **October 1855 in Maulden, Bedfordshire** — the village where Mary had grown up and where her parents [[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]] and [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] still lived. Mary was 28 at marriage; Daniel 26.

The 1861 census captures the family at **Snow Hill, Maulden**: Daniel (31, labourer), Mary (33, stay maker), and infant Sarah (1). Mary was already working as a stay maker alongside her husband — a trade she would continue long after his death.

Daniel died in 1868, aged ~39, when the youngest child was about four. Mary was widowed at 41. Daniel's death came in the same year as her sister [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]]'s marriage to Caleb Bunker — two milestones in that family's year, one a loss and one a beginning.

## Children

1. [[sarah-bass-1859|Sarah Bass]] (Jul 1859 – Mar 1951) — married James Lay 1887; see [[lay-bass-1887]]
2. [[florence-bass-1862|Florence Bass]] (1862–date unknown) — domestic servant in Islington 1881; married **Sidney David Shotbolt** of Maulden, 1889 Ampthill; settled in Ampthill; 3 children
3. [[edward-ernest-bass-1864|Edward Ernest Bass]] (1864–1936) — carpenter; lived entire life near Maulden; died Flitwick 1936

## Family Life

Mary raised her three children in Maulden as a widow. The 1871 census places the family at **Duck End, Maulden**: Mary (43, widowed, staymaker, head), Sarah (11, scholar), Florence (9), and Edward (7). A decade on, in 1881, only Sarah (21, dressmaker) and Edward Ernest (17) remained at home — Florence had left for London by then, working as a domestic servant in Islington. She returned to the Ampthill area, married Sidney Shotbolt in 1889, and settled there permanently. Mary continued as stay maker and head of household, with a lodger, Richard Henderson Appleby (83, born "At Sea"), also in the dwelling.

Her mother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] — living in Maulden, also widowed after 1870 — was a close presence through these decades. The 1891 census shows Mary herself living as *daughter* in the household of an elderly widowed woman indexed as "Sarah Sall" (84) — almost certainly Sarah Ward (née Ward, married name Tall), Mary's mother, with the surname garbled in transcription. The two widows shared the village for over twenty years: Sarah Ward until her death in January 1894, Mary until she left for Lavendon, Buckinghamshire around 1901.

Mary followed her eldest daughter to Lavendon, where Sarah had settled with her husband James Lay in 1887. By 1901 Mary (73) was living as "mother-in-law" in the Lay household. She died in Lavendon in April 1907 at ~79.

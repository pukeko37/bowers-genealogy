## Parents

**Joseph Sinfield** (born ~1844, Bedford — died 1914) and **[[martha-sarah-bunker-1841|Martha Sarah Bunker]]** (1841–1901) married on **25 May 1863** at Saint Paul's Church, Bedford. Martha was 21 and Joseph was about 19 — a young marriage by the standards of the family. Both were from the Bedford area; Martha had grown up in Millbrook before moving to Bedford after her father's death in 1852.

Martha was the sixth child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]] of Millbrook. Her family of origin is [[bunker-watts-1830]].

Joseph was christened 28 January 1844 at All Saints', Kempston — a parish adjoining Bedford, though census records give his birthplace as "Bedford." His father **William Sinfield** (born ~1811, Kempston) was a labourer; his mother **Sarah** (born ~1812) came from the Eynesbury/Eaton Socon area of Huntingdonshire. The Sinfield family had been rooted in Kempston for at least two generations. He trained as a metalworker — a **Hammerman** — before joining the Luton Borough Police in the late 1870s.

## Children

Two daughters:

1. **Annie Maria Sinfield** (born ~1864, Bedford) — by 1911 married **Alfred John Hyde** (born ~1859, City of London), a **Blocker in the Straw Hats industry**, Luton. Joseph Sinfield was living with them in 1911 as widower father-in-law.
2. **Elizabeth Julia Sinfield** (born ~1867, Bedford) — married **George Linger** on 24 December 1886 in Luton. Their son **Arthur Linger** (born ~1888) appears in the Sinfield household in 1891, aged 3, suggesting the young Linger family were nearby or temporarily resident.

## Family Life

**Bedford years (1863–~1880).** The couple began married life in Bedford where they had both daughters. Martha's younger sister [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann]] joined the household as "sister-in-law" by 1871 and remained with the family through their Luton years.

**Joseph's police career.** By 1881 Joseph had joined the Luton Borough Police as a **Police Constable**, and the whole household — Joseph, Martha, and Comfort Ann — relocated to Luton. He remained a Police Constable through 1891. By the 1901 census he had retired from the force and was working as a **General Labourer** — a significant step down, likely reflecting the end of his service years and limited options thereafter.

**Martha's working life.** Martha was a straw industry worker her entire recorded life: Plaiter (1851, age 10, Millbrook), Bonnet Sewer (1861, Bedford), Straw Hat Sewer (1871, Bedford and 1901, Luton). Luton was the centre of Britain's straw hat trade, and the move there placed Martha and Comfort Ann at the heart of their industry.

**Martha's death.** Martha died on 7 July 1901 in Luton, aged 60, just three months after the 1901 census captured her still alive and working. She was registered as "Martha Sarah Sinfield" and buried in Luton on 10 July. An obituary was published in the *Luton Bedfordshire Advertiser and Luton Times* on 12 July 1901; the text has not yet been retrieved. Joseph survived her by 13 years.

## Wider Family

Martha's sister Comfort Ann lived with this family from at least 1871 to 1891, before leaving to marry Henry Ewington in 1892. The Luton connection also links this family to Caleb Bunker's son [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick]], who later settled in Luton as a bricklayer — though Frederick arrived after Martha had already been there for two decades.

The Sinfield surname carries a genealogical resonance: Martha Sarah's great-great-grandfather [[thomas-bunker-1738|Thomas Bunker]] married **Martha Sinfield** at Millbrook in 1761. Whether Joseph Sinfield descended from that Millbrook Sinfield family is an open question.

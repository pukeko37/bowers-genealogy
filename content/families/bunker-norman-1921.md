## Parents

[[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Herbert Bunker]] (known as Ron, 1898–1978) and [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Helen Norman]] (1899–1994) married in June 1921 at DeBeauvoir Town, Middlesex.

They met through the Thody connection. Ivy's aunt Lucy Thody's son Harry lived at Rose Cottage, Studham. Harry served in WW1 from 1914, and married Ron's sister Beulah in 1920. Harry, Ron, Beulah and Ivy were good friends.

## Children

1. [[norman-kenneth-bowers-1925|Norman Kenneth Bowers]] (1925–?) — born under the name Bunker; surname changed to Bowers in 1936
2. [[alan-geoffrey-bowers-1928|Alan Geoffrey Bowers]] (1928–2012) — born under the name Bunker; surname changed to Bowers in 1936

Both sons were born in [[portsmouth|Portsmouth]] while Ron was serving in the Royal Navy.

## Family Life

After their marriage in 1921, Ivy and Ron stayed at Rose Cottage, [[studham|Studham]], before moving to [[portsmouth|Portsmouth]] where Ron was stationed with the Royal Navy.

Ron was discharged from the Navy in 1930. He went into business with [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]], starting a furniture shop in Luton as an adjunct to Harry's furniture moving and upholstery business. The business started in difficult circumstances — Harry's furniture van was a worn-out converted WW1 ambulance that Ron kept running by a drastic improvisation, filing oversize pistons oval to match the worn cylinders. Not long after, using Ron's Navy pension and savings as collateral, they took out a bank loan and bought two pantechnicon furniture vans. This expansion dates to approximately 1932 or 1933. The family moved to [[harpenden|Harpenden]] and over forty years owned three houses there: **32 Park Hill** (from ~1930, confirmed 1939), **"Journey's End"** (purchased in the 1950s after the sons left home, not far from Park Hill), and a house in the **Kinsbourne Green** development on the northern outskirts of Harpenden (~1962). Ivy managed the business books for the life of the business.

During WW2, Ron went to work at the de Havilland Aircraft Company in Hatfield, and Ivy managed the furniture shop. Ivy's sister Lillian lived at Park Hill during the war and kept house. Later, sister Mabel, John Mason, and Nellie moved to Park Hill after their grocery shop at 150 Acre Lane, Brixton was damaged by a V1 bomb.

In 1968, Ron and Ivy visited Alan in Auckland and decided to retire to New Zealand. They returned to England, wound down the furniture business with Harry Thody, and sold their Kinsbourne Green house. In 1970, they emigrated to [[auckland|Auckland]], shipping their possessions. Ron died in 1978. Ivy died in Auckland on 25 May 1994.

- **Why did Ron change his name?** The reason was never explained and was taken to the grave. The most coherent interpretation — developed in [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898]] — is that the change was deliberate social ascent: a renunciation of a working-class name, timed to come after Frederick's death, and consistent with a broader pattern of separation from his origins that included abandoning Wesleyan nonconformism entirely. Eliza is reported to have been very cross indeed and may have ceased contact with Ron as a result. The choice of "Bowers" remains unexplained — it does not appear in either family line.

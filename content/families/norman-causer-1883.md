## Parents

[[mary-ann-norman-1861|Mary Ann Norman]] (1861–1951), daughter of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] of the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]], married [[george-causer-1859|George Causer]] (1859–1898) in Huntingdonshire in 1883. He was born in Staffordshire and worked as an Engine Driver — the only non-local spouse in the Swavesey Norman family. Nothing is known of his origins or how they met.

## Children

Six children, all born in Swavesey/Cambridgeshire:

1. **Elizabeth Ann Causer** (19 Jun 1885–1974) — lived with her mother in 1939 at Middle Watch, Swavesey; age 54 in 1939; died 1974
2. **Ethel Kate Causer** (1887–1957)
3. **Fred Daniel Causer** (1889–1969)
4. **Harriet Causer** (1892–1920) — died young, age 28
5. **Frances Ellen Causer** (1894–1980)
6. **Louisa Causer** (1895–1992) — the longest-lived, reaching approximately 97

## Family Life

The family lived in Swavesey throughout the 1880s and 1890s. George's occupation as Engine Driver was unusual for the village — he may have worked on the railways (the St Ives branch opened 1847, Swavesey had a nearby station) or on steam-powered farm machinery.

George died in 1898, leaving Mary Ann with six children aged between 3 and 13. By 1901 she was working as a **laundress** to support them; by 1911 as a **charwoman**. Two daughters — Louisa (15) and Harriet (19) — were still at home in 1911.

By 1939 Mary Ann was 78, widowed, and living at Middle Watch, Swavesey — the same hamlet as her brother [[robert-henry-norman-1868|Robert Henry Norman]] — with daughter Elizabeth and a lodger Richard Williams (19). She had never left Swavesey. She died in 1951 aged 89. There is no probate record for her death, suggesting she died with an estate below the threshold or nothing to leave.

Elizabeth's occupation in the 1939 register is given as **Private Means** — meaning unearned income rather than wages, typically savings, a pension, or an annuity. Since Elizabeth never married and the family was of working-class origin, this is slightly unexpected. There is no probate for Elizabeth either (died 1974), which suggests whatever "private means" she had was modest — perhaps a small occupational pension, savings accumulated over a working life not captured in the records, or simply a self-reported formality for someone not currently employed. The two absences of probate together point to a household that remained poor throughout.

## Wider Family

Mary Ann was the fifth child of the [[norman-tabram-1850|Norman-Tabram family]] of Swavesey. Her nephew [[charles-norman-jr-1895|Charles Norman Jr]] (born 1895, son of her brother Charles) and her nieces and nephews in London were the branch of the family that eventually connected to the Bunker/Bowers family.

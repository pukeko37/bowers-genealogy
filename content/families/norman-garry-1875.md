## Parents

[[george-norman-1851|George Norman]] (1851–September 1903) was born in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire — the eldest son of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]]. He arrived in West Hartlepool by late 1875, part of the same migration that brought his brother [[richard-norman-1853|Richard]] north to County Durham.

[[margaret-j-garry-1854|Margaret J Garry]] (1854–1933) was born in Hartlepool, Durham — confirmed by census records placing her birthplace as "East H Pool" (East Hartlepool) in 1881 and Hartlepool in 1901 and 1911. Her maiden name was Garry; her middle initial is recorded as "J" but the name behind it is not yet known. The 1901 census records her under the name "May" (age 47, born Hartlepool) — likely a middle name or household nickname; she appears as "Margaret" in all other census years. She outlived George by thirty years and died in July 1933.

They married on **7 December 1875** at **Christ Church, West Hartlepool, County Durham** — approximately two months after George's brother Richard married Ann Elizabeth Randall in Hartlepool. Both elder Norman brothers arrived in Hartlepool and married there within the same autumn.

## Family Life

George, Margaret, and their growing family lived in **Stranton**, the inner township of West Hartlepool, through both the 1881 and 1891 censuses. By 1901 they were recorded at West Hartlepool proper.

George's working life in Hartlepool followed the town's port and industrial economy:
- **1881:** Grocers Porter — supplying the port and growing town
- **1891:** Grain Porter, NE Railway — working the railway freight traffic out of West Hartlepool, a major coal and grain export port
- **1901:** Malster at a brewery — a skilled manual trade; West Hartlepool had several breweries serving the large working-class population

The son William's christening at the **Lynne Street United Free Methodist Chapel** in 1879 places the family in the Nonconformist tradition that was common among working families in the Durham coalfield and port towns.

## Wider Family

George and his brother [[richard-norman-1853|Richard Norman]] (see [[norman-randall-1875]]) both settled in the Hartlepool area and raised large families there. Their households would have been within walking distance through the 1880s and 1890s. Richard and Ann named their ninth child **George Norman** (born 1895) — an honour to George while he was still alive. George himself named a son George (born 1889).

George died in September 1903 in Hartlepool, aged about 51. The children were still young — Susan was only six — and Margaret raised the younger children alone. She survived until 1933.

## Parents

[[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook]] (born 1843, Whipsnade – died 1908) and [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] (born 1843, Whipsnade – died 1924) married on 21 March 1868 at Whipsnade. Lois's father [[jeremiah-bransom-1808|Jeremiah Bransom]] was present at the ceremony.

Charles was the son of [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]] (1816–1900) and his first wife Elizabeth Frost — making him Ruth Cooke's nephew through her brother David. He is not to be confused with [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook (1847)]], Ruth's own son, who bore the same name and lived in the same village. Lois was the sister of [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]], who had married [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] three years later — see [[cook-bransom-1871]].

## Children

Eight children, all born in Whipsnade:

1. [[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cooke]] (1869–Deceased) — christened 11 October 1868
2. Henry Cooke (1870–Deceased) — christened 3 April 1870
3. William Cooke (1872–Deceased) — christened 16 June 1872
4. James Cook (1873–Deceased) — christened 1 April 1874
5. Charles Cooke (1876–Deceased) — christened 19 November 1876
6. George Cook (1877–Deceased) — christened 19 November 1876 (same day as Charles; possibly a recording error)
7. Edward Cooke (1880–Deceased)
8. [[albert-ralph-cook-1886|Albert Ralph Cook]] (1886–1932)

## Family Life

The family lived at Whipsnade throughout. Census records show the household in 1871 (Charles, Lois, Walter age 2, Henry age 1), 1881 (six children at home, plus Lois's mother Sarah Brandsom age 68 and sister Betsy Brandsom age 43), 1891 (Charles, Lois, and three sons: Walter 22, William 19, George 14, Albert 5), and 1901 (Charles, Lois, Albert 15).

Charles worked as an Agricultural Labourer, General Labourer, and latterly as a Wood Man. He died on 5 January 1908 aged 64, resident at the Chequers Inn. He left a will, and probate was granted just eight days later on 13 January 1908 with Lois named as executor and sole beneficiary — his estate valued at £134 5s. 5d. The eight-day turnaround was almost certainly driven by the need to secure the Chequers Inn licence without interruption. Lois outlived him by sixteen years, dying in 1924. Both deaths were registered in the Luton district.

## Connection to the Thody-Cook Wedding

[[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cook]], eldest son of this family, stood as a witness at the 1894 marriage of [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] at Whipsnade. The other witness was [[florence-bransom-1873|Florence Bransom]], the daughter of Walter's uncle [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] — making Walter and Florence first cousins.

## Parents

[[allen-thody-1813|Allen Thody]] (5 October 1813 – 22 March 1898) and [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]] (baptised 25 August 1811 – 11 October 1898) married 19 October 1834 at St. Mary Cardington, Bedfordshire. Allen was a farm labourer. He was born in Spain on 5 October 1813 — his father, [[william-thody-1774|William Thody]], was a soldier of the 40th Regiment of Foot serving in the Peninsular War. Elizabeth was the illegitimate daughter of [[rebecca-arnold-1778|Rebecca Arnold]] (widow of [[joseph-sanders-1774|Joseph Sanders]], shoemaker) and an unknown father; her maternal grandfather was Samuel Arnold, a farmer at Cotton End — the very hamlet where Allen and Elizabeth would settle. The witnesses at the wedding were **John Arnold** and **Elizabeth Arnold**, almost certainly relatives of Elizabeth's maternal Arnold family. See [[sanders-arnold-1799]].

## Family Life

The family was rooted in Cotton End, the hamlet in the Cardington registration district west of Bedford. Allen is recorded as a Farm Labourer in the 1851 census, and his mark on William's birth certificate suggests he could not write. The family appears to have remained in Bedfordshire throughout; all five children are recorded with Bedfordshire birthplaces.

By the time William had grown up and emigrated to America (c. 1870), both parents were still alive. Allen is listed as William's father on the 1894 Whipsnade marriage certificate, still described as a Labourer. Both Allen and Elizabeth lived into their mid-eighties, dying in 1898.

The remarkable longevity in this family is striking: Ann (1835–1913) and George (1844–1935) both lived into their late seventies and nineties. Elizabeth Thody (1847–1940) reached the age of 92 or 93.

## Parents

[[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick William Tall Bunker]] (1869–1932) and [[eliza-geary-1859|Eliza Geary]] (1859–1942) married in 1892 at Ampthill, Bedfordshire. Eliza was ten years Frederick's senior — she was 33 and he was 23.

**How they met:** The 1891 census places Eliza in Maulden — the same village where Frederick had been raised by his grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] after 1870. Eliza's parents [[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]] were also in Maulden in 1891, having moved from their lifelong home in Haynes. John Geary died and was buried at St Mary the Virgin, Maulden in February 1895. The marriage in Ampthill in 1892 — the nearest town — followed naturally from this shared Maulden presence the previous year. See [[geary-morris-1848]].

Frederick was a bricklayer and builder. He had been orphaned at one — his mother [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] died in 1870 — and was raised by his grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] in Maulden.

Eliza was one of eleven children of [[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]] of Haynes. She was named after an elder sister who had died in 1856. Both her parents died in 1895 — the year before Beulah was born.

## Children

1. [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Rebecca Lucy Bunker]] (1896–1948) — born Kettering, Northamptonshire
2. [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland Herbert Bunker]] (1898–1978) — born Kettering, Northamptonshire

## Family Life

Both children were born in Kettering, Northamptonshire — confirming Frederick and Eliza were living there in 1896–1898, possibly for work in the boot and shoe trade for which Kettering was known. By 1901 they had moved to Ilford, Essex, and by 1911 to Luton, Bedfordshire.

**1911 census.** The household at **105 Hartley Road, Luton** (RG14, piece 249, ED 8) comprised: Frederick (41, bricklayer, builder's), Eliza (49), Beulah (14, apprentice dressmaker), Roland (13), and a boarder — **Dora Emily Bunker** (20, single, born Wavendon, Buckinghamshire, occupation: Upper Closer, Boot Making). Frederick and Eliza had been married 18 years per the return. The family had moved to 50 Hampton Road by August 1920 at the latest — Beulah's marriage certificate (2 August 1920) gives that as her address.

**1921 census.** On census night (June 1921), Frederick was at 50 Hampton Road, Luton with Beulah and Harry Thody, working for the Kentish Town Building Scheme in north London. Eliza was at 12 Gordon Road, **Southend-on-Sea**, on a seaside holiday with her widowed sister [[lydia-rose-geary-1868|Lydia Rose Geary]] and Lydia's son Jasper Fred Geary. Eliza is recorded as "Married." The census is a single-night snapshot and does not establish whether the marriage had permanently broken down — she was back in Luton by 1939, living at 9 Oakley Road with Beulah and [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]]. Whether the marriage drifted apart gradually or had already effectively ended by 1921 is not established from available sources.

Frederick died in Luton on 23 December 1932. Eliza died at 9 Oakley Road on 19 January 1942.

In 1936 — four years after Frederick's death — Roland changed the family name from Bunker to Bowers. The reason was never explained to anyone, including his sons. See [[bunker-norman-1921]] for more on this mystery.

Eliza died in 1942, aged 83.

## Open Questions

- Did Frederick and Eliza's marriage break down, and if so when? The 1911 census shows them together in Luton with both children — so if separation came, it was after 1911. By 1921 they are at different addresses but Eliza was on a seaside holiday that night, not necessarily living apart permanently.
- If the marriage did break down, why? No source gives a reason.
- When did Eliza return from Southend-on-Sea to Luton? (She was in Southend in 1921; back in Luton by 1939.)
- What happened to Lydia Rose Geary (Eliza's sister, widowed, at Southend 1921)? She died in 1932 — the same year as Frederick.
- Did Frederick's early orphaning shape his distance from family?
- **Who was Dora Emily Bunker?** The 1911 census records her as a boarder in the household — aged 20, single, born Wavendon, Buckinghamshire, Upper Closer in Boot Making. The shared surname may indicate a distant family connection, but Wavendon is not an area where any known Bunker branch settled. It is also possible her surname was misrecorded: in 1911 householders completed their own schedules, and the enumerator then copied from those — leaving two points at which a similar surname (e.g. Booker) could be rendered as Bunker. The original schedule (TNA, RG14/249) or a Wavendon birth registration for ~1891 would resolve this.

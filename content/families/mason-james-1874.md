## Parents

[[richard-mason-1850|Richard Mason]] (1850–1890) and [[catherine-james-1852|Catherine James]] (1852–1900) married on 5 May 1874 in Aberystwyth. Both came from **Cardiganshire (Ceredigion)** and were part of the well-documented Welsh dairy migration to London — not generic economic migrants but people entering a specific trade already established by their countrymen.

Richard was born in Aberystwyth. His parents were **Morgan Mason** (b. 1822, Llanbadarn Fawr) and **Catherine Mason** (b. 1826, Trerddol) — a married couple, both recorded as such in the 1851 and 1861 censuses. Morgan was an ostler in 1851 and a postillion in 1861, working with horses in the Aberystwyth area. Richard grew up on Thespian Street in Aberystwyth and was still in Wales for the 1861 census.

Catherine James was born in Llanfihangel Geneur Glynn, a parish north of Aberystwyth. In 1861 she was in the household of her brother Thomas James (a groom) in Cynnull-mawr parish.

## Children

1. [[richard-william-mason-1875|Richard William Mason]] (1875–1948) — eldest; born Clerkenwell; dairy shop keeper and grocer; lifelong bachelor
2. [[john-james-mason-1878|John James Mason]] (Jack, 1878–1962) — married [[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel Norman]] 1912. See [[mason-norman-1912]].
3. [[isaac-mason-1882|Isaac Mason]] (1882–1955) — married [[alice-norman-1890|Alice Norman]] 1918. See [[mason-norman-1918]].

All three sons were born in **Clerkenwell** — the family were in the City fringe before they appear in the 1881 census in Bethnal Green.

## Family Life

Richard and Catherine came to London as part of the established **Cardiganshire Welsh dairy migration**. By 1881 Richard is recorded as **Cowkeeper (Milk Seller)** in Bethnal Green — a specific occupational designation, not a labourer's catch-all. The 1881 household also included a lodger, Rachel Evans (18, Cardiganshire) — a younger Welsh migrant from the same community, a pattern common in the Ceredigion network.

By the time of Richard's death in 1890, the family were at **27 Hosier Lane, Smithfield** — close to Smithfield Market, a natural location for a dairy supplying the market workers and surrounding streets. Richard died there on 6 January 1890, aged 40. He was buried at Abney Park Cemetery, Stoke Newington.

After Richard's death, Catherine continued running the dairy alone. The **1891 census** records her as head of household at St Sepulchre Newgate (the Holborn/Smithfield parish), occupation **Dairy** — explicitly operating the business in her own right for a decade as a widow with three sons to raise. She died at 27 Hosier Lane on 29 October 1900, aged 50. She was buried at Abney Park Cemetery alongside Richard, two days after her death.

The [[norman-birchmore-1883|Norman-Birchmore family]] lived nearby in Hosier Lane, where [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] was foreman of the Carter Paterson stables.

## The Welsh dairy trade context

The Masons were not an isolated case — they were part of a well-documented pattern. **Welsh families from Cardiganshire (Ceredigion) dominated London's dairy trade throughout the Victorian period.** The trade was concentrated along the Paddington/Marylebone/Euston corridor (the Great Western Railway route from Wales), but extended through the Smithfield/City/Fleet Street area too. Documented examples include:

- **Jones Dairy** (Fleet Street, 1902–1939) — Welsh, with eight dairy cows, approximately 0.5 miles from Hosier Lane
- **Oxford Express Dairies** (26 Frith Street, Soho, 1934–1985) — Pugh family from Ceredigion
- **J Evans Dairy Farmer** (near Warren Street, Marylebone) — tile shopfront survives
- **Two Welsh-speaking ladies' dairy** (North Gower Street) — operating until the 1960s

All trace to West Wales, particularly Ceredigion/Cardiganshire. The pattern: families emigrated from the same Welsh counties, established dairy businesses along well-worn migration routes, and operated until mid-20th century consolidation. The early morning **milk train from the West Coast of Wales** brought fresh dairy products daily. Richard and Catherine Mason married in Aberystwyth in 1874 — a town marriage that marks the transition point between Welsh and London life. The Hosier Lane operation (c.1874–1900) predates Jones Dairy (1902) by over 25 years.

Both Richard and Catherine were buried at **Abney Park Cemetery, Stoke Newington** — the principal Nonconformist cemetery in London, widely used by the Welsh community.

## Legacy

Catherine died in 1900, leaving three orphaned sons aged 25, 22, and 18. The two younger were barely adults. All three brothers remained in the food trade — Richard William as dairy shop keeper then grocer, the two younger eventually as newsagents. John and Isaac both married daughters of the neighbouring Norman family. Two Mason brothers married two Norman sisters: John married Mabel (1912), Isaac married Alice (1918). This double marriage created a mutual support network for the widowed Ellen Norman's family. See [[mason-norman-1912]] and [[mason-norman-1918]].

The family trade evolved from dairy to grocer to newsagent. John settled at 150 Acre Lane, Clapham; Isaac at 4 Yukon Road, Balham. Richard William worked alongside his brothers through the dairy and grocer years, then by 1939 was living at 150 Acre Lane — incapacitated and in John's care — alongside Nellie Norman. The probate record confirms 150 Acre Lane was Richard's settled residence at death, and that the business there was a **sub-post office** — John's probate occupation is given as "subpostmasters assistant."

The three brothers converged at the end as they had begun. Richard William died in 1948, buried at Abney Park alongside his parents. Isaac died in 1955 at 17 Daysbrook Road, Streatham. John died in 1962 at 16 Daysbrook Road — literally next door. Two brothers, who had run parallel newsagent businesses across south London, ended their lives as neighbours on the same Streatham street.

## Open Questions

- How did the Mason brothers and Norman children come to know each other well enough to produce two marriages? Proximity alone at Hosier Lane is the obvious answer, but there may be more.
- **The milkman connection:** [[george-birchmore-plummer-jr-1850|George Birchmore Plummer Jr]], half-brother of [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Norman]], was a milkman operating out of St Pancras — one mile north of Hosier Lane. If GBP Jr supplied or dealt with the Mason milk shop, this would create yet another thread connecting the Norman and Birchmore families in the same London neighbourhood. See [[birchmore-morse-1874]].
- Morgan and Catherine Mason's marriage record — not yet found, though their married status is confirmed by census records.
- Catherine James's parents — not documented; her brother Thomas James (groom, Llanfihangel Geneur Glynn) is the only other family member identified.
- Isaac's Barclays Bank executor — was Alice unable to act? Or was this Isaac's deliberate choice? The substantial estate (£7102) suggests he was more financially prosperous than John at death.

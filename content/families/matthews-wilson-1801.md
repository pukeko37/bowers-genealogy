## Parents

[[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews]] (1767–1862) was born in Kensworth, Hertfordshire, and died in Whipsnade, Bedfordshire at approximately 94 or 95. He was an agricultural labourer — by 1851 listed as "Pauper Ag Lab," a designation that reflects the grinding poverty of rural Bedfordshire in his old age. [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]] (b. 21 Dec 1779, Stanbridge, Bedfordshire) married Thomas on **3 June 1801 at Kensworth** — his home parish. By that point the couple had already been together for several years and had at least two children born before the marriage: Thomas (christened Kensworth 1798) and James (christened Kensworth December 1800).

The identification of this family across the records rests on several converging points. The 1851 census gives Thomas's birthplace as **Kensworth** and Mary's as **Stanbridge** — an exact match to Thomas Matthews (1767, Kensworth) and Mary Wilson (b. 1779, Stanbridge). The marriage at Kensworth in 1801 is consistent with the pre-marital christenings there in 1798 and 1800. And all five children christened between 1798 and 1808 were registered at Kensworth, before the family's move to Whipsnade is first reflected in Anne's 1815 christening. Together these form a coherent and consistent picture. The only gap is that the 1798 and 1800 christening entries name the mother only as "Mary" with no maiden name — but no alternative Thomas-and-Mary Matthews family has been identified in the parish at this date.

The family's first home was in Kensworth, Hertfordshire, Thomas's birthplace. They moved to Whipsnade, Bedfordshire, sometime between 1808 and 1815 — the last Kensworth christening was John in 1808; the next recorded, Anne's in 1815, was in Whipsnade. Their Whipsnade address — Houchen End, By Whipsnade Green — was where they remained for the rest of their lives.

## Children

Ten children are now confirmed from christening records and other sources:

1. [[thomas-matthews-1797|Thomas Matthews (the younger)]] (christened 12 Aug 1798, Kensworth) — born before the formal marriage; stayed in Kensworth all his life; married twice; died 1862.
2. **James Matthews** (christened 7 December 1800, Kensworth) — born before the formal marriage; father recorded as Thomas, mother as Mary. Almost certainly died young — he appears in no later record, not in the 1841 or 1851 household, and was unknown to FamilySearch sources. His christening confirms the couple were in Kensworth in late 1800, six months before the June 1801 marriage at the same parish.
3. [[sarah-matthews-1803|Sarah Matthews]] (christened 30 Jan 1803, Kensworth) — no further record found; did not appear in 1841 or 1851 with parents.
4. [[william-matthews-1806|William Matthews]] (christened 20 Apr 1806, Kensworth) — in parental home in 1851 census; emigrated to Utah 1853 with the Wheelock Company.
5. [[john-matthews-1808|John Matthews]] (christened 28 Feb 1808, Kensworth) — in 1841 household aged 30; died **Whipsnade 1849**, buried St Mary Magdalene. Never emigrated; his absence from 1851 is explained by his death two years prior.
6. [[able-matthews-1813|Able Matthews]] (b. 18 Apr 1813) — emigrated to America 1855; wife died St. Louis; settled Lehi, Utah.
7. [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] (christened 7 May 1815, Whipsnade) — married [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] 1835; stayed in England; died 1858.
8. [[david-matthews-1817|David Matthews]] (christened 1817, Whipsnade) — **died in infancy, 1817**; buried St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade. Never survived to childhood.
9. [[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]] (christened 17 Dec 1820, Whipsnade) — emigrated to Utah 1853 on the "Ellen Marie"; died Virgin, Utah 1903.
10. [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte Matthews]] (christened 29 Jun 1823, Whipsnade) — married [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]]; stayed in Whipsnade her entire life; died 1910.

**Note on Susan Matthews (1800):** A previous version of this page listed Susan Matthews (1800–1857) as a child of this family, attributed via FamilySearch ID KZB3-93C. The May 2026 ingest of the full sources for that record establishes that this Susan was the daughter of [[peter-matthews-1770|Peter Matthews (1770)]] and [[mary-walker-1766|Mary Walker]] — a different Matthews line also rooted in Whipsnade. She married Thomas Batchelar in 1826. She has been removed from this family's children list accordingly.

## Family Life

### Kensworth years (before ~1815)

Thomas Matthews was a Kensworth man, and the family remained rooted there for the first decade of this marriage. All five children born before 1809 were christened at Kensworth: Thomas (1798), James (1800), Sarah (1803), William (1806), John (1808). The marriage itself was at Kensworth in June 1801. Thomas and James were both born before the formal marriage; James almost certainly died in childhood and left no further record. The family then made the move to Whipsnade, Bedfordshire, a few miles to the north-west, sometime between 1808 and 1815 — by Anne's christening in 1815 they were settled at Houchen End. Thomas the younger, already in his mid-teens by the time the family moved, likely remained in Kensworth — he spent his entire life there.

### Whipsnade years (1815 onward)

The Whipsnade years were the bulk of the family's life. Five more children were christened there between 1815 and 1823. Thomas worked as an agricultural labourer throughout — Whipsnade was a farming village, and his work would have been tied to the seasonal rhythms of arable and livestock farming on the chalk downland. By 1851 he was classified as "Pauper Ag Lab," reflecting the collapse of rural wages and the strain of extreme old age.

The specific address — **Houchen End** — is the same hamlet associated with GBP Sr (Thomas's future son-in-law), who lived there by the 1861 census. The two families were long-standing neighbours before Anne's 1835 marriage.

### Census snapshots

**1841** (Houchen End, Whipsnade): Thomas (70), Mary (60), John (30), Daniel (20). Susan had presumably died or was elsewhere; Sarah, William, David, Able, Anne, Charlotte are not in the household. Anne was married and living with GBP Sr; Able and Daniel had not yet emigrated (that came 1853–1855). Thomas the younger is absent — by 1841 he was married and living in Kensworth with his own family.

**1851** (Whipsnade): Thomas (84, born **Kensworth**), Mary (70, born **Stanbridge**), William (45). John, Daniel, Able are gone. The census birthplaces are an independent confirmation of the identifications: Kensworth matches Thomas Matthews (1767) and Stanbridge matches Mary Wilson (b. 1779, Stanbridge). Daniel emigrated to Utah in June 1853 — he was already absent at the 1851 census, suggesting the LDS emigration movement was pulling the younger generation away before the ships sailed. William, still home in 1851, followed just two years later.

## The Mormon Emigration

In the 1840s the family converted to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. At least three children emigrated:

- **William** — baptised LDS 5 April 1848, Bedfordshire; still in the parental home in 1851; sailed with the **Cyrus H. Wheelock Company** departing 13 June 1853, arriving Utah 16 October 1853.
- **Daniel Willis** — baptised LDS 12 September 1846; sailed from Liverpool on the **"Ellen Marie"** 13 June 1853 with 332 fellow converts; arrived Utah October 1853; died Virgin, Washington County, Utah, 1903.
- **Able** — emigrated 1855 with wife Mary Ann Walker Matthews and family; stopped in St. Louis (where a daughter, Ellen, was born, and his wife died); crossed the plains to Utah 1861; naturalised Utah 1866.

Notably, William and Daniel Willis both departed England on 13 June 1853 — William is recorded in the Wheelock Company, Daniel on the "Ellen Marie". Whether they travelled together or took separate passages on the same day is unclear.

**Mary Wilson herself was baptised LDS on 4 January 1851**, at the Hensworth Branch, British Mission — just weeks before the 1851 census found her still in Whipsnade with Thomas. Whether she subsequently emigrated is unknown; no Utah record for her has been found. She would have been over 70 by 1851 and the overland crossing to Utah was brutal; it seems more probable she stayed in England.

**Anne and Charlotte did not emigrate.** Anne was married to GBP Sr (since 1835) and died in 1858. Charlotte married David Cook and spent her entire life in Whipsnade.

**Thomas the younger** stayed in Kensworth and died there in 1862. Whether he converted to Mormonism is unknown.

**John** disappears after the 1841 census because he **died in Whipsnade in 1849**, two years before the next census. Burial records (National Burial Index and Bedfordshire Burials index) confirm he was buried at St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade. He never emigrated.

## Wider Family and Connections

The Cook family of Kensworth/Whipsnade was deeply entangled with the Matthews family:

- [[charlotte-matthews-1823|Charlotte]] married [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]], brother of [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]]
- Ruth Cooke later became GBP Sr's third wife — meaning he married two women from interlinked Whipsnade families
- The Cook family's mother, [[mary-birchmore-1785|Mary Birchmore]], shares her maiden name with GBP Sr's own family — an intriguing further thread

### The Houchen End convergence

The Thomas Matthews (1767) household was at **Houchen End**, Whipsnade — the same hamlet address recorded for [[william-mathews-1837|William Mathews (1837–1914)]], grandson of [[peter-matthews-1770|Peter Matthews (1770)]], the parallel Kensworth-origin Matthews line. Both Matthews families were rooted in Kensworth, both settled in Whipsnade, and both are documented at the same hamlet. This coincidence of address across two lines is the strongest available evidence that Thomas (1767) and Peter (1770) — born three years apart in the same Hertfordshire village — were related, most likely as brothers or cousins. Establishing the link would require Kensworth parish records for a Matthews family in the 1760s–1780s.

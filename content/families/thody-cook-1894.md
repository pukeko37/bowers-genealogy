## Parents

[[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] (1850–1911) and [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856–1930) married on 6 February 1894 at [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], Bedfordshire. William was 43, a Police Officer and widower of [[mathilde-parent-1853|Mathilde Parent]], who had died in 1892 leaving four children: [[george-allen-thody-1874|George Allen]], [[louis-albert-thody-1876|Louis Albert]], [[clarence-james-thody-1884|Clarence James]], and [[lena-florence-thody-1889|Lena Florence]]. Lucy was 37, a spinster; no father was named on the marriage certificate.

The ceremony was conducted by William T. Holden, Rector (14 December 1838 – 18 December 1919; buried at St Mary Magdalene). The two witnesses were [[florence-bransom-1873|Florence Bransom]] and [[walter-cook-1869|Walter Cook]]. Florence was the daughter of Lucy's sister [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] and [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] — Lucy's niece. Walter was the son of [[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook (1843–1908)]] and [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] — not a blood relation of Lucy's, but connected through the Bransom family: Lois was Edwin Bransom's sister, making Walter and Florence first cousins. The two witnesses thus represented both branches of the local family web: Florence from the Cook–Bransom line through Mary, Walter from the Bransom–Cook line through Lois.

Lucy was the youngest illegitimate daughter of Ruth Cooke. Ruth later married [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] in 1867, making Lucy the step-sister of [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] — though they were only four years apart and raised together more like sisters. This step-sisterhood became the crucial link that eventually connected the Norman and Bunker families.

## Children

1. [[harry-thody-1894|Harry Thody]] (1894–1986) — born Markyate, Hertfordshire

## Blended Household

The 1901 census shows that two of William's children from his first marriage were still living in the household at Studham: [[clarence-james-thody-1884|Clarence Archibald Thody]] (16, born Dunstable) and [[lena-florence-thody-1889|Florence Lena Thody]] (11, born Totternhoe). Young Harry (6) grew up alongside these step-siblings. The older half-brothers — George Allen and Louis Albert — had by then left home.

## Family Life

The family lived in the [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]]/[[studham|Studham]] area, with William serving as Police Constable at Studham until retirement. William died in 1911, and Lucy moved to Rose Cottage, Studham.

Rose Cottage became an important gathering place. [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy]] and [[norah-winifred-norman-1902|Norah Norman]] often visited Lucy there. Lucy was like a second mother to Ivy. Harry grew up there before serving in WW1 from 1914.

After Harry married [[beulah-bunker-1896|Beulah Bunker]] in 1920, Rose Cottage hosted Ivy and Roland when they married in 1921 — they stayed there before moving to Portsmouth. The 1921 census caught them at Rose Cottage in the same month as their wedding.

Lucy died in early 1930. FamilySearch sources give 25 February; the gravestone inscription recorded at Find a Grave reads 25 January. See [[lucy-cook-1856]] for the discrepancy note.

## Significance

This small family — just three people — was the keystone connecting the Birchmore/Norman world of London to the Bunker world of Bedfordshire. Without the step-sisterhood of Lucy and Ellen, and without Harry's friendships, Ivy and Roland would likely never have met.

## Parents

[[newman-thulborn-1821|Newman Thulborn]] (abt 1821–1888) was an agricultural labourer, born and lifelong resident of **Oakington**, Cambridgeshire — a village five miles north-west of Cambridge. He married [[hannah-how-1824|Hannah How]] (abt 1824–unknown), born in Kingston, Cambridgeshire, probably around 1849–1850. Their marriage record has not been located; the first child Lydia was born 1851.

Newman's occupation never changed: "Ag Lab" / "Ag Laborer" / "Agricultural Labourer" in every census from 1841 (when he was still living with his parents) through 1881. He died in 1888 in Chesterton — the registration district encompassing Oakington — at an age that suggests he was born in 1821 (per the death registration) or 1824 (per the bishop's transcript). The census ages consistently point to ~1824.

## Children

Eight children, all born at Oakington between 1851 and 1872:

1. **Lydia Thurlborn** (1851–1883) — the eldest; married Samuel Trundley at Swavesey on 15 July 1873 (confirmed by a parish register entry in which Newman is named as her father); died 1883 aged ~32
2. **[[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]]** (21 Aug 1856–1922) — born Oakington; married [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] 30 October 1877 at Swavesey; nine children; lived at Alconbury Weston and then Eaton Socon; died 1922 in Huntingdonshire. See [[norman-thulborn-1877]].
3. **Joseph Thurlborn** (1859–1947) — long-lived; died aged ~88; not yet researched
4. **Ezra Thurlbon** (1861–Deceased) — present in the 1871 census (age 10, Oakington) and 1881 census (age 20, Oakington); not yet researched
5. **Lemuel / Samuel Thurlbourn** (c.1864–1959) — a name discrepancy exists: he appears as "Lemuel Thurlbon" (age 7) in the 1871 census and as "Samuel Thurlbourn" (age 17) in the 1881 census. Both birth years are consistent (~1864). This is almost certainly the same person; either a transcription variant or he went by different names at different points. If born 1864 and died 1959, he lived to approximately 95. Not yet researched.
6. **Albert Thurlbon** (1866–Deceased) — present in the 1871 census (age 5) and 1881 census (age 14); not yet researched
7. **Clara Thurlbon** (1870–Deceased) — present in 1871 (age 1) and 1881 (age 11); not yet researched
8. **Walter Thurlbourn** (1872–Deceased) — present in 1881 (age 9); not yet researched

## Family Life

The Thulborn family were part of the agricultural labouring community of the Oakington–Cambridge area throughout the mid-nineteenth century. Newman never rose above "Ag Lab" — he worked the land his entire life, presumably for local farmers. Oakington in this period was a small fenland village with a population of a few hundred, surrounded by arable fields.

The children began leaving home for work by the 1870s. Emily (then ~15) was living at Willingham, about 6 miles away, in 1871 — presumably in domestic service, a common path for girls from labouring families. Lydia (then ~20) was also absent from the 1871 household, and married in 1873. By 1881, the household was Newman and Hannah with four younger children still at home (Ezra, Samuel/Lemuel, Albert, Clara, Walter).

The two most documented children at this point are Lydia (who died young in 1883, three years after her 1873 marriage) and Emily, who married out to Swavesey and then to Huntingdonshire, where she lived until her death in 1922.

After Newman's death in 1888, Hannah's fate is unknown. She does not appear in later censuses that have been checked, and no death record has been found. She would have been in her mid-sixties in 1888 — possibly she moved in with one of her children.

## Wider Family

Newman's own parents — **James Thulbourn** (1781–1851) and **Sarah Curtis** (1779–1867) — were also Oakington people, married there on 12 October 1800. They had a large family: Newman was among the youngest of at least eleven siblings. James Thulbourn died in 1851, the same year as Newman's first child was born. The Thulbourn/Thurlborn name was common in the Oakington and Willingham area in the early nineteenth century.

The family's connection to the Norman line comes entirely through Emily's marriage to [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] in 1877. Their son **Albert Newman Norman** (1888–1970) carried forward the Thulborn name in his middle name — a common Victorian practice of commemorating the maternal family.

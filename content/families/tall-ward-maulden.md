## Parents

[[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]] (1806–1870) and [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] (1806–1894), both born in 1806 in neighbouring Bedfordshire villages (Ampthill and Flitwick). They married on **4 May 1827 in Maulden** — Sarah's village, where the family would remain rooted. Edward was a **carpenter**; the family attended **Howard Chapel**, a Nonconformist congregation in Bedford.

**Sarah's maiden name confirmed as Ward.** The Find a Grave record for Frederick Charles explicitly names her as "Sarah (nee Ward)." The firstborn daughter was named **Mary Ward Tall** — carrying the maternal surname forward as a middle name, a practice seen repeatedly in this research. Mary was born 12 September 1827 — four months after the May wedding — conceived before the marriage.

The family's geography was split: census records consistently place Sarah in Maulden, while death records link Edward and the children to Bedford addresses (Grey Friars Walk, Derby Street, Albert Street). William Toll was in St Pancras, London by 1861. Edward died at Albert Street, Bedford in 1870. Sarah never left Maulden.

## Children

Six children, four of whom died before their parents:

1. [[mary-ward-tall-1827|Mary Ward Tall]] (1827–1907) — eldest; "Ward" middle name preserves Sarah's maiden name; longest-lived sibling
2. [[william-tall-1830|William Toll / Tall]] (1830–1862) — carpenter; married Fanny Webb 1852; 3 children; moved to St Pancras London by 1861; died Bedford aged 31
3. James Toll (1832–1833) — died in infancy
4. Susan Toll (1837–1848) — died aged ~11
5. [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] (1844–1870) — married Caleb Bunker at Howard Chapel 1868; died July 1870
6. [[frederick-charles-tall-1846|Frederick Charles Tall]] (1847–1859) — died aged 12, Bedford; buried Foster Hill Road plot D9-76

Of six children, only Mary Ward and Rebecca survived to adulthood — and Rebecca died at 26. The family was struck by repeated child loss across three decades.

## Family Life

The family was rooted in Maulden, where Edward and Sarah married and christened their children. Edward worked as a carpenter. By the 1850s, children were dying in Bedford — suggesting Edward had established a work base there while Sarah remained in Maulden. William had moved further, to St Pancras, London by 1861, before dying in Bedford in 1862. Edward died in Bedford in 1870.

The double blow of July 1870 — Rebecca died 7 July, Edward died 16 July, nine days later — left [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah]] alone with her infant grandson [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick]] (age 1). She raised him in Maulden through at least 1871, and remained there until her death in January 1894 at 87.

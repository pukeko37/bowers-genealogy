## Parents

[[samuel-plackett-lane-1841|Samuel Plackett Lane]] (abt 1841–1872) and [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]] (1833–1922) married on **21 September 1863** at **St Peter's Martin, Bedford**. Samuel was 22, single; Lydia was 30, single. Samuel's father was George Lane. Lydia's father was [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]].

The marriage joined two families already connected by trade and parish. In the 1861 census, Lydia's brother [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] (age 15) was recorded as a shoe maker at Dane Street, St Paul's parish, Bedford. Samuel (age 18) was a boot maker apprentice at Hassett Street in the same parish, in the same census district (RG09, piece 991), working under John Howard — a master boot maker employing four men. Both men were in the boot and shoe trade, in the same small parish, two years before Samuel married Caleb's sister. The families were almost certainly acquainted before the marriage.

## Children

Three daughters:

1. **Edith Mercy Lane** (born ~1865, Bedford) — present in 1871 (age 6) and 1881 (age 18) censuses with her mother. Married **Frank Humphries Locke** in 1884. Children: Florence Locke (b.~1887), Samuel Locke (b.~1889), Hilda Locke (b.~1894), Gertrude Locke (b.~1897). Lydia lived with Edith and Frank in 1901.
2. **Mary A Lane** (born ~1868, Bedford) — present in 1881 census (age 13) with Lydia. No later records traced.
3. **Elizabeth Mary Ann Lane** (born ~1869, Bedford) — present in 1871 census (age 2). Married **John White** on 24 April 1890 at Holy Trinity, Bedford/Weymouth.

No person pages have been created for these children yet; they are documented in the source records.

## Family Life

Samuel and Lydia lived in **St Paul's parish, Bedford** throughout their marriage. The 1871 census shows Samuel (28, Boot & Shoemaker) as head of household, with Lydia (37), daughters Edith Mercy (6) and Elizabeth Maryanne (2), and Albert John Lane (14, Maulden) — relationship to Samuel unclear, listed among "Other People." Samuel had established himself independently in the boot trade by this date, seven years after his apprenticeship under John Howard.

Samuel died in **1872 in Bedford**, aged 29, leaving Lydia a widow with three daughters under seven. He had spent his entire short working life in Bedford's boot and shoe trade.

After Samuel's death Lydia remained in Bedford, living as head of household in Bedford Western Ward in 1881 with daughters Edith (18) and Mary A (13). By 1891 she was living with grandchildren Florence and Samuel Locke — Edith's children by Frank Locke — suggesting she moved in to help after Edith's family grew. In 1901 she is mother-in-law in Edith and Frank Locke's household.

By 1911 Lydia had moved to the **Barton Almshouse at Turvey, Bedfordshire** — she was 77, widowed for 39 years, and was supported by the almshouse charity. She died in 1922 in Bedfordshire, aged 89, having outlived Samuel by half a century.

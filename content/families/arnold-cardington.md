## Parents

**Samuel Arnold** was a farmer at **Cotton End** in the Parish of Cardington, Bedfordshire. He signed his will in May 1790; it was proved in November 1790, indicating he died in the autumn of that year. His wife was **Mary Arnold** (maiden name unknown). Person pages for Samuel and Mary have not been created; their dates and fuller biographies are unestablished.

Samuel's brothers were **Robert Arnold** and **John Arnold**, who were appointed co-executors of the will and guardians of the children.

## Children

The 1790 will names four children:

1. **[[rebecca-arnold-1778|Rebecca Arnold]]** (born about 1778) — the eldest known child; received the specific bequest of £150 in the will; married Joseph Sanders in 1799 and is the mother of [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]]
2. **Samuel Arnold** (dates unknown) — named in the will; not yet researched
3. **Mary Arnold** (dates unknown) — named in the will; not yet researched
4. An **unborn child** (named in the will as such) — sex and fate unknown

Rebecca was approximately twelve years old when her father died.

## Family Life

The Cotton End district of Cardington was farming country in the Bedfordshire heartland. Samuel Arnold was evidently a man of some substance — a farmer of sufficient means to leave £150 to Rebecca specifically, and to arrange formal guardianship for his children. The bequest to Rebecca was almost certainly intended as a marriage portion.

The will's explicit provision for guardians by Samuel's brothers Robert and John Arnold suggests that Mary Arnold (the wife) was either already dead or was not considered in a position to raise the children alone.

Cotton End remained the family's ground across the next generation: [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]] (Rebecca's illegitimate daughter) grew up there, married [[allen-thody-1813|Allen Thody]] in Cardington in 1834, and raised her family there.

## Arnold Family in 1834

The witnesses at Elizabeth Saunders's 1834 marriage to Allen Thody were **John Arnold** and **Elizabeth Arnold**, both of whom signed with marks. John Arnold was Samuel's brother, named in the 1790 will as co-executor and guardian. Whether this is the same John Arnold (by then in his late sixties or seventies, if born c. 1755) or a member of the next Arnold generation is uncertain — but the appearance of the Arnold name at Elizabeth's wedding confirms that the family connection to Cotton End endured into the 1830s.

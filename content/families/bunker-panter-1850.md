## Parents

[[james-bunker-1824|James Bunker]] (1824–1904) and Sophia Panter (1829–1903) married on **25 December 1850** at Stevington, Bedfordshire. James was the youngest surviving son of [[joseph-bunker-1792|Joseph Bunker (1792)]] and Mary Markham. Sophia's origins are incompletely recorded: the 1861 census gives her birthplace as "Warrington, Bedfordshire," which appears to be a transcription error for an unidentified Bedfordshire village. Her surname is given as "Panter" in FamilySearch sources; family notes record it as "Painter."

The couple lived their entire married lives at Silver Street, Stevington — the same address as James's parents before them — where James worked as an agricultural labourer through every census to 1901.

## Family life

James and Sophia lived quietly at Silver Street throughout their lives. James is recorded as a labourer in all census returns; Sophia as wife and mother. They raised four daughters, all of whom outlived their parents by substantial margins.

Sophia died in 1903, one year before James. James died on 26 November 1904, aged approximately 80, at Stevington — the same village where he had been born, christened, married, and had lived his entire life.

The most eventful story in the family is that of daughter **Ruth**, who brought a bastardy order against her future husband in June 1880, married him three months later, had eleven children, and emigrated with the family to Quebec in 1885. See [[ruth-bunker-1855]].

- The "Miss Bunker" appearing in Stevington and Stagsden community events in 1886, 1889, and 1892 is likely one of the unmarried Bunker daughters — Jane, Hannah, or Mary Ann — from this family. Ruth was married from September 1880. See [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-findmypast]].
- "J. Bunker" at the 1896 Stevington Flower Show (2nd prize, damsons) is James himself, aged 72.

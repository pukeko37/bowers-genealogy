## Parents

**[[joseph-bunker-1754|Joseph Bunker]]** (1754–1808) was the youngest son of Thomas Bunker (1704) and Mary Reade — born and raised in Millbrook, christened 13 October 1754. He was a **cordwainer** (maker of new shoes): a parish apprenticeship indenture of 8 January 1784 records Jonathan Bunker of Maulden being apprenticed to "Jos Bunker, Millbrook, Cordwainer."

**[[martha-garratt-1756|Martha Garratt]]** (abt 1756–unknown) brought the name Garratt into the family; her origins are undocumented. They married on 1 October 1777 at Millbrook, when Joseph was twenty-three.

## Family life

Joseph and Martha raised their large family in Millbrook, in the same village where Joseph had grown up and where his father Thomas (1704) had died in 1784. As a cordwainer, Joseph was a craftsman rather than an agricultural labourer — a skilled trade that placed him in the village's artisan tier. He died there on 27 November 1808, aged 54, when his youngest surviving child Joseph Garratt was still a boy of about eight.

The children's names show two distinct registers. Some are conventional — Samuel, Job, Joel, Ruth, Arthur. Others carry the heavy Old Testament gravity favoured by nonconformists: Jesse, Jehu (in Thomas Jehu), Jonas. No chapel affiliation is yet confirmed, but the pattern matches the Wesleyan and Baptist naming conventions seen in the wider Millbrook Bunker community.

Martha survived her husband but her subsequent life — and her death — are not yet documented.

## Wider family

Joseph was the youngest of his father Thomas (1704)'s three named lines. His older brother **Thomas (1738)** married Martha Sinfield in 1761: see [[bunker-sinfield-1761]]. His brother **Edward (1742)** married Mary Ashby in 1766: see [[bunker-ashby-1766]]. These three lines — Thomas, Edward, and Joseph — represent the three branches of the Millbrook Bunker community that survive into the nineteenth century, though Joseph's line is the least documented in the census era.

## Parents

**William Norman** (1687–1747) and **Alice Briggs** (1685–1766) married on 3 May 1710 at Cottenham, Cambridgeshire. Person pages for William and Alice have not yet been created.

William died in 1747, aged about 60. Alice outlived him by nineteen years and died in 1766 — the same year their youngest son Richard moved permanently to Over as a working weaver.

## Children

FamilySearch records multiple christenings at Cottenham for this family, though infant mortality was high. Known children, in probable birth order:

- **Susan** (1710) — died in infancy, the same year as the marriage
- **Alice** (1711)
- **Thomas** — multiple short-lived Thomas entries in 1712, 1713, 1714, and 1715 suggest several sons given the same name died young, a common practice when a name was expected to survive
- **William** (1717)
- **Lydia** (1718)
- **Susanna** (1722)
- [[richard-norman-1723|Richard Norman]] (christened 26 November 1723 – 1783) — the youngest known child, and our ancestor

Richard was the only child we have followed forward. He was about 24 when his father died, and about 43 when his mother died. Of his siblings, nothing further is currently documented.

## Family Life

The family were settled at Cottenham, a Cambridgeshire fen-edge village with a long weaving tradition. William and Alice were there across at least three decades of marriage. The naming pattern — multiple short-lived Thomas entries, a daughter called Susanna who then appears again as Susanna (not Susan) in 1722 — reflects the ordinary reality of high infant mortality in a Georgian village household.

Richard, the youngest child, eventually moved to Over, a few miles to the west, where he worked as a weaver. The move was almost certainly vocational — Over had its own weaving economy and parish — but it took him out of the Cottenham world where his parents and siblings lived and died.

## Wider Family

There was at least one other Norman family in Cottenham in the same generation. A second FamilySearch profile, **L565-ZVH** (Richard Norman, 1718–1753), records a man five years older than our Richard who also lived at Cottenham and married Mary Essex there in 1740. Whether L565-ZVH was a brother, cousin, or an unrelated Cottenham Norman is unknown. The coincidence of the same Christian name in the same village within five years of birth is striking. See [[norman-essex-1740]] for the full account of how a FamilySearch contamination error briefly merged these two Richard Normans.

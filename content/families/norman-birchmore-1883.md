## Parents

[[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] (1859–1907) and [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] (1860–1940) married in 1883 in Kensington, London. They met when Charles, working for the haulage firm Carter Paterson & Co., hauled hay from Hall Farm, Whipsnade to London. Ellen was employed as a cook at the farm. Hall Farm later became Whipsnade Zoo.

## Family Life

The family's early residential history is traced through the children's birth places. They married in Kensington (1883), then moved through the north London arc:

- **1883–c.1889: Islington** — Mabel (1883), Thomas George (1886), and Elizabeth Ann (1888) were all born in Islington; Nellie (1885) was also born there
- **c.1889–1891: Hackney** — Alice was born in Hackney in November 1890; the 1891 census confirms Hackney
- **c.1891–1907: City of London / Hosier Lane** — Lillian (1893), Charles Jr (1895), Ivy (1899), and Norah (1902) were all born in the City of London. The family lived in [[hosier-lane-city-of-london|Hosier Lane]], near Smithfield Market. Charles was by then a foreman in charge of the horses at the Carter Paterson stables at 128 Goswell Road — a short walk away.

Charles died on 1 September 1907, aged 48, of tongue cancer — he was a heavy pipe smoker. Ivy was seven. The oldest children were between 18 and 24, and presumably the family was supported by them and the extended family.

Living near them in [[hosier-lane-city-of-london|Hosier Lane]] were the Mason family at number 27, who ran a milk shop. The Mason parents — Richard and Catherine — both died by 1901, and the orphaned brothers carried on. [[john-james-mason-1878|John Mason]] was at St Sepulchre, Newgate in 1891 (directly adjacent to Smithfield) — almost certainly how the families met. Two Mason brothers married into the Norman family: [[john-james-mason-1878|John]] married Mabel in 1912; Isaac married Alice in 1918. John and Mabel ran a newsagent and stationer's shop at 150 Acre Lane, Clapham. [[nellie-norman-1885|Nellie]] lived with them and worked as shop assistant.

After Charles's death, the family moved to the [[dalston|Kingsland]] area of Hackney — specifically the parish of St John at Hackney, South West Hackney (the De Beauvoir Town neighbourhood). By 1921 Charles Jr was at 143 De Beauvoir Road; by 1939 both Ellen and Elizabeth Ann were also at 143 De Beauvoir Road, and Thomas had moved to 6 Englefield Road nearby. The family settled into that neighbourhood and stayed.

The **1911 census** (RG14/477, schedule 236) confirms 11 people in the dwelling. [[thomas-george-norman-1886|Thomas George Norman]] (24) is listed as head of household — not Ellen. He was a warehouseman in the drapery and fancy lines trade, and principal earner.

The 11th person was [[mary-elizabeth-angus-1885|Mary Elizabeth Angus]] (born Little Thurlow, Suffolk), listed as a visitor. She was Thomas's future wife — they married later in 1911 at DeBeauvoir Town. By 1939 she was at 6 Englefield Road, Kingsland, Thomas's address.

The Mason brothers were **not** in this household. All three (Richard William, John James, Isaac) were enumerated separately at Lambeth, Norwood. The family oral history's description of a joint Norman-Mason household at Kingsland is not supported by the census; the Masons and Normans were in separate parts of London by 1911, though John Mason married Mabel in Hackney the following year (1912).

Ellen's step-sister Lucy Thody was an important figure in the family's life. Ivy and Norah often visited Lucy at Rose Cottage, Studham. Ivy likely learned her cooking skills from her mother (who had been a cook at Chequers Inn and Hall Farm) and from Aunt Lucy.

Ellen died on 28 February 1940.

## Wider Family

- **Charles's parents:** Thomas Norman (1827–1905) & Ann Tabram (1829–1897), married 1850, Cambridgeshire. Charles was 4th of 7 children.
- **Ellen's parents:** [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] (1813–1884) & Eliza Batchelor (1829–1867). See [[birchmore-plummer-batchelor-1859]].
- **Mason connections:** John Mason married daughter Mabel; Isaac Mason married daughter Alice. These marriages intertwined the Norman and Mason families.
- **Thody connections:** Lucy Thody (Ellen's step-sister) and her son Harry Thody were close to the family. Harry later connected Ivy to her husband Roland Bunker.
- **Children's families:** Ivy married Roland Bunker in 1921 — see [[bunker-norman-1921]].

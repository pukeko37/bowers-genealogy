## Parents

[[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] (1827–1905) and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] (1829–1897) married 3 February 1850 in Cambridgeshire. Thomas was an agricultural labourer who lived in Swavesey his entire life. Ann was the eldest daughter of [[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]] and [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary Eusden]] of the [[tabraham-eusden-1828|Tabraham-Eusden family]] — Fen Stanton, Huntingdonshire.

Thomas's mother Elizabeth Fear died when he was five (1832). Ann's mother Mary Eusden died when Ann was twelve (1841). Both lost their mothers young — a pattern that recurs across these families.

## Children

1. [[george-norman-1851|George Norman]] (1851–September 1903) — left Swavesey between 1871 and 1875; moved to **West Hartlepool, County Durham**; married Margaret J Garry December 1875; 10 children; died Hartlepool 1903; see [[norman-garry-1875]]
2. [[richard-norman-1853|Richard Norman]] (1853–?) — married Ann Elizabeth Randall October 1875 at Hartlepool; settled County Durham; 11 children; see [[norman-randall-1875]]
3. [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] (1856–1925) — married [[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]] 1877 Swavesey; 9 children; Farm Bailiff 1891; beerhouse keeper Eaton Socon 1911; see [[norman-thulborn-1877]]
4. [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] (1859–1907) — the only sibling to leave for London; see [[norman-birchmore-1883]]
5. [[mary-ann-norman-1861|Mary Ann Norman]] (1861–1951) — married [[george-causer-1859|George Causer]] 1883 Swavesey; 6 children; widowed 1898; later Chesterton; see [[norman-causer-1883]]
6. [[alfred-eusden-norman-1865|Alfred Eusden Norman]] (1865–1896) — born and died Swavesey; single; predeceased both parents
7. [[robert-henry-norman-1868|Robert Henry Norman]] (1868–1954) — married [[ada-squires-1864|Ada Squires]] 1899; 2 daughters; Middle Watch Swavesey; see [[norman-squires-1899]]

Ann named her sixth child Alfred **Eusden** Norman after her mother's family — memorialising Mary Eusden 24 years after her death.

## Family Life

Thomas and Ann lived in Swavesey, Cambridgeshire — a rural agricultural community on the Cambridgeshire/Huntingdonshire border. Three of the seven children left the area:

- **George** and **Richard** both migrated to **West Hartlepool, County Durham** — 240 miles north — and both married there in late 1875: Richard in October, George in December. They lived in the same township (Stranton) through the 1880s and 1890s. George died in Hartlepool in September 1903; Richard's later date is unknown. Together they raised twenty-one children in the Hartlepool area. Hartlepool's rapid industrial growth (coal, iron, shipbuilding) drew them north — and they went together.
- **Charles** went to London and built a career and family there at Hosier Lane, Smithfield — the most prominent of the migrants.

By 1891, [[tom-norman-1856|Tom]] had moved to Alconbury Weston, where he was working as **Farm Bailiff** — the only Norman sibling to hold a supervisory role. Thomas's movements after Ann's death in 1897 are uncertain: FamilySearch records him at Alconbury Weston by 1901, but his death in 1905 was registered in the **St Ives registration district** (which covers Swavesey and surrounding parishes), not the Huntingdon district that covers Alconbury Weston. Whether he moved to Alconbury Weston briefly and returned, or whether the 1901 record conflates father and son, is not yet resolved. Thomas died in 1905, aged about 78. [[alfred-eusden-norman-1865|Alfred Eusden]] predeceased both parents, dying in Swavesey in 1896. [[robert-henry-norman-1868|Robert Henry]] was the longest-lived, dying in 1954.

Among the siblings who stayed in the region, three built families: Tom (9 children with Emily Thulborn), Mary Ann (6 children with George Causer), and Robert Henry (2 daughters with Ada Squires). Only Alfred died unmarried. This represents a considerable Cambridgeshire/Huntingdonshire Norman presence into the late 20th century, parallel to the London branch that descends through Charles.

## Wider family

Ann's siblings (the other Tabraham children — Susan, John, George Richard) remained in the Fenstanton area. The Swavesey Norman family was therefore part of a broader Huntingdonshire agricultural community. The "Eusden" name in Alfred's forename is the most visible trace of Ann's Ely/Fenstanton origins.

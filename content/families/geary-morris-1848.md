## Parents

[[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] (1825–1895) and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]] (1826–1895) married in 1848 in Haynes, Bedfordshire. John was a woodman; Lucy's father was Thomas Morris of Haynes. Both were lifelong Haynes residents until their final years, when they moved to Maulden.

Both died in 1895 — John on 6 February (buried at St Mary the Virgin, Maulden), Lucy later the same year. They did not live to see their grandchildren: Beulah was born in 1896 and Roland in 1898.

John's parents were Joseph Geary (1804–1890) and Rhoda Spring Bottoms (1806–1884), married at Clophill, Bedfordshire — the village immediately adjacent to Maulden. The Geary family roots were always in this tight cluster: Clophill, Haynes, Maulden, Ampthill.

## Children

Eleven children, of whom four died in childhood:

1. Eliza Geary (1849–1856) — died aged 7
2. [[ann-geary-1851|Ann Geary]] (1851–1922) — married William Whitmore; settled Bedford; straw platter then charwoman
3. [[phoebe-geary-1854|Phoebe Geary]] (1854–1910) — married Walter Southgate; settled Bedford; died aged 56; buried Foster Hill Road Cemetery
4. [[emma-geary-1856|Emma Geary]] (1856–1874) — died unmarried aged 18 at Bedford; buried Foster Hill Road Cemetery
5. [[elizabeth-geary-1858|Elizabeth Geary]] (1858–1936) — never married; straw bonnet sewer in Luton; lived independently to age 78
6. [[eliza-geary-1859|Eliza Geary]] (1859–1942) — named for deceased elder sister; married Frederick Bunker; see [[bunker-geary-1892]]
7. John Geary (1862–?) — absent from census records after 1861; fate unknown
8. Ellen Geary (1864–1865) — died in infancy
9. [[albert-geary-1866|Albert Geary]] (1866–1942) — woodman at Maulden (married Charlotte, a Maulden woman); later railway platelayer and gardener at Luton; died 1942
10. [[lydia-rose-geary-1868|Lydia Rose Geary]] (1868–1932) — widowed by 1921; helped nephew Bertie Southgate after Phoebe's death; buried Foster Hill Road Cemetery Bedford
11. Frederick Louis Geary (1871–1872) — died in infancy

The family experienced significant child mortality: four losses (Eliza 1849, Ellen 1865, Emma 1874 in young adulthood, and Frederick Louis 1872). The second Eliza (our Eliza, 1859) was named for the first Eliza who had died three years before her birth — a common naming practice.

**Note on "Ernest Geary" / Francis Ernest Geary:** The 1871 census lists "Ernest Geary" (M, age 1) and the 1881 census lists "Ernest Geary" (M, age 11) as members of John and Lucy's household. This is [[francis-ernest-geary-1870|Francis Ernest Geary]], [[ann-geary-1851|Ann]]'s illegitimate son (born 8 Feb 1870, no father named), raised by his grandparents John and Lucy. He was christened at Haynes on 11 May 1873 alongside Lydia Rose — both receiving late christenings on the same day. He went by "Ernest." After 1881 he moved away from Haynes and eventually settled in **Reading, Berkshire**, where he married Eva Maud Geary and died 28 September 1936.

## Family Life

The family were settled in Haynes (also written Hawnes), a scattered rural parish south of Bedford, from John and Lucy's marriage in 1848 through to at least 1881. John worked as a woodman — a rural trade appropriate to the parish's mixed farmland and woodland. Named addresses include Plum's Lane and Church End (John), Silver Street and Church End (Lucy); by 1881 the family was at The Warren, Haynes.

The children grew up in the straw plaiting trade, a cottage industry that dominated Bedfordshire's rural economy. In the 1871 census, Ann (19), Phoebe (17), Emma (15), Elizabeth (13), and Eliza (11) are all listed as **Platters** — straw platters making the plait that fed the Luton hat trade. Even Albert (4) is listed as Platter, almost certainly an enumerator's error; children as young as four did help with plaiting, but the entry likely reflects the household's general occupation.

By the 1891 census, John, Lucy, their daughter Eliza, and son Albert had all moved to **Maulden**, Bedfordshire. The census (5 April 1891, RG12, piece 1260, ED 5) shows two separate Geary households in the village:

- **John's household** (page 28, folio 98, household 2007390): John Geary, head, 66, woodman; Lucy Geary, wife, 66; Eliza Geary, daughter, 30.
- **Albert's household** (page 21, folio 95, household 2007347): Albert Geary, head, 23, woodman; Charlotte Geary, wife, 31; Ernest Geary, son, 0 days.

They were in the same enumeration district but not immediate neighbours — seven pages apart in the enumerator's walk, suggesting different streets. Albert had married Charlotte (a Maulden woman) and was following his father's trade in the same village. Ernest was a newborn on census day itself — John and Lucy had moved to Maulden just in time to be present for their first grandchild's birth.

This Maulden connection is also how Eliza came to meet [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]], who had been raised in Maulden by his grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]]. Frederick and Eliza married in Ampthill in 1892. See [[bunker-geary-1892]].

**Dispersal to Luton:** The surviving adult children gravitated toward **Luton** and **Bedford** as the straw hat industry drew Bedfordshire's rural workers into the towns. Ann settled in Bedford (married William Whitmore). Phoebe settled in Bedford (married Walter Southgate). Elizabeth moved to Luton as a domestic servant and later straw bonnet sewer. Albert moved to Luton by 1901. Eliza (our Eliza) settled in Luton after marriage. Only Emma died before this dispersal (1874), and John's fate is unknown.

## Open Questions

- What became of **John Geary** (b. ~1862)? He is absent from the 1861 census (too young), not found in 1871 or 1881, and unrecorded elsewhere.
- Who was Ann Geary's **Francis Ernest Geary** (christened Haynes 11 May 1873, no father named)? He disappears from records after the christening.
- Who was **Lydia Rose's husband**? She was widowed by 1921 (1921 census) but no marriage or husband's death record has been found.
- What became of **John Geary** (b. ~1862)? He is absent from all census records from 1861 onward and unaccounted for.
- Did any of Eliza's siblings maintain contact with her after her marriage and moves? The 1921 seaside visit at Southend (Lydia and Eliza together) suggests the sisters stayed close.
- What happened to **Charlotte Geary** (Albert's first wife)? She appears in 1911 but no death record has been found; Albert remarried in 1928.

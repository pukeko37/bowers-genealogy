## Parents

[[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph Bunker (1840–1914)]] and [[mary-ann-wilcox-1842|Mary Ann Wilcox (1842–after 1921)]] married on 2 October 1859 at Millbrook. Both were Millbrook-born; both lived in Millbrook all their lives. Joseph was nineteen; Mary Ann was seventeen.

Joseph died in 1914 in Ampthill (the registration district town for Millbrook), aged approximately 74. Mary Ann outlived him by at least seven years: the 1921 census shows her living with son Albert and his wife Emma in the South Normanton area, aged approximately 79. She is widely believed to be the **Mrs Bunker** of the 1919 Huett effigy story — the aged widow who led archaeologists to the buried effigies and produced the stone head her late husband had kept. (See [[bunker-dean]] for a competing claim regarding Harriet Dean, wife of Samuel Bunker.)

## Family life

Joseph and Mary Ann raised a large family in Millbrook across the second half of the nineteenth century. With eleven children between 1859 and 1888, there was rarely a time before 1900 when the household was not full of young children or adolescents. The 1891 census shows six people at home; by 1901 the children had all left or died, and Joseph and Mary Ann were alone.

Joseph's recorded occupation throughout was farm labourer. Whether he also held the sexton's role is not confirmed in the census record, but it is strongly suggested by the 1919 newspaper story.

## The migration north

The defining event for this family is the wholesale migration of the elder sons to the South Normanton coalfields of Derbyshire. Thomas, Albert, George, and James all appear in Nottinghamshire by 1891; Richard was also there around the same time before returning to Bedfordshire.

**James** led the way: he was already settled in South Normanton by January 1893, when he married there at age nineteen — meaning he must have left Millbrook no later than 1891 or 1890, perhaps as young as sixteen or seventeen. His recorded occupation was Collier. The trigger is not documented, but the likely pull was coal-mine labour demand. The Phoebe Alice Bunker branch had already moved to Pilsley, Derbyshire in 1863 (see [[bunker-fensome-1863]]), so there was pre-existing Bunker knowledge of the region.

**Thomas** married Charlotte Roberts at Moggerhanger in December 1885 and was in Nottinghamshire by 1891; **Albert** married Emma Roberts (probably Charlotte's sister) at Moggerhanger in April 1887 and followed the same path. The Roberts women at Moggerhanger may have been a family the Bunker brothers already knew through some intermediate connection. **George** appears in Nottinghamshire in 1911 and married there in 1896; **Richard** was there in 1891 but had returned to Bedfordshire by 1901.

Of those who went north, Thomas and George are buried at South Normanton; James stayed in Derbyshire until his death in 1945.

## The daughters

John stayed in Millbrook; the daughters' trajectories were more varied. Ellen Maria married at Millbrook in 1904 and then moved to Luton. Mary Eliza moved to Luton around 1901 and remained there. Mabel was a parlour maid in the Millbrook vicarage in 1901, then moved to Luton, where Grace was born in 1902. Annie May was in Luton by 1901 (aged twelve or thirteen — likely in service) before settling at Marston Moretaine.

**Mabel's 1921 census** confirms [[grace-bunker-1902|Grace Bunker]] as Mabel's daughter: Grace is listed as "Grace Bunker Cook" in the Cook household at 15 Ashton Street, High Town, Luton, explicitly identified as Mabel's daughter. The "Bunker Cook" surname encodes her pre-marital birth: registered Bunker in 1902, acknowledged Cook after 1904, but never losing the Bunker name entirely.

## Family in 1919

When the Huett effigies were recovered in 1919, Joseph had been dead five years. Mary Ann, now approximately 77, still lived in Millbrook. She knew the location of the buried effigies because Joseph — while digging in the churchyard on some occasion during his working life — had struck stone and come upon the lost figures. She had kept a stone head. Her evidence directed the excavation, and the two Tudor effigies of William and Mary Huett were recovered intact after over sixty years underground.

## The First World War

The war of 1914–1918 fell on the grandchildren's generation with particular force. At least five grandsons of Joseph and Mary Ann died:

- **Harold William Page Bunker** (1881–1917) — Richard's eldest son
- **Joseph Bunker** (1894–1917) — Richard's son
- **Arthur James Bunker** (1895–1916) — Albert's son
- **Albert Edward Bunker** (1897–1916) — James's son
- **Albert Edward Bunker** (1894–1917) — John's son

## Christening anomaly

FamilySearch records the christening date of George (born 1868), John (born 1871), and James (born 1873) identically as **19 October 1873**. For James this is plausible (born May 1873, christened five months later). For George and John, the date is almost certainly an error — either a data import problem, or these three siblings were christened together in a batch on that day, perhaps a catch-up after the family had been attending nonconformist services. The Bunker family had longstanding nonconformist connections (see [[bunker-nonconformity-2026-06-27]]); if George and John had not been baptised in church at birth, a late mass christening in 1873 is plausible.

## Open questions

- What was Mary Ann Wilcox's parentage? Her maiden name is established but no Wilcox family has been traced in Millbrook records.
- What is the date and place of Mary Ann's death?
- Were Charlotte Roberts (Thomas's wife) and Emma Roberts (Albert's wife) sisters from the same Moggerhanger Roberts family?
- Why did Richard return to Bedfordshire when his brothers remained in Derbyshire?
- Is there a Millbrook parish vestry record or churchwardens' account naming a Bunker as sexton?
- When and where did Mabel Bunker die?
- What became of [[grace-bunker-1902|Grace Bunker Cook]] after 1921?
- Confirm the christening date anomaly: was there a batch christening at Millbrook on 19 October 1873?

## Parents

[[albert-bransom-1871|Albert Bransom]] (1871–1 April 1940) and his wife Mary (born 1872, died 30 October 1950) married on 26 September 1893. Mary's maiden name is not yet established. Albert was the eldest child of [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] and [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]], and through his mother a grandson of [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] of the Chequers Inn. Both Albert and Mary are buried at St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade.

## Children

1. [[frederick-bransom-1894|Frederick Bransom]] (abt 1894 – 1 August 1916) — Private, 2nd Battalion Bedfordshire Regiment; died of wounds at the Somme; the first Whipsnade man to fall in the Great War; buried at La Neuville British Cemetery, France

Other children, if any, are not established from current sources.

## Family Life

Albert and Mary lived at Yew Tree Cottages, Whipsnade, close to the northern edge of the parish — near what became the entrance to the Whipsnade Tree Cathedral. Albert worked the land locally; Frederick before him had worked at Hall Farm for over nine years before his enlistment around 1916.

After Frederick's death Albert became Edmund Blyth's sole helper in planting the Tree Cathedral from 1930 to 1939 — nine years of shared work on twenty acres laid out as a living First World War memorial. They are described in one source as friends; Blyth had previously lived at Rosemary Cottage in the same village before purchasing Chapel Farm in 1927, so the two men were neighbours before they became collaborators. Blyth's own commemorated comrades were Arthur Bailey and John Bennett; Albert was not the subject of the memorial but spent nine years building it alongside a man who had also lost people he loved to the same war.

## Wider Family

Albert's parents — [[edwin-bransom-1850|Edwin Bransom]] and [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] — lived By Whipsnade Green. Albert's sister [[margaret-anne-bransom-1880|Margaret Anne Bransom]] married James Hain of Dell Farm in 1905. The Bransom family was doubly connected to the village's Cook families through both Albert's mother (Ruth Cooke's daughter) and his aunt [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Brandsom]] (who married Charles Cook). See [[cook-bransom-1871]].

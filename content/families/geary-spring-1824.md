## Parents

[[joseph-geary-1804|Joseph Geary]] (1804–1890) and [[rhoda-spring-bottoms-1806|Rhoda Spring Bottoms]] (1806–1884) married on 20 April 1824 at Clophill, Bedfordshire. He was about 20; she was 17. Clophill lies immediately adjacent to Haynes and Maulden — the cluster where the Geary family had long been established.

Rhoda was the daughter of [[william-spring-1779|William Spring]] (1779) of Shillington and [[sarah-bottoms-1786|Sarah Bottoms]] (1786–1829). Her surname "Spring Bottoms" reflects her parents' relationship — her christening record gives the dual form "Spring or Bottoms." Joseph's own parents are not established in current sources.

The couple settled in Haynes (Hawnes), Bedfordshire, where they would spend the rest of their lives. Joseph's occupation is not specified in sources, but the rural character of Haynes — mixed farmland and woodland — suggests agricultural or woodland work consistent with the trade his son John followed (Woodman). Rhoda died in January 1884 at Haynes and was buried at St Mary's Churchyard there; Joseph died in 1890, six years later.

The 1841 census places Joseph (35) and Rhoda (35) at Plum's Lane, Haynes with seven children: John (15), Pamala (13), Sarah (11), Martha (8), Mary (6), Joseph (4), and George (0).

## Family Life

The Geary household at Plum's Lane in Haynes was a substantial rural family — ten children born over 27 years. Four of the children have confirmed death dates (Henry died in infancy; the others lived to adulthood). Several of the surviving children — Pamela, Sarah, Martha, Mary, Joseph, George, Walter, Caleb — have no further research in this wiki.

The family's staying power in Haynes is notable: Joseph and Rhoda lived there from at least 1824 to their deaths in 1884 and 1890. When their son John married Lucy Morris in 1848, he too settled in Haynes. It was only in 1891 — very late in John's life — that the household moved to Maulden.

## Parents

[[richard-tabraham-1807|Richard Tabraham]] (1807, Elsworth, Cambridgeshire) and [[mary-eusden-1807|Mary Eusden]] (abt 1807, Ely, Cambridgeshire) married on 3 August 1828 in Fen Stanton, Huntingdonshire. Richard was christened in Elsworth; Mary was christened at Trinity Church, Ely — both within the Cambridgeshire/Huntingdonshire border country.

Richard was a lifelong Elsworth/Fenstanton man. Mary's father was [[thomas-euseden-1764|Thomas Euseden]] (1764–1842); her mother was [[mary-eusden-1771|Mary Euseden]] (1771–1825), identified from the Fenstanton burial register as the only unaccounted-for Eusden death across the whole period 1797–1850. Mary's mother died in 1825, the year before her daughter's marriage.

## Children

1. [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] (1829–1897) — married [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] 1850 in Swavesey; seven children; grandmother of the London Norman family through their son Charles; see [[norman-tabram-1850]]
2. [[susan-tabram-1831|Susan Tabram]] (abt 1831–1911) — married John Levitt 11 October 1859 at Fenstanton; six children, including a daughter named **Mary Eusden Levitt** (1869) in memory of her mother and a youngest daughter christened **Annie Tabrain Levitt** (1874), the "Tabrain" middle name preserving the Tabram surname; died 1911 (Chesterton registration district — actual location unknown)
3. [[john-tabram-1836|John Tabram]] (1836–after 1901) — married Elizabeth Lewis 8 November 1857 at Fenstanton; agricultural labourer all his life; never left Fenstanton; seven children, three of whom died young; absent from 1911 census; death unconfirmed
4. George Richard Tabram (1840–1857) — died at seventeen; no known descendants

## Family Life

The family lived in the Fenstanton area of Huntingdonshire. Mary died around 1841 when her youngest child George Richard was less than a year old and her eldest Ann was twelve. Richard remained in Fenstanton through the 1841, 1851, and 1871 censuses, at Bell Lane. He died 26 February 1874 in Elsworth.

The 1841 census — taken around the same time as Mary's death — reveals something striking. John Tabram (age 5) appears not in Richard's household but in a separate one at **Paduck Gve, Elsworth**, headed by **Richerd Tabram (age 65)** and **Frances Tabram (60)** — John's paternal grandparents, born approximately 1776 and 1781. Also in this household: William Tabram (35, John's uncle), Ann Tabram (25, an aunt), and the surviving Tabraham children: Ann (12), Susan (10), John (5), George (2). Richard senior (who would be about 34) is absent from this enumeration. It appears the children were placed with the grandparents around the time of or after Mary's death — the grandparents were providing the immediate care. These grandparents had not previously appeared in the wiki; they are Richard's parents, a generation further back.

A census check confirms no "William" child among the Tabraham children. Richard himself is enumerated separately at Bell Lane, Fenstanton, with his father-in-law Thomas Euseden.

A separate household entry for 1841 records [[thomas-euseden-1764|Thomas Euseden]] (Mary's father, age given as 70) at Bell Lane with Richard Tabraham. The FamilySearch citation reads: *"Entry for Richard Tabram and Thomas Euseden, 1841."* Thomas was living with his son-in-law, presumably at or around the time of his daughter's death, and may have stayed on to help. He was buried at Fenstanton seventeen months later, on 14 November 1842.

The death of Mary when the children were young shaped the family in lasting ways. Ann, the eldest, moved to Swavesey by 1851 following her marriage to Thomas Norman. When she named her sixth child "Alfred **Eusden** Norman" in 1865, she was memorialising her mother's maiden name — an act of remembrance 24 years after Mary's death.

## Wider Family

The Eusden family was a Huntingdonshire family — not, as the Ely christening might suggest, a Cambridgeshire one. The Huntingdonshire marriages index shows the family concentrated around **Ellington** in the 1760s–1770s, and then clustering at **Fenstanton** from the 1820s. Mary's siblings — [[william-eusden|William]] (1797–1843), [[thomas-eusden-1799|Thomas]] (1799–1835), [[sarah-eusden-1804|Sarah]] (1804–1833), and [[henry-eusden|Henry]] (1809–1839) — all married at Fenstanton between 1825 and 1833. Their father [[thomas-euseden-1764|Thomas Euseden]] (1764–1842) was almost certainly a son of the Ellington Eusdens; the best candidate for his father is "Rt Eusden" who married Eliz Bellamy at Ellington in 1762, with Thomas born two years later.

The Fenstanton marriage of "Thos Eusden × Mary Ann James (widow)" on 21 February 1833 was [[thomas-eusden-1799|Thomas Eusden son]] (1799–1835), confirmed by FamilySearch records. He was 34 at marriage. Their son [[alfred-eusden-1834|Alfred Eusden]] was christened at Fenstanton on Christmas Day 1834. Thomas died two months before Alfred's first birthday, buried at Fenstanton 2 September 1835 aged 36. The 1836 index entry "Mary Ann (widow) Eusden × Jn Wm Skinner, Kimbolton" is confirmed as his widow remarrying, taking infant Alfred into the Skinner household. Alfred kept the Eusden name and married Mary Arthur in Shoreditch, London, in 1859.

The Tabraham family was centred in Elsworth and Fenstanton. The name "Tabram" (used interchangeably with "Tabraham" in the records) is the form that persisted into Ann's generation — FamilySearch consistently records Ann as "Ann Tabram."

## The Eusden Deaths

All five children of Thomas and Mary Euseden died between 1833 and 1843, all comparatively young:

| Child | Born | Died | Age |
|-------|------|------|-----|
| William | 1797 | 1843 | 46 |
| Thomas | 1799 | 1835 | 36 |
| Sarah | 1804 | 1833 | ~29 |
| Mary | 1807 | ~1841 | ~34 |
| Henry | 1809 | 1839 | ~30 |

An average age at death of ~35 is not explained by period life expectancy alone — adults who survived childhood typically had a reasonable chance of reaching their 50s or 60s. Their father lived to 78; their mother to 54.

**Tuberculosis is the most plausible primary explanation.** TB was responsible for roughly 25% of all deaths in early 19th-century England, killing primarily in the 20s–40s, with an untreated mortality rate of around 50%. Susceptibility has a significant genetic component, and familial TB clustering — multiple siblings dying of consumption across a decade — was well documented. The deaths span ten years rather than a single epidemic year, consistent with TB's slow individual progression rather than cholera or typhus. Sarah at ~29 and Henry at ~30 fit the classic consumption profile particularly well.

**Ague (malaria) as a contributing factor.** Fenstanton sits squarely in the Fenland basin — low-lying, waterlogged, historically notorious for ague among residents and visitors alike. A documented malaria outbreak struck the Fen region between 1826 and 1829, precisely in the years before the first Eusden deaths. While malaria was rarely fatal on its own in England — death rates ran to roughly 1 in 2,267 cases — it is a significant immunosuppressant. Repeated bouts of ague would weaken a person's defences, accelerate the progression of latent TB, and compound the effects of poverty on an already vulnerable constitution. The combination of Fenland malaria exposure, poverty, and familial TB susceptibility offers a more complete picture than any single cause alone.

The parents' longevity does not contradict this: if susceptibility was inherited from one line, or if the children were exposed to a source the parents were not, the parents could be unaffected while all five children succumbed.

This cannot be confirmed without cause-of-death entries in the Fenstanton burial register, which may or may not record them.

## Open Questions

- **Richard Tabraham's parents** — The 1841 census identifies them as Richerd Tabram (born ~1776) and Frances Tabram (born ~1781), living at Paduck Gve, Elsworth, with the grandchildren. No further records for this couple in the current sources. Their origin and full family are unknown.
- Did Richard Tabraham remarry after Mary's death? No evidence in current sources.
- **George Richard Tabram (1840–1857)** — listed as dying at 17; no further detail known.
- **Alfred Eusden (1834–?)** — Thomas son's only child; raised by stepfather Skinner after 1836; married Mary Arthur in Shoreditch 1859. No further records known. His descendants, if any, would carry the Eusden name into London.
- **Rt Eusden × Eliz Bellamy, Ellington 1762** — obtain the parish register entry to confirm as Thomas Euseden's parents.
- **HEWSDEN index** — the FindMyPast source directs "see also HEWSDEN." Pull the Hewsden marriages index for pre-1762 records that may document an earlier generation.
- **Mary Euseden's mother** — identified as [[mary-eusden-1771|Mary Euseden]] (1771–1825), buried Fenstanton, from burial register elimination. Maiden name still unknown. The Ely christening of 1808 may point to Cambridgeshire roots; finding Thomas's marriage record (probably ~1795–1796) would confirm her maiden name.
- **William and Henry Eusden** — birth and death dates unknown; find their census records to establish their life histories.

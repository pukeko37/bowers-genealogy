## Parents

[[richard-norman-1853|Richard Norman]] (1853–Deceased) married [[ann-elizabeth-randall-1854|Ann Elizabeth Randall]] (1854–1933) in **October 1875** at Hartlepool, Durham. Richard had come from Swavesey, Cambridgeshire; Ann was born in **Fenstanton, Huntingdonshire** — the same village where Richard's grandmother Ann Tabram was christened, and where Richard himself was baptised in 1853.

Richard's death date is unknown; Ann died in 1933, outliving him by an uncertain number of years.

## The Fenstanton thread and the Randall connection

Ann's Fenstanton birthplace is among the most striking details in this family. Fenstanton is where the Norman family's maternal roots ran deepest — through Ann Tabram (christened Fenstanton 1829), Mary Eusden (died and buried there c.1841), and Thomas Euseden (buried there November 1842). Richard Norman, Ann Tabram's grandson, married a woman from the same small Fenland village his grandmother had left forty years earlier.

Census research into the broader Stranton migration community adds a further dimension. The **Randall family was a major Fen Drayton presence in Stranton**: at least five Randall men from Fen Drayton, Cambridgeshire were in Stranton across the 1871–1891 censuses (Richard b.1834, John b.1847, John b.1848, Robert b.1846, William b.1846 — four to five separate households). Fen Drayton and Fenstanton are adjacent parishes separated only by a county boundary. Whether Ann Elizabeth Randall was from a Fenstanton branch of the same Fen Drayton Randall family, or whether her birthplace was recorded with characteristic imprecision for adjacent parishes, is not yet established.

What is clear is that when Richard Norman arrived in Stranton in 1875 and married Ann Randall that October, he was almost certainly marrying into a family already embedded in the fenland community around him. The Randalls had been in Stranton since at least 1871. His marriage was not a chance encounter with a local stranger — it was a marriage within the transplanted network. See [[swavesey-stranton-diaspora]] for the full analysis.

## Children

Eleven children, born 1878–1900 in West Hartlepool:

1. **Charles Norman** (1878–1878) — died in infancy; first of three sons named Charles
2. **Mary Elizabeth Norman** (1878–1908) — died aged 30
3. **Charles Norman** (~1880–1898) — died aged 18; second son named Charles
4. **Frances Ann Norman** (1882–Deceased)
5. **John Thomas Norman** (1885–Deceased)
6. **Richard Randall Norman** (1886–1969) — carries father's first name and mother's surname; lived to 83
7. **Willie H Norman** (1891–Deceased)
8. **Albert Norman** (1893–Deceased)
9. **George Norman** (1895–Deceased) — named after father's eldest brother, who lived nearby
10. **Annie Louisa Norman** (1898–1945)
11. **Charles Norman** (1900–1987) — third son named Charles; survived to 87

The 1901 census lists a "Mary E" (daughter, aged 12, born ~1889) who is distinct from Mary Elizabeth (born 1878, aged 23 in 1901) — suggesting there was a second daughter named Mary, possibly not recorded in the FamilySearch print view. "Annie Eliz Norman" (daughter, 3) in 1901 is Annie Louisa.

No person pages yet exist for any of the children.

## Family Life

The family lived at **13 Baily Street, Stranton** in 1881, and in West Hartlepool proper by 1901. Richard's occupation:

| Year | Occupation |
|------|-----------|
| 1881 | Labourer |
| 1891 | General Labourer |
| 1901 | Labourer (Cement Works) |
| 1911 | General Labourer, Corporation |

His entire Hartlepool career was unskilled manual labour. By 1901 he was working in the cement and chemical industries that were prominent in West Hartlepool's industrial economy. By 1911, as a Corporation labourer, he was in municipal employment — road maintenance, sanitation, or other public works for West Hartlepool Borough Council.

Ann managed a household of up to nine children. By 1911 seven were still at home; daughter Frances Ann (28) was unmarried and living with her parents.

The persistence of the name "Charles" through three sons — the first dying as an infant, the second at 18, the third surviving to 87 — is a striking feature of this family. Richard Randall Norman (1886–1969), combining both parents' names, is the most traceable descendant from living memory.

## Wider Family

Richard and his brother [[george-norman-1851|George Norman]] (see [[norman-garry-1875]]) lived within walking distance in Stranton and West Hartlepool through the 1880s and 1890s. George died in September 1903. Richard named his son **George Norman** (1895) while George was still alive — a living honour to a present brother, not a posthumous memorial. George himself had no George among his surviving sons.

A Swavesey neighbour, William Mole/Moule, lived near Richard in Stranton in both 1881 and 1891 — evidence the Swavesey-to-Hartlepool migration was wider than just the Norman brothers. The community that formed in Stranton may have included several families who left the same Cambridgeshire village together.

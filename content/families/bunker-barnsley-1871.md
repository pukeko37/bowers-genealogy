## Parents

[[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] (1845–1918) and [[margaret-barnsley-1850|Margaret Barnsley]] (1850–1922) married in 1871 at the **Wesleyan Chapel, Matlock Bath**, Derbyshire — roughly 13 months after Rebecca Tall's death. Caleb was 26, Margaret 21. The marriage is recorded in the Derbyshire Registrars Marriage Index (ref. RO/07/020) with the parish given as "Wesleyan Chapel" and the place as "Matlock Bath." See [[caleb-bunker-marriage-1871-derbyshire-index]].

Margaret was the daughter of [[thomas-barnsley-1817|Thomas Barnsley]] (1817–1890) and **Eliza Bradwall** — both from Bradwell in the Hope Valley, Derbyshire, married 1838. Thomas had spent his working years in Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire (where all four children were born), before moving to Matlock at some point before 1871. He was a versatile small retailer: "Dealer in Old and New Clothes" in 1851, "Stationer" in Matlock by 1871. The Barnsley family was in Matlock in 1871 in the same enumeration district as Caleb, exactly when he arrived from Bedfordshire. Margaret (20) was working as a shop assistant with her parents that year.

## Children

Six children, born across Matlock and briefly New Tupton:

1. [[annie-elizabeth-bunker-1872|Annie Elizabeth Bunker]] (1872–1951) — eldest; died aged ~79
2. [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker]] (1875–1917) — born **New Tupton, Derbyshire**; middle name carries mother's maiden name
3. [[maggie-eliza-bunker-1876|Maggie Eliza Bunker]] (1876–1915) — died aged ~39
4. [[agnes-maud-bunker-1878|Agnes Maud Bunker]] (1878–1919) — died aged ~41
5. [[may-gertrude-bunker-1880|May Gertrude Bunker]] (1880–1960) — longest-lived daughter
6. [[ernest-caleb-bunker-1882|Ernest Caleb Bunker]] (1882–1967) — youngest; named for his father; died aged ~85

Three of the six — Herbert, Maggie, and Agnes — predeceased their father Caleb (died 1918). Only Annie, May, and Ernest survived him. Ernest outlived his half-brother [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] by 35 years.

The middle name **Barnsley** given to Herbert mirrors Caleb's practice in his first marriage, where Frederick was named William **Tall** Bunker after his mother Rebecca Tall. Caleb consistently honoured his wives' surnames in his sons' names.

## Family Life

Caleb and Margaret spent their married life in Matlock through most of 1881, then the wider Derbyshire area through 1891, before settling in Derby. By 1901 and through 1911 they were at **60 High Street, Derby** — Caleb recorded as "Boot Maker and Small Shopkeeper" with several lodgers in the household, consistent with a combined shop and boarding house at that address. Their six children were born between 1872 and 1882.

Herbert's birth in **New Tupton** (~1875) rather than Matlock indicates Caleb briefly relocated or spent extended time at New Tupton around that year. This coincides precisely with his brother [[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]]'s move to the same village with his family (George's son Albert was also born in New Tupton ~1875). The two brothers were living in the same small north Derbyshire village at the same time; sister [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] was at nearby Pilsley. This was the only period when three Bunker siblings were in close geographic proximity as adults. See [[new-tupton-derbyshire]].

**The 1880 bankruptcy.** In October 1880, Caleb instituted proceedings for Liquidation by Arrangement in the County Court of Derbyshire — a negotiated composition with creditors rather than full personal bankruptcy. Two creditors' meetings were convened in Derby in November 1880. The *London Gazette* notices describe him as of **Matlock Bath** (the spa village in the Derwent valley, more precise than the "Matlock" of census records) and give his trade as **Boot and Shoe Maker, and Toy and Smallware Dealer**. Crucially, he was **trading as Thomas Barnsley** — his father-in-law's name. Whether [[thomas-barnsley-1817|Thomas Barnsley]] had originally capitalised or formally lent his name to the shop, the business was publicly known by the Barnsley name rather than Caleb's own. The timing is stark: May Gertrude was born in 1880, so the crisis unfolded while Margaret was either pregnant or nursing a newborn. The arrangement apparently succeeded — Caleb was still at his combined trade in the 1881 census. See [[london-gazette-1880-10-19-caleb-bunker-bankruptcy]] and [[london-gazette-1880-11-05-caleb-bunker-bankruptcy]].

By 1881 Caleb's occupation had evolved to **Toys Small Dealer Boot & Shoe Maker** — a combined trade suggesting he supplemented shoe-making income with a small retail sideline. The toy trade continued into the 1890s: a *Derby Telegraph* report from January 1894 records the theft of balls and a top from the till at his shop in **Traffic Street, Derby**, confirming he was still selling toys over a decade after that census entry. Margaret gave evidence at Derby Borough Court; she had been running the shop alone when the theft occurred, tracked the offenders, and engaged the detective. The boys (both 12) received six strokes of the birch rod. See [[derby-telegraph-1894-01-25-bunker-theft]]. [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] (Caleb's son from his first marriage, age 11) is confirmed in the 1881 household at Matlock, living with Caleb and Margaret alongside the young family.

**Annie Elizabeth Bunker** (born 1872) had married by 1911, appearing in that year's census as **Annie Elizabeth Borrington** — her married surname. She was still living at 60 High Street with her parents that year.

Caleb's son from his first marriage, [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]], was raised by his maternal grandmother [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] in Maulden and had limited contact with his father. Whether Frederick personally knew his six half-siblings is not documented. However, Frederick's son [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Roland]] did maintain contact with this branch of the family: as a boy he used to stay with [[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert]] and his family during school holidays, and he later took his own son [[alan-geoffrey-bowers-1928|Alan]] to visit [[ernest-caleb-bunker-1882|Ernest]] and his two sons in Bristol in the 1930s. The connection between the two Bunker branches was alive across at least two generations.

Caleb died suddenly on **22 May 1918** at Lullington, Derbyshire — a small village near Swadlincote, away from home on business. He had moved from 60 High Street to **Osmaston Road, Derby** at some point after 1911. Obituary notices appeared in Derby papers on 23–24 May; he was buried at Nottingham Road Cemetery, Chaddesden, Derby, on 25 May 1918. Margaret survived him by four years, dying in April 1922. Both are buried at Nottingham Road Cemetery. See [[caleb-bunker-death-notices-1918]].

## Open Questions

- What became of Annie, Maggie, and May — did they remain in Derby or disperse?
- Did Frederick Bunker have any personal contact with his half-siblings?
- Herbert died in 1917 during the war years — was his death war-related?
- Agnes died in 1919 — possibly influenza pandemic?
- What were the names of Ernest's two sons in Bristol?
- When did Ernest move to Bristol, and what was his occupation?
- **Trading as Thomas Barnsley** — the 1881 census shows Thomas describing himself as "Formerly Shop Keeper" six months after the bankruptcy, having retired to Darley Dale. The probate entry (1890) describes him as "formerly of Matlock Bath in the Parish of Matlock" — the same Matlock Bath address given in the bankruptcy notices. The most coherent reading: Thomas held the shop premises and trading name; Caleb ran the day-to-day business and pivoted the trade from stationery to boots and toys. When the crisis came in 1880, Caleb bore the bankruptcy; Thomas retired. Whether Thomas was a silent partner, a landlord, or simply a name lent for credit purposes remains open. The outcome of the creditors' composition and any subsequent court record may survive in Derbyshire archives.

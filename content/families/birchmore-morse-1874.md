## Parents

[[george-birchmore-plummer-jr-1850|George Birchmore Plummer Jr]] (1850–1920) and [[elizabeth-sarah-morse-1852|Elizabeth Sarah Morse]] (1852–1928) married on **25 July 1871 at Kentish Town, Middlesex** — three years before Rosa was born. They raised their family in [[st-pancras|St Pancras]], London. George used the name George Birchmore rather than the full Birchmore Plummer; all four children used the Birchmore surname. Elizabeth was born in Swindon, Wiltshire and had moved to London by 1871.

## Children

Four children, all using the Birchmore surname:

1. [[rosa-birchmore-1874|Rosa Birchmore]] (abt 1874–?) — married George Henry Lomax, September 1898, Pancras (GRO confirmed); fate after 1898 not yet established
2. [[edith-eleanor-birchmore-1876|Edith Eleanor Birchmore]] (1876–1947) — married Alfred Browne, 1898, St Pancras; no children recorded; remained in London; probate London 1947
3. [[george-william-birchmore-1880|George William Birchmore]] (1880–1943) — no documented spouse; moved to Sidmouth, Devon by 1911; Grocer Servant; died Exeter, Devon; probate Devon 1943
4. [[florence-eliza-birchmore-1884|Florence Eliza Birchmore]] (abt 1884–1951) — married Charles Henry Eyland (born 1877); no marriage date/place recorded; no children listed; died Devon 1951

## Family Life

The family lived in St Pancras for their entire London period — 1881, 1891, 1901, and 1911 census records all place them in St Pancras, latterly at St Jude, Gray's Inn Road (King's Cross end of Gray's Inn Road). George Jr was born in Whipsnade in 1850 and christened there — his family's roots were in [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], not London. His move to St Pancras came as an adult; the reason is not documented.

George Jr's half-sister [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] married [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]] in 1883 and moved to [[hosier-lane-city-of-london|Hosier Lane]], Smithfield — about one mile south of St Pancras. The two branches of the Birchmore family were therefore living in adjacent London neighbourhoods throughout the 1880s–1900s, though no direct contact between them is documented.

Among the next generation, a southwest dispersal pattern is visible for at least two of the four children:

- **George William** → Sidmouth, Devon by 1911; died Exeter 1943
- **Florence** → Devon, died 1951
- **Edith** → stayed in London
- **Rosa** → unknown after 1898 marriage; a "Rosa Lomax" appears in a Bournemouth probate (1934/35) but the link to this family is not yet established

Whether Rosa joined her siblings in the southwest remains an open question.

George William's move to Sidmouth appears to have been the catalyst. He went first, by 1911. His father George Jr followed and died at St Thomas (Exeter) in 1920. His mother Elizabeth died in Devon in 1928, probate administered by George William; her estate was **£2177 18s. 10d.** — substantial for the period. Her address of death was Belmont, **Withycombe Raleigh** (Exmouth area), not Sidmouth itself, so she and George William were in adjacent but distinct East Devon locations. Florence died in Devon in 1951.

This is a family that spent its entire working life in a single London neighbourhood — St Pancras, Gray's Inn Road — and then at least two of the four children, plus both parents, moved to the Devon coast after 1900.

## The Milkman and Mason Connection

George Jr's occupation as a milkman raises a tantalising possibility. The [[mason-james-1874|Mason family]] — Richard and Catherine Mason, Welsh immigrants — ran a **milk shop at 27 Hosier Lane**, Smithfield. Richard Mason died in 1890, leaving his sons (including [[john-james-mason-1878|John Mason]] who later married [[mabel-annie-norman-1883|Mabel Norman]]) to carry on.

The geographic and occupational overlap is striking:
- GBP Jr was a milkman operating out of St Pancras / Gray's Inn Road
- The Masons ran a milk shop at Hosier Lane, Smithfield — about one mile south
- Smithfield was the centre of London's meat and dairy trade; milkmen and milk shops in this corridor would naturally do business together
- GBP Jr's half-sister Ellen Norman lived *at* Hosier Lane from the early 1890s

It is plausible that George Jr, as a milkman supplying the Smithfield area, had a working relationship with the Mason milk shop — and that this is part of the web of connections that eventually produced the double Mason-Norman marriages of 1912 and 1918.

**This remains a hypothesis.** No document directly links GBP Jr to the Mason family. The coincidence of occupation and geography is suggestive, not proven. The most productive next research step would be to find George Jr's delivery records, trade directories, or census occupation entries that might pinpoint where he sold milk.

## Open Questions

- Did GBP Jr have any contact with his half-siblings Ellen, Elizabeth, and Thomas Birchmore — who were growing up in the same city?
- Is there evidence of a direct connection between the Birchmore milkman and the Mason milk shop?
- What became of Rosa and George Henry Lomax after 1898? (1901 and 1911 census searches not yet done)
- Is the "Rosa Lomax" of Bournemouth (probate 1935, husband Alfred Lomax) the same person as Rosa Birchmore? The name discrepancy (George Henry vs Alfred) and missing documentary chain mean this cannot currently be assumed.
- What drew George William to Sidmouth specifically — employment, family connection, or the seaside resort boom?
- Who was Charles Henry Eyland? Origins, occupation, and fate after Florence's death in Devon are unknown.
- Did Alfred Browne (Edith's husband) survive her? He is listed as "Living" in FamilySearch with no death date.

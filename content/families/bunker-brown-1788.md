## Parents

[[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] (1764–1824) and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]] (abt 1769–1842) married on 31 December 1788 at St Paul's Walden, Hertfordshire. Lydia was from **Cranfield, Bedfordshire**, the daughter of [[edward-brown-1732|Edward Brown]] (abt 1732) and his wife Lydia (maiden name unknown). Cranfield is about 6 miles from Millbrook; the St Paul's Walden marriage is slightly unusual given both families were Bedfordshire-based, but the parish lies just over the county border between the two villages. Both lived out their lives in Millbrook, Bedfordshire.

James Thomas was the son of [[thomas-bunker-1738|Thomas Bunker]] (1738–1825) and [[martha-sinfield|Martha Sinfield]], who had married at Millbrook in 1761. Lydia Brown's origins are unknown; her maiden name survives only in the marriage record.

## Children

All children were born in Millbrook. The family's religious practice appears to have been Nonconformist — most likely **Wesleyan Methodist**: seven of the eight children were christened together at Millbrook parish church on **7 April 1806** in a single mass ceremony, despite birth years spanning 1790 to 1804. This is the standard pattern of Dissenting families registering their children belatedly with the Church of England for civil record purposes while actually worshipping in a chapel. A Wesleyan Methodist chapel was later built in Millbrook in 1858 (part of the Bedford Circuit), and the next generation's records confirm the affiliation: George Bunker's daughter was christened Wesleyan Methodist in the Bedford Circuit in 1860, and Caleb Bunker married at a Nonconformist chapel in Bedford in 1868. See [[millbrook-wesleyan-methodist-chapel]].

Note: The Caleb Bunker (1790) above is a different person from [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker (1845)]] who is Roland's grandfather. The name Caleb recurs in this family; so do Ebenezer, Ruth, and Phoebe in the children of Caleb (1790) and Joseph (1792).

**James (1807) not in the Millbrook baptism register.** The Millbrook parish register records the seven-child mass christening of April 1806 in detail, including Martha (1801). But James, born 1807, does not appear in the register at all through at least 1811. The family apparently used the 1806 ceremony as a one-off clearing of civil requirements and did not return to the established church for subsequent children. This is the clearest evidence yet of a sustained Nonconformist commitment rather than mere occasional conformity.

## Family Life

James Thomas Bunker died in 1824 when his youngest son James was just seventeen. Lydia remained in Millbrook for another 18 years, recorded in the 1841 census before dying on 6 September 1842. The family's roots in Millbrook were deep — James Thomas was born and died there, his father Thomas (1738) was christened there, and his grandfather Thomas (1704) had settled there before 1738.

## Wider Family

- **James Thomas's parents:** Thomas Bunker (1738–1825) + Martha Sinfield. See [[bunker-sinfield-1761]].
- **Grandparents:** Thomas Bunker (1704–1784) + Mary Reade (1702–1775). See [[bunker-reade-1728]].
- **Next generation:** James Bunker (1807) continued the Millbrook line, marrying Elizabeth Watts in 1830 and fathering nine children including [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker (1845)]]. See [[bunker-watts-1830]].

## Wider Family — The Stevington Branch

The two eldest brothers moved to Stevington, Bedfordshire together around 1814 and both married there — Joseph in October 1814, Caleb in May 1815. Joseph was buried at Stevington Baptist Chapel Chapelyard, revealing that the Stevington branch adopted Baptist affiliation, distinct from the Wesleyan Methodism of the main Bunker-Watts line.

Both brothers had children, and those children were christened together in a single mass ceremony on **26 September 1824** at Stevington — Caleb's children Ebenezer (1820), Ephraim (1821), Ruth (1822), and Lydia (1826), and Joseph's children Ebenezer (1822) and James (1824) — repeating the Dissenting practice of their own 1806 christening.

**The Australian emigration.** Two of these cousins emigrated to New South Wales together. [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer Bunker (1822)]], son of Joseph, married **Sarah Cocking** at Stevington on **28 November 1844** — taking his new wife with him. [[ephraim-bunker-1821|Ephraim Bunker (1821)]], son of Caleb, travelled as a single man. Both sailed on the ***Elizabeth***, arriving in New South Wales on **31 March 1845** as assisted immigrants; both were engaged on arrival to **Robert Coopershaw of Rocky Point, Cooks River** (south of Sydney). From there both cousins moved to the **Camden / Campbelltown** district.

Their subsequent paths diverged. **Ebenezer** settled at Campbelltown, where his first child was christened in September 1850. He and Sarah had seven children; he died on 21 June 1902 at Campbelltown, buried at St Peters Anglican Church Cemetery. **Ephraim** remained single for seven years after arrival, marrying **Mary Ann Betts** at Camden, NSW in 1852. He later moved inland to **Crookwell** in the Southern Tablelands, where he died on 5 July 1887 and is buried at Crookwell General Cemetery. See [[ephraim-bunker-1821]], [[ebenezer-bunker-1822]], [[ephraim-bunker-1821-familysearch]], [[ebenezer-bunker-1822-familysearch]].

**Joseph's line at Stevington.** While Ebenezer emigrated, Joseph's other surviving sons stayed in Bedfordshire. [[james-bunker-1824|James Bunker (1824–1904)]] married **Sophia Panter** at Stevington on Christmas Day 1850 and spent his entire life at Silver Street, Stevington, working as an agricultural labourer. His daughter [[ruth-bunker-1855|Ruth Bunker (1855)]] is the most fully documented of the Stevington third generation: she brought a bastardy order against fellow-servant George Draper Whiting in June 1880, married him three months later, had eleven children, and emigrated to Sherbrooke, Québec in 1885. John Bunker (1817–1887), Joseph's eldest surviving son, remained at Stevington until his death but is otherwise unresearched. See [[bunker-panter-1850]].

Lydia Bunker (1804) also shows Baptist affiliation: her children with Thomas Inskip were registered at Mill Street Baptist, Bedford. Whether this reflects Thomas Inskip's denomination or a broader family openness to Baptist practice in Bedford is unclear.

## Open Questions

- ~~Was Lydia Brown from St Paul's Walden?~~ **Partly resolved.** Lydia Brown was from **Cranfield, Bedfordshire** — daughter of [[edward-brown-1732|Edward Brown]] and his wife Lydia — not from St Paul's Walden. The St Paul's Walden marriage (1788) remains slightly puzzling: both families were Bedfordshire-based, and the parish is just over the Hertfordshire border, roughly equidistant from Cranfield and Millbrook. Joseph Bunker (1792)'s FamilySearch birthplace is listed as "Saint Pauls Walden" — if accurate rather than a data error, Lydia may have had family or personal connections there that brought her to that parish for the marriage and occasionally for births.
- What became of Martha Bunker (born 21 Dec 1801)? The Millbrook parish baptism register confirms her birth year as 1801 and records her at the 1806 mass christening — she was alive on 7 April 1806, ruling out death in very early childhood. No marriage, burial, census, or later record has yet been found. She may have moved away and married under an unidentified name, or died between 1806 and 1841 without a traceable record.
- ~~Ephraim's Bedfordshire marriage record has not yet been identified. Ebenezer's Australian marriage is unconfirmed.~~ **Resolved.** Ephraim did not marry in Bedfordshire — he arrived in NSW in March 1845 as a single man and married Mary Ann Betts in Camden, NSW in 1852. Ebenezer married Sarah Cocking at Stevington on 28 November 1844, before emigrating. The previous wiki text had the two marriages reversed. Both cousins are now confirmed on the same vessel: the *Elizabeth*, 31 March 1845 (NSW Assisted Immigrants Inwards register). Ebenezer (age 23) and wife Sarah (age 20) appear separately from Ephraim (age 24, alone), both parties engaged to Robert Coopershaw at Rocky Point, Cooks River.
- What happened to Joseph's surviving children (John 1817–1887, [[ebenezer-bunker-1822|Ebenezer 1822–1902]], [[james-bunker-1824|James 1824–1904]])? Ebenezer emigrated to Australia in 1845. James stayed at Silver Street, Stevington for his entire life; his family is documented. John (1817–1887) is unresearched. Newspaper evidence identifies several third-generation Stevington Bunkers: **[[ruth-bunker-1855|Ruth Bunker (1855)]]**, daughter of James (1824), brought a bastardy order against George Draper Whiting in June 1880, married him in September 1880, had 11 children, and emigrated to Sherbrooke, Quebec in 1885; **"J. Bunker"** at the 1896 Stevington Flower Show (2nd prize, damsons) is James (1824) himself aged 72; **"Miss Bunker"** in Stevington/Stagsden community events 1886–1892 is likely one of James's unmarried daughters (Jane 1852, Hannah 1864, or Mary Ann 1866). **Jane Bunker (aged 20 in 1872)**, charged with theft at Stevington, may be James's daughter Jane (1852) — she would be ~20 in 1872. See [[bunker-panter-1850]], [[bunker-stevington-newspapers-findmypast]].
- Did Phoebe (1796) move to Bedford before her death in 1849, or did she move directly from Millbrook? The 1841 census confirms she was still in Millbrook; something prompted the move before September 1849.
- ~~Was the family affiliated with a specific Nonconformist congregation?~~ **Probable answer: Wesleyan Methodist (main line) and Baptist (Stevington/Bedford branches).** The 1806 mass christening is consistent with Dissenter practice. The next generation confirms: Wesleyan Methodist for the James (1807) / Bedford-Millbrook line; Baptist for Joseph (1792)'s Stevington line and Lydia (1804)'s Inskip family in Bedford. Bedford Circuit records (Wesleyan Methodist Archive, Manchester) and Stevington Baptist records may illuminate both branches.

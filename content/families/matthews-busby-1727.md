## Parents

[[peter-matthews-1702|Peter Matthews (~1702–1748)]] was the son of [[peter-matthews-1665|Peter Matthews (1665)]] and [[mary-jenkins-1665|Mary Jenkins]], buried at Dunstable 26 June 1748.

[[mary-busby-1709|Mary Busby]] (1709–1760) was born 2 February 1709 at Dunstable and christened at Millbrook. Her parents were Thomas Busby (1674–1724) and Elizabeth (1660–Deceased). She is also recorded as "Mary Bushbee."

They married on **21 May 1727** (no place recorded).

## Children

Nine children, all christened at Dunstable:

1. **Frances Matthews** (christened 6 Nov 1728)
2. **James Matthews** (~1729–1740) — died aged ~11
3. **Mary Matthews** (1731–1731) — died in infancy
4. **[[peter-matthews-1734|Peter Matthews]]** (christened 28 Jun 1734) — our line; died Kensworth 1812
5. **Elizabeth Matthews** (1737–1737) — died in infancy
6. **James Matthews** (~1740–1754) — second James; buried 28 May 1754 Dunstable, aged ~14
7. **Thomas Matthews** (christened 23 Sep 1741, Dunstable) — survived; adult life unresearched
8. **Russell Matthews** (christened 27 Jul 1744) — fate unresearched
9. **Sarah Matthews** (christened 30 Jul 1746) — fate unresearched

## Family Life

The family lost three children young — Mary (1731) and Elizabeth (1737) both died in the year of their birth, and the first James died aged ~11. The second James died at ~14. Peter (1702) himself was buried at Dunstable in 1748, leaving Mary a widow; she lived until 1760.

The four younger surviving children — Thomas (1741), Russell (1744), Sarah (1746) — have not been further traced. Their fate after growing to adulthood is unknown.

## The Thomas Matthews (1741) Question

**Thomas Matthews (1741)** — the seventh child — is the key unresearched figure in this family. The question of whether Thomas (1767), patriarch of the parallel LDS/Wilson Matthews family at Whipsnade, was his son is the central open question for the entire Whipsnade Matthews cluster. If Thomas (1741) had a son Thomas in 1767:

- He would have been ~26 at the time — entirely plausible
- That son would have been born in Kensworth, where his uncle Peter (1734) had settled — also plausible
- Thomas (1767) and Peter (1770) would be **first cousins**, both grandsons of Peter (1702) and Mary Busby

Establishing what happened to Thomas Matthews (1741) after his 1741 christening would resolve the longest-standing open question in the Whipsnade Matthews research.

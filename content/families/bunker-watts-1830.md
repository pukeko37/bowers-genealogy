## Parents

[[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] (1807–1852) and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]] (1810–1868) married on 22 November 1830 in Millbrook, Bedfordshire. James was the youngest son of [[james-thomas-bunker-1764|James Thomas Bunker]] and [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]]. Elizabeth was born in Cranfield, Bedfordshire — the daughter of [[john-watts-1790|John Watts]] (1790–1814, died when Elizabeth was four) and [[sarah-capel-1791|Sarah Capel]] (1791–1851+), themselves from the Cranfield/Lidlington cluster. See [[watts-capel-1809]].

James died in 1852 when his youngest child Comfort was only four. Elizabeth survived as a widow until 1868, raising the younger children. A note in George Bunker's record states the family moved from Millbrook to Bedford in 1852 on James's death — the entire household relocated. Elizabeth's death in August 1868 is now confirmed by the GRO Death Registration Index (registered as "Elizabeth Bunker," volume 3B, page 230) and a Find a Grave burial record placing her at **Foster Hill Road Cemetery, Bedford** — the same burial ground used by the Tall family, into which Caleb married later that same year.

## Children

Nine children, all born in Millbrook:

1. [[john-watts-bunker-1831|John Watts Bunker]] (1831–1899) — remained in Bedford area; died Ampthill
2. [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]] (1833–1922) — married [[samuel-plackett-lane-1841|Samuel Plackett Lane]] 1863; widowed 1872; Bedford then Barton Almshouse Turvey; died aged 89. See [[bunker-lane-1863]].
3. [[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]] (1836–1917) — married [[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]] 1858 Bedford; 5 children; Bedford then New Tupton/Derby, Derbyshire. See [[bunker-pedder-1858]].
4. [[david-bunker-1837|David Bunker]] (1837–1920) — married Marian Bentley 1859 Bedford; 4 children; Groom → railway Storekeeper; Peterborough 1871–1920; died aged ~83
5. [[charles-bunker-1839|Charles Bunker]] (1839–?) — married Maria (Devonshire); west London (Paddington, Kensington, Hammersmith); Dairyman → Carman → Gardener; death unknown
6. [[martha-sarah-bunker-1841|Martha Sarah Bunker]] (1841–1901) — married **Joseph Sinfield** 25 May 1863 St Paul's Bedford; 2 daughters; Luton 1881–1901; died 7 Jul 1901 as "Martha Sarah Sinfield". See [[bunker-sinfield-1863]].
7. [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe Alice Bunker]] (1843–1923) — married [[samuel-fensome-1842|Samuel Fensome]] 1863 Bedford; 8 children; Pilsley, Derbyshire then Hucknall Torkard, Nottinghamshire. See [[bunker-fensome-1863]].
8. [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] (1845–1918) — Matlock/Derby; Roland Bunker's grandfather
9. [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann Bunker]] (1848–?) — known as "Jane Bunker" from 1871; lived in Martha's Sinfield household until 1892; married Henry Ewington 28 March 1892 Luton; straw hat sewer throughout

## Family Life

This was a large Millbrook family that dispersed widely after James Bunker's death in 1852. The surviving children scattered to Bedford (John, Lydia, George initially), Peterborough (David), London (Charles), Luton (Martha, Comfort), Derbyshire (George, Phoebe, Caleb), and Huntingdonshire/Peterborough (David).

**1841 household at Millbrook:** The 1841 census captures the family at its fullest extent — eight children under one roof, with [[lydia-brown-1769|Lydia Brown]] (70), James's mother, also resident. She would die the following September 1842. James and Elizabeth are both recorded as 30; the children are John (9), Lydia (8), George (6), David (4), Charles (2), and infant Martha (0). This is the only census where the grandmother appears in the household.

**1851 household at Millbrook:** James (43, head, **Agricultural Labourer**), Elizabeth (41, Lace Maker). Present children: George (15), David (13), Charles (11), Martha (10), Phoebe (8), Caleb (5), Comfort (2). John (~20) is absent — he had already left home. James died in 1852 as an agricultural labourer in Millbrook; his wife's lace-making provided a vital second income in a labouring household. This was the classic Bedfordshire domestic economy: a field labourer's wages supplemented by the wife's pillow lace work.

**1861 household at Dane Street, Bedford:** The 1861 census captures the family at a transitional moment — nine years after James's death, seven working-age children under one roof at Dane Street, St Paul's parish, Bedford. Elizabeth (51, widowed) was head of household and a **lace maker**, tapping into Bedford's major pillow lace industry. Occupations of the children present:

- [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia]] (27) — house servant
- [[charles-bunker-1839|Charles]] (23) — labourer
- [[martha-sarah-bunker-1841|Martha]] (20) — bonnet sewer
- [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] (18) — bonnet sewer
- [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb]] (15) — shoe maker (apprenticeship)
- [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort Ann]] (12, listed as "Ann") — scholar

John, George, and David were not present — already established elsewhere. The three eldest sons had left; the household was Elizabeth and her daughters and youngest son. See [[caleb-bunker-1861-census]].

The Derbyshire migration was a notable cluster: George and Phoebe both settled in the Tupton/Pilsley area, and Caleb settled at Matlock. The convergence is tighter than previously known: both George's son Albert (~1875, New Tupton) and Caleb's son Herbert T. B. (~1875, New Tupton) were born in the same Derbyshire village in the same year — placing the two brothers in close physical proximity at New Tupton around 1875. George moved as a married man with children; Phoebe moved with her husband Samuel Fensome and their growing family — their last Bedford-born child was Reginald Edwin (1873), and their first Pilsley-born child was Sidney Thomas (1877), pinning the Fensome migration to c.1873–1877; Caleb arrived as a widower in 1871, the first of the three. What drew them to this specific part of Derbyshire — whether work opportunities in the coalfields, the railway, or simply one sibling following another — is not documented.

The Luton connection is also notable: Martha and Comfort both settled there, pre-dating the next generation's arrival. When Frederick Bunker (Caleb's son) later settled in Luton as a bricklayer, he was not the first Bunker in the town — his aunts Martha and Comfort had been there for decades.

## Religious affiliation

The family's Nonconformist practice is well evidenced and most likely **Wesleyan Methodist**. James Thomas Bunker mass-christened seven children at Millbrook church on 7 April 1806 — the standard practice of Dissenters registering belatedly with the Church of England. George Bunker's daughter Sarah Ann was christened at a Wesleyan Methodist chapel in the Bedford Circuit in December 1860. Caleb married Rebecca Tall at Howard Chapel, Bedford, a Nonconformist chapel. The naming pattern — Caleb, Ebenezer, Ruth, Phoebe, Comfort — is saturated with Old Testament and Puritan virtue names, consistent with Wesleyan practice. A Wesleyan Methodist chapel was built in Millbrook in 1858, under the Bedford Circuit that also covered the Bunkers in Bedford; the community behind it likely predated the building. See [[millbrook-wesleyan-methodist-chapel]] and [[millbrook]].

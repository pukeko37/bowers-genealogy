## Parents

[[peter-matthews-1770|Peter Matthews]] (1770–?) was born in Kensworth, Hertfordshire and christened there on 7 January 1770. He was the son of Peter Matthews (1734–1812) and Susannah Durrant (1739–1821), who had married at Flamstead, Hertfordshire in 1761. He married [[mary-walker-1766|Mary Walker]] (1766–1859) on 28 June 1798 at [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], Bedfordshire, where they settled and raised their family. Mary was the daughter of Richard Walker and Mary (surname unknown) of Kensworth.

## Children

Eight children, all christened at Whipsnade (except Richard, christened at Studham):

1. **Mary Matthews** (christened 12 May 1799, died 1871)
2. **[[susan-matthews-1800|Susan Matthews]]** (17 September 1800 – 20 November 1857) — married [[thomas-batchelar-1803|Thomas Batchelar]] 1826; mother of [[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]]
3. **Peter Matthews** (christened 24 October 1802, died 1892)
4. **Thomas Matthews** (christened 8 April 1804)
5. **James Matthews** (christened 25 March 1806, died 1889)
6. **Sarah Matthews** (christened 19 June 1808, died 1869) — married John Grace; in 1851 in Studham with mother Mary Walker living as mother-in-law
7. **Richard Matthews** (christened 21 April 1810, Studham, died 1883)
8. **John Matthews** (christened 1813, died 1890)

The family was entirely rooted in the Whipsnade/Studham area of Bedfordshire. Unlike the adjacent [[matthews-wilson-1801|Matthews-Wilson]] family (Thomas Matthews and Mary Wilson), no emigration to Utah is recorded for any member of this branch.

## Family Life

Peter and Mary settled on Whipsnade Green. The 1841 census places Peter (70) and Mary (70) at "On The Green," Whipsnade, with two young children in the household (likely grandchildren Elizabeth and Sarah Matthews, aged 9 and 4 respectively). By this point all eight of their children had left home — Susan had married in 1826, and the youngest, John (1813), was 28.

Mary outlived Peter by several years. By 1851 she was widowed and living in Studham with her daughter Sarah's household (John and Sarah Grace), described as "Pauper Formerly on Ag Lab Wife" and aged 84. She died 20 November 1859 at an advanced age — probably around 93 or 89 depending on which birth year is correct (see [[mary-walker-1766]]).

## Wider Family

The Peter Matthews (1770) family is distinct from the Thomas Matthews (1767) + Mary Wilson family which produced [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] (GBP Sr's first wife) and several LDS emigrants. Both Matthews families were rooted in Kensworth and Whipsnade across the same period, almost certainly connected in an earlier generation. George Birchmore Plummer's first wife was Anne Matthews (from the Wilson line); his second wife Eliza Batchelor was the granddaughter of Peter Matthews (this line) — making the Matthews family of Whipsnade the in-law family for GBP Sr through two consecutive marriages.

## Open Questions

- Were Peter Matthews (1770) and Thomas Matthews (1767) related? Both from Kensworth, same generation. The Houchen End evidence (see below) strengthens the case.
- What happened to Thomas (1804), James (1806, 1889) after leaving Whipsnade? Peter (1802)'s family is now documented — see [[matthews-gates-1825]].
- Is Peter Matthews (1734–1812, father of Peter 1770) related to the Thomas Matthews (1767) branch — i.e., is there a common ancestor in the Kensworth/Flamstead Matthews family?

### The Houchen End convergence

[[peter-matthews-1802|Peter Matthews (1802)]]'s son [[william-mathews-1837|William Mathews (1837–1914)]] spent his entire life at **Houchen End**, Whipsnade — the same hamlet recorded for the [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews Sr.]] household (the LDS-connected branch) and for [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer Sr.]] Two Matthews families — the Peter (1770) line and the Thomas (1767) line — both from Kensworth, both settling in Whipsnade, both at the same hamlet address across multiple generations. This is difficult to explain except through kinship. The most likely reading is that the two lines share a common ancestor in the Kensworth Matthews family, and that Houchen End was effectively a Matthews enclave within the parish. Documenting this would require working backward through Peter Matthews (1734) and whatever Thomas Matthews preceded Thomas (1767).

## Parents

[[peter-matthews-1802|Peter Matthews]] (1802–1892) was the third son of [[peter-matthews-1770|Peter Matthews (1770)]] and [[mary-walker-1766|Mary Walker]] of Whipsnade. He married **[[sarah-gates-1803|Sarah Gates]]** (1803–1851) on **17 January 1825** at Studham — confirmed by multiple marriage records, though one register entry reads "Sarah Yates or Gates."

[[sarah-gates-1803|Sarah]] was born at **Aldbury, Hertfordshire** (christened 27 November 1803), not a local Bedfordshire woman but from across the county border near Tring. Her parents were **Joseph Gates** (1781) and **Elizabeth Inward** (1780–1843), who had married in June 1803 just months before Sarah's birth. The Studham marriage venue makes geographic sense: Studham was the nearest Bedfordshire parish to Aldbury, suggesting Sarah retained ties to that side of the border even as she settled into Whipsnade life with Peter.

## Children

Eleven children are recorded, all christened at Whipsnade:

1. **Jonah Matthews** (1827–1833) — died aged ~6
2. **Sarah Mathews** (1827–Deceased) — fate unknown; no death or marriage record found
3. **George Matthews** (1829–1833) — died aged ~4
4. **Ann Mathews** (1830–1833) — died aged ~3
5. **John Mathews** (1833–1833) — died in infancy
6. **Hannah Mathews** (1834–1909) — survived to 74; no further record yet traced
7. **[[william-mathews-1837|William Mathews]]** (1837–1914) — farm labourer and roadman; lifelong Houchen End, Whipsnade; see below
8. **James Mathews** (1839–1839) — died in infancy
9. **David Matthews** (1841–1919) — survived; no further record yet traced
10. **Sarah Elizabeth Matthews** (1843–Deceased) — fate unknown
11. **Elizabeth Mathews** (1846–Deceased) — fate unknown

## Family Life

### The years of bereavement (1827–1839)

The decade from 1827 to 1839 was defined by child death. Of the first eight children, five died — Jonah, George, Ann, and John all within the single span of 1827–1833, and James in 1839. The concentration of deaths between 1829 and 1833 is striking: three children — George, Ann, John — died within roughly two years. Whether disease, a particularly harsh winter, or simple unluck drove this is not recorded, but the family's experience was far from unusual in rural Bedfordshire at the time, where infant and child mortality rates remained high throughout the 1820s and 1830s.

The survivors — Sarah (1827), Hannah (1834), William (1837), David (1841), Sarah Elizabeth (1843), Elizabeth (1846) — grew up in a household that had buried more children than it raised.

The 1841 census recorded the family at **Houchen End** and also captured Sarah's younger sister, **Charlotte Gates** (then 15), living with them — an unremarkable domestic arrangement, a younger sibling from across the border staying in a married sister's home. Charlotte died in 1846 aged about 24, having spent at least the early part of her adult life under the same roof.

### Sarah Gates's death and Peter's long widowhood

Sarah Gates died in 1851. She was 47 or 48. Peter was left with children ranging from about 5 (Elizabeth) to 24 (Sarah). He was widowed at roughly 49 and survived for another forty years, dying in 1892 at approximately 90. This longevity — exceptional but not unique in this family: his mother [[mary-walker-1766|Mary Walker]] lived to roughly 89–93, his father [[peter-matthews-1770|Peter Matthews (1770)]] at least to 71 — suggests a strain of resilience running through the line.

### William at Houchen End

Of the known surviving children, [[william-mathews-1837|William Mathews (1837–1914)]] is the best-documented. He lived his entire life at **Houchen End**, the western hamlet of Whipsnade parish. This address is the same one associated, across the same decades, with the household of [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews Sr.]] (the LDS-connected branch of the Matthews family) and with [[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer Sr.]] (GBP Sr.'s address is recorded as Houchen End in census records). That two Matthews families — descended from different lines, both ultimately rooted in Kensworth — were living at the same small hamlet address across multiple generations is difficult to explain except through kinship. See Open Questions below.

William married Elizabeth Bolton in 1862 and had nine children at Whipsnade, the eldest of whom, [[william-matthews-1865|William Matthews (1865)]], became a gamekeeper and lived briefly at [[studham|Studham]] in 1901.

## Wider Family

This family is the second confirmed generation of the [[matthews-walker-1798|Peter Matthews (1770) and Mary Walker]] line. It is distinct from the [[matthews-wilson-1801|Thomas Matthews (1767) and Mary Wilson]] line — the LDS family that produced [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]] and the Utah emigrants — but the two lines shared Whipsnade as their home territory and, through William (1837)'s Houchen End address, the same specific hamlet.

The connection runs deeper through marriage. GBP Sr.'s first wife was [[anne-matthews-1815|Anne Matthews]], from the Thomas (1767) line. His second wife [[eliza-batchelor-1829|Eliza Batchelor]] was a granddaughter of Peter Matthews (1770) — from this line. GBP Sr. thus married into the Matthews family twice, from two branches.

## Open Questions

- Were Peter Matthews (1770) and Thomas Matthews (1767) — the patriarchs of these two parallel Whipsnade Matthews families — related? The shared address of Houchen End across their descendants is now a second piece of evidence alongside the shared Kensworth origin. A common ancestor in the Kensworth/Flamstead Matthews family is the most likely explanation, but has not been documented.
- What became of Hannah (1834–1909), David (1841–1919), Sarah Elizabeth (1843), and Elizabeth (1846)? Did they remain in Whipsnade or disperse to Luton/Dunstable?
- How did the Gates family of Aldbury, Hertfordshire come to have connections in the Studham/Whipsnade area? Was there earlier migration, or is the Studham marriage purely a case of Peter and Sarah meeting across the parish boundary? Joseph Gates (1781) and Elizabeth Inward (1780–1843) could be worth tracing if the Gates family connection to this area deepens.

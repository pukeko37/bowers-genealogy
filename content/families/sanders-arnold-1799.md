## Parents

**[[joseph-sanders-1774|Joseph Sanders]]** (30 October 1774 – 22 June 1810) was baptised at Cardington, the son of John Sanders and his wife Elizabeth. He was a shoemaker — a skilled tradesman, a step above the farm labourer class, though still in the village artisan tier. He signed his name at the marriage, suggesting some literacy.

**[[rebecca-arnold-1778|Rebecca Arnold]]** (born about 1778 – buried August 1812) was the daughter of **Samuel Arnold**, a farmer at Cotton End, Cardington. Her father's will of 1790 left her £150 — a meaningful inheritance from a farming household. She signed with a mark at the marriage, suggesting no literacy. She was about 21 when she married.

They married at St Mary's Cardington on 14 May 1799, both of the same parish, by banns.

## Children

1. **Mary Saunders** — born 28 December 1800, christened 26 May 1801, Cardington
2. **Anne Saunders** — christened 29 July 1804, Cardington
3. **Sarah Saunders** — born c. 1808, christened 25 August 1811, Cardington (a delayed baptism of over three years)

**[[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]]** (baptised 25 August 1811) was Rebecca's daughter but not Joseph's: she was born thirteen months after Joseph's death, and is recorded as illegitimate. She is a half-sibling to Mary, Anne, and Sarah. Her father is unknown. She is not listed among the children of this family, but she grew up within its orbit — or rather, in its absence, as Rebecca died the following year and Elizabeth was left an orphan.

## Family life

Joseph and Rebecca's was a modest Cardington marriage — a shoemaker and a farmer's daughter, both illiterate. They had three children in ten years. Joseph died in June 1810 at 35, leaving Rebecca widowed with three young girls. Two years later, Rebecca died in August 1812, leaving all four children without parents. Elizabeth was barely fourteen months old.

Cotton End was the family's ground: Samuel Arnold had farmed there, and it was in Cotton End that Elizabeth Saunders would later raise her own family with Allen Thody.

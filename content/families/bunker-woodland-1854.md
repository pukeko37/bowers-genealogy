## Parents

[[john-watts-bunker-1831|John Watts Bunker]] (1831–1899) and **Ann Woodland** (born ~1835, Ampthill) married on **1 September 1854** at Ampthill, Bedfordshire. John was 22 and Ann was approximately 19. John was the eldest child of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]] of Millbrook; he moved to the Bedford/Ampthill area after his father's death in 1852 and never migrated to Derbyshire or London, unlike several of his siblings. His middle name Watts was his mother's maiden name — a family practice of carrying the maternal surname forward.

Ann Woodland was from Ampthill — the town where they married and where at least one child was christened. Her parentage has not been established.

John died in 1899 in Ampthill, aged ~67 (GRO vol 3B, page 252). Ann's death date and burial are not recorded.

## Children

Seven children, all born in the Bedford area:

1. **James Richard Bunker** (christened 5 August 1855, Millbrook) — the christening at Millbrook, two years after the family left, suggests a deliberate return to the family's ancestral village for the occasion. No further records traced.
2. **Ann Elizabeth Bunker** (born 21 September 1857, christened Ampthill) — no further records traced.
3. **John Bunker** (b. ~1860, Bedford) — present at a mass christening in 1867; no further records.
4. **Emma Bunker** (b. ~1863, Bedford) — present at a mass christening in 1867; no further records.
5. **Harry Bunker** (christened 1 September 1867, St Mary's Bedford) — the christening record at St Mary's Bedford on this date appears to cover Harry, John, and Emma together — three children of different ages christened on the same day, echoing the family's Nonconformist practice of delayed registration.
6. **George Bunker** (b. ~1869, Bedford) — no records traced beyond the birth.
7. **William Henry Bunker** (christened 5/6 March 1871, St Mary's Bedford) — the youngest child; christened at St Mary's at the time of the 1871 census.

No person pages have been created for these children; they are documented in the FamilySearch print view. Their fates after the early 1880s are unknown — none has been traced beyond the census or christening record that establishes their birth.

## Family Life

John and Ann settled in the Bedford and Ampthill area and remained there for life. Unlike his brothers George, Caleb, and Phoebe (who migrated to Derbyshire in the 1870s) or Charles (who went to London), John was the sibling who stayed closest to the family's original Bedfordshire base.

**Occupation.** John's working life was in the metal trades. In the **1871 census** he is recorded as a **Shoesmith** at St Mary's parish, Bedford — a metalworker who shapes and fits horseshoes, distinct from the boot and shoe *making* trade that his brother Caleb trained in and that Samuel Plackett Lane (Lydia's husband) also followed. By the **1881 census** John had moved to **Hammer Man** in Bedford Western Ward St Mary — heavy forging work, physically demanding, a trade closely related to blacksmithing. This occupational arc contrasts with his brother David's trajectory toward a white-collar railway stores role; John remained in manual metal work his entire life.

**Residence.** The family's 1871 and 1881 census addresses are both in St Mary's parish, Bedford. John died in Ampthill in 1899 — the town where he married and where his eldest child was christened.

**The mass christening pattern.** The 1867 event at St Mary's Bedford — where Harry, John, and Emma appear to have been christened together — mirrors the family's known Nonconformist practice: James Thomas Bunker christened seven children at Millbrook on a single day in April 1806. Similarly, John and Ann's first christening was back in Millbrook in 1855 despite the family having moved away. These patterns suggest the family maintained religious ties outside the Anglican parish structure, using the church for registration rather than as their primary place of worship.

## Parents

[[edward-bunker-1742|Edward Bunker]] (1742–1814) was born in Millbrook, the eighth child of Thomas Bunker and Mary Reade. He married **Mary Ashby** (1745–1813) on 16 November 1766 at Millbrook. Mary was three years his junior. The Ashby name was a local one; the family's origins are not yet documented.

Edward and Mary spent their entire lives in Millbrook. Mary died in 1813, Edward in February 1814 — neither outlived the other by more than a year. Both burials are confirmed in the Millbrook parish register (FMP): Mary at XAUTO/FHS/BEDFORDSHIRE/BUR/00319877, Edward at XAUTO/FHS/BEDFORDSHIRE/BUR/00319885. He was 71.

## Family Life

The family was rooted in Millbrook for Edward's entire life. His father Thomas Bunker (1704–1784) had settled the family there by the 1730s, and Edward never left. All ten of his children were christened at the Millbrook parish church. Edward's brother [[thomas-bunker-1738|Thomas Bunker (1738)]] — the direct ancestor of Roland Bunker — was also raising his family in Millbrook during the same decades, making this a period when multiple Bunker households overlapped in the same small village.

Of the ten children, two are traced in detail through the census record. [[william-bunker-1767|William (1767)]] married Sarah Beal and remained in Millbrook until his death in 1847; he appears in the 1841 census aged ~70 (actual 74 — the census rounded down to nearest 5), with Sarah and their son Samuel. [[james-bunker-1777|James (1777)]] had three wives and survived to 1864 aged ~87, the last of his generation in the village. Through his son [[john-bunker-1803|John (1803–1889)]], by first wife Sarah Pedder, James is the progenitor of all the Victorian and Edwardian Millbrook Bunkers — including [[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph (1840–1914)]], the longest-serving Millbrook resident of the line and the probable parish sexton, and the two first cousins killed at Passchendaele in 1917. See [[millbrook]] for the full census story.

The ninth child, [[mark-ashby-bunker-1789|Mark Ashby Bunker]], bears his mother's maiden name — a gesture preserved in the record even as he became the family's most notorious member. His sheep stealing conviction in 1817 and transportation to Australia ended his Millbrook story, though before transportation he had already fathered three illegitimate children in the area.

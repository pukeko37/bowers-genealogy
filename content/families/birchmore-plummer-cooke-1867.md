## Parents

[[george-birchmore-plummer-1813|George Birchmore Plummer]] (1813–1884) and [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] (1820–1904) married 26 December 1867 at [[whipsnade|Whipsnade]], Bedfordshire — nine months after George's second wife Eliza Batchelor died. This was George's third marriage.

Ruth was born in 1820 in Kensworth, Hertfordshire (christening date 10 August 1819 suggests born late 1819), the fifth of seven children of [[daniel-cook-1790|Daniel Cook]] (abt 1790–1857) and [[mary-birchmore-1785|Mary Birchmore]] (1785–?). Ruth's mother was a Birchmore from Whipsnade — a family that may share ancestry with GBP Sr's own Birchmore line, potentially making George and Ruth second cousins before their marriage. See [[cook-birchmore-1810]].

## Children

No children from this marriage. However, Ruth brought three illegitimate children from before the marriage:

1. [[charles-cook-1847|Charles Cook]] (1847–?) — born Whipsnade; lost to record after 1861
2. [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] (1848–1937) — born Market Street, Hertfordshire; lifelong Whipsnade resident; death registered in the Luton district
3. [[lucy-cook-1856|Lucy Cook]] (1856–1930) — the most significant, as she became [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore's]] step-sister

Lucy was only four years older than Ellen. They were raised together after this marriage, more like sisters than step-sisters. This relationship is the crucial link connecting the Norman family (through Ellen) to the Bunker family (through Lucy's son Harry Thody).

## Family Life

George and Ruth held the license for **The Chequers**, Whipsnade's only public house. The Chequers had been operating since at least 1822, when it first appears in the county licensing register. In the early 19th century it was owned by Edward Burr's Dunstable Brewery; an 1843 sale description records it as a working rural pub with a bar, tap room, parlour, cellar, three bedrooms, cow house, two wood barns, and a two-acre paddock — a substantial property. Ruth and George held the licence from their 1867 marriage onward.

George died in 1884, twenty years before Ruth. She continued running the Chequers alone for another twenty years. Ruth died at the Chequers Inn on 22 May 1904, aged 84. Probate was granted on 7 October 1904.

On her death the licence passed to her nephew [[charles-cook-1843|Charles Cook (1843–1908)]] — son of her brother George Cook (1815) — who held it until his own death in 1908. His wife [[lois-brandsom-1843|Lois Cook (née Brandsom)]] then held the licence briefly before the inn passed out of the family network entirely. The Chequers remained in family hands for over sixty years — from Ruth and GBP Sr's 1867 marriage through to 1908.

Ruth's address in all census records from 1851 to 1901 is Whipsnade, specifically By Whipsnade Green — the same hamlet as the Matthews and Cook families, and as GBP Sr himself.

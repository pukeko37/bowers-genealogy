Cardington is a village in Bedfordshire, about two miles south-east of Bedford. It was the ancestral home of the Thody family, documented there from at least **1774** — when [[william-thody-1774|William Thody]] appears as a 7-year-old boy in the 1782 Cardington inhabitants survey, son of Samuel Thody and Elizabeth Pearsey. William enlisted in the 40th Regiment of Foot, fought in the Peninsular War and at Waterloo, and retired on a Chelsea pension in 1816. His son [[allen-thody-1813|Allen Thody]] was born in Spain in 1813 during the Peninsular War campaign; he returned to Cardington and spent his entire life there, marrying [[elizabeth-saunders-1811|Elizabeth Saunders]] at St. Mary Cardington on 19 October 1834. Their five children — including [[william-allen-thody-1850|William Allen Thody]] — were born at Cotton End, a hamlet within the Cardington registration district.

Elizabeth Saunders was also born in Cardington: baptised at St Mary's on 25 August 1811 as "Elizabeth, illegitimate Child of Rebecca Saunders, Widow, aged nine weeks." Her mother was **[[rebecca-arnold-1778|Rebecca Arnold]]**, daughter of **Samuel Arnold**, a farmer at Cotton End — the same hamlet. Rebecca had married [[joseph-sanders-1774|Joseph Sanders]] (a Cardington shoemaker) in 1799; he died in 1810. Rebecca died in August 1812, leaving Elizabeth an orphan at fourteen months. The Arnold family's brothers Robert and John Arnold were Samuel's co-executors; John Arnold and Elizabeth Arnold witnessed Elizabeth and Allen's 1834 wedding. Cotton End is thus the ancestral ground of both families who founded the Thody line.

Cardington lies approximately **20 miles north-east of Whipsnade** — within the same rural Bedfordshire county, and within what would have been overlapping marriage markets over the generations. The Thody-Cardington longevity (Allen and Elizabeth both reaching their mid-to-late eighties; their children averaging ~86 years) sits alongside the Whipsnade Cook-Birchmore longevity as expressions of the same regional genetic pool rather than independent coincidences. See [[longevity-analysis-2026-06-10]].

William Allen Thody's son [[clarence-james-thody-1884|Clarence Thody]] (killed France, 1918) is identified in CWGC records as "son of W.A. Thody of Dunstable" — the family's later connection to Dunstable, the market town serving Whipsnade, further links the two communities.

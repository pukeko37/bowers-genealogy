Maulden is a village in Bedfordshire, lying between Ampthill and Flitwick. It was the home of [[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]] and [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]], and the place where their infant grandson [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] was raised after the double bereavement of 1870.

## The Tall and Ward Family

[[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]] (1806–1870) was born in Ampthill and settled in Maulden, appearing there in the 1851 and 1861 censuses. [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] (1806–1894), born in nearby Flitwick, lived in Maulden for the entirety of her recorded life — every census from 1851 to 1891 places her there. She died in the village on 6 January 1894, aged 87.

Their daughter [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] (1844–1870) was raised in Maulden. She married [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] on Christmas Day 1868 at Howard Chapel, Bedford — a Nonconformist chapel, not the parish church — and their son [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick]] was born in October 1869 in Ampthill. See [[tall-ward-maulden]] and [[bunker-tall-1868]].

## The Summer of 1870

Rebecca died on 7 July 1870 in Ampthill, aged about 26. Her father Edward died nine days later, on 16 July, in Bedford. Two deaths in nine days left [[sarah-ward-1806|Sarah Ward]] alone in Maulden with her infant grandson Frederick, then less than a year old. Caleb Bunker departed for Matlock, Derbyshire.

Sarah raised Frederick in Maulden through at least the 1871 census. It was in these years, living among the Tall family in the building trade, that Frederick likely acquired the skills that would shape his working life — he became a bricklayer and builder. By 1881 Frederick had moved to Matlock to be with his father Caleb.

Sarah did not remain entirely alone. Her eldest daughter [[mary-ward-tall-1827|Mary Ward Tall]] (1827–1907) had married [[daniel-bass-1829|Daniel Bass]] in Maulden in 1855 and was widowed herself in 1868 — also in Maulden, with three young children: [[sarah-bass-1859|Sarah]] (1859–1951), Florence (1862), and Edward Ernest (1864–1936). The two widows, mother and daughter, shared the village for over twenty years. Mary Ward stayed in Maulden through the 1891 census — that year she is recorded living as *daughter* in her own mother's household — before moving to Lavendon, Buckinghamshire around 1901, to live with her eldest daughter Sarah, who had married James Lay there in 1887. See [[bass-tall-1855]] and [[lay-bass-1887]].

Sarah died in Maulden in January 1894, aged 87 — having outlived her husband, four of her six children, and the years of Frederick's childhood.

## The Geary Family

A further connection to Maulden emerges through Eliza Geary, who married [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] in 1892. Eliza's 1891 census entry places her in Maulden — as does her parents [[john-geary-1825|John Geary]] and [[lucy-morris-1826|Lucy Morris]]. The Gearys had spent their entire lives in Haynes, a few miles to the north, but moved to Maulden in their final years: John died in February 1895 and was buried at St Mary the Virgin, Maulden. See [[geary-morris-1848]].

This almost certainly explains how Frederick and Eliza met. Frederick had been raised in Maulden by Sarah Ward until c.1881 and may have retained connections there. When the Geary family arrived in Maulden by 1891, both families were in the same village — and the marriage followed in Ampthill in 1892. Two Maulden households, one marriage: the Tall–Ward and Geary–Morris families intersecting in this small Bedfordshire village.

The Gearys' Maulden connection was not accidental: John Geary's parents (Joseph Geary and Rhoda Spring Bottoms) had married in Clophill in 1824, a village immediately adjacent to Maulden. The family had always been rooted in this cluster of parishes.

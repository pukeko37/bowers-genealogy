Kensworth is a village in Hertfordshire, on the chalk ridge south of Dunstable and immediately adjacent to the Bedfordshire border. It was the birthplace and lifelong home of **Thomas Matthews** (christened 12 Aug 1798, Kensworth) — eldest son of [[thomas-matthews-1767|Thomas Matthews]] and [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]] of Whipsnade — and the ancestral home of the Matthews family in Hertfordshire.

## Key events at Kensworth

- **1798** — [[thomas-matthews-1797|Thomas Matthews the younger]] christened at Kensworth
- **1827–1832** — Thomas's first wife Mary bore children in Kensworth
- **1840** — [[thomas-matthews-1797|Thomas Matthews]] married [[elizabeth-hopkins-abt-1801|Elizabeth Hopkins]] at Kensworth, 1 November
- **1842** — [[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]] married [[sarah-garner-1822|Sarah Garner]] at Kensworth, 9 October — though Daniel himself was from Whipsnade; the choice of Kensworth reflects family connections there
- **1862** — Thomas Matthews the younger died at Kensworth, the same year as his father

The village is also where Thomas's children Mary, Sarah, and Charles grew up. Naomi Matthews (Thomas's daughter by Elizabeth Hopkins) later moved to Luton.

## The Kensworth Branch (LDS)

A branch of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints existed at Kensworth during the 1840s–1850s, part of the same circuit as the Edlesborough Branch (the reconstituted Whipsnade Branch) to the north-west. In LDS missionary journals of the period, the branch appears as "Hensworth Branch" — a consistent phonetic transcription that appears in Elder Job Smith's diary and in the baptismal record of [[mary-wilson-1779|Mary Wilson]], who was baptised there on **4 January 1851**. A Kensworth Branch Record (film no. 86979, Family History Library, Salt Lake City) is cited in the scholarly literature on this circuit. The branch's existence is further confirmed by Elder Smith's diary entry recording a visit to "the branches of Luton, Hensworth, and Eaton Bray" — all within the Bedfordshire Conference area, running along the same chalk ridge that connects Kensworth, Whipsnade, and Edlesborough.

The Matthews family's long connection with Kensworth (the family's birthplace before their move to Whipsnade ~1815) makes this the natural congregation for Mary Wilson. See [[bartholomew-byu-studies-buckinghamshire-lds-1849]].

## The Cook family

The Cook family has deep roots here too. [[david-cook-1816|David Cook]] — brother of [[ruth-cooke-1820|Ruth Cooke]] and probable grandfather of [[thomas-cook-1882|Thomas Edward Cook]] — married Elizabeth Frost at Kensworth on 8 May 1842. Thomas Edward Cook (1882–1955) and his wife [[alice-dumpleton-1883|Alice Mabel Dumpleton]] (1883–1940) are buried in the Kensworth churchyard — three generations after David's marriage in the same parish.

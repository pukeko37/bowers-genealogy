South Normanton is a coal-mining village in north Derbyshire, on the Nottinghamshire border. It was a major destination for the elder sons of [[joseph-bunker-1840|Joseph Bunker (1840–1914)]] and [[mary-ann-wilcox-1842|Mary Ann Wilcox]], who migrated here from Millbrook during the late 1880s and 1890s.

The brothers who settled in this area:

- **[[thomas-bunker-1862|Thomas Bunker (1862–1902)]]** — buried South Normanton April 1902
- **[[albert-bunker-1865|Albert Bunker (1865)]]** — at South Normanton 1901; Nottinghamshire 1911
- **[[george-bunker-1868|George Bunker (1868–1916)]]** — buried South Normanton 1916
- **[[james-bunker-1873|James Bunker (1873–1945)]]** — the earliest mover; married at South Normanton January 1893; explicitly recorded as Collier (1895)

Richard Bunker (1859) also appears in this area in 1891 before returning to Bedfordshire.

The migration was almost certainly driven by coal mining employment. South Normanton sat within the Derbyshire coalfield, where labour demand was high in the 1880s–1890s. James's early arrival — already settled and married there by January 1893 at age nineteen — suggests the move may have been facilitated by personal contacts or prior knowledge of the area, possibly through the Phoebe Alice Bunker–Fensome family who had been in Pilsley (a nearby Derbyshire village) since 1863 (see [[bunker-fensome-1863]]).

Several members of the family are also buried here: Thomas (1902) and George (1916) despite George dying at Grimsby.

Oakington is a village in South Cambridgeshire, approximately five miles north-west of Cambridge. It sits in low-lying fenland country between the River Cam and the fen-edge villages — the same agricultural landscape as Swavesey, Willingham, and Longstanton.

## The Thulborn family

Oakington was the lifelong home of the **Thulborn family** through the mid-nineteenth century. [[newman-thulborn-1821|Newman Thulborn]] (abt 1821–1888) was born here, worked here as an agricultural labourer his whole life, and died in the Chesterton registration district (which encompassed Oakington) in 1888. His parents **James Thulbourn** (1781–1851) and **Sarah Curtis** (1779–1867) had married at Oakington on 12 October 1800 and raised a large family there. Newman was among the younger of their children.

Newman married [[hannah-how-1824|Hannah How]] (abt 1824–unknown), born in Kingston, Cambridgeshire. All eight of their children were born in Oakington between 1851 and 1872. See [[thulborn-how-oakington]] for the full family.

The Thulborn name — spelled variously as Thurlborn, Thurlbone, Thurlbourn, Thurlbon — was common in the Oakington and Willingham area throughout this period, reflecting a family embedded in the local community over several generations.

## Connection to the Norman family

The Thulborn-Norman link came through [[emily-thulborn-1856|Emily Thulborn]] (21 Aug 1856–1922), Newman and Hannah's second child, who married [[tom-norman-1856|Tom Norman]] of Swavesey on 30 October 1877. Emily had left Oakington by 1871 (recorded at Willingham, likely in domestic service at age 15); she married into the Norman family of Swavesey and spent the rest of her life in Huntingdonshire. Her son **Albert Newman Norman** (1888–1970) carried the Thulborn name forward as a middle name.

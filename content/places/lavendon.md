Lavendon is a village in Buckinghamshire, situated near Olney and close to the Bedfordshire border. It was the home of James Lay — stone mason, Lavendon-born — and became the home of [[sarah-bass-1859|Sarah Bass]] after her marriage to James in April 1887.

## The Lay–Bass Family

[[sarah-bass-1859|Sarah Bass]] (1859–1951) grew up in Maulden, Bedfordshire, but crossed the county border on marriage. She and James settled in Lavendon and stayed for the rest of their lives. By 1891 they were running a general shop there alongside James's stone masonry; Sarah worked in the shop through at least 1911. Their four children — Herman Vivian, Beatrice Eva, Gertrude Florence, and Oliver Cecil — were all born in Lavendon. See [[lay-bass-1887]].

Sarah's mother [[mary-ward-tall-1827|Mary Ward Tall]] (1827–1907), widowed in Maulden, moved to Lavendon around 1901 to live with the family in her old age. She died there in April 1907. The 1901 census places Mary as "mother-in-law" in the Lay household — the last chapter of a long Maulden life ending in this Buckinghamshire village.

Kinsbourne Green is a small hamlet on the northern edge of Harpenden, strung along the A1081 Luton Road. In the early twentieth century it was quite detached from Harpenden; by the 1960s, when [[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Ron]] and [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy Bowers]] moved there, it was being absorbed into greater Harpenden while retaining a distinct rural character. It falls within the parish of Harpenden Rural, created in 1898 when the old Harpenden parish was split into urban and rural districts.

## Ron and Ivy's final English home (c.1962–1970)

[[roland-herbert-bunker-1898|Ron]] and [[ivy-helen-norman-1899|Ivy]] moved to Kinsbourne Green around 1962 from "Journey's End" in Harpenden proper. They lived here until their emigration to [[auckland|Auckland]] in 1970. The property had a private tennis court — unusual for the period, and not something either of them used, being then in their sixties. The tennis court suggests a substantial property, bought in part for what it represented.

The development they moved to was almost certainly the **Annables Estate** — 32 individually designed detached houses on two privately owned roads (Chamberlaines and Spring Road). The privately owned roads give the estate a private character, consistent with family memory of it as a gated community. The first house on the estate was built in the 1930s; individual plots were sold off over the following decades. *The specific street address has not been confirmed.*

## Character of the area

Kinsbourne Green in the 1960s was semi-rural and affluent — a step up even from Harpenden proper. The Luton Hoo Estate lay to the west. The hamlet had four pubs (only The Fox survives today), a small school (closed 1955), and a Wesleyan Methodist chapel. New house building was bringing in a professional class: when the church expanded its nave in 1968, it was specifically because the new housing had swelled congregation numbers.

The choice of Kinsbourne Green over central Harpenden continued the pattern of social ascent that had brought Ron and Ivy from London to Harpenden in the first place. See [[harpenden]] for the broader context.

## The Wesleyan Methodist chapel

In 1856 three Luton Wesleyans paid £5 for a plot at Kinsbourne Green and built a small chapel, which burned down and was rebuilt on the same foundations. It served the community for nearly 150 years, closing 28 April 2002; the building was demolished 2006 and houses built on the site. The Wesleyan presence at Kinsbourne Green connects quietly to the [[bunker-geary-1892|Bunker family]]'s documented Wesleyan Methodist background, though whether Ron had any awareness of or connection to this chapel is not known.

## Green Lawns Garage and the tennis courts

Green Lawns Garage, built c.1930, was a local landmark on the Luton Road. Behind the garage was a tea room and commercial tennis courts (opened June 1937, closed early 1950s) and a short-lived 18-hole golf course. These courts pre-date Ron and Ivy's arrival and were in any case public, not private. The tennis court at their property was a separate feature of their own house.

## Later development

The market garden (Kinsbourne Nursery, later Perry's) behind the Luton Road shops was sold in 1968 to builders Pearce and Barker and redeveloped as Kinsbourne Close — the major housing expansion came just as Ron and Ivy were preparing to leave. The area is now among the most expensive residential addresses in Hertfordshire, with detached properties averaging over £1.3m.

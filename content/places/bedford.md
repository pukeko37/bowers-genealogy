Bedford, the county town of Bedfordshire, appears in this research principally through the Bunker family during the 1850s–1880s, and through the marriage and death records associated with the Tall family.

## The Bunkers Arrive: 1852

When [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] died in Millbrook in 1852, his widow [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]] moved the family to Bedford — a note in George Bunker's record makes this migration explicit. At the time, the youngest child [[comfort-ann-bunker-1848|Comfort]] was four and [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb]] was seven. The older children would have been teenagers. The move from a rural village to the county town was a significant change.

## Dane Street, St Paul's Parish: 1861

The 1861 census places the Bunker household at **Dane Street, St Paul's parish**, Bedford. Elizabeth was head of household, widowed, aged 51, working as a **lace maker** — Bedford was a major centre of the Bedfordshire pillow lace industry and this is her first recorded occupation. Six of the nine children were still with her:

| Name | Age | Occupation |
|------|-----|------------|
| Lydia | 27 | House servant |
| Charles | 23 | Labourer |
| Martha | 20 | Bonnet sewer |
| Phoebe | 18 | Bonnet sewer |
| **Caleb** | **15** | **Shoe maker** |
| Ann (Comfort Ann) | 12 | Scholar |

The occupation picture is instructive: the trades here — lace making, bonnet sewing, shoe making — all reflect the domestic industries of mid-Victorian Bedfordshire and the boot and shoe corridor stretching into Northamptonshire. **Caleb's shoe making** at age 15 was almost certainly the beginning of an apprenticeship. The shoe and boot trade would prove formative: his son [[frederick-bunker-1869|Frederick Bunker]] spent part of the 1890s in **Kettering**, one of England's premier boot and shoe manufacturing centres — a connection that runs directly from Caleb's apprenticeship at Dane Street. See [[caleb-bunker-1861-census]].

In the same 1861 census, **[[samuel-plackett-lane-1841|Samuel Plackett Lane]]** (age 18) was a **boot maker apprentice** at **Hassett Street**, also in St Paul's parish — in the same census piece (RG09, piece 991) as the Dane Street household. His master was John Howard, a boot maker employing four men. Samuel and Caleb were in the same trade, in the same small parish, at the same time.

The three eldest sons — John, George, and David — were not in the household; they had already established independent residences by 1861.

## Bedford Bunkers: Persistent Presence

Three of the nine children settled in Bedford for significant periods:

- **[[john-watts-bunker-1831|John Watts Bunker]]** (1831–1899) — remained in the Bedford area; recorded in Bedford in 1871 and in Bedford Western Ward St Mary in 1881. He died in nearby Ampthill in 1899, never straying far from the family's Bedfordshire roots.
- **[[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]]** (1833–1922) — married [[samuel-plackett-lane-1841|Samuel Plackett Lane]] at **St Peter's Martin, Bedford** in 1863; widowed 1872; recorded in Bedford Western Ward St Paul in 1881; later moved to Barton Almshouse, Turvey. See [[bunker-lane-1863]].
- **[[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]]** (1836–1917) — married [[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]] in Bedford in 1858 and worked as a Storekeeper there through 1871 before moving the whole family to New Tupton, Derbyshire between 1872 and 1875. His brother [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb]] had already moved to Matlock in 1871; [[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe]] moved to nearby Pilsley at the same time as George. See [[bunker-pedder-1858]] and [[new-tupton-derbyshire]].

The other six children dispersed elsewhere — Peterborough, London, Luton, Derbyshire — but the Bedford cluster held for at least two of them into the 1880s. Elizabeth Watts herself died in 1868, sixteen years after the move, likely also in Bedford.

## Howard Chapel, Mill Street, and Foster Hill Road Cemetery

Howard Chapel was a Nonconformist chapel on Mill Street, Bedford, and is the religious anchor of the Tall family in the town. Edward Tall (carpenter), his probable son [[william-tall-1830|William Tall]], and his son [[frederick-charles-tall-1846|Frederick Charles Tall]] all appear in Howard Chapel's death register. The chapel was the Tall family's congregation for at least three decades.

**Foster Hill Road Cemetery** is where all three were actually buried. Howard Chapel's own burial ground was closed in 1855; from that date, the chapel continued its death register but interments went to Foster Hill Road Cemetery. This distinction matters: earlier wiki entries stated Edward was "buried at Howard Chapel," which is incorrect — he was buried at Foster Hill Road, registered at Howard Chapel.

Burials at Foster Hill Road from this family:
- [[frederick-charles-tall-1846|Frederick Charles Tall]], buried 25 May 1859 (aged 12), plot D9-76
- [[william-tall-1830|William Tall]], buried 14 July 1862 (aged ~32)
- [[edward-tall-1806|Edward Tall]], buried 19 July 1870 (aged ~63)

[[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]] and [[rebecca-tall-1844|Rebecca Tall]] were married at Howard Chapel on Christmas Day, 25 December 1868 — in the Tall family's own chapel. Rebecca herself was buried at Ampthill in July 1870 (not at Howard Chapel/Foster Hill Road). See [[bunker-tall-1868]].

The Tall family's Bedford addresses span three parishes and thirty years: Grey Friars Walk, St Paul's (Frederick Charles, 1859); Derby Street, St Peter's (William, 1862); Albert Street, St Peter's (Edward, 1870).

## In the 1870s–1880s

The period 1868–1880s was the most concentrated phase of Bedford activity in this research: the wedding at Howard Chapel (1868), Frederick's birth in Ampthill (1869), the two deaths (1870), and the lingering presence of John, Lydia, and George Bunker through the censuses of 1871 and 1881.

After that, the family lines move away. [[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb]] was already in Matlock from 1871. Frederick grew up in nearby Maulden before moving to Matlock and later Luton. John died in Ampthill in 1899. Lydia drifted to Turvey. George, Ann, and their children left for New Tupton, Derbyshire between 1872 and 1875 — the largest family unit to leave, taking three Bedford-born children with them into the Derbyshire coalfield.

## St Peter's Martin: The Lane–Bunker Marriage, 1863

[[samuel-plackett-lane-1841|Samuel Plackett Lane]] and [[lydia-bunker-1833|Lydia Bunker]] were married on **21 September 1863** at **St Peter's Martin, Bedford**. Samuel was 22, single; Lydia was 30, single. Samuel's father was George Lane. This marriage directly linked the Lane and Bunker families — two households connected by trade and parish since at least 1861. Samuel died in 1872 in Bedford, leaving Lydia a widow. See [[bunker-lane-1863]].

## Open Questions

- Where did Elizabeth Watts spend her final years and where did she die in 1868? The 1861 census places her at Dane Street, St Paul's; she presumably remained in Bedford until 1868 but no death record has been located.
- What drew the family to Bedford specifically in 1852 — were there existing connections, work opportunities, or kin in the town?
- Did the Bedford Bunkers (John, Lydia) have any documented contact with Caleb's Derbyshire household or Frederick's Maulden upbringing?
- What was the family's Nonconformist affiliation — Howard Chapel, Mill Street is the only evidence of it.
- Who was Samuel Lane's father George Lane, and are there other Lane family members in Bedford records?
- What was Albert John Lane (14, Maulden, 1871) doing in Samuel and Lydia's household — a younger relative of Samuel's?

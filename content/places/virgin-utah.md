Virgin (also known as Virgin City) is a settlement in Washington County, in the extreme south of Utah — canyon country on the Virgin River, some 300 miles south of Salt Lake City. It was one of the remotest LDS frontier settlements, irrigated from the river and surrounded by desert cliffs.

[[daniel-willis-matthews-1820|Daniel Willis Matthews]] (1820–1903) settled in the **Virgin City / Grafton area** after his arrival in Utah following the 1853 emigration. He farmed there for fifty years, appeared in the 1880 and 1900 censuses at Virgin or Grafton, and died at Virgin on **15 April 1903**, aged about 82. He is buried in the **Virgin Cemetery, Block C #066**.

[[william-abel-matthews-1848|William Abel Matthews Sr.]] (1848–1917), son of [[able-matthews-1813|Able Matthews]], also settled in Virgin after his father's death at Lehi and died there on **16 August 1917**.

The two Matthews men who ended their lives in this remote southern Utah valley — uncle and nephew, both born in Whipsnade, Bedfordshire — represent the furthest point of the family's emigration arc from the chalk downs of south England.

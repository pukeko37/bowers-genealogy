Dell Farm sits at the western end of Whipsnade village, reached by one of the two roads that cross the green. The other road leads to Hall Farm — where [[ellen-birchmore-1860|Ellen Birchmore]] worked as a cook in the 1870s–1880s before marrying [[charles-norman-1859|Charles Norman]]. The two farms define the working agricultural spine of the village green; the church, rectory, Chequers Inn, and the hamlet of Houchen End (By Whipsnade Green) occupy the spaces between them.

The farm's documented history runs from 1795 to the present and is recorded in the Bedford Borough Council community history archives.

## Ownership and Tenancy History

**Origins (to 1827).** The farm derives its name from Isaac Dell, who received it under the will of John Sutton (died 1795). Dell sold the property to **John Pedley** in 1827; the estate passed down the Pedley line as the Pedley Settled Estates.

**Macnamara ownership (1842–1915).** By 1842 the estate had passed to **Arthur Macnamara**, with Thomas Fawcett as tenant (1841 census: Fawcett, wife Mary, servant Jane Benson).

**Henry Perkins (1851–1871).** The tenant across three census years was **Henry Perkins**, farmer:
- 1851: 125 acres, five labourers, servant Mary Griffith
- 1861: 142 acres, six men and a boy
- 1871: 140 acres, five men and a boy, servant Fanny Minards

Perkins is the same man who acted as sole executor of [[thomas-batchelar-1803|Thomas Batchelar]]'s will — probate granted 5 March 1873, Batchelar having died at Whipsnade in December 1872. That Perkins, a Dell Farm farmer, was trusted to execute the will of Eliza Batchelor's father is a small but telling mark of the tight social web of the village.

**William Gladman (1882).** Tenant, farming 141 acres 2 roods 10 poles (including 3 acres in Eaton Bray). Building repairs in this period cost £866/14/8.

**Hain tenancy (1905–1915).** By 1905 the farm — now 132 acres — was let jointly to **James Hain** and **James Purton Turvey** at £132/7/- per annum. James Purton Turvey died on 13 February 1910 — his absence from the 1911 census was death, not departure. He is buried at St Mary Magdalene alongside his widow **Sarah Turvey** (née Bird, 1873–1956) and several of their children. [[james-hain-1877|James Hain]] (33, Farmer) was therefore sole occupant by 1911, living with his widowed father [[james-edward-hain-1832|James Edward Hain]] (private means), his sister Margaret D Hain, and his sister-in-law [[minnie-rose-bransom-1884|Minnie Rose Bransom]] (27, Housekeeper). James Edward Hain died later in 1911 and is buried at St Mary Magdalene, Whipsnade.

## The 1915 Pedley Auction

In November 1915 the [[pedley-settled-estates|Pedley Settled Estates]] — approximately 2,050 acres across Eaton Bray, Whipsnade, Totternhoe, Studham, Caddington, Markyate, and Flamstead — were auctioned in London by Joseph Stower of Chancery Lane. The Dell Farm lot comprised 125 acres, 3 roods, 39 perches.

The farmhouse at auction was described as a "superior residence" — brick and stucco with a slate roof — with five bedrooms, dining room, drawing room, kitchen, pantry, store cupboards, and cellar. Farm buildings included three barns, cow houses, calf pen, loose boxes, stables, trap house, hay store, wagon shed, piggery, and fold yards. Tenants had grazing rights for nine cows on Whipsnade Green.

**James Hain purchased the farm at this auction**, becoming owner rather than tenant. A 1926 valuation assessment records him as a "Good Farmer" — the only personal character note in the documentary record. The same assessment described the homestead as "badly arranged" despite its extent.

## Later History

In 1967 Luton County Borough purchased the farmhouse and approximately 30 acres; 20 acres were transferred to the National Trust. The farm buildings were converted to an Educational Activity Centre with overnight accommodation for children. Ownership passed to Bedfordshire County Council in 1974 and back to Luton in 1997 when it became a Unitary Council. Dell Farm continues to operate as an Outdoor Education Centre.

## A Further Connection: Cook and Turvey

In 1915, **[[albert-ralph-cook-1886|Albert Ralph Cook]]** — youngest son of Charles Cook (1843–1908) and Lois Brandsom, and thus a nephew of [[mary-saunders-cook-1848|Mary Saunders Cook]] — married **Hilda Kate Turvey**, daughter of the late James Purton Turvey. The two families who had farmed Dell Farm together (Hain and Turvey) and the Cook family of the Chequers Inn were thus joined by marriage a few years after Turvey's death. Their daughter Hilda Jessie carried "Cook" as a middle name throughout her life.

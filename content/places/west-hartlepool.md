West Hartlepool was a Victorian industrial port town on the County Durham coast, created by the Hartlepool Dock and Railway Company in 1835 as a purpose-built port adjacent to the ancient town of Hartlepool. By the 1870s it had grown rapidly into a significant coal and grain export centre with a large working-class population. The two Normans who settled here — [[george-norman-1851|George]] and [[richard-norman-1853|Richard]] — would have found a town quite unlike anything in Swavesey.

## The Norman brothers arrive, 1875

Both elder sons of [[thomas-norman-1827|Thomas Norman]] and [[ann-tabram-1829|Ann Tabram]] of Swavesey migrated to West Hartlepool in 1875, arriving together or within months of each other. Their simultaneous arrival and near-simultaneous marriages are the primary evidence that this was a joint decision rather than independent migration:

- **October 1875** — [[richard-norman-1853|Richard Norman]] married [[ann-elizabeth-randall-1854|Ann Elizabeth Randall]] in Hartlepool
- **7 December 1875** — [[george-norman-1851|George Norman]] married [[margaret-j-garry-1854|Margaret J Garry]] at **Christ Church, West Hartlepool**

Both families settled in **Stranton**, the inner township making up much of West Hartlepool, and are recorded there in the 1881 and 1891 censuses. Their households would have been within walking distance.

## The Norman families in West Hartlepool

**George Norman's family** ([[norman-garry-1875]]): George worked as Grocers Porter (1881), Grain Porter NE Railway (1891), and Malster at a brewery (1901) — following the port and industrial economy of the town. He and Margaret had ten or eleven children. Their son William was christened at **Lynne Street United Free Methodist Chapel** in 1879, placing the family in the Nonconformist tradition common among Durham working families. George died in September 1903 aged ~51.

**Richard Norman's family** ([[norman-randall-1875]]): Richard worked as a labourer across a long working life — labourer (1881), general labourer (1891), labourer at cement works (1901), general labourer for West Hartlepool Borough Council (1911). He and Ann had eleven children. Richard named his ninth child **George Norman** (1895) after his brother who lived nearby.

West Hartlepool was absorbed into the enlarged Hartlepool borough in 1967.

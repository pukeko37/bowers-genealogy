New Tupton is a village in North Derbyshire, part of the Tupton/Clay Cross area in the Chesterfield registration district. In the 1870s it was expanding industrial country: the North Midland Railway ran through the area, and the surrounding villages were home to collieries, ironworks, and the associated labouring population that served them.

## The Bunker convergence, c.1875

Around 1875 New Tupton was home to at least two branches of the Bunker family simultaneously — a coincidence that reveals the extent of sibling solidarity behind the Derbyshire migration.

**[[george-bunker-1836|George Bunker]]** (1836–1917) had moved his family from Bedford to New Tupton between 1872 and 1875. His wife [[ann-pedder-1838|Ann Pedder]] was from Westoning, Bedfordshire; they had three Bedford-born children when they left. Their son **Albert Bunker** was born in New Tupton ~1875 — the first Derbyshire-born child, placing the family in the village at that moment. But the stay was brief: George's next child, Amy, was born in **Derby** (~1878), and the 1881 census confirms the whole family in **St Peter's parish, Derby**. George had passed through New Tupton on his way to Derby, not settled there. Albert Bunker spent the rest of his life in Derby and died there on 11 October 1924. George worked as a General Labourer — a step down from his Storekeeper role in Bedford. See [[bunker-pedder-1858]].

**[[caleb-bunker-1845|Caleb Bunker]]** (1845–1918) had been in Matlock since 1871, some 15 miles south of Tupton, working first as a Porter and then rebuilding his shoe-making trade after his first wife's death. But his son **[[herbert-thomas-barnsley-bunker-1875|Herbert Thomas Barnsley Bunker]]** was born in New Tupton ~1875 — placing Caleb (and wife [[margaret-barnsley-1850|Margaret]]) in the village at the same time as George and Ann. The 1881 census records Herbert's birthplace as New Tupton before the family returned to Matlock. See [[bunker-barnsley-1871]].

**[[phoebe-alice-bunker-1843|Phoebe Alice Bunker]]** (1843–1923) settled at **Pilsley**, the adjacent village to Tupton, in the same period — not alone, but with her husband [[samuel-fensome-1842|Samuel Fensome]] and their young family. Samuel was a Bedfordshire man (born Ravensden, near Bedford) who had married Phoebe in August 1863. Their last child born in Bedford was Reginald Edwin (1873); their next child, Sidney Thomas, was born in Pilsley in 1877 — placing the Fensome migration between 1873 and 1877. The family remained in Pilsley through 1881, 1891, and 1901, raising four more children there. See [[bunker-fensome-1863]].

Pilsley and New Tupton are neighbouring settlements; the Fensome household was within easy walking distance of George's family throughout the late 1870s and 1880s.

Three of the nine children of [[james-bunker-1807|James Bunker]] and [[elizabeth-watts-1810|Elizabeth Watts]] of Millbrook — George, Phoebe, and Caleb — were all living within a mile or two of each other in north Derbyshire around 1875. This was the only period in their adult lives when they were this close together outside Bedfordshire.

## The migration sequence

1. **Caleb arrives first (1871)** — as a widower, seeking work in Matlock. He came alone and found labouring work before rebuilding his shoe trade. He was drawn to Matlock specifically, not the Tupton coalfield.
2. **George passes through New Tupton (~1875)** — George arrives with his whole family, Albert born there ~1875. But within two to three years he has moved the family on to **Derby** (Amy born there ~1878; family in St Peter's Derby at 1881 census). New Tupton was a staging post, not his settled destination.
3. **Phoebe and Samuel Fensome follow (1873–1877)** — Phoebe moved with her husband and their growing family to neighbouring Pilsley. Their last Bedford-born child was Reginald Edwin (1873); their first Pilsley-born child was Sidney Thomas (1877). The Fensomes settled in Pilsley and stayed there for two decades.
4. **The New Tupton moment (~1875)** — Caleb and Margaret's presence in New Tupton at Herbert's birth suggests Caleb visited or temporarily relocated to be near George. This is the convergence point — all three siblings in overlapping proximity simultaneously, even if only Caleb's presence is fleeting.
5. **Divergence** — George moves to Derby within a few years (~1877), well before the others. The Fensomes stay at Pilsley through 1901 before moving to Nottinghamshire. Caleb consolidates in Matlock and moves to Derby by 1891.

## After 1875

The three siblings do not appear to have lived this closely again. George died in Derby in 1917; Caleb died in Burton upon Trent in 1918 (both buried Derby). Phoebe died in Yorkshire in 1923. The Derbyshire chapter that began with Caleb's solitary arrival in Matlock in 1871 ended with all three dying within six years of each other, scattered across the East Midlands and North of England — none of them in the Bedfordshire village where they had all been born.

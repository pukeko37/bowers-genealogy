Great Gaddesden is a village in Hertfordshire, immediately east of Flamstead. The two parishes share a boundary, and families moved between them freely. The manor house, Gaddesden Place, was the seat of the Halsey family for several centuries.

## Batchelor-Bingham connection

The most significant link to our research is the **Bingham family**, who were a settled Great Gaddesden family across at least four generations (c.1742–1920). The senior couple in the churchyard listing are Rev William Bingham (1742–1819) and Agnata Bingham (1747–1827).

**Sarah Bingham** (born 1758), wife of [[joseph-batchelor-1755|Joseph Batchelor]] of Flamstead, is almost certainly from this family. The geography is exact — Flamstead and Great Gaddesden are adjacent parishes — and the timing fits. Sarah most likely fits as a younger sibling of Rev William, sharing parents born c.1715 who have not yet been identified. The Great Gaddesden parish registers (Hertfordshire Archives) should contain her baptism and would name her parents, extending the Batchelor ancestry through the maternal line. See [[batchelor-bingham-flamstead]] and [[st-john-baptist-great-gaddesden-findagrave]].

## Birchmore-Andrew connection

Thomas Birchmore (1729–1806) married **Sarah Andrew** at St John the Baptist, Great Gaddesden in 1758 — the same year Sarah Bingham was born, and possibly in the same register. By convention, the marriage in the bride's parish suggests Sarah Andrew was a Great Gaddesden native. No Andrew entries appear in the Find a Grave listing, but the churchyard coverage is sparse for the 18th century. The Andrew family may be the pre-Redding generation of the Flamstead Birchmore ancestry. See [[george-birchmore-1762]].

## St John the Baptist Churchyard

125 memorials recorded in Find a Grave (as of June 2026). Dominated by the Halsey baronets (lords of the manor, 1615–1954) and the Bingham family. Coverage appears thin for the 18th century and earlier. See [[st-john-baptist-great-gaddesden-findagrave]].
